The lymphatic system is the body's **fluid recovery service**. Capillaries leak about **20 L of plasma into the interstitium per day**; only ~17 L returns directly to the venous end. The other ~3 L must return via lymphatic vessels - or interstitial pressure builds and edema follows. Lymph also returns absorbed dietary fat (chyle, via lacteals), and routes antigen and immune cells through nodes for surveillance.

<Figure name="lymphatic/lymphatic-circulation" caption="Lymphatic circulation - blind-ended lymph capillaries collect interstitial fluid, drain into progressively larger vessels, pass through lymph nodes for filtration, and ultimately return to the venous circulation at the right lymphatic duct (right upper body) and the thoracic duct (everything else, into the left subclavian-jugular junction)." />

## Starling forces - the law that governs all edema

Fluid moves across a capillary wall by **filtration** vs **reabsorption**, governed by four pressures:

`Jv = Kf × [(Pc − Pi) − σ(πc − πi)]`

| Symbol | What it is | Direction it drives fluid |
|---|---|---|
| **P_c** - capillary hydrostatic pressure | "Push" of blood pressure | OUT of capillary |
| **P_i** - interstitial hydrostatic pressure | Tissue back-pressure (small, near 0) | INTO capillary |
| **π_c** - capillary oncotic pressure | "Pull" of plasma proteins (esp. albumin) | INTO capillary |
| **π_i** - interstitial oncotic pressure | Pull of interstitial protein (small) | OUT of capillary |
| **K_f** - filtration coefficient | Permeability × surface area | (modulates magnitude) |
| **σ** - reflection coefficient | How well capillary wall holds protein back (1 = perfect; 0 = freely leaky) | (modulates oncotic effect) |

At the **arteriolar end** of a capillary, P_c (~32 mmHg) > π_c (~25 mmHg) → net filtration out. At the **venous end**, P_c (~15 mmHg) < π_c (~25 mmHg) → net reabsorption in. The small daily mismatch is what the lymphatic system collects.

## The four causes of edema (memorize)

<Figure name="lymphatic/lymphatic-detail" caption="Lymph vessel detail. Edema arises when local capillary filtration exceeds lymphatic return capacity, from any of four mechanisms." />

Edema = excess interstitial fluid. Every cause is one of:

| Mechanism | Example | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **↑ Capillary hydrostatic pressure** | CHF, venous obstruction, DVT, pregnancy, prolonged standing | Pushes more fluid out |
| **↓ Plasma oncotic pressure** | Nephrotic syndrome (lose albumin in urine), cirrhosis (can't synthesize albumin), kwashiorkor, protein-losing enteropathy | Less pull holding fluid in |
| **↑ Capillary permeability** (↑ K_f, ↓ σ) | Burns, sepsis, anaphylaxis, ARDS | Protein leaks out → oncotic gradient lost |
| **Lymphatic obstruction** | Filariasis, post-mastectomy, malignancy, congenital | Cannot remove the 3 L/day overflow |

This 4-mechanism framework explains every edema syndrome. Pulmonary edema, hepatic ascites, peripheral edema, even cerebral edema all fit.

## Lymph composition and flow

<Figure name="lymphatic/lymphatic-overview" caption="Lymphatic overview - 2-3 L/day flows through the thoracic duct. Lymph composition similar to interstitial fluid (~2 g/dL protein, lower than plasma 7 g/dL)." />

**Lymph** has the same protein composition as interstitial fluid (~2 g/dL) - lower than plasma. **Chyle** is the fat-rich lymph leaving intestinal lacteals; milky in appearance from chylomicrons.

**Daily lymph flow:** 2-3 L into the thoracic duct.

**What drives lymph forward** (it has no central pump):
- **Skeletal muscle pump** - rhythmic contraction compresses vessels; one-way valves direct flow.
- **Respiratory pump** - inspiration drops intrathoracic pressure, sucks lymph centrally.
- **Smooth muscle in larger lymphatics** - spontaneous contractions at ~1-10 per minute.
- **Arterial pulsations** - adjacent arteries massage lymph vessels.
- **Negative central pressure** - thoracic duct empties against the low pressure of the great veins.

When any of these fail (immobility, paralysis), lymph stagnates → edema.

## Lymphatic anatomy in brief

<Figure name="lymphatic/lymphatic-overview" caption="Lymphatic system overview - lymph nodes (cervical, axillary, inguinal), lymphatic vessels, spleen, thymus. Thoracic duct drains everything below the diaphragm + left thorax/head/arm; right lymphatic duct drains right upper body." />

<Figure name="lymphatic/lymphatic-trunks-ducts" caption="Lymphatic trunks and ducts - the converging drainage system. The thoracic duct empties at the left subclavian-internal jugular junction; the right lymphatic duct empties on the right." />

| Vessel | Source | Drains to |
|---|---|---|
| **Lymph capillaries** | Most tissues (NOT CNS, bone, cartilage, cornea, epidermis) | Lymph collectors |
| **Right lymphatic duct** | Right head/neck, right arm, right thorax | Right subclavian-internal jugular junction |
| **Thoracic duct** | Everything else (lower body, left side, GI from cisterna chyli) | Left subclavian-internal jugular junction |

A blockage anywhere along this chain produces edema in the territory it drained.

## Lymphedema vs other edemas - a clinical distinction

| Feature | Lymphedema | Venous edema (CHF, DVT) | Hypoalbuminemia |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pitting | Non-pitting (late) | Pitting | Pitting |
| Skin | Thickened, brawny, peau d'orange | Often normal | Normal |
| Distribution | Unilateral (post-surgery), distal | Bilateral lower extremities | Generalized (anasarca) |
| Hair | Lost | Preserved | Preserved |
| Stemmer sign | Positive | Negative | Negative |
| Cause | Lymphatic obstruction | ↑ hydrostatic pressure | ↓ oncotic pressure |

**Stemmer sign** = inability to pinch the skin at the dorsum of the second toe. Pathognomonic for lymphedema.

<Clinical>
**Lymphedema management:**
- **Compression** (multilayer bandaging → custom garments) - the cornerstone.
- **Manual lymphatic drainage** by trained therapist.
- **Exercise** - muscle pump propels lymph.
- **Skin care** - prevents cellulitis (which damages further lymphatics → vicious cycle).
- **Pneumatic compression devices** for severe cases.
- **Lymphovenous anastomosis** or **vascularized lymph node transfer** - surgical options.
- **Antibiotics** - prophylactic in recurrent cellulitis.

Diuretics do not help isolated lymphedema and may worsen it (lymph protein content concentrates).
</Clinical>

## The Frank-Starling Forces in pulmonary capillaries

<Figure name="respiratory/alveolus" caption="Alveolar capillary - normal Pc only ~7 mmHg (vs ~25 πc) = strong net reabsorption keeps alveoli dry. Cardiogenic pulmonary edema = Pc rises; ARDS = permeability rises." />

Pulmonary capillaries are special: P_c is normally only ~7 mmHg (vs ~25 in systemic capillaries) and π_c is the same ~25 mmHg → strong net reabsorption. The lung is built to stay dry.

**Pulmonary edema** can be:
- **Cardiogenic** - ↑ P_c from LV failure / mitral stenosis → P_c rises above ~20-25 mmHg → fluid floods alveoli. Crackles, frothy sputum, S3 gallop. BNP elevated. Treat with IV diuretic, nitrates (preload reduction), positive-pressure ventilation.
- **Non-cardiogenic (ARDS)** - ↑ permeability from sepsis, aspiration, pneumonia, pancreatitis. Normal P_c, very leaky capillaries. Treat with **lung-protective ventilation** (low tidal volume 6 mL/kg, plateau pressure < 30 cmH₂O), prone positioning, address underlying cause. Diuretics don't help much.

## Chylothorax and chylous ascites

Lymph leakage from the thoracic duct into the pleural space (chylothorax) or abdomen (chylous ascites) gives milky fluid with triglycerides > 110 mg/dL.

**Causes:**
- **Trauma / surgery** - especially thoracic surgery, central line placement
- **Malignancy** - lymphoma classic
- **Tuberculosis** of mediastinal nodes
- **Congenital lymphatic malformation**

Treatment: low-fat / medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) diet (MCTs bypass lacteals, go straight into portal vein), drainage, octreotide (reduces splanchnic lymph flow), surgical ligation if persistent.

## Filariasis - parasitic lymphedema

*Wuchereria bancrofti* is a nematode transmitted by mosquitoes (especially Culex). Adult worms live in lymphatic vessels → chronic inflammation → fibrosis → severe limb or scrotal lymphedema ("elephantiasis"). Treat with diethylcarbamazine or ivermectin + doxycycline (kills Wolbachia endosymbiont that the worm depends on).

## Recap of the immune/filtration role of nodes

Even though this page focuses on flow, remember that lymph passing through nodes is filtered by macrophages and presents antigen to lymphocytes - the bridge between innate and adaptive immunity. (See **innate-immunity** and **adaptive-immunity** for the immune detail.)

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Edema vignette patterns:**
- Bilateral pitting LE edema + JVD + S3 + crackles → CHF (↑ P_c)
- 6-year-old with periorbital edema + 3+ proteinuria + hypoalbuminemia → minimal change disease nephrotic syndrome (↓ π_c)
- Post-mastectomy + ipsilateral arm swelling that won't pit → lymphedema (lymphatic obstruction)
- Tense ascites + caput medusae + spider angiomas + low albumin → cirrhosis (↑ P_c portal + ↓ π_c hepatic)
- Sudden facial swelling + urticaria + wheeze → anaphylaxis (↑ permeability)
- Newborn with congenital lymphedema of dorsum of feet, webbed neck → **Turner syndrome (45,X)**
- Bilateral lower extremity non-pitting edema in a child from sub-Saharan Africa → filariasis
</HighYield>
