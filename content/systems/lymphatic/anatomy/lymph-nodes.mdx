Bean-shaped organs (~1–25 mm) clustered along lymphatic vessels. They filter lymph and serve as the meeting ground for **antigen + naïve lymphocytes**. There are 500–600 in the adult body.

<Figure name="lymphatic/lymph-node-fyi-slide" caption="Lecture slide showing the cortex, paracortex, medulla, and the direction of afferent and efferent lymph flow." />

## Internal architecture (memorize this)

Lymph enters via **afferent vessels** → flows through the **subcapsular sinus** → **cortex** → **paracortex** → **medullary cords/sinuses** → exits via a single **efferent vessel** at the **hilum**.

| Zone | Cells | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Cortex (outer) | B cells in **follicles**; active follicles have **germinal centers** | B-cell activation, somatic hypermutation, class switching |
| Paracortex | T cells, dendritic cells | T-cell priming; expands in viral infections |
| Medulla | Plasma cells, macrophages | Antibody secretion; final filtration |

## Major node groups

- **Cervical** (head & neck drainage)
- **Axillary** (upper limb + breast - clinically important for breast cancer staging)
- **Mediastinal** (lungs)
- **Para-aortic + iliac** (abdomen, pelvis)
- **Inguinal** (lower limb + perineum)

## Lymphatic flow, big picture

All lymph eventually returns to the venous system:
- **Thoracic duct** drains left side, abdomen, and both legs → empties into the **left subclavian–internal jugular junction**.
- **Right lymphatic duct** drains right head/neck, right arm, right thorax → empties into the **right subclavian–internal jugular junction**.

<Clinical>
**Lymphadenopathy** patterns:
- *Localized* → drains an infected or malignant region (cervical node in pharyngitis; axillary in breast cancer).
- *Generalized* → systemic illness (mononucleosis, HIV, lymphoma, SLE).

**Sentinel lymph node biopsy**: inject dye/tracer near a tumor → first node it reaches is the sentinel. If negative, downstream nodes are likely negative too - used in melanoma and breast cancer.

**Lymphedema**: chronic obstruction (post-axillary dissection, filariasis, congenital). Stagnant interstitial fluid → fibrosis → "non-pitting" swelling.
</Clinical>

## Every lymphoid structure - what it does and why it matters

### Lymph node compartments

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Capsule** | Dense connective tissue covering | Holds the node together; thickens in chronic inflammation |
| **Subcapsular sinus** | Space beneath capsule receiving afferent lymph | First place lymph enters; macrophages here filter incoming antigens |
| **Cortex (outer)** | Houses **B-cell follicles** | Primary follicles (resting) or secondary follicles (with germinal centers - active B-cell proliferation) |
| **Germinal center** | Site of B-cell affinity maturation, somatic hypermutation, class switching | Only here does antibody quality improve; lymphomas often originate from these cells |
| **Mantle zone** | Ring of resting B cells around the germinal center | Source of mantle cell lymphoma |
| **Paracortex** | T-cell zone with high endothelial venules (HEVs) | Naïve lymphocytes enter the node via HEVs; expands in viral infections (paracortical hyperplasia) |
| **High endothelial venules (HEVs)** | Specialized post-capillary venules with cuboidal endothelium | Express addressins that bind lymphocyte L-selectin → site of lymphocyte trafficking into nodes |
| **Medullary cords** | Strings of plasma cells, macrophages, B cells | Plasma cells secrete antibody here |
| **Medullary sinuses** | Channels through medulla | Final filtration step before lymph exits |
| **Afferent lymphatic vessels** | Enter at convex surface; multiple per node | Bring lymph + antigens + APCs from upstream tissues |
| **Efferent lymphatic vessel** | Exits at hilum; usually single | Carries filtered lymph downstream + activated lymphocytes back to circulation |
| **Trabeculae** | Fibrous extensions of capsule into node | Provide structural framework + conduit for vessels |

### Lymphoid organs (primary vs secondary)

| Organ | Type | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bone marrow** | Primary | B-cell maturation; also hematopoiesis | Site of B-cell negative selection; failure → SCID (variable forms), agammaglobulinemia |
| **Thymus** | Primary | T-cell maturation, including positive + negative selection | Largest in childhood, involutes in adulthood; absent in DiGeorge syndrome (22q11) → T-cell deficiency |
| **Lymph nodes** | Secondary | Filter lymph; site of adaptive immune activation | ~500–600 in body; clinically palpable enlargement = lymphadenopathy |
| **Spleen** | Secondary | Filters blood; site of immune surveillance; reservoir for monocytes/platelets | **Splenectomy → ↑ risk of encapsulated bacterial sepsis** (S. pneumo, H. flu, N. meningitidis) |
| **Tonsils (Waldeyer's ring)** | Secondary (MALT) | Sample oropharyngeal antigens | Includes palatine, pharyngeal (adenoid), lingual, tubal |
| **Peyer's patches** | Secondary (GALT) | Sample gut luminal antigens | Where typhoid (S. typhi) classically invades; M cells overlie them |
| **Appendix** | Secondary (GALT) | Lymphoid reservoir of gut microbiota | Vestigial-but-not-quite; involvement in IgA priming |
| **MALT (mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue)** | Secondary | Mucosal immunity across all mucous membranes | Source of MALT lymphoma (H. pylori in stomach) |

### Spleen structures

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Red pulp** | Cords (of Billroth) + venous sinusoids | Filters RBCs - old/damaged ones removed (splenectomy → Howell-Jolly bodies in RBCs) |
| **White pulp** | Periarterial lymphatic sheath (PALS - T cells) + follicles (B cells) | Mounts adaptive immune response to blood-borne antigens |
| **Marginal zone** | Border of red and white pulp | Houses specialized B cells and macrophages - first to encounter blood antigens; site of marginal-zone lymphoma |
| **Splenic artery + vein** | Vascular supply (artery from celiac trunk; vein joins SMV to form portal vein) | Splenomegaly in portal hypertension; splenic artery aneurysms in pregnancy |

### Lymphatic vessel anatomy

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Lymphatic capillaries** | Blind-ended; collect interstitial fluid | One-way valves at endothelial junctions; key in fluid balance |
| **Collecting lymphatics** | Larger vessels with valves and smooth muscle | Propel lymph against gravity |
| **Cisterna chyli** | Dilated origin of thoracic duct in abdomen | Receives intestinal trunks rich in chyle (fat from chylomicrons) |
| **Thoracic duct** | Drains lower body + left upper body | Empties at left subclavian–IJ junction; injury → chylothorax |
| **Right lymphatic duct** | Drains right head/neck + right arm + right thorax | Empties at right subclavian–IJ junction |
| **Cervical nodes** | Head/neck drainage | Mononucleosis, dental infections, TB |
| **Axillary nodes** | Upper limb + breast drainage | Breast cancer staging - sentinel lymph node biopsy |
| **Mediastinal nodes** | Lung drainage | Lung cancer staging on PET/CT |
| **Para-aortic nodes** | Abdominal viscera, gonads | Testicular cancer drains here (not inguinal!) |
| **Inguinal nodes (superficial + deep)** | Lower limb + perineum + vulva | Vulvar cancer staging |

### Cells of immunity (functional summary)

| Cell | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Neutrophil** | First responder; phagocytosis | Acute bacterial infection; pus = dead neutrophils |
| **Eosinophil** | Anti-parasitic; allergic inflammation | Elevated in helminth infection, asthma, drug reactions |
| **Basophil + mast cell** | Release histamine, heparin | Type I hypersensitivity; mast cell in tissue, basophil in blood |
| **Monocyte/macrophage** | Phagocytosis, antigen presentation, cytokine release | Differentiates into tissue macrophages (Kupffer, microglia, alveolar) |
| **Dendritic cell** | Most potent antigen-presenting cell | Bridge innate → adaptive immunity in lymph nodes |
| **Natural killer (NK) cell** | Kill cells lacking MHC I or expressing stress ligands | Anti-viral, anti-tumor - without B/T-cell specificity |
| **CD4⁺ T helper** | Subtypes Th1, Th2, Th17, Treg, Tfh | Directs every other adaptive immune response |
| **CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cell** | Kills MHC-I-presented antigen targets | Antiviral and antitumor cytotoxicity |
| **B cell** | Antibody-secreting precursor; APC | Mature in marrow; differentiate into plasma cells or memory B cells |
| **Plasma cell** | Terminal antibody factory | Lives days–months; multiple myeloma when malignant |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX nursing priorities:**
- **Post-mastectomy lymphedema prevention**: no BP / IV / blood draws in affected arm; elevate limb; compression sleeve; avoid cuts, burns, tight jewelry.
- **Neutropenic precautions** (ANC &lt;500): private room (if possible), no fresh flowers, no raw fruits/vegetables, no live vaccines, strict hand hygiene.
- **Splenectomy patient**: lifelong infection risk; vaccinate against **encapsulated organisms** (pneumococcal, Hib, meningococcal); fever = emergency.
- **Lymph node assessment**: palpate while patient relaxed; mobile + tender = inflammatory; **fixed + firm + painless** = concerning for malignancy.
- **Bone marrow biopsy**: prone position, pressure dressing, monitor for bleeding.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Lymphoma patterns:**
- **Hodgkin lymphoma**: contiguous spread; **Reed-Sternberg cells** (CD15+, CD30+); bimodal age; **B symptoms** (fever, night sweats, weight loss) imply worse prognosis.
- **Non-Hodgkin lymphoma**: discontiguous spread; multiple subtypes. **Burkitt** (Africa, EBV, t(8;14) c-myc, "starry sky"); **Follicular** (t(14;18) BCL-2); **DLBCL** most common adult.
- **Splenic functions** (4): **filter** (old RBCs), **immune** (encapsulated organism response), **storage** (platelets), **hematopoiesis** (fetal).
- **Howell-Jolly bodies** in RBC smear = asplenia.
- **Lymphoid vs myeloid**: lymphoid → B, T, NK cells; myeloid → RBC, neutrophil, basophil, eosinophil, monocyte, platelet.
</HighYield>

### Spleen anatomy

<Figure name="lymphatic/spleen" caption="Spleen - bean-shaped intraperitoneal organ in the LUQ. Hilum receives splenic artery + drains splenic vein; red pulp filters blood (cords of Billroth + sinusoids); white pulp houses lymphocytes around central arterioles (PALS, follicles, marginal zone)." />

The spleen sits in the **left hypochondrium** at ribs **9–11**, behind the stomach and lateral to the left kidney. It's intraperitoneal, suspended by the **gastrosplenic** (with short gastric vessels) and **splenorenal** (with splenic vessels + tail of pancreas) ligaments. Normally not palpable - palpation suggests **2× normal size** (splenomegaly).

Functions to memorize:
1. **Filter old/damaged RBCs** (asplenia → Howell-Jolly bodies, target cells, acanthocytes)
2. **Immune surveillance of bloodborne pathogens** (asplenia → encapsulated organism sepsis risk)
3. **Reservoir** for platelets (~30%) and monocytes
4. **Hematopoiesis** in fetus + extramedullary in disease

<Clinical>
**Splenic rupture**: blunt abdominal trauma (LUQ); referred pain to **left shoulder (Kehr sign)** - phrenic nerve.

**Felty syndrome**: triad of RA + splenomegaly + neutropenia.

**Hereditary spherocytosis**: spectrin/ankyrin mutation → small spherical RBCs → trapped + destroyed in spleen → splenomegaly + hemolytic anemia; splenectomy curative.
</Clinical>
