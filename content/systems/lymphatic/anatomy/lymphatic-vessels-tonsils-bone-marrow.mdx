This page covers the structures the lecture deck treats as essential but that are easy to lose when lymphatic content gets reduced to only nodes and spleen: **lymphatic vessels, tonsils, and bone marrow**.

## Lymphatic vessels

<Figure name="lymphatic/lymph-vessels-slide" caption="Lecture slide showing the thoracic duct and the low-pressure vessel network that carries lymph, immune cells, and absorbed lipids." />

The lecture highlights three vessel features:

- **Open-ended capillary beginnings** that collect interstitial fluid
- **Valves** that keep lymph moving one way
- **Low flow rate**, which is why skeletal-muscle pumping matters so much for return

The **thoracic duct** is the largest lymphatic vessel and returns most of the body's lymph to the venous system.

## Tonsils

<Figure name="lymphatic/tonsils-slide" caption="Lecture slide showing the palatine tonsils where incoming pharyngeal antigens first meet mucosal immune tissue." />

<Figure name="lymphatic/tonsils-adenoids" caption="Waldeyer's lymphatic ring guarding the entrance to the upper aerodigestive tract (Blausen). Adenoid (pharyngeal tonsil) at the back of the nasopharynx; palatine tonsils at the sides of the oropharynx (the ones visible on exam); lingual tonsil at the back of the tongue." />

The tonsils are exposed directly to the pharynx, so they act like **front-door immune sentries** for inhaled and ingested pathogens. They are part of **MALT** and are often a patient's most visible lymphoid tissue during infection.

## Bone marrow

<Figure name="lymphatic/bone-marrow-slide" caption="Lecture slide showing vascular marrow packed with hematopoietic cells." />

Bone marrow is the **production floor** for blood cells:

- **RBCs**
- **WBCs**
- **Platelets**

In immune-system terms, marrow is also a **primary lymphoid organ** because **B-cell maturation** begins there.

## Immune-cell lineages

<Figure name="lymphatic/immune-cells-slide" caption="Lecture slide tracing lymphoid and myeloid differentiation from a shared hematopoietic stem cell." />

The lineage slide ties the anatomy together:

- **Lymphoid lineage** gives rise to **B cells, T cells, plasma cells, and NK cells**.
- **Myeloid lineage** gives rise to **neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils, monocytes/macrophages, mast cells**, and the erythroid-megakaryocyte branch.

That is why bone marrow belongs in the lymphatic block even though it is also part of the skeletal system.
