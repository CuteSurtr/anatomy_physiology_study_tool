The **spleen** and **thymus** are the two **encapsulated lymphoid organs** that aren't nodes. The spleen filters blood; the thymus matures T cells. They both shrink with age, both have specific surgical landmarks, and both have unique disease patterns.

<Figure name="lymphatic/spleen" caption="External shape of the spleen: the smooth diaphragmatic surface (left) and the visceral surface (right), where the groove of the hilum admits the splenic artery and drains the splenic vein." />

<Figure name="lymphatic/thymus" caption="The thymus in situ in a newborn - a large two-lobed gland lying on the pericardium behind the manubrium, between the lungs and below the thyroid. In an adult this space holds mostly fat. (Gray's Anatomy 1918.)" />

## Spleen - anatomy

<Figure name="lymphatic/spleen-detail" caption="Spleen in section. The middle panel places it under the left ribs against the diaphragm with the splenic artery and vein at the hilum; the right panel enlarges the parenchyma - dark red pulp under the capsule and trabeculae, with white pulp cuffing an arteriole. The micrograph below labels the trabecula, marginal zone, central arteriole, germinal centre, venous sinus and red pulp." />

- **Location**: LUQ, intraperitoneal, posterior to ribs **9–11** on the left side. Long axis follows the 10th rib.
- **Size**: ~12 cm long, ~150 g; varies with body habitus + age.
- **Surfaces**: diaphragmatic (smooth) + visceral (with impressions: gastric, renal, colic).

### Ligaments + relations
| Ligament | Connects | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Gastrosplenic** | Spleen ↔ greater curvature of stomach | Short gastric + left gastroepiploic vessels |
| **Splenorenal (lienorenal)** | Spleen ↔ left kidney | **Splenic artery + vein + tail of pancreas** |
| **Phrenicocolic** | Diaphragm ↔ splenic flexure | Supports spleen inferiorly |

### Blood supply
- **Splenic artery** - largest branch of the celiac trunk; tortuous course along superior pancreas. Gives off **short gastric** + **left gastroepiploic** + **pancreatic** branches before entering hilum.
- **Splenic vein** - runs posterior to the pancreas; joins **superior mesenteric vein** to form the **portal vein**.

<Clinical>
**Splenic rupture** - most commonly from blunt abdominal trauma (left lower rib fractures, MVC). Causes **Kehr sign**: referred pain to **left shoulder** (diaphragmatic irritation via phrenic C3–C5). Surgical or angiographic embolization. Splenectomy a last resort because of **post-splenectomy sepsis (OPSI)** risk - vaccinate against encapsulated organisms (S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae b, N. meningitidis).
</Clinical>

## Spleen - microanatomy

The splenic artery branches into **trabecular arteries** that run in the connective-tissue trabeculae; each gives off a **central arteriole**, and the sleeve of lymphoid tissue around that arteriole is the white pulp. Everything else is red pulp:

| Zone | Location | Composition | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Red pulp** | ~80% of volume | Splenic cords (of Billroth) + sinusoids | **Filters blood** - macrophages remove old/damaged RBCs ("erythrophagocytosis"), opsonized particles, encapsulated bacteria |
| **White pulp** | ~20% of volume | PALS (T-cell area around central artery) + lymphoid follicles (B-cell areas) + marginal zone | **Immune surveillance** of blood-borne antigens |

The **marginal zone** between red and white pulp is critical - it's where antigen-presenting macrophages and dendritic cells trap blood-borne antigens and present them to B + T cells. **Splenic marginal-zone lymphoma** arises here.

### Functions of the spleen - three Fs
1. **Filtration** - removes senescent RBCs (~120 days old), damaged cells (sickle, spherocytes), Howell-Jolly bodies, Heinz bodies, parasites.
2. **Fight infection** - synthesizes opsonizing antibodies (especially against encapsulated organisms); houses ~30% of body's lymphocytes.
3. **Form blood cells** - extramedullary hematopoiesis in fetal life + in adults with severe anemia or marrow infiltration.

### Splenomegaly causes (memory aid: "CHIASMA")
- **C**HF (congestion)
- **H**ematologic (CML, AML, lymphoma, hemolytic anemia)
- **I**nfectious (mononucleosis = EBV, malaria, kala-azar)
- **A**utoimmune (SLE, RA → Felty)
- **S**torage diseases (Gaucher, Niemann-Pick)
- **M**etabolic / portal hypertension (cirrhosis)
- **A**myloid

## Thymus - anatomy

<Figure name="lymphatic/thymus-lobule" caption="A single thymic lobule at high magnification (NIH BioArt). The dense outer rim is cortex and the paler centre is medulla; the two insets name every resident cell type, including the whorled Hassall's corpuscle in the medulla. Far more detail than you need to memorise - use it to see that cortex and medulla contain different epithelial cells, which is why selection differs between them." />

- **Location**: **superior + anterior mediastinum**, behind the manubrium of sternum. Two lobes.
- **Size**: maximal at puberty (~35 g) → involutes with age + replaced with fat ("fatty involution"). Adult thymus is mostly remnant.
- **Origin**: **3rd pharyngeal pouch** (with the inferior parathyroids - explains DiGeorge).

### Blood supply
- Inferior thyroid + internal thoracic arteries (small + variable).
- Venous drainage: brachiocephalic veins.

### Microanatomy - two zones + Hassall corpuscles

<Figure name="lymphatic/thymus-detail" caption="Thymus microanatomy. The micrograph shows the fibrous capsule sending trabeculae inward to divide the gland into lobules, each with a dark, densely packed cortex and a paler medulla. The schematic below names the cells: thymocytes packed among cortical epithelial cells, and in the medulla the medullary epithelial cells, dendritic cells and macrophages that carry out negative selection." />
| Zone | Composition | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Cortex** | Dense immature thymocytes (CD4+CD8+) + cortical epithelial cells | **Positive selection** - keep T cells whose TCR can bind self-MHC weakly |
| **Medulla** | Mature thymocytes (CD4+ or CD8+) + medullary epithelial cells + Hassall corpuscles | **Negative selection** - delete T cells that bind self-peptides strongly (mediated by AIRE transcription factor presenting tissue antigens) |

**Hassall corpuscles** = concentric whorls of keratinised medullary epithelial cells. Histologically they are the giveaway that a section is thymus. Their function is not settled; the leading proposal is that they help induce regulatory T cells via TSLP.

### The two-step T-cell selection
1. **Positive selection** (cortex): TCRs that can engage self-MHC → survive. The rest die by neglect.
2. **Negative selection** (medulla): TCRs that strongly bind self-peptide on self-MHC → deleted (clonal deletion) → prevents autoimmunity. Mediated by AIRE.

Failure of AIRE → **APECED / APS-1**: autoimmune polyendocrinopathy syndrome.

<Clinical>
**Myasthenia gravis** is often associated with **thymic hyperplasia (65%)** or **thymoma (15%)**. Anti-AChR antibodies; ptosis + diplopia + fatigable weakness. **Thymectomy** can induce remission, especially in younger patients with generalized MG.

**Thymoma associations**: myasthenia gravis (#1) + pure red cell aplasia + hypogammaglobulinemia (Good syndrome) + lupus.

**DiGeorge syndrome (22q11.2 deletion)** - failure of 3rd + 4th pouches → thymic aplasia (T-cell deficiency) + parathyroid aplasia (hypocalcemia) + cardiac outflow defects + cleft palate. **"CATCH-22"**.
</Clinical>

## Function reference - every component

| Structure | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Splenic white pulp PALS** | T-cell zone | Site of T-cell activation; rich in dendritic cells |
| **Splenic lymphoid follicles** | B-cell zone | Germinal centers form in immune response; site of class switching |
| **Marginal zone** | Interface between red + white pulp | Encapsulated organism trapping; site of marginal-zone lymphoma |
| **Splenic sinusoids** | Discontinuous endothelium with slits | Filter cells through narrow gaps; deformability test |
| **Splenic macrophages** | Phagocytose effete RBCs + opsonized particles | Loss = Howell-Jolly bodies, target cells, ↑ susceptibility to encapsulated bacteria |
| **Accessory spleens** | ~10–15% of people; small splenic tissue near hilum or gastrocolic ligament | Cause of recurrent ITP after splenectomy; must remove all during therapeutic splenectomy |
| **Thymic cortex** | Positive selection | T-cell tolerance failure here → no tolerance to self-MHC |
| **Thymic medulla + AIRE** | Negative selection | AIRE mutation → APECED |
| **Hassall corpuscles** | Whorls of keratinised medullary epithelial cells; proposed to help induce regulatory T cells | The histological marker that identifies a section as thymus |
| **Thymic Hassall corpuscle keratin** | Marker of medullary epithelial cells | - |
| **Thymopoietin / thymosin** | Hormones promoting T cell maturation | - |

## Why this matters

<HighYield exam="boards">
- **Post-splenectomy sepsis (OPSI)** is fatal in ~50% - encapsulated organisms (S. pneumoniae most common). **Vaccinate**: pneumococcal (PCV13 + PPSV23), Hib, meningococcal. Daily prophylactic penicillin in children. Watch for fever lifelong.
- **Howell-Jolly bodies** on blood smear = functional asplenia (post-splenectomy, sickle cell auto-infarction, celiac).
- **Sickle cell auto-splenectomy**: repeated infarctions by 5–10 years of age → functional asplenia → susceptibility to encapsulated organisms.
- **Spleen vs liver** for old RBCs: spleen for normal senescence (extravascular hemolysis); liver/reticuloendothelial picks up the rest (Kupffer cells).
- **Felty syndrome**: triad of **RA + splenomegaly + neutropenia**.
- **Thymic involution by 20s** - only fatty remnants by 40s. Persistent thymus on chest CT in adult = think thymoma or hyperplasia.
- **Hereditary spherocytosis** - splenectomy is curative (removes site of destruction). RBCs survive normally afterward; do it after age 6.
</HighYield>
