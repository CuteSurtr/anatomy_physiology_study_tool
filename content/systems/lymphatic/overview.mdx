Two intertwined jobs. **Lymphatic drainage**: lymph capillaries pick up interstitial fluid the venous side of capillaries failed to reabsorb (~3 L/day), filter it through nodes, and return it to the venous circulation via the thoracic duct (left side and lower body) and right lymphatic duct. **Immunity**: lymphoid organs house leukocytes - **primary** (red marrow, thymus) where lymphocytes mature, and **secondary** (lymph nodes, spleen, MALT, tonsils) where they meet antigen.

## Three structural layers

The system is easiest to hold in your head as three layers:

- **Vessels and ducts** move fluid and chyle.
- **Secondary lymphoid organs** - lymph nodes, spleen, tonsils and the rest of MALT - are where circulating lymphocytes *meet antigen*.
- **Primary lymphoid organs** - red bone marrow and thymus - are where lymphocytes are *made and matured* before they ever see a foreign antigen.

The primary/secondary split is the one distinction to get right: B cells mature in **bone marrow**, T cells mature in the **thymus**, and both then patrol the secondary organs.
