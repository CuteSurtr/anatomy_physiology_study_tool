## The conducting zone

<Figure name="respiratory/trachea-detail" caption="Trachea (OpenStax) - 10-12 cm tube from larynx to carina (T4-T5). C-shaped hyaline cartilage rings prevent collapse (open posteriorly where the trachealis smooth muscle bridges; this lets the esophagus distend). Bifurcates into right + left primary bronchi - right is wider, shorter, and more vertical, so aspiration goes right." />

- **Trachea** (C-shaped cartilage rings, ~10–12 cm) bifurcates at the **carina** (T4/T5) into right and left **main bronchi**. The right main bronchus is wider and more vertical - **aspirated objects preferentially go right**.
- **Lobar bronchi** (3 right, 2 left) → **segmental bronchi** (10 right, 8–10 left, one per bronchopulmonary segment) → **bronchioles** → **terminal bronchioles**.
- No gas exchange here - only conduction, humidification, filtration. Cartilage progressively disappears; smooth muscle increases.

## The respiratory zone

<Figure name="respiratory/respiratory-zone" caption="Structures of the respiratory zone - where gas exchange actually happens. Conducting zone ends at terminal bronchioles. Respiratory zone begins at respiratory bronchioles (occasional alveoli budding off the wall) → alveolar ducts (entirely lined with alveoli) → alveolar sacs (clusters of alveoli). (OpenStax)" />

Respiratory bronchioles → alveolar ducts → **alveoli**. Two cell types in the alveolar wall:

- **Type I pneumocytes** - 95% of surface area, thin for gas exchange.
- **Type II pneumocytes** - secrete **surfactant** (phospholipids + proteins that reduce surface tension and prevent collapse).

## Lobes & fissures

<Figure name="respiratory/lungs-gross" caption="Gross anatomy of the lungs (OpenStax). Right lung: 3 lobes (superior + middle + inferior), 2 fissures (oblique + horizontal). Left lung: 2 lobes (superior + inferior), 1 fissure (oblique), with the cardiac notch + lingula instead of a middle lobe. Each lung has apex, base, mediastinal + costal surfaces, hilum." />

<Figure name="respiratory/pleura" caption="Pleura - serous membrane around each lung. Visceral pleura (on lung surface) + parietal pleura (lining thoracic cavity) separated by a thin film of pleural fluid in the pleural cavity. Pathologic accumulations: pleural effusion (fluid), pneumothorax (air), hemothorax (blood)." />

| Lung | Lobes | Fissures |
|---|---|---|
| Right | Upper, middle, lower | Horizontal + oblique |
| Left | Upper, lower (+ lingula as a "middle-lobe equivalent") | Oblique only |

The **cardiac notch** in the left upper lobe accommodates the heart.

<Clinical>
**Neonatal respiratory distress syndrome (NRDS)**: preterm infants lack adequate surfactant (Type II pneumocytes mature ~24–28 weeks gestation). Alveoli collapse on expiration → hypoxemia, increased work of breathing. Treated with exogenous surfactant + CPAP.
</Clinical>

## Every respiratory structure - what it does and why it matters

### Upper airway

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Nasal cavity** | Warms, humidifies, filters incoming air | First defense; turbinates create turbulence for filtering; richly vascular (epistaxis) |
| **Conchae (turbinates)** | Increase mucosal surface area | Trap particulates; humidification |
| **Olfactory epithelium (roof of nose)** | Smell receptors (CN I) | Only place CNS neurons are exposed to environment - entry point for some pathogens |
| **Pharynx (naso-, oro-, laryngo-)** | Shared aerodigestive conduit | Site of tonsillar tissue (Waldeyer's ring); crossing point of food + air |
| **Epiglottis** | Cartilage flap covering laryngeal inlet during swallow | Prevents aspiration; absent or scarred → aspiration risk |
| **Larynx (cartilages: thyroid, cricoid, arytenoid, ×2)** | Voice box; cricoid is the only complete ring in airway | Cricothyrotomy site; phonation via vocal cords |
| **Vocal cords (folds)** | Vibrate during phonation; close during cough | Innervated by recurrent laryngeal n. (CN X branch); palsy → hoarseness |
| **Recurrent laryngeal nerves** | Motor to all intrinsic laryngeal muscles except cricothyroid | **Left RLN loops under aortic arch** (vulnerable in lung Ca, aortic surgery); right loops under right subclavian |

### Trachea and bronchi

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Trachea** | Rigid conducting airway; C-shaped cartilage rings keep it patent | Tracheostomy site (between 2nd–3rd ring); ~10–12 cm long |
| **Carina** | Bifurcation point of trachea at T4/T5 (sternal angle) | Cough reflex sensitive; widened in left atrial enlargement |
| **Right main bronchus** | Wider, shorter, more vertical than left | **Aspirated foreign bodies preferentially enter** |
| **Left main bronchus** | Narrower, longer, more horizontal | Passes under aortic arch |
| **Lobar bronchi (R: 3, L: 2)** | Supply each lobe | Match lung lobe anatomy |
| **Segmental bronchi (R: 10, L: 8–10)** | Supply bronchopulmonary segments | Surgical units - segments can be resected without damaging others |
| **Bronchioles** | &lt;1 mm diameter, no cartilage, smooth muscle predominant | Bronchoconstriction site (asthma) |
| **Terminal bronchioles** | Last purely conducting airway | After this, gas exchange begins |
| **Respiratory bronchioles** | Mixed conduction + gas exchange (alveoli budding from walls) | Beginning of respiratory zone |
| **Alveolar ducts** | Lead into alveolar sacs | Distribute air to clusters of alveoli |
| **Alveoli** | Sites of gas exchange; ~480 million in adult lung | Total surface area ~70 m² - gas exchange engine |

### Alveolar cells

| Cell type | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Type I pneumocyte** | Squamous; covers ~95% of alveolar surface | Thin barrier for gas diffusion |
| **Type II pneumocyte** | Cuboidal; secretes **surfactant**; precursor for Type I after injury | Surfactant ↓ surface tension; deficiency → NRDS; Type II repopulate alveolar epithelium after damage |
| **Alveolar macrophage (dust cell)** | Phagocytose particulates, surfactant turnover | First-line defense; "heart failure cells" laden with hemosiderin in pulmonary edema |
| **Club (Clara) cells** | Detoxification; surfactant component secretion; bronchiolar stem cells | Cytochrome P450; vulnerable to oxidative damage |
| **Goblet cells** | Mucus secretion in larger airways | ↑↑ in chronic bronchitis (Reid index) |
| **Ciliated columnar cells** | Mucociliary escalator clears debris | Smoking paralyzes cilia → mucus retention, infection risk; Kartagener syndrome: defective cilia |

### Lungs gross + pleura

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Right lung** | 3 lobes (upper, middle, lower) - horizontal + oblique fissures | Larger; aspirated objects more likely here |
| **Left lung** | 2 lobes (upper + lower) - oblique fissure only; cardiac notch + lingula | Smaller; cardiac notch accommodates heart |
| **Hilum** | Site of vessels, bronchi, lymphatics entering lung | Lymphadenopathy here on CXR is high-yield |
| **Visceral pleura** | Adherent to lung surface; insensitive | No pain fibers - lung lesions only hurt when pleura involved |
| **Parietal pleura** | Lines chest wall; **pain-sensitive (intercostals + phrenic)** | Pleuritic chest pain; phrenic referral to shoulder |
| **Pleural cavity** | Potential space with ~10 mL serous fluid | Site of pleural effusion, pneumothorax, hemothorax |
| **Costodiaphragmatic recess** | Most inferior pleural reflection | Collects effusion fluid (best site for thoracentesis) |

### Mechanics

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Diaphragm** | Primary inspiratory muscle (75% of tidal volume) | Phrenic-innervated; loss → respiratory failure |
| **External intercostals** | Elevate ribs (pump handle + bucket handle) | Active inspiration |
| **Accessory muscles** (SCM, scalenes, pec minor) | Recruited in respiratory distress | "Tripod position" visible signs |
| **Internal intercostals + abdominal muscles** | Forced expiration | Cough, exercise |
| **Surfactant (dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine)** | Reduces alveolar surface tension | Prevents collapse (atelectasis); enables small alveoli to coexist with large (Laplace) |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX respiratory priorities:**
- **Position for respiratory distress**: high-Fowler's or **tripod** (leaning forward, arms on bedside table).
- **Pneumothorax**: sudden chest pain, dyspnea, **absent breath sounds**, hyperresonance; tension PTX → tracheal deviation **away** from affected side, hypotension - needle decompression at 2nd ICS midclavicular.
- **Chest tube**: monitor water-seal chamber for **tidaling** (good) and continuous bubbling (air leak); never clamp without order.
- **Tracheostomy care**: suction PRN, sterile technique; have **obturator + spare trach at bedside**.
- **Aspiration risk**: HOB ≥30° during/after feeds; assess gag reflex before oral intake post-stroke.
- **Postural drainage**: time around meals to avoid emesis.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Anatomy gotchas:**
- **Aspirated objects** preferentially enter **right main bronchus** (wider, more vertical, less angled).
- **Right lung 3 lobes, left 2** - left has lingula instead of middle lobe.
- **Carina** at sternal angle (T4–T5) - widens with **left atrial enlargement** (pushes the carina up).
- **Cardiac notch** is in the **left upper lobe**.
- **NRDS** in preemies: Type II pneumocytes mature 24–28 wk; **L/S ratio &gt;2** signals lung maturity; treat with surfactant + CPAP.
- **Cystic fibrosis**: CFTR mutation → thick mucus; recurrent Pseudomonas/Staph; nasal polyps, infertility (absent vas deferens), pancreatic insufficiency.
</HighYield>

## Alveolus - the gas-exchange unit in detail

<Figure name="respiratory/alveolus" caption="A pulmonary alveolus showing Type I + Type II pneumocytes, the surrounding capillary, alveolar macrophages, and surfactant lining. The respiratory membrane (gas-exchange barrier) is only ~0.5 µm thick." />

The alveolus is where the conducting zone ends and gas exchange begins. The **respiratory (blood-gas) membrane** is the diffusion barrier between alveolar air and capillary blood:

1. **Alveolar fluid layer** (with surfactant) - 0.1 µm
2. **Type I pneumocyte** - 0.1 µm flat cell
3. **Fused basement membranes** of pneumocyte and capillary endothelium - 0.1 µm
4. **Capillary endothelial cell** - 0.1 µm

Total: **0.5 µm thick** - engineered for gas diffusion at the speed of perfusion (RBC transit time in capillary ~0.75 s; gas equilibration in ~0.25 s, leaving safety margin).
