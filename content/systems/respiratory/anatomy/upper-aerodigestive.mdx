The **upper aerodigestive tract** is where the breathing and swallowing pathways share real estate. Above the **larynx**, air and food cross paths at the **pharynx** - a clever and dangerous bit of anatomy that lets us swallow + breathe + speak, but kills ~3 in 100,000 people a year through aspiration. Pharmacologically this region matters because intranasal, sublingual, buccal, and inhaled routes all use it; clinically it matters for intubation, NG tubes, swallowing, and dental disease.

## Nasal cavity

<Figure name="respiratory/nasal-cavity" caption="Sagittal view of the nasal cavity with the three nasal conchae (superior, middle, inferior turbinates), the hard and soft palate, and entry into the nasopharynx." />

Two cavities separated by the **nasal septum** (perpendicular plate of ethmoid + vomer + septal cartilage). Floor = hard + soft palate; roof = cribriform plate of ethmoid; lateral walls bear the **conchae (turbinates)**.

### Conchae and meatuses

Three pairs project medially from the lateral wall:
- **Superior** + **middle** conchae - from ethmoid bone
- **Inferior** concha - separate bone

Each concha overhangs a **meatus** (a horizontal recess):

| Meatus | What drains into it |
|---|---|
| **Superior meatus** | Posterior ethmoid air cells |
| **Middle meatus** | Frontal sinus, maxillary sinus, anterior + middle ethmoid air cells |
| **Inferior meatus** | **Nasolacrimal duct** (tears → nose, why crying → runny nose) |
| **Sphenoethmoidal recess** | Sphenoid sinus |

The conchae:
- ↑ Surface area for **warming, humidifying, filtering** inspired air
- Create turbulence that helps with **odorant sampling** at the **olfactory epithelium** in the superior posterior nasal cavity
- Vasoconstriction here = **decongestants** (oxymetazoline, phenylephrine, pseudoephedrine - α1 agonists)

### Paranasal sinuses

Four pairs of air-filled cavities in skull bones. Functions: lighten skull, resonate voice, humidify air. **Sinusitis** = inflammation of these mucous-membrane linings.

| Sinus | Bone | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Frontal** | Frontal bone | Above eyebrows; drains to middle meatus |
| **Ethmoidal** (anterior, middle, posterior) | Ethmoid | Between orbits; thin lamina papyracea - orbital infection spreads here easily |
| **Sphenoidal** | Sphenoid | Posterior; close to pituitary, optic chiasm, internal carotid (surgical approach to pituitary tumors) |
| **Maxillary** | Maxilla | Largest; below eye, above maxillary teeth - **dental infections can spread here**; drains UP into middle meatus (gravity disadvantage) |

## Intranasal drug administration

The nasal mucosa is **highly vascular** (rich plexus from sphenopalatine + ethmoidal aa. - site of epistaxis at Kiesselbach's plexus anteriorly + Woodruff's plexus posteriorly). This makes it a fast absorption surface:

- **Bypasses first-pass metabolism** (drains directly to systemic venous return)
- **Fast onset**: useful in emergencies (intranasal naloxone for opioid OD, intranasal midazolam for seizure, intranasal fentanyl)
- **Aim laterally + superiorly** - toward the turbinates where mucosa is most vascular. Straight back = nasopharynx → swallowed (PO route, much less F). Straight at septum = avascular cartilage.

## Pharynx - the crossroads

The pharynx is a muscular tube ~12 cm long from skull base to ~C6 (where it transitions to esophagus). Three regions, named by what's anterior to them:

| Region | Posterior to | Lined by | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nasopharynx** | Nasal cavity | **Pseudostratified ciliated columnar** (respiratory) | Eustachian tube openings; pharyngeal tonsil (adenoid); auditory tube tonsil |
| **Oropharynx** | Oral cavity | **Stratified squamous** | Palatine + lingual tonsils; common pathway for air + food |
| **Laryngopharynx** | Larynx | Stratified squamous | Posterior to larynx; piriform recesses (where aspirated foreign bodies lodge) |

Above is **air only**; below the larynx splits the path - **esophagus posteriorly, larynx/trachea anteriorly**. The **epiglottis** folds back over the laryngeal inlet during swallowing to keep food out.

### Waldeyer's ring - pharyngeal lymphoid tissue

A ring of lymphoid tissue guarding the entrance to the GI + respiratory tracts:

- **Pharyngeal tonsil (adenoid)** - roof of nasopharynx
- **Tubal tonsils** - around Eustachian tube openings
- **Palatine tonsils** - between palatoglossal + palatopharyngeal arches (the ones you see + that get removed in tonsillectomy)
- **Lingual tonsil** - posterior tongue base

Often enlarged in childhood. Recurrent tonsillitis or adenoidal hypertrophy → tonsillectomy + adenoidectomy.

## Pharyngeal muscles + innervation

Three **constrictors** (superior, middle, inferior) propel the bolus down. Three **elevators** (stylopharyngeus, palatopharyngeus, salpingopharyngeus) lift the pharynx during swallowing.

| Muscle | Innervation |
|---|---|
| **All constrictors + elevators** | **CN X (vagus)** - via pharyngeal plexus |
| **Stylopharyngeus** | **CN IX (glossopharyngeal)** - the lone exception |
| **Tensor veli palatini** | **CN V3 (mandibular)** - also exception |
| **Tongue muscles (intrinsic + most extrinsic)** | CN XII |
| **Palatoglossus** | CN X |

<Clinical>
**Dysphagia after stroke**: cortical lesion in motor area for swallow → loss of pharyngeal coordination → aspiration risk. NPO until cleared by swallow eval; if persistent, NG tube → PEG tube. Bedside test: ice chips, 30° elevation, observe for cough.

**Zenker diverticulum**: outpouching of pharyngeal mucosa at **Killian's triangle** between thyropharyngeus + cricopharyngeus fibers (the inferior constrictor). Trapped food regurgitates after meals, halitosis, neck mass. Treat with diverticulectomy or myotomy.
</Clinical>

## Larynx - the voicebox

<Figure name="respiratory/larynx" caption="Larynx anatomy showing the thyroid cartilage (Adam's apple), cricoid (the only complete ring), arytenoid cartilages, and the epiglottis above." />

A cartilaginous box at C3–C6 with 9 cartilages, 5 in clinical importance:

| Cartilage | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Thyroid** | Anterior shield-shape; "Adam's apple" prominence; thyroid notch palpable |
| **Cricoid** | **Only complete ring** in the airway (the rest are C-shaped) - at C6 level; landmark for cricothyrotomy |
| **Arytenoids (paired)** | Move vocal cords; sit atop posterior cricoid |
| **Corniculate (paired)** | Atop arytenoids |
| **Cuneiform (paired)** | In aryepiglottic folds |
| **Epiglottis** | Single, leaf-shaped; passively folds back during swallow |

### Vocal cords

Two pairs of mucosal folds across the laryngeal lumen:
- **False vocal cords (vestibular folds)** - superior; protective, not for phonation
- **True vocal cords (vocal folds)** - inferior; vibrate for phonation; opening between them = **glottis** = **rima glottidis**

### Laryngeal muscles

All **intrinsic laryngeal muscles** are innervated by **CN X (vagus)** branches:

| Branch | Muscles | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Recurrent laryngeal n.** | All intrinsic muscles EXCEPT cricothyroid | Open, close, tense vocal cords |
| **External laryngeal n.** (branch of superior laryngeal) | **Cricothyroid only** | Tenses cords (high pitch) |
| **Internal laryngeal n.** (branch of superior laryngeal) | (Sensory above cords) | Sensation above vocal cords |

<Clinical>
**Recurrent laryngeal n. injury** → vocal cord paralysis → **hoarseness** (unilateral) or **stridor + aphonia** (bilateral).
- **Left RLN** loops around the **aortic arch** under the ligamentum arteriosum - vulnerable in: lung cancer (Pancoast, AP window LN), aortic aneurysm/dissection, cardiothoracic surgery.
- **Right RLN** loops around the **right subclavian artery** - vulnerable in: thyroid surgery (both RLNs are anyway, since they run in the tracheoesophageal groove).

**Internal laryngeal n. injury** → loss of cough reflex above cords → silent aspiration.

**Cricothyrotomy**: emergency airway. Slit through cricothyroid membrane (between thyroid cartilage above + cricoid below). Bypasses obstructed upper airway.

**Tracheostomy**: more elective. Below cricoid, between 2nd–3rd tracheal rings. Long-term ventilation, head/neck cancer.
</Clinical>

## Oral cavity

| Structure | Function | Innervation |
|---|---|---|
| **Lips** | Mechanical food handling; speech; sensation | CN V (sensory), CN VII (motor) |
| **Hard palate** (anterior 2/3) | Maxilla + palatine bones; rigid roof | CN V2 sensory |
| **Soft palate** (posterior 1/3) | Muscular; elevates during swallow to seal nasopharynx | CN X motor (palatoglossus + others); CN V3 tensor veli palatini |
| **Uvula** | Hangs from soft palate; helps seal nasopharynx | Deviation AWAY from CN X lesion |
| **Tongue** | Bolus formation, swallowing, taste, speech | Anterior 2/3 sensation = CN V3; anterior 2/3 taste = CN VII; posterior 1/3 sensation + taste = CN IX; motor (all but palatoglossus) = CN XII |
| **Teeth** | Mechanical digestion | Innervation: V2 (upper) + V3 (lower) |

### Salivary glands

<Figure name="digestive/salivary-glands" caption="Three pairs of major salivary glands (Blausen). Parotid (largest, in front of ear, drains via Stensen's duct to opposite upper 2nd molar). Submandibular (under the mandible, drains via Wharton's duct to floor of mouth). Sublingual (floor of mouth, multiple small ducts). Plus minor glands scattered throughout." />

Three pairs of major glands + minor glands throughout oral cavity. Secrete ~1.5 L/day:

| Gland | Location | Duct | Innervation (parasymp) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Parotid** | Wraps angle of mandible; in front of ear | Stensen's duct → opposite upper 2nd molar | **CN IX** via lesser petrosal → otic ganglion |
| **Submandibular** | Below mandible | Wharton's duct → floor of mouth | CN VII via chorda tympani → submandibular ganglion |
| **Sublingual** | Floor of mouth | Multiple small ducts | CN VII via chorda tympani → submandibular ganglion |

<Clinical>
**Mumps** (paramyxovirus): inflames parotid → bilateral painful swelling. Can also cause orchitis, meningitis, pancreatitis.

**Parotid tumors**: 80% benign (pleomorphic adenoma most common). **CN VII passes through the parotid** - must be preserved in surgery.

**Sjögren syndrome**: autoimmune destruction of lacrimal + salivary glands → dry eyes + dry mouth. Anti-SSA/Ro + anti-SSB/La antibodies.

**Dental abscess** in maxillary molars can erode into the **maxillary sinus** → odontogenic sinusitis.
</Clinical>

## Swallowing - three phases

1. **Oral (voluntary)**: tongue pushes bolus to oropharynx. CN V, VII, XII.
2. **Pharyngeal (reflex)**: soft palate elevates (CN X) → seals nasopharynx; epiglottis tips back; larynx elevates (CN X, XI); pharyngeal constrictors contract sequentially. **Apnea** during this phase (~1 sec).
3. **Esophageal**: peristaltic wave propels bolus to stomach. CN X.

**Aspiration** = food/fluid enters airway below the vocal cords. Risk increases with neurologic injury, sedation, intubation/extubation, NG tubes, age. **Penetration** = above cords (less serious).

## NG tube + intubation - anatomy

### Nasogastric tube

Path: nostril → inferior meatus (along floor of nose) → nasopharynx → curves inferiorly → oropharynx → laryngopharynx → **esophagus** (posterior) → stomach. Must **NOT** enter the larynx (anterior).

- Confirm placement: chest X-ray (gold standard) or pH testing of aspirate (gastric pH < 5.5)
- Position: lateral decubitus or sitting with chin to chest (closes laryngeal inlet, helps tube go esophageal)

### Endotracheal intubation

Path: mouth → oropharynx → laryngopharynx → through vocal cords into **trachea** (anterior path).

- Direct laryngoscopy lifts epiglottis to visualize vocal cords
- Confirm: **end-tidal CO₂** + bilateral breath sounds + chest X-ray
- Drugs:
  - **Sedative**: midazolam, propofol, ketamine, etomidate
  - **Paralytic**: succinylcholine (depolarizing - fast onset, brief; hyperK risk) or rocuronium (non-depolarizing - reversed by sugammadex)
- Pulled too distally → right mainstem bronchus (more vertical) → left lung underventilation

## Every upper-aerodigestive structure - function reference

| Structure | What it does | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Nasal septum** | Divides nasal cavities | Deviated septum → obstruction |
| **Conchae (turbinates)** | ↑ Surface area, warm/humidify/filter air | Decongestant target site |
| **Cribriform plate** | Roof of nasal cavity; transmits CN I filaments | Fractures with head trauma → CSF rhinorrhea + anosmia |
| **Olfactory epithelium** | Smell receptors | Pseudostratified columnar specialized; site of CN I axons |
| **Frontal sinus** | Resonance, lightness | Drains to middle meatus; sinusitis pain in forehead |
| **Maxillary sinus** | Resonance, lightness | Largest; dental infection spread; drains upward (poor gravity drainage) |
| **Ethmoidal sinuses** | Lightening | Lamina papyracea separates from orbit (orbital cellulitis spread) |
| **Sphenoid sinus** | Resonance | Transsphenoidal pituitary surgery passes through |
| **Nasopharynx** | Air conduit; pharyngeal tonsil | Adenoid hyperplasia in kids → mouth breathing, sleep apnea |
| **Oropharynx** | Common air + food path | Where pathways cross |
| **Laryngopharynx** | Posterior to larynx | Piriform recess = aspiration trap |
| **Palatine tonsils** | Immune sampling | Tonsillitis, peritonsillar abscess |
| **Soft palate** | Elevates during swallow | Bifid uvula = subtle midline defect |
| **Hard palate** | Rigid oral roof | Cleft palate = developmental failure |
| **Tongue (intrinsic + extrinsic mm.)** | Speech, swallowing, taste | CN XII motor; multi-sensory |
| **Epiglottis** | Folds back during swallow | Acute epiglottitis (H. influenzae) = airway emergency in kids |
| **Thyroid cartilage** | Largest laryngeal cartilage | "Adam's apple" |
| **Cricoid cartilage** | Only complete ring in airway | Cricothyrotomy landmark |
| **Vocal cords (true)** | Vibrate to phonate | RLN injury → paralysis → hoarseness |
| **Glottis** | Space between true vocal cords | Narrowest point of adult airway |
| **Stensen's duct (parotid)** | Saliva delivery | Opens opposite upper 2nd molar |
| **Wharton's duct (submandibular)** | Saliva delivery | Common stone site (more vertical course) |
| **Eustachian tube** | Equalizes middle-ear pressure | Opens into lateral nasopharynx; shorter/horizontal in kids → OM |
| **Adenoid (pharyngeal tonsil)** | Immune sampling at nasopharynx | Hypertrophy → mouth breathing |
| **Recurrent laryngeal n.** | All intrinsic laryngeal mm. (except cricothyroid) | Left RLN under aorta - vulnerable; right RLN under R subclavian |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX upper-aerodigestive nursing priorities:**
- **NG tube placement confirmation**: pH < 5.5 of aspirate, X-ray gold standard; mark the tube at the nostril after confirmation; check residuals before feeds.
- **Tracheostomy care**: keep obturator + spare cannula at bedside; inner cannula cleaned with sterile saline; sterile technique; monitor for accidental decannulation.
- **Aspiration precautions**: HOB ≥30°; small bites; thickened liquids if dysphagia; bedside swallow eval after stroke/extubation.
- **Epistaxis nursing**: lean forward (not back - prevents swallowing blood), pinch soft part of nose 10 min, ice to bridge. If posterior bleed → ENT for posterior pack.
- **Post-tonsillectomy**: monitor for bleeding (D5–D10 = scab fall risk); offer cool liquids, avoid red beverages (mistaken for blood); pain management is critical.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Pearls:**
- **Recurrent laryngeal asymmetry**: left under aortic arch (longer, vulnerable to lung Ca, aortic disease); right under right subclavian (shorter).
- **Epiglottitis** in unvaccinated kid = H. influenzae type B, "thumb sign" on lateral neck X-ray, **don't examine throat** (laryngospasm) - get to OR for intubation.
- **Croup** = laryngotracheobronchitis = parainfluenza; "steeple sign" on AP neck X-ray; barking cough; treat with humidified air + dexamethasone + racemic epinephrine.
- **Zenker diverticulum** at Killian's triangle (inferior pharyngeal constrictor) → halitosis + regurgitation of undigested food.
- **Cribriform plate fracture** → CSF rhinorrhea + anosmia (CN I avulsion).
- **Cleft palate** = failure of palatal shelves to fuse (week 7–10); cleft lip = failure of medial nasal + maxillary processes to fuse (week 5–7).
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Drug delivery via upper aerodigestive:**
- **Intranasal naloxone (Narcan)** for suspected opioid OD - aim laterally + superiorly toward turbinates. Onset minutes.
- **Intranasal midazolam, diazepam, fentanyl** - useful when no IV access (peds, EMS, status epilepticus).
- **Sublingual** - bypasses first-pass: nitroglycerin (angina), buprenorphine (opioid use disorder), ondansetron ODT.
- **Inhaled** - bronchial absorption: bronchodilators (albuterol), ICS (fluticasone), anesthetics (sevoflurane).
- **Decongestants** (α1 agonists): pseudoephedrine (oral), phenylephrine (oral, less effective), oxymetazoline (topical - rebound congestion if > 3 days = **rhinitis medicamentosa**).
</HighYield>
