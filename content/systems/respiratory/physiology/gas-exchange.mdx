Gas exchange follows **partial-pressure gradients**, not concentrations - Fick's law of diffusion.

<Figure name="respiratory/alveolus" caption="Alveolar–capillary interface - site of gas exchange." />

## Partial pressures (mmHg, sea level, resting)

| Site | PO₂ | PCO₂ |
|---|---|---|
| Atmospheric (inspired) | 160 | 0.3 |
| Alveolar | 100 | 40 |
| Mixed venous (entering lung) | 40 | 46 |
| Arterial (leaving lung) | ~100 | 40 |
| Tissue | &lt;40 | &gt;46 |

The alveolar–arterial (A-a) gradient is normally **less than 10 mmHg** in young adults; an elevated A-a gradient suggests a problem with gas exchange itself (V/Q mismatch, shunt, diffusion impairment).

## Oxygen transport

**98% bound to hemoglobin**, ~2% dissolved. Each Hb tetramer carries 4 O₂. The **oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve** is sigmoidal because of **cooperative binding** - once one O₂ binds, the others bind more readily.

Curve shifts:

- **Right shift** (Hb releases O₂ more easily) - ↑ CO₂, ↑ H⁺ (acidosis), ↑ temperature, ↑ 2,3-BPG. Helpful at the tissues.
- **Left shift** - opposite. Helpful at the lungs. **Fetal Hb (HbF)** sits left of adult - pulls O₂ from maternal blood across the placenta.

<Mnemonic>
**Right shift = CADET**: **C**O₂, **A**cid, **2,3-BPG** (D for diphosphoglycerate), **E**xercise, **T**emperature.
</Mnemonic>

## CO₂ transport

Three forms:
- **~70% as bicarbonate** (HCO₃⁻) - CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ (catalyzed by carbonic anhydrase in RBCs). HCO₃⁻ exits via the chloride shift.
- **~23% bound to Hb** (carbaminohemoglobin) at amino groups, not the heme.
- **~7% dissolved**.

<Clinical>
**Carbon monoxide poisoning**: CO binds Hb with ~240× the affinity of O₂, forming carboxyhemoglobin. This (a) reduces O₂-carrying capacity and (b) **left-shifts** the remaining curve, impairing release at tissues. Treat with 100% O₂ (or hyperbaric).
</Clinical>

## Every gas-exchange component - function reference

### Hemoglobin and its variants

| Form | Composition | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HbA (adult)** | 2α + 2β | Normal adult hemoglobin | 97% of adult Hb |
| **HbA2** | 2α + 2δ | Minor adult form | Elevated in β-thalassemia trait |
| **HbF (fetal)** | 2α + 2γ | Higher O₂ affinity (left-shifted curve) | Allows fetal Hb to grab O₂ from maternal Hb across placenta |
| **HbS** | β6: Glu→Val | Sickles in low O₂ | Sickle cell disease |
| **HbC** | β6: Glu→Lys | Mild hemolysis | Less severe than HbS |
| **Methemoglobin** | Fe³⁺ (oxidized) | Cannot bind O₂ | Caused by nitrites, dapsone, benzocaine; treat with methylene blue |
| **Carboxyhemoglobin** | CO bound to heme | Affinity ~240× O₂; left-shifts remaining curve | CO poisoning - treat with 100% O₂ or hyperbaric |

### V/Q regional differences (upright lung)

| Zone | V/Q | Notes | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Apex (Zone 1)** | High V/Q | Less perfusion (gravity), some ventilation | Wasted ventilation; TB grows here (high PO₂) |
| **Middle (Zone 2)** | ~1 | Optimal matching | Best gas exchange |
| **Base (Zone 3)** | Low V/Q | More perfusion than ventilation | Wasted blood flow; in supine position the dependent area is posterior |

### Control of breathing

| Component | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Central chemoreceptors (medulla)** | Respond to CSF pH (driven by PCO₂) | Main day-to-day driver of breathing |
| **Peripheral chemoreceptors (carotid + aortic bodies)** | Respond to PO₂, PCO₂, pH | Hypoxic drive - especially important when central is blunted (e.g., chronic CO₂ retainers) |
| **Dorsal respiratory group (medulla)** | Inspiratory pattern generator | Diaphragm via phrenic n. |
| **Ventral respiratory group (medulla)** | Forced inspiration + expiration | Active expiration |
| **Apneustic center (lower pons)** | Prolongs inspiration | Lesion → apneusis (gasping) |
| **Pneumotaxic center (upper pons)** | Limits inspiratory duration | Tunes rate |
| **Stretch receptors (Hering-Breuer)** | Prevent overinflation | Trigger expiration |
| **Irritant receptors** | Trigger cough, bronchospasm | Protective |
| **J receptors (juxtacapillary)** | Respond to interstitial edema | Tachypnea in pulmonary edema |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**ABG interpretation (a critical NCLEX skill):**
| pH | PaCO₂ | HCO₃⁻ | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| ↓ | ↑ | nl/↑ | Respiratory acidosis (COPD, opioid OD, NM weakness) |
| ↑ | ↓ | nl/↓ | Respiratory alkalosis (anxiety, PE, pain) |
| ↓ | nl/↓ | ↓ | Metabolic acidosis (DKA, lactic, renal failure, diarrhea) |
| ↑ | nl/↑ | ↑ | Metabolic alkalosis (vomiting, diuretics, hyperaldosteronism) |

- **Oxygen therapy COPD pearl**: don't oversaturate (target 88–92%) - hypoxic drive in chronic CO₂ retainers; high O₂ can blunt their respiratory drive.
- **Pulse ox &lt;90% = call**; **&lt;88% = intervene**.
- **Incentive spirometer**: post-op 10×/h while awake to prevent atelectasis.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**V/Q and exercise/altitude:**
- **Exercise**: ↑ CO, ↑ ventilation; A-a gradient widens slightly; lactate rises at threshold.
- **High altitude**: ↓ PO₂ → hyperventilation → respiratory alkalosis → renal compensation (↑ HCO₃⁻ excretion); chronic → ↑ EPO + RBC mass + 2,3-BPG.
- **Pulmonary embolism**: V/Q mismatch (high V/Q dead space); ABG shows hypoxia + hypocapnia (from hyperventilation).
- **Shunt (V/Q = 0)**: hypoxia not corrected with 100% O₂ (unlike V/Q mismatch).
- **CO₂ dissociation curve**: Haldane effect - deoxygenated Hb binds CO₂ better.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Respiratory pharm:**
- **Albuterol** (SABA): β2 agonist - rescue inhaler; tachycardia + tremor.
- **Salmeterol / formoterol** (LABA): maintenance only, never monotherapy in asthma (mortality risk).
- **ICS** (fluticasone, budesonide): rinse mouth → prevent thrush, dysphonia.
- **Ipratropium / tiotropium**: anticholinergic; preferred in COPD.
- **Montelukast**: leukotriene receptor antagonist; black box for neuropsychiatric.
- **Omalizumab** (anti-IgE), **mepolizumab** (anti-IL-5): severe asthma biologics.
- **Theophylline**: narrow window; CYP1A2 interactions (ciprofloxacin ↑↑).
</HighYield>
