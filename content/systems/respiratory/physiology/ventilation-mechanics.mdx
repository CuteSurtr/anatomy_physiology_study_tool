Breathing is a **mechanical pump** problem: pressure gradients move air in and out, and elastic properties of the lung + chest wall determine resistance + recoil. Lung volumes, compliance, surface tension, and airway resistance form the core of pulmonary mechanics - and underlie every restrictive vs obstructive distinction on PFTs.

## The basic mechanics

<Figure name="respiratory/diaphragm-movement" caption="Diaphragm movement during inspiration vs expiration. Inspiration: diaphragm contracts + descends, external intercostals lift ribs → thoracic cavity expands → intrapleural pressure falls → air flows in. Expiration: passive elastic recoil." />

### Boyle's law in action
- Inspiratory muscles contract → thoracic volume ↑ → intrapleural pressure becomes more negative → transpulmonary pressure increases → alveolar pressure drops below atmospheric → **air flows in**.
- Expiration: passive at rest (elastic recoil); active during exercise / forced expiration (abdominals + internal intercostals).

### Pressures during quiet breathing (cmH₂O)
| Phase | Atmospheric | Alveolar | Intrapleural | Transpulmonary (A – IP) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **End-expiration** | 0 | 0 | –5 | +5 |
| **Mid-inspiration** | 0 | –1 | –7 | +6 |
| **End-inspiration** | 0 | 0 | –8 | +8 |
| **Mid-expiration** | 0 | +1 | –6 | +5 |

The pleural space is **always negative** (sub-atmospheric) - keeps the lung expanded against the chest wall. Loss of negativity = pneumothorax.

## Inspiratory muscles

<Figure name="respiratory/respiratory-overview" caption="Respiratory anatomy review - external intercostals + diaphragm = primary inspiratory muscles; SCM, scalenes, pec minor = accessory." />

| Muscle | Innervation | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Diaphragm** | Phrenic (C3–C5) | Primary; 75% of tidal volume |
| **External intercostals** | Intercostal nn. | Bucket-handle rib elevation |
| **Scalenes** | C3–C8 | Elevate ribs 1–2; accessory at rest |
| **Sternocleidomastoid + serratus anterior + pec minor** | - | Accessory; only during forced inspiration (asthma, exercise) |

**Expiration** is passive at rest. Active expiration uses **internal intercostals** + **abdominals** (rectus abdominis, internal/external obliques, transversus).

## Lung volumes + capacities

<Figure name="respiratory/spirometry-tracing" caption="Spirometry tracing showing the major lung volumes + capacities. Tidal volume + inspiratory/expiratory reserve volumes + residual volume (which can't be exhaled). FEV1 + FVC are the diagnostic measurements." />

```
      Inspiratory reserve volume (IRV)
      ─────────────────────────────────
              Tidal volume (TV)              ← Normal breath
      ─────────────────────────────────
      Expiratory reserve volume (ERV)
      ─────────────────────────────────
      Residual volume (RV)                   ← Can't be measured spirometrically
```

| Volume / capacity | Definition | Typical (70 kg adult) |
|---|---|---|
| **Tidal volume (TV)** | Normal breath | 500 mL |
| **Inspiratory reserve (IRV)** | Max additional inhale from end-inspiration | 3000 mL |
| **Expiratory reserve (ERV)** | Max additional exhale from end-expiration | 1000 mL |
| **Residual volume (RV)** | Air left after max exhale | 1200 mL |
| **Inspiratory capacity (IC)** | TV + IRV | 3500 mL |
| **Functional residual capacity (FRC)** | ERV + RV - at the **resting equilibrium** between lung recoil + chest wall recoil | 2200 mL |
| **Vital capacity (VC)** | IRV + TV + ERV | 4500 mL |
| **Total lung capacity (TLC)** | All four volumes | 5700 mL |

**RV and any capacity that includes it (FRC, TLC) cannot be measured by spirometry** - need **helium dilution** or **body plethysmography**.

### Anatomic vs physiologic dead space
- **Anatomic dead space** - volume of conducting airways (no gas exchange). ~150 mL (~1 mL per pound).
- **Physiologic dead space** - anatomic + alveolar (ventilated but not perfused). Equal to anatomic in healthy lungs; ↑ in disease (PE, ARDS).
- **Bohr equation**: V_D/V_T = (PaCO₂ – PECO₂) / PaCO₂.

### Minute ventilation vs alveolar ventilation
- **Minute ventilation** = RR × TV (e.g., 12 × 500 = 6 L/min).
- **Alveolar ventilation** = RR × (TV – V_D) (e.g., 12 × (500 – 150) = 4.2 L/min). **The metric that matters** for gas exchange.

This is why **rapid shallow breathing** can be inefficient - most of each breath fills dead space.

## Compliance - how easily lungs distend

```
Compliance = ΔV / ΔP
```

- **High compliance** = lungs distend easily (think floppy balloon). Emphysema, aging - lose elastin.
- **Low compliance** = stiff lungs. Pulmonary fibrosis, pneumonia, ARDS, pulmonary edema, infant RDS.

### Pressure-volume (P-V) curve
- Plots lung volume vs transpulmonary pressure.
- **Hysteresis**: inflation curve differs from deflation (surface tension drops at inflation).
- Emphysema shifts curve up + left (high compliance).
- Fibrosis shifts curve down + right (low compliance).

## Surface tension + surfactant

- **Surface tension** at the air-alveolar interface tends to collapse alveoli (Laplace: P = 2T/r → smaller alveoli have higher collapsing pressure).
- **Surfactant** (dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine - DPPC) from **type II pneumocytes** lowers surface tension proportionally more as alveoli shrink → equalizes pressures → prevents collapse.
- Synthesized after ~24 weeks gestation; mature levels at ~35 weeks.

<Figure name="respiratory/alveolus" caption="Alveolar structure matters for mechanics too: the air-fluid interface creates surface tension, while type II pneumocytes secrete surfactant to keep small alveoli from collapsing into larger ones." />

### Neonatal respiratory distress syndrome (NRDS)
- Premature infants (&lt;32 weeks) lack surfactant → diffuse atelectasis → hypoxemia + ↓ compliance.
- L/S (lecithin/sphingomyelin) ratio >2 in amniotic fluid = mature lungs.
- Treat: **exogenous surfactant** (poractant, beractant) + maternal antenatal steroids.

## Airway resistance - Poiseuille's law

<Figure name="respiratory/upper-airway" caption="Related figure for context — Airway resistance - Poiseuille's law." />

```
R = 8ηL / πr⁴
```

The **4th-power radius** dominates. Halve the airway radius → resistance jumps 16×.

- Total airway resistance is greatest in **medium-sized bronchi** - not the smallest. Total cross-sectional area is enormous at the bronchiolar/alveolar level (many tubes in parallel), so resistance per unit length is low.
- The **bronchioles** are the major site of resistance change in disease (constriction in asthma, mucus plugging in COPD).

### Modifiers
| ↑ Resistance | ↓ Resistance |
|---|---|
| Parasympathetic (M3 → contraction) | Sympathetic (β2 → relaxation) |
| Histamine (asthma) | NO + circulating epi |
| Cold + dry air | Warm + humid air |
| Low lung volumes (small airways collapse) | High lung volumes (radial traction holds airways open) |

## Obstructive vs restrictive disease - the PFT signature

<Figure name="respiratory/lung-volumes-spirometry" caption="Lung volumes on spirometry. Obstructive (asthma, COPD): FEV1/FVC < 0.7. Restrictive (fibrosis, kyphoscoliosis, NMD): reduced TLC, FEV1/FVC normal-or-high." />

| Feature | Obstructive (asthma, COPD) | Restrictive (fibrosis, NMD) |
|---|---|---|
| **FEV1** | ↓↓ | ↓ |
| **FVC** | ↓ | ↓↓ |
| **FEV1/FVC** | **↓ (&lt;0.7)** | **Normal or ↑** |
| **TLC** | ↑ (hyperinflation) | ↓ |
| **RV** | ↑↑ | ↓ |
| **FRC** | ↑ (air trapping) | ↓ |
| **DLCO** | ↓ in emphysema; normal in asthma; normal/↓ in chronic bronchitis | ↓ in interstitial lung disease; normal in chest wall/NMD |
| **Compliance** | ↑ in emphysema | ↓↓ |

### Obstructive - types
| Disease | Mechanism | Hallmark |
|---|---|---|
| **Asthma** | Reversible bronchoconstriction; eosinophilic inflammation | Wheeze, ↑ DLCO; reversible with bronchodilator on PFT (>12% + 200 mL) |
| **COPD chronic bronchitis** | Goblet cell hyperplasia + mucus hypersecretion; "blue bloater" | Productive cough ≥3 mo for 2 consecutive years; hypoxia + hypercapnia early |
| **COPD emphysema** | Loss of elastin → permanent enlargement of distal airspaces; "pink puffer" | ↓ DLCO; bullae; barrel chest; α1-AT deficiency in young |
| **Bronchiectasis** | Permanent airway dilation from chronic inflammation/infection | Copious purulent sputum; CF, post-TB |

### Restrictive - types
| Type | Examples | DLCO |
|---|---|---|
| **Interstitial lung disease** (parenchymal) | IPF, sarcoidosis, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, pneumoconioses, drug-induced (bleomycin, amiodarone, methotrexate) | ↓ |
| **Chest wall** | Kyphoscoliosis, obesity, ankylosing spondylitis | Normal |
| **Neuromuscular** | ALS, Guillain-Barré, myasthenia, muscular dystrophy | Normal |

## Control of ventilation

<Figure name="respiratory/respiratory-centers" caption="Brainstem respiratory centers - medullary (rhythm) + pontine (modulation). Central chemoreceptors (CSF H⁺/PCO₂) + peripheral chemoreceptors (carotid + aortic bodies; respond to PaO₂, PaCO₂, pH)." />

### Central control (medulla + pons)
- **Dorsal respiratory group** (NTS) - sets inspiratory rhythm
- **Ventral respiratory group** - expiratory + accessory inspiratory drive in heavy breathing
- **Pneumotaxic center** (pons) - limits inspiration; ↑ rate
- **Apneustic center** (pons) - prolongs inspiration if disinhibited

### Chemoreceptors
| Type | Location | Senses | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Central** | Medulla | ↑ CO₂ (via ↓ CSF pH) - most powerful drive at sea level | ↑ ventilation |
| **Peripheral** | Carotid + aortic bodies | ↓ PaO₂ (especially &lt;60), ↑ PaCO₂, ↓ pH | ↑ ventilation; carotid via CN IX, aortic via CN X |

In chronic CO₂ retainers (COPD), central chemoreceptors reset → **hypoxic drive** (peripheral chemoreceptors) becomes the primary stimulus. **Caution**: excess supplemental O₂ can blunt drive → CO₂ narcosis.

## Function reference - every key player

| Component | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Diaphragm** | Primary inspiratory muscle | Phrenic injury (CN cervical or thoracic surgery) → paradoxical movement on sniff; paralysis halves vital capacity |
| **Pleural fluid + pressure** | Couples lung to chest wall; allows sliding | Pneumothorax = loss of negative pressure → lung collapse |
| **Surfactant** | Reduces surface tension | NRDS in premature; ARDS damages type II pneumocytes |
| **Type II pneumocytes** | Surfactant production + stem cells of alveolar epithelium | Proliferate after type I damage; source of repair |
| **Type I pneumocytes** | 97% of alveolar surface area; gas exchange | Squamous, very thin; killed in ARDS, viral pneumonia |
| **Pores of Kohn** | Inter-alveolar pores | Allow collateral ventilation; spread of pus/infection |
| **Clara (club) cells** | Bronchioles; surfactant components + xenobiotic metabolism (CYP) | Targets of paraquat toxicity; source of bronchiolar epithelial stem cells |
| **Goblet cells** | Mucus secretion in conducting airways | Hyperplastic in chronic bronchitis |
| **Cilia** | Mucociliary escalator | Disabled by smoking; Kartagener (immotile cilia) |
| **Hering-Breuer reflex** | Stretch receptors prevent overinflation | Active in infants + extreme inflation in adults |
| **Pneumotaxic + apneustic centers** | Modulate brainstem rhythm | Brainstem strokes can produce ataxic or apneustic breathing |

## Why this matters

<HighYield variant="boards">
- **Pneumothorax**: spontaneous (tall thin males, Marfan, ruptured bleb); tension (one-way valve from injury) - tracheal deviation away, distended neck veins, hypotension. **Needle decompression** at 2nd ICS midclavicular line → chest tube.
- **PFT bedside read**: FEV1/FVC &lt;0.7 = obstructive. Then check reversibility (asthma vs COPD).
- **A-a gradient**: A – a O₂ tension. Normal = (age/4) + 4. ↑ A-a gradient = V/Q mismatch, shunt, or diffusion abnormality. Normal A-a gradient with hypoxia = hypoventilation or low FiO₂.
- **Shunt vs V/Q mismatch**: shunt won't correct with 100% O₂ (no ventilation to that area). V/Q mismatch will (more O₂ at remaining ventilated alveoli compensates). PE = high V/Q (dead space). Pneumonia/atelectasis = low V/Q (shunt physiology).
- **CO₂ narcosis**: in COPD chronic CO₂ retainers, give O₂ to keep SpO₂ 88–92%. Higher targets risk eliminating hypoxic drive.
- **CPAP + BiPAP**: CPAP = continuous (one pressure, useful in OSA + cardiogenic pulmonary edema). BiPAP = inspiratory + expiratory (different pressures, useful in COPD exacerbation + neuromuscular weakness).
- **Cheyne-Stokes** breathing (crescendo-decrescendo with apneas): CHF, brainstem injury, high altitude.
- **Kussmaul respirations**: deep + rapid + regular (metabolic acidosis compensation, classically DKA).
</HighYield>
