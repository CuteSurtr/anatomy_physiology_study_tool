**Cardiac output (CO) = heart rate (HR) × stroke volume (SV).** Typical resting CO is ~5 L/min; during heavy exercise it can rise 5-6× to ~25-30 L/min in a trained athlete. Every cell in the body depends on the heart matching delivery to demand. Three control systems do this: the **intrinsic Frank-Starling mechanism** of the myocardium itself, **autonomic (sympathetic + parasympathetic) regulation** of HR and contractility, and **humoral influences** (catecholamines, RAAS, ADH).

## The four levers - preload, afterload, contractility, heart rate

<Figure name="cardiovascular/cardiac-cycle-phases" caption="Cardiac cycle phases. CO = HR × SV; SV depends on preload (Frank-Starling), afterload (resistance to ejection), contractility." />

### Preload

Preload is the **stretch of the ventricle just before contraction** - approximated clinically by end-diastolic volume (EDV) or end-diastolic pressure. Determined by:

- **Venous return** (most important - what comes back fills the heart)
- **Atrial contraction** (the atrial kick - 20% of EDV)
- **Ventricular compliance** (stiff ventricle → less filling at same pressure)
- **Heart rate** (very fast HR → less filling time)
- **Body position, intrathoracic pressure** (positive-pressure ventilation reduces preload)

### Afterload

Afterload is the **load the ventricle must overcome to eject blood**. For the LV, ≈ aortic pressure / SVR. For the RV, ≈ pulmonary pressure / PVR. Determinants:
- Arterial blood pressure
- Aortic stiffness (atherosclerosis, age)
- Aortic valve disease (stenosis → ↑ afterload)
- Systemic vascular resistance (vasoconstrictors ↑ afterload)

### Contractility (inotropy)

Force generated at a given preload, *independent of preload and afterload*. Determined by:
- **Sympathetic tone** (β₁ → ↑ contractility via cAMP → ↑ Ca²⁺ entry + ↑ SR Ca²⁺ release)
- **Circulating catecholamines** (Epi from adrenal medulla)
- **Drugs** - digoxin (↑), milrinone (↑), dobutamine (↑); β-blockers (↓), CCBs (↓)
- **Disease state** - HF reduces contractility; hyperthyroidism mildly ↑

### Heart rate

Set by SA node firing rate (autonomic balance):
- **Parasympathetic (vagal, ACh on M2)** - slows the SA node; dominant at rest. Cut the vagus → intrinsic SA rate ~100/min.
- **Sympathetic (β1, NE)** - speeds the SA node; recruited in exercise, stress.

## The Frank-Starling law

<Figure name="cardiovascular/frank-starling-curve" caption="Frank-Starling curve - increased preload (EDV) stretches sarcomeres toward optimal length, increasing stroke volume. ↑ Contractility (sympathetic, dobutamine) shifts the curve UP and LEFT; HFrEF shifts it DOWN and RIGHT (Wikimedia, CC BY-SA)." />

**Increased EDV → increased SV (within limits).** Mechanistically: stretching the ventricle stretches sarcomeres toward optimal length (~2.0-2.2 μm) → more cross-bridge formation → more force. Plus stretch ↑ troponin C Ca²⁺ sensitivity.

This is **intrinsic** to the cardiac muscle - no nerves required. It is what synchronizes left and right heart outputs: if RV pumps a little more, LV preload rises, LV pumps a little more on the next beat. Otherwise blood would pool somewhere.

The **Frank-Starling curve** plots SV (or CO) vs preload. Different curves correspond to different contractility states. ↑ Contractility shifts curve **up and left** (more SV at any preload). ↓ Contractility shifts curve **down and right** (HFrEF).

## Autonomic control - the moment-to-moment regulators

<Figure name="nervous/autonomic-overview" caption="Autonomic control of the heart. Sympathetic (β1) ↑ HR + contractility; parasympathetic (vagus M2 at SA + AV nodes) ↓ HR + AV conduction." />

### Sympathetic outflow

Originates in **rostral ventrolateral medulla (RVLM)** → preganglionic neurons in T1-T4 IML → postganglionic neurons in the **cervical and upper thoracic ganglia and stellate ganglia** → release NE onto:
- SA node, AV node (β₁) → ↑ HR, ↑ conduction velocity
- Atrial + ventricular myocardium (β₁) → ↑ contractility
- Vasculature (α₁) → vasoconstriction (skin, splanchnic); β₂ → vasodilation (skeletal muscle, coronaries)
- Adrenal medulla → Epi + NE release into blood

### Parasympathetic outflow

**Vagus nerve (CN X)** → ACh on M2 receptors at:
- **SA node** → slows firing (chronotropic ↓)
- **AV node** → slows conduction (dromotropic ↓)
- Modest atrial myocardial effects; minimal ventricular effects (sparse vagal innervation)

Resting heart rate (~70/min) reflects predominantly parasympathetic tone. Atropine (muscarinic blocker) reveals intrinsic SA rate of ~100/min. Athletes have higher vagal tone → lower resting HR.

## Reflexes - integrating the inputs

### Baroreceptor reflex - the main short-term BP regulator

**Carotid sinus** (CN IX) and **aortic arch** (CN X) baroreceptors fire in proportion to stretch (BP). Project to NTS in medulla. **BP ↑** → ↑ baroreceptor firing → NTS → ↑ vagal tone, ↓ sympathetic outflow → ↓ HR, ↓ contractility, vasodilation → BP normalizes. **BP ↓** → reflex tachycardia + vasoconstriction.

Tested clinically by **carotid sinus massage** (vagal maneuver) and assessing orthostatic vitals. Reflex tachycardia confirms an intact baroreflex.

### Bainbridge reflex

Stretch receptors in the **right atrium** (volume receptors) → vagal afferents → ↑ HR when atrial pressure rises. Helps match HR to venous return.

### Chemoreceptor reflex

Peripheral chemoreceptors (carotid + aortic bodies) sense hypoxia, hypercapnia, acidosis → ↑ ventilation + ↑ sympathetic outflow → ↑ HR, ↑ BP.

### Cushing reflex

Rising intracranial pressure → cerebral ischemia → sympathetic surge → severe HTN → reflex bradycardia (baroreflex) → respiratory irregularity (brainstem compression). The **Cushing triad**: HTN, bradycardia, irregular breathing - a sign of impending herniation.

## Humoral influences on cardiac output

<Figure name="cardiovascular/raas" caption="The renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system - JG cells secrete renin → angiotensin I → ACE → angiotensin II → vasoconstriction + aldosterone + ADH + thirst + proximal Na⁺/H₂O reabsorption." />

<Figure name="cardiovascular/wiggers-diagram" caption="Wiggers diagram - the integrated cardiac cycle. Stacks aortic + LV + LA pressures, ventricular volume, ECG, and phonocardiogram on the same time axis. Sympathetic stimulation shortens systole + diastole proportionally." />

| Mediator | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Epinephrine** (adrenal medulla) | β₁ (heart) > β₂ (vessels) at moderate doses → ↑ CO; at high doses, α₁ effects vasoconstrict → ↑ BP |
| **Norepinephrine** (sympathetic) | α₁ vasoconstriction; β₁ ↑ HR + contractility (often reflex bradycardia from BP rise) |
| **Angiotensin II** | Direct vasoconstrictor + indirect via aldosterone + ADH + thirst |
| **ADH (vasopressin)** | V1: vasoconstrictor at high doses; V2: water retention → ↑ preload over hours |
| **ANP / BNP** | Reduce preload (natriuresis) and afterload (vasodilation) |
| **Thyroid hormone** | Permissive for catecholamines; ↑ HR, ↑ contractility |
| **Cortisol** | Permissive; vascular tone falls without it (Addisonian shock) |

## Exercise - the integrated response

Resting CO ~5 L/min. Heavy exercise: up to ~25 L/min (untrained) or 30+ L/min (athlete).

| Variable | Resting | Heavy exercise | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| HR | 70 | 180 | Sympathetic ↑, vagal withdrawal |
| SV | 70 mL | 130 mL | ↑ Preload (skeletal muscle pump, sympathetic venoconstriction) + ↑ contractility |
| CO | 5 L/min | 25 L/min | Multiplicative |
| MAP | 90 | 100-110 | Slight rise (vasodilation in working muscle offsets the CO rise) |
| Muscle blood flow | 20% of CO | 85% of CO | β₂ + metabolic vasodilation |
| Splanchnic / renal flow | 50% of CO | 5% of CO | α₁ vasoconstriction |
| Skin flow | low | high | Increases for heat dissipation |

## Heart failure - what goes wrong

<Figure name="cardiovascular/heart-schematic" caption="Heart anatomy — failure can be left-sided (pulmonary congestion), right-sided (peripheral edema, JVD), or biventricular." />

| Type | EF | Predominant problem |
|---|---|---|
| **HFrEF** | < 40% | Systolic - reduced contractility. CO low. Frank-Starling curve depressed and right-shifted |
| **HFpEF** | > 50% | Diastolic - impaired filling. EF preserved but EDV low. Often HTN, LVH, restrictive disease |
| **High-output failure** | Normal-high | Heart works hard but demand outstrips it - hyperthyroidism, anemia, AV fistula, beriberi |

In chronic HFrEF, sympathetic overdrive, RAAS activation, and ADH retention initially compensate but eventually drive **remodeling** (LV dilation, fibrosis). Modern therapy targets the maladaptive neurohormonal response:

| Drug class | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **ACE-I / ARB / ARNI** | Block RAAS; reduce afterload + remodeling |
| **β-blockers** (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol) | Block toxic sympathetic effects; long-term improvement in contractility and survival |
| **MRA** (spironolactone, eplerenone) | Block aldosterone; reduce fibrosis and mortality |
| **SGLT2 inhibitors** | Surprising mortality benefit in HFrEF *and* HFpEF; mechanism multifactorial (natriuresis, metabolic, anti-fibrotic) |
| **Loop diuretics** | Symptomatic relief; don't extend life |
| **Digoxin** | Mild contractility ↑; reduces hospitalizations; no mortality benefit |
