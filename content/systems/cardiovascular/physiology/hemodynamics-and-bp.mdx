Blood pressure is the central regulated variable in cardiovascular physiology. The body monitors it second-by-second and adjusts cardiac output, vascular tone, and blood volume to keep it within a narrow window (~mean 90–95 mmHg in healthy adults). Three lines of defense - **baroreceptor reflex** (seconds), **RAAS + ADH** (minutes to hours), and **renal pressure natriuresis** (days) - govern long-term BP control.

## The fundamental equation

<Figure name="cardiovascular/cardiac-output-factors" caption="Factors controlling cardiac output - CO = HR × SV. HR set by autonomic balance at the SA node; SV set by preload (Frank-Starling), afterload (resistance to ejection), and contractility." />

```
BP = CO × TPR
CO = HR × SV
```

| Variable | Determinants |
|---|---|
| **HR** (heart rate) | Autonomic input (β1 ↑, M2 ↓); intrinsic SA rate; circulating catecholamines |
| **SV** (stroke volume) | **Preload** + **contractility** + **afterload** |
| **TPR** (total peripheral resistance) | Arteriolar radius (4th power - Poiseuille), blood viscosity, vessel length |

Any persistent BP elevation reflects ↑ in **at least one** of these. Essential hypertension is usually ↑ TPR; CHF often shows ↓ SV with compensatory ↑ HR + ↑ TPR; pheochromocytoma drives ↑ HR + ↑ TPR via catecholamines.

One useful way to organize the whole topic is: **the heart sets flow, arteries distribute pressure, arterioles regulate resistance, and veins store volume**. Most BP drugs work by changing one of those four buckets.

## Preload, contractility, afterload - the three SV levers

<Figure name="cardiovascular/frank-starling-curve" caption="Frank-Starling curve - increased preload (EDV) stretches sarcomeres → ↑ stroke volume. Inotropes shift the curve up; HFrEF shifts it down." />

| Lever | Definition | Determinants | What raises it | What lowers it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Preload** | End-diastolic ventricular volume → wall stretch | Venous return, ventricular compliance, atrial kick | Volume load, lying down, exercise, ↑ venous tone | Hemorrhage, dehydration, venodilators (nitrates), tachycardia (less filling time) |
| **Contractility** (inotropy) | Force at any given preload | Sympathetic tone, Ca²⁺ handling | β1 agonists, digoxin, ↑ extracellular Ca²⁺ | β-blockers, acidosis, hypoxia, MI |
| **Afterload** | Pressure ventricle must overcome to eject | Aortic pressure, TPR, valve stenosis | Hypertension, aortic stenosis, vasoconstriction | Vasodilators (hydralazine, ACE inhibitors) |

### Frank-Starling - the heart's intrinsic preload response
Greater end-diastolic volume → greater stretch of cardiac myocytes → optimal actin-myosin overlap → **stronger contraction**. This is why both ventricles eject the same volume despite no central coordination - venous return = cardiac output at steady state.

<Mnemonic>
**"More in → more out"** - within physiological range. Beyond the peak (overstretched), force *falls* (descending limb), which is what happens in decompensated heart failure.
</Mnemonic>

## Poiseuille's law - why arterioles control TPR

```
Q = (π × ΔP × r⁴) / (8 × η × L)
```

The 4th-power dependence on **radius** is the key. Halve the radius → flow drops 16×. This is why a small change in arteriolar smooth muscle tone produces massive shifts in resistance. **Capillaries** have small radius but enormous total cross-sectional area + are in parallel → low resistance, slow flow (ideal for exchange).

<Figure name="cardiovascular/artery-vein-layers" caption="Arteries invest in a thick muscular media because they handle pressure; veins invest in capacitance and one-way valves because they return volume." />

## Three timescales of BP regulation

<Figure name="cardiovascular/raas" caption="BP regulation timescales - seconds (baroreceptor reflex via ANS), minutes (RAAS, vasopressin), days (kidney pressure-natriuresis, long-term setpoint)." />

### 1. Seconds - Baroreceptor reflex (neural)

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Sensors** | Stretch receptors in **carotid sinus** (CN IX → glossopharyngeal) + **aortic arch** (CN X → vagus) |
| **Integrator** | Nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS) in the medulla |
| **Effectors** | Sympathetic (↑ HR, ↑ contractility, ↑ TPR, ↑ venous tone) + parasympathetic (vagal slowing of HR) |

**↑ BP** → ↑ baroreceptor firing → NTS → **↑ parasympathetic + ↓ sympathetic** → ↓ HR + vasodilation → BP returns to setpoint.

**↓ BP** → ↓ firing → reverse → reflex tachycardia + vasoconstriction.

<Clinical>
**Orthostatic hypotension** - failure of the baroreflex to compensate on standing. Drop in SBP ≥ 20 or DBP ≥ 10 within 3 minutes of standing. Causes: volume depletion, autonomic neuropathy (diabetes, Parkinson's, multiple system atrophy), α-blockers, ganglion blockers.

**Carotid sinus hypersensitivity** - exaggerated baroreflex; pressure on the carotid (tight collar, shaving) → bradycardia + syncope.

**Vasovagal syncope** - emotional/painful stimulus → paradoxical reflex bradycardia + vasodilation → drop in cerebral perfusion → faint. Common; benign.
</Clinical>

### 2. Minutes - Chemoreceptors + ADH

**Peripheral chemoreceptors** (carotid + aortic bodies) respond to **↓ PaO₂**, ↑ PaCO₂, ↓ pH → activate sympathetics → ↑ HR, ↑ BP, ↑ ventilation. Important in shock + hypoxia.

**Central chemoreceptors** (medulla) respond only to ↑ CO₂ (via CSF pH) - mainly for ventilation, not BP.

**ADH/vasopressin** (posterior pituitary):
- Released in response to ↑ plasma osmolarity (primary) or large ↓ BP (>10%, via baroreceptors)
- V1 receptors → vasoconstriction (BP support)
- V2 receptors → aquaporin-2 insertion in collecting duct → water reabsorption → volume expansion

### 3. Hours to days - RAAS + renal pressure natriuresis

The **renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS)** is the master volume + BP regulator.

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | ↓ renal perfusion (sensed by JG cells) OR ↓ NaCl at macula densa OR ↑ sympathetic input (β1 on JG cells) |
| **Renin** | Released by JG cells; cleaves angiotensinogen (liver) → **Angiotensin I** |
| **ACE** | Lung endothelium converts AI → **Angiotensin II** |
| **Angiotensin II actions** | (1) Potent **vasoconstrictor** (efferent arteriole > afferent → preserves GFR); (2) stimulates **aldosterone** (adrenal zona glomerulosa) → Na⁺ + water retention in distal tubule/collecting duct; (3) stimulates **ADH**; (4) stimulates thirst (hypothalamus); (5) increases sympathetic outflow |
| **Net effect** | ↑ BP + ↑ blood volume |

**Renal pressure natriuresis** - when arterial BP rises, the kidneys excrete more salt and water → blood volume falls → BP normalizes. This is the **dominant long-term regulator**. Hypertension persists when the kidney's pressure-natriuresis curve is shifted rightward - needs a higher BP to excrete the same Na⁺.

## Drugs that target each lever - pharmacology backbone

<Figure name="foundations/signal-transduction" caption="Related figure for context — Drugs that target each lever - pharmacology backbone." />

| Class | Site | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **ACE inhibitors** (lisinopril) | ACE in lung | ↓ AII → vasodilation + ↓ aldosterone + ↓ ADH |
| **ARBs** (losartan) | AT1 receptor | Same end result; no cough |
| **Direct renin inhibitor** (aliskiren) | Renin | Blocks the cascade entry |
| **Aldosterone antagonists** (spironolactone, eplerenone) | Distal tubule MR | ↓ Na/water retention; K⁺ sparing |
| **Thiazides** (HCTZ) | Distal convoluted tubule NCC | ↓ blood volume |
| **Loop diuretics** (furosemide) | Thick ascending limb NKCC2 | Larger volume effect; useful in CHF, HTN |
| **β-blockers** (metoprolol) | β1 in heart + JG cells | ↓ HR, ↓ contractility, ↓ renin |
| **α1-blockers** (prazosin) | Arterial + venous smooth muscle | Vasodilation |
| **Dihydropyridine CCBs** (amlodipine) | L-type Ca²⁺ channels in arterial smooth muscle | Vasodilation (low cardiac effect) |
| **Non-DHP CCBs** (verapamil, diltiazem) | L-type Ca²⁺ channels in heart > vessels | ↓ HR + contractility |
| **Hydralazine** | Arteriolar smooth muscle | Direct vasodilation (preferred in pregnancy + CKD) |
| **α2 agonists** (clonidine) | Brainstem | ↓ sympathetic outflow |
| **Sodium nitroprusside** | Arterial + venous (NO donor) | Hypertensive emergency |

## Mean arterial pressure (MAP)

<Figure name="cardiovascular/pulse-wave-analysis" caption="Arterial pulse-wave analysis - systolic peak, dicrotic notch (aortic valve closure), diastolic decay. Pulse pressure (SBP - DBP) widens with stiffer arteries (aging, atherosclerosis, AR)." />

Because diastole is twice as long as systole:

```
MAP ≈ DBP + (1/3 × pulse pressure)
    = DBP + (1/3 × [SBP – DBP])
```

**MAP > 60** required for organ perfusion. **MAP < 65** = shock. In sepsis resuscitation, MAP is the explicit target (>65 mmHg).

**Pulse pressure** (SBP – DBP):
- ↑ in aortic regurgitation, aortic stiffening (aging), anemia, thyrotoxicosis, exercise
- ↓ in aortic stenosis, cardiogenic shock, hemorrhage

## Shock states - when BP regulation fails

<Figure name="cardiovascular/circulation-overview" caption="" />


| Type | CO | TPR | PCWP / preload | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hypovolemic** | ↓ | ↑ (compensatory) | ↓ | Hemorrhage, dehydration |
| **Cardiogenic** | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ | Pump failure (MI, severe valve dz) |
| **Obstructive** | ↓ | ↑ | varies | Tamponade, tension pneumothorax, massive PE |
| **Distributive - septic** | ↑ early, ↓ late | ↓↓ | ↓ or normal | Cytokine-mediated vasodilation |
| **Distributive - anaphylactic** | ↑ early | ↓↓ | ↓ | Massive histamine release |
| **Distributive - neurogenic** | normal | ↓↓ | ↓ | Loss of sympathetic tone (spinal cord injury T6+) |

<Mnemonic>
**Warm shock vs cold shock**: distributive (septic, anaphylactic, neurogenic) → warm extremities (vasodilation). Hypovolemic + cardiogenic + obstructive → cold + clammy (vasoconstriction).
</Mnemonic>

## Function reference - every BP regulator

| System | Sensor | Target | Time course |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Baroreflex** | Carotid sinus + aortic arch stretch receptors | Sympathetic + parasympathetic output | Seconds |
| **Peripheral chemoreflex** | Carotid + aortic body O₂/CO₂/pH | Sympathetic output | Seconds |
| **ADH (vasopressin)** | Hypothalamic osmoreceptors + baroreceptors | V1 (vessels) + V2 (kidney) | Minutes |
| **RAAS** | JG cells + macula densa | AII + aldosterone | Minutes-hours |
| **Renal pressure natriuresis** | Renal arterial pressure | Na + water excretion | Days |
| **Atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP)** | Atrial stretch | Vasodilation + natriuresis (opposes RAAS) | Hours |
| **B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP)** | Ventricular stretch | Similar to ANP; **biomarker in CHF** | Hours |
| **Endothelin-1** | Local endothelium | Vasoconstriction; ↑ in pulmonary HTN | Minutes |
| **Nitric oxide (NO)** | Endothelial NOS, shear stress | Vasodilation via cGMP | Seconds |
| **Prostacyclin (PGI₂)** | Endothelium | Vasodilation + anti-platelet | Seconds |

## Why this matters

<Figure name="cardiovascular/erythrocytes" caption="" />


<HighYield variant="boards">
- **Essential hypertension** (90% of cases) = no single identifiable cause; mostly ↑ TPR + abnormal renal pressure natriuresis. First-line: thiazide, ACEi/ARB, CCB.
- **Secondary HTN clues** - young patient, abrupt onset, refractory, hypokalemia (primary hyperaldosteronism, Cushing, renin-secreting tumor), episodic (pheochromocytoma), bruit (renal artery stenosis), coarctation (BP arm > leg).
- **Pheochromocytoma**: episodic HTN + headache + sweating + tachycardia. Diagnosis: 24-h urine metanephrines. Treat: **α-blockade first, then β-blockade** (never β alone - unopposed α → hypertensive crisis).
- **Renal artery stenosis** - ACE inhibitors can crash GFR (efferent arteriolar dilation removes the only compensatory mechanism). Bilateral RAS or RAS in a solitary kidney = contraindication for ACEi/ARB.
- **Postural hypotension drug culprits** - α-blockers (prazosin), nitrates, diuretics, TCAs (anti-α), antipsychotics.
- **Cushing reflex** (rising ICP): **hypertension + bradycardia + irregular respirations**. Ischemic medulla → reflex sympathetic surge to maintain cerebral perfusion → baroreflex slows HR. **Pre-terminal sign** of brainstem compression.
</HighYield>
