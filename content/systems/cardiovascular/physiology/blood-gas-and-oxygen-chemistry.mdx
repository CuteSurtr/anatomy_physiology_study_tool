The chemistry of hemoglobin is one of the cleanest stories in physiology - a single protein that ferries about 1.34 mL of O₂ per gram, switches between two conformations on cue, and senses the chemistry of its surroundings (CO₂, H⁺, 2,3-BPG, temperature) to deliver oxygen exactly where it's needed.

## Hemoglobin structure

<Figure name="cardiovascular/hemoglobin-structure" caption="Hemoglobin quaternary structure - 4 globin subunits (2α + 2β in adult HbA), each holding one heme group with central Fe²⁺. T (tense, deoxy) vs R (relaxed, oxy) conformations underpin cooperative O₂ binding (Wikimedia, CC BY-SA)." />

<Figure name="cardiovascular/rbc-blausen" caption="Erythrocytes (red blood cells) - anucleate biconcave discs, ~7-8 μm. Pack ~280 million Hb tetramers per cell. Lifespan ~120 days." />

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Subunits** | 4 globin chains, 2α + 2β in adult HbA. Each subunit holds one heme group |
| **Heme** | Protoporphyrin IX ring + central Fe²⁺ (ferrous) - only ferrous Fe binds O₂ |
| **Total O₂ binding sites** | 4 (one per subunit) |
| **Quaternary states** | **T (tense)** low affinity, deoxy; **R (relaxed)** high affinity, oxy |
| **Cooperativity** | Binding one O₂ stabilizes R-state in the others → sigmoid binding curve |
| **Bohr / Haldane effects** | T-state stabilized by ↑ CO₂, ↑ H⁺, ↓ pH; R-state by ↑ O₂ |

## The O₂-Hb dissociation curve

<Figure name="cardiovascular/o2hb-dissociation" caption="Oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve - sigmoidal because of cooperative O₂ binding. Right shift (↑T, ↑PCO₂, ↑H⁺, ↑2,3-BPG) releases O₂ at the tissue (Bohr effect). Left shift hoards O₂ (OpenStax)." />

<Figure name="cardiovascular/fetal-vs-adult-hb" caption="Fetal vs adult O₂-Hb dissociation curves. HbF (α₂γ₂) binds 2,3-BPG poorly → left-shifted curve → higher affinity → favors O₂ transfer across placenta (OpenStax)." />

`Y = PO₂ⁿ / (P50ⁿ + PO₂ⁿ)` - the Hill equation, where **Y** is fractional saturation and **n ≈ 2.7–3.0** for adult Hb. An n above 1 is the mathematical signature of cooperativity; n = 1 would give the plain hyperbola of a non-cooperative carrier such as myoglobin.

| Point | PaO₂ | SaO₂ |
|---|---|---|
| Alveolus | 100 | 100% |
| Steep-slope inflection | 60 | 90% |
| Mixed venous | 40 | 75% |
| P50 (50% saturation) | 26 | 50% |

**Right shift (lower affinity, dumps O₂ at tissue):**
- ↑ Temperature
- ↑ PCO₂
- ↑ H⁺ (↓ pH) - Bohr effect
- ↑ 2,3-BPG (chronic anemia, altitude, hypoxia)

**Left shift (higher affinity, hoards O₂):**
- Cold, hypocapnia, alkalemia
- HbF (lower 2,3-BPG binding)
- CO and methemoglobin (left shift residual Hb)

## Hemoglobin variants and disorders

| Variant | Cause | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **HbA** | α2β2 | Normal adult |
| **HbA2** | α2δ2 | ~2.5% of adult Hb; elevated in β-thalassemia |
| **HbF** | α2γ2 | Fetal; left-shifted; persists in hereditary persistence of HbF (HPFH) |
| **HbS** | β6 Glu→Val (sickle) | Polymerizes when deoxygenated → sickling, vaso-occlusion. Hydroxyurea raises HbF, reducing crises |
| **HbC** | β6 Glu→Lys | Mild hemolysis, crystallization |
| **HbH** | β4 (no α) | α-thalassemia; only β chains form tetramers; left-shifted, hemolysis |
| **Hb Barts** | γ4 (no α) | Fatal α-thal hydrops fetalis |
| **HbCO (carboxyhemoglobin)** | CO binding | 240× O₂ affinity; left-shifts remaining Hb; cherry red. Treat 100% O₂ ± hyperbaric |
| **MetHb (methemoglobin)** | Fe³⁺ instead of Fe²⁺ | Can't bind O₂; cyanosis with normal PaO₂; pulse ox ~85%. Treat methylene blue (cofactor for cytochrome b5 reductase) |

## Iron chemistry - the source of Hb function

<Figure name="cardiovascular/erythrocytes" caption="Erythrocytes - the central Hb-carrying cell. Iron deficiency, anemia of chronic disease, thalassemia, and sickle cell disease all manifest as RBC abnormalities." />

Adult Hb requires ~25 mg iron/day cycled through plasma transferrin. Body holds ~3-4 g total iron, mostly in Hb.

| Form | Where | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Heme iron (Fe²⁺)** | Hb, myoglobin, cytochromes | Binds O₂, electron transport |
| **Ferritin** | Liver, spleen, marrow | Storage; serum ferritin is acute phase reactant + iron-status marker |
| **Hemosiderin** | Macrophages | Insoluble iron from broken-down ferritin |
| **Transferrin** | Plasma | Iron carrier; saturation = serum Fe / TIBC |
| **Hepcidin** | Liver-produced hormone | **Master regulator** - blocks ferroportin → reduces iron absorption + release from macrophages. ↑ in inflammation = anemia of chronic disease |

### Iron deficiency vs anemia of chronic disease vs thalassemia

| | Iron deficiency | ACD | β-thalassemia minor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MCV** | ↓ | Normal or ↓ | ↓↓ |
| **Serum Fe** | ↓↓ | ↓ | Normal |
| **TIBC** | ↑ | ↓ | Normal |
| **Ferritin** | ↓↓ | ↑ or normal | Normal |
| **HbA2 on electrophoresis** | Normal | Normal | ↑↑ |

## CO₂ transport chemistry

<Figure name="respiratory/o2-co2-curve" caption="O2/CO2 dissociation comparison. CO2 carried as bicarbonate (70%), carbamino-Hb (23%), and dissolved (7%). Haldane effect: deoxyHb binds CO2 better." />

CO₂ is 20× more soluble than O₂, but most is carried as bicarbonate:

| Form | % of total | Chemistry |
|---|---|---|
| **HCO₃⁻** | 70% | CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ (carbonic anhydrase in RBC) |
| **Carbamino-Hb** | 23% | CO₂ + Hb-NH₂ → Hb-NH-COO⁻; Haldane effect: deoxy-Hb favors CO₂ binding |
| **Dissolved** | 7% | Obeys Henry's law (PaCO₂) |

**Chloride shift**: HCO₃⁻ generated in RBC exits via band 3 (AE1) antiporter exchanging for Cl⁻; reverses in lung.

## Carbon monoxide poisoning - the chemistry

<Figure name="cardiovascular/o2hb-dissociation" caption="CO poisoning effect on O2-Hb curve - CO binds Hb with 240× affinity AND left-shifts remaining Hb → impaired O2 unloading. Pulse ox can't distinguish HbCO from HbO2." />

CO binds Hb at the same heme Fe²⁺ as O₂, but with ~240× the affinity. Three pathological consequences:

1. **Reduced O₂ carrying capacity** - any Hb with CO bound can't carry O₂
2. **Left-shifted residual Hb** - O₂ that IS bound won't release at tissue
3. **Direct mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase inhibition** at high doses

**Treatment:** 100% O₂ via non-rebreather mask. HbCO half-life on room air = 5 h; on 100% O₂ = 80 min; on hyperbaric O₂ (2.5 atm) = 23 min.

Hyperbaric O₂ indicated for: HbCO > 25%, neurologic symptoms, syncope, cardiac ischemia, pregnancy, persistent symptoms despite normobaric O₂.

## Methemoglobinemia

<Figure name="cardiovascular/hemoglobin-structure" caption="Hemoglobin structure - in metHb, Fe²⁺ is oxidized to Fe³⁺ and can't bind O2. Causes: dapsone, benzocaine, nitrites. Treat with methylene blue." />

Fe²⁺ in heme spontaneously oxidizes to Fe³⁺ at low rate; cytochrome b5 reductase (NADH-dependent) reduces it back. When the system is overwhelmed or deficient (congenital), MetHb accumulates.

**Causes:**
- Topical anesthetics: **benzocaine** (especially in infants), **prilocaine**
- **Dapsone**, primaquine, sulfa drugs
- Nitrites, nitrates (e.g., baby formula made with well water - "blue baby")
- Aniline dyes
- Congenital cytochrome b5 reductase deficiency

**Findings:** chocolate-brown blood, cyanosis NOT responding to supplemental O₂, normal PaO₂ but low SpO₂ (pulse ox reads ~85% as MetHb fraction rises).

**Treatment:** **Methylene blue**, which does *not* work through the usual cytochrome b5 reductase route - it bypasses it. NADPH methemoglobin reductase reduces methylene blue to leucomethylene blue, which then reduces Fe³⁺ back to Fe²⁺ non-enzymatically. Because that pathway runs on NADPH from the pentose phosphate shunt, methylene blue is ineffective and potentially haemolytic in **G6PD deficiency** - use ascorbic acid (or exchange transfusion) instead.

## Oxygen content equation - full version

<Figure name="cardiovascular/o2hb-dissociation" caption="O2 content = 1.34 × Hb × SaO2 + 0.003 × PaO2. Most O2 is bound to Hb; dissolved fraction (<2%) only matters in hyperbaric therapy." />

`CaO₂ = (1.34 × [Hb] × SaO₂) + (0.003 × PaO₂)`

Where:
- 1.34 mL O₂/g Hb = Hüfner constant (max O₂ Hb can carry)
- 0.003 mL O₂/dL/mmHg = solubility coefficient

**Normal values:** Hb 15 g/dL, SaO₂ 100%, PaO₂ 100 → CaO₂ = 20.4 mL O₂/dL.

**O₂ delivery:** `DO₂ = CO × CaO₂` (~1000 mL/min at rest).

**O₂ consumption:** ~250 mL/min at rest; can rise 10× in heavy exercise.

**O₂ extraction ratio:** `O₂ER = (CaO₂ − CvO₂) / CaO₂` (~25% at rest, up to 80% in heavy exercise or shock).

## Hemoglobinopathy clinical chemistry

<Clinical>
**Sickle cell disease (HbSS)** - homozygous β6 Glu→Val mutation. Sickling in deoxy state:
- **Vaso-occlusive crises**: pain in long bones, chest (acute chest syndrome - emergency), spleen → autosplenectomy (Howell-Jolly bodies on smear, encapsulated bacteria infection risk)
- **Hemolytic anemia**: chronic; elevated retics, indirect bilirubin, LDH; aplastic crisis with parvovirus B19
- **Stroke**: kids, screen with transcranial doppler; chronic transfusion to keep HbS &lt; 30%
- **Renal medullary necrosis** + isosthenuria
- **Avascular necrosis** of femoral head
- **Priapism**
- **Hydroxyurea** is the cornerstone disease-modifying therapy - induces HbF expression, reducing sickling
- **Allogeneic stem cell transplant** can be curative; gene therapy (exa-cel) is now approved

**β-thalassemia major** (homozygous β⁰):
- No β chains → α tetramers precipitate → ineffective erythropoiesis + extramedullary hematopoiesis (chipmunk facies, hair-on-end skull)
- Transfusion-dependent from infancy
- Iron overload → cardiac and hepatic damage (treat with deferasirox, deferoxamine, deferiprone)
- Curative therapy: allogeneic SCT, gene therapy
</Clinical>
