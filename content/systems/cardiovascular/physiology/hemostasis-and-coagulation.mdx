Hemostasis is the body's response to vascular injury: stop the bleed, plug the hole, then dismantle the plug when the wall is healed. It happens in three overlapping phases - **vascular**, **platelet (primary hemostasis)**, **coagulation (secondary hemostasis)** - followed by **fibrinolysis** to clean up.

## The three phases of hemostasis

<Figure name="cardiovascular/coagulation-simple" caption="Simplified coagulation cascade - extrinsic (tissue factor / VII), intrinsic (XII → XI → IX → VIII), and common pathway (X → II/thrombin → I/fibrinogen → fibrin clot)." />

### 1. Vascular phase (seconds)

Damaged vessel wall **vasoconstricts** to slow blood loss. Triggered by:
- Local myogenic spasm (smooth muscle reflex to injury)
- Endothelin release from damaged endothelium
- Pain reflexes via sympathetic nerves

A small artery can fully occlude itself for minutes - long enough for the platelet plug to form.

### 2. Platelet phase / primary hemostasis (minutes)

<Figure name="cardiovascular/platelets" caption="Platelets - anucleate cell fragments shed from megakaryocytes. Roughly 150,000-400,000 per μL. Half-life 8-10 days. Carry dense granules (ADP, serotonin, Ca²⁺) and α granules (vWF, fibrinogen, PDGF)." />

When endothelium is breached, subendothelial **collagen** and **von Willebrand factor (vWF)** are exposed. Three steps:

1. **Adhesion** - Platelet GP1b binds vWF, which is bound to collagen. (vWF acts as the bridge.)
2. **Activation** - Bound platelets release granule contents: **ADP**, **thromboxane A₂ (TXA₂)**, **serotonin**, **Ca²⁺**. Shape changes from disc to spiky to expose GPIIb/IIIa receptors.
3. **Aggregation** - GPIIb/IIIa on activated platelets binds **fibrinogen**, cross-linking platelets into a soft white plug.

<Note title="Drug targets in primary hemostasis">
- **Aspirin** - irreversibly acetylates COX-1 → no TXA₂ for the life of the platelet (~7-10 days)
- **Clopidogrel, prasugrel, ticagrelor** - block the platelet P2Y₁₂ ADP receptor
- **Abciximab, eptifibatide, tirofiban** - block GPIIb/IIIa (the final common step)
- **Desmopressin (DDAVP)** - releases vWF from endothelial Weibel-Palade bodies; useful in mild vWD and uremic platelet dysfunction
</Note>

### 3. Coagulation phase / secondary hemostasis (minutes)

The platelet plug is **friable** - it has to be stabilized with fibrin mesh. The coagulation cascade is the engine.

**Two pathways, one common end:**

| Pathway | Initiator | Factors | Lab test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Extrinsic** | Tissue factor (TF/III) released from injured cells | VII | **PT / INR** |
| **Intrinsic** | Subendothelial contact (collagen, kallikrein, HMWK) | XII → XI → IX → VIII | **aPTT** |
| **Common** | Both pathways converge | X → II (prothrombin) → I (fibrinogen → fibrin) | PT, aPTT |

**Thrombin (factor IIa) is the central player.** It:
- Converts fibrinogen → fibrin (monomers polymerize into mesh)
- Activates factor XIII → cross-links fibrin into a stable clot
- Amplifies its own production by activating V, VIII, XI (positive feedback)
- Activates platelets (PAR receptors)
- Stimulates **protein C** when bound to thrombomodulin (negative feedback)

<Mnemonic title="Vitamin K-dependent factors">
**"1972"** - factors **II, VII, IX, X** plus **protein C** and **protein S** all require vitamin K-dependent gamma-carboxylation by hepatic γ-glutamyl carboxylase.

Warfarin blocks vitamin K epoxide reductase → depletes functional factors II, VII, IX, X. The order of decline (and recovery) reflects each factor's half-life: VII (6 h) goes first, II (60 h) goes last.
</Mnemonic>

## Anticoagulant counterregulation

<Figure name="cardiovascular/coagulation-simple" caption="Coagulation cascade + endogenous brakes. Antithrombin III (potentiated by heparin), protein C + S (degrade Va + VIIIa), TFPI, fibrinolysis (plasmin)." />

Without brakes, every cut would clot the whole vasculature. Four major endogenous anticoagulants:

| System | Mechanism | Clinical relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Antithrombin III (AT-III)** | Inactivates thrombin and factors IXa, Xa, XIa, XIIa | Potentiated 1000× by heparin |
| **Protein C / Protein S** | Activated by thrombin–thrombomodulin complex; degrades factors Va + VIIIa | Factor V Leiden mutation = resistant Va → thrombophilia |
| **Tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI)** | Blocks TF–VIIa complex after Xa is generated | Limits extrinsic pathway |
| **Prostacyclin (PGI₂) + NO** | Endothelium-derived; inhibit platelet aggregation, vasodilate | Lost in damaged endothelium → local clotting |

## Fibrinolysis - dissolving the clot

<Figure name="cardiovascular/coagulation" caption="Fibrinolysis - plasminogen → plasmin (via tPA) → cleaves fibrin to D-dimers. tPA (alteplase), TXA (anti-fibrinolytic), urokinase, streptokinase all act here." />

<Clinical>
Once the vessel wall has healed (days), the clot must come down. **Plasminogen** is incorporated into the clot during formation. **Tissue plasminogen activator (tPA)** released from endothelial cells converts plasminogen → **plasmin**, which cleaves fibrin into **D-dimers** and other fibrin degradation products (FDPs).

- **Elevated D-dimer** indicates recent fibrin turnover - sensitive but not specific marker for VTE/PE/DIC.
- **Therapeutic thrombolytics** (alteplase, tenecteplase) are recombinant tPA - given for acute ischemic stroke (within 4.5 h), STEMI without PCI access, massive PE.
- **Tranexamic acid** blocks lysine binding on plasminogen → prevents plasmin formation → used to reduce surgical bleeding and trauma hemorrhage.
</Clinical>

## Lab tests and what they mean

| Test | What it measures | Normal | Prolonged in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PT / INR** | Extrinsic + common (VII, X, V, II, fibrinogen) | INR 0.8-1.2 | Warfarin, liver disease, vitamin K deficiency, DIC |
| **aPTT** | Intrinsic + common (XII, XI, IX, VIII, X, V, II, fibrinogen) | 25-35 s | Heparin, hemophilia A/B, vWD, lupus anticoagulant |
| **Thrombin time** | Fibrinogen → fibrin step | 14-19 s | Heparin, dysfibrinogenemia, DIC |
| **Bleeding time** | Platelet function | 2-7 min | vWD, qualitative platelet defects, uremia |
| **Platelet count** | Quantity | 150-450 ×10⁹/L | ITP, TTP, HIT, DIC, marrow failure |
| **D-dimer** | Fibrinolysis | < 500 ng/mL | DVT/PE, DIC, recent surgery, pregnancy |

## Pharmacology summary

<Figure name="foundations/signal-transduction" caption="Related figure for context — Pharmacology summary." />

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Anticoagulants by mechanism:**
- **Unfractionated heparin (UFH)** - potentiates AT-III; monitored by aPTT; reversed with protamine. Half-life ~90 min.
- **LMWH (enoxaparin)** - selective for Xa; monitor anti-Xa if needed; partial protamine reversal.
- **Fondaparinux** - pure Xa inhibitor; no HIT risk; no good reversal.
- **Warfarin** - vitamin K antagonist; monitored by INR; reversed with vitamin K + 4F-PCC (or FFP). Bridge with heparin because protein C drops first (transient prothrombotic state).
- **DOACs - dabigatran** (direct thrombin) reversed with **idarucizumab**; **rivaroxaban/apixaban/edoxaban** (direct Xa) reversed with **andexanet alfa**.

**Antiplatelets by step:**
- **Aspirin** → COX-1 → no TXA₂
- **P2Y₁₂ inhibitors** (clopidogrel, prasugrel, ticagrelor) → no ADP signaling
- **GPIIb/IIIa inhibitors** → no fibrinogen bridging (IV only, used in PCI)
- **Dipyridamole** → ↑ cAMP via PDE inhibition

**Thrombolytics:** alteplase, tenecteplase, reteplase, streptokinase. Major risk: hemorrhage (especially intracranial).
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Classic exam patterns:**
- **Hemophilia A** (factor VIII deficiency) → ↑ aPTT, normal PT, normal platelets. X-linked recessive.
- **Hemophilia B** (factor IX deficiency, "Christmas disease") → identical labs to A.
- **von Willebrand disease** → ↑ bleeding time, ± ↑ aPTT (vWF carries VIII), normal PT.
- **Vitamin K deficiency / warfarin** → ↑ PT first, then ↑ aPTT (because factor VII has shortest half-life and is monitored by PT).
- **Liver failure** → ↑ PT first (factor VII), then ↑ aPTT, low fibrinogen if severe.
- **DIC** → ↑ PT, ↑ aPTT, ↓ platelets, ↓ fibrinogen, ↑ D-dimer. Schistocytes on smear.
- **HIT** (heparin-induced thrombocytopenia) → drop in platelets 5-10 days after heparin start; **paradoxically thrombotic**. Stop heparin, start argatroban or bivalirudin.
- **TTP** → pentad: thrombocytopenia, MAHA, neurologic, renal, fever. ADAMTS13 deficiency → uncleaved vWF multimers. Treat with plasma exchange.
</HighYield>
