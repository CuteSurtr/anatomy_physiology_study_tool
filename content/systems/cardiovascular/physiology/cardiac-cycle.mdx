One full cardiac cycle is roughly **0.8 s at 75 bpm**: ~0.3 s in systole and ~0.5 s in diastole. The cycle is the same on both sides of the heart - only the pressures differ.

## Phases (left heart)

1. **Atrial systole** - atrial contraction tops off ventricular filling (the "atrial kick," ~20% of EDV).
2. **Isovolumetric contraction** - all four valves shut, ventricle squeezes against a closed system, pressure spikes.
3. **Ventricular ejection** - aortic valve opens once LV pressure > aortic pressure (~80 mmHg). Stroke volume leaves the LV.
4. **Isovolumetric relaxation** - aortic valve closes (dicrotic notch), ventricle relaxes with all valves shut.
5. **Rapid ventricular filling** - mitral valve opens; passive filling of LV from LA.
6. **Diastasis** - slow continued filling.

## Pressure–volume loop, in one paragraph

Start at the bottom-left of the loop: ventricle full of ~50 mL (end-systolic volume), pressure low. Filling (5–6) sweeps right along the bottom until **end-diastolic volume (~120 mL)** - that's **preload**. Isovolumetric contraction shoots straight up. Ejection sweeps left along the top until **end-systolic volume**. Isovolumetric relaxation drops straight down. Stroke volume = EDV − ESV (~70 mL). Width of the loop = stroke volume; area = stroke work.

<Note title="Vocabulary that gets confused">
- **Preload** ≈ EDV ≈ how stretched the ventricle is before contracting
- **Afterload** ≈ the pressure the ventricle must overcome to eject (aortic pressure)
- **Contractility** ≈ inotropy ≈ force at a given preload (independent of preload/afterload)
- **Ejection fraction** = SV / EDV. Normal ~55–70%.
</Note>

<Clinical>
**Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF)**: contractility ↓ → the P-V loop is shifted right (higher ESV, larger EDV) and narrower (smaller SV). EF drops below 40%. Treat by reducing afterload (ACE inhibitors), reducing preload (diuretics), and improving long-term remodeling (beta blockers, ARNI, SGLT2i).
</Clinical>

## Heart sounds & the Wiggers diagram

<Figure name="cardiovascular/wiggers-diagram" caption="Wiggers diagram - the canonical cardiac-cycle figure. Stacks aortic pressure, LV pressure, LA pressure, ventricular volume, ECG, and phonocardiogram against time. Read top-to-bottom along any vertical slice to see what's happening simultaneously." />

The classic Wiggers diagram stacks (top to bottom) aortic pressure, LV pressure, LA pressure, ventricular volume, the ECG, and the phonocardiogram. Key landmarks:

- **S1** ("lub") - closure of AV valves (mitral + tricuspid) at start of systole.
- **S2** ("dub") - closure of semilunar valves (aortic + pulmonary) at start of diastole. Physiologic splitting widens on inspiration (delayed P2).
- **S3** - early diastolic, rapid ventricular filling against a non-compliant ventricle. Normal in kids/athletes; pathologic in volume overload (HF, MR).
- **S4** - late diastolic, atrial contraction against a stiff ventricle (HTN, LVH, HCM). Never normal.

## ECG basics - the electrical view of the cycle

<Figure name="cardiovascular/ecg-sinus" caption="Normal sinus rhythm with labeled P wave (atrial depolarization), QRS complex (ventricular depolarization), T wave (ventricular repolarization), PR interval, ST segment, and QT interval." />

The ECG records the summed electrical activity of the heart at the body surface. Each wave maps to a mechanical event:

| ECG feature | Electrical event | Mechanical correlate |
|---|---|---|
| **P wave** | Atrial depolarization | Atrial systole begins |
| **PR interval** | Atrial depolarization + AV nodal delay | Time for atria to fill ventricles |
| **QRS complex** | Ventricular depolarization (masks atrial repolarization) | Onset of ventricular systole |
| **ST segment** | Ventricles depolarized; ejection phase | Ventricular ejection |
| **T wave** | Ventricular repolarization | End of systole, isovolumetric relaxation |
| **QT interval** | Total ventricular electrical activity | Prolonged → torsades risk |

## Cardiac output essentials

**CO = HR × SV** (~5 L/min at rest).
**MAP = CO × SVR** (≈ DBP + 1/3 pulse pressure).
**EF = SV / EDV** (normal ~55–70%).

Three knobs on SV: **preload** (Frank-Starling: ↑ EDV stretch → ↑ contraction force), **afterload** (↑ aortic pressure → ↓ SV), **contractility** (sympathetic, calcium handling).

## Every cardiac-cycle component - function reference

### Phases (one full cycle)

| Phase | Duration (at HR 75) | Valve status | Key event |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atrial systole | ~0.1 s | AV open, SL closed | Atrial kick - final 20% of ventricular filling |
| Isovolumetric contraction | ~0.05 s | All closed | LV pressure rises sharply (no volume change) |
| Ventricular ejection | ~0.25 s | AV closed, SL open | Stroke volume leaves; pressure peaks |
| Isovolumetric relaxation | ~0.08 s | All closed | LV pressure falls below aortic (aortic valve closes - dicrotic notch) |
| Rapid ventricular filling | ~0.11 s | AV open, SL closed | Passive filling from atria |
| Diastasis | ~0.2 s | AV open | Slow filling phase |

### Heart sounds - what each signifies

| Sound | Cause | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 ("lub")** | Mitral + tricuspid valve closure at start of systole | Loud in mitral stenosis (forceful closing of stiff valve); soft in MR |
| **S2 ("dub")** | Aortic + pulmonary valve closure at end of systole | Physiologic splitting widens on inspiration (delayed P2); paradoxical (widens on expiration) in LBBB |
| **S3** | Early diastolic rapid filling against compliant or volume-overloaded ventricle | Normal in youth/athletes; pathologic in HF, MR ("ventricular gallop") |
| **S4** | Late diastolic atrial contraction into a stiff ventricle | **Never normal**; HTN, LVH, HCM, ischemia ("atrial gallop") |
| **Opening snap** | Forceful opening of stenotic mitral valve | Early diastolic, classic for rheumatic mitral stenosis |
| **Ejection click** | Bicuspid aortic valve opening | Early systolic, fixed |
| **Friction rub** | Pericarditis | Triphasic, scratchy; varies with position |
| **Murmur (systolic)** | Stenosis of SL valve or regurgitation of AV valve | Crescendo-decrescendo (AS) vs holosystolic (MR) |
| **Murmur (diastolic)** | Regurgitation of SL valve or stenosis of AV valve | Usually pathologic |

### Hemodynamic parameters and their drivers

| Parameter | Determinants | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Cardiac output (CO)** | HR × SV | Total perfusion to body; falls in shock |
| **Stroke volume (SV)** | Preload × contractility ÷ afterload | The work done per beat |
| **Preload (EDV)** | Venous return, atrial kick, compliance | Frank-Starling: ↑ stretch → ↑ contraction |
| **Afterload** | Aortic pressure / SVR | The pressure the LV must overcome - ↑ afterload ↓ SV |
| **Contractility (inotropy)** | Sympathetic tone, Ca²⁺, drugs | Independent of preload/afterload; ↑ by β1 agonists, digoxin |
| **MAP** | DBP + 1/3 pulse pressure ≈ CO × SVR | Driving force for tissue perfusion |
| **Ejection fraction (EF)** | SV / EDV | Index of contractile function; HFrEF if &lt;40% |

### Pharmacology referenced

| Drug class | Action | Effect on cycle |
|---|---|---|
| **Beta blockers** | β1 antagonism | ↓ HR, ↓ contractility, ↓ O₂ demand; long-term remodeling benefit in HFrEF |
| **ACE inhibitors / ARBs** | Block RAAS | ↓ afterload + preload; remodeling benefit |
| **Loop diuretics** | NKCC2 block | ↓ preload (decongestion) |
| **Nitrates** | NO donors | Venodilate → ↓ preload; high doses arteriodilate → ↓ afterload |
| **Digoxin** | Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase inhibition → ↑ intracellular Ca²⁺ | ↑ contractility; vagal effect ↓ HR; narrow therapeutic window |
| **Calcium channel blockers (non-DHP)** | L-type Ca²⁺ block | ↓ HR, ↓ contractility |
| **SGLT2 inhibitors** | Glucosuria + natriuresis | Surprisingly large mortality benefit in HFrEF and HFpEF |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX hemodynamic priorities:**
- **Normal CO ~5 L/min**; **CI &lt;2.2 L/min/m²** = cardiogenic shock.
- **MAP &lt;65 mmHg** = inadequate organ perfusion - escalate.
- **Frank-Starling teaching**: ↑ preload → ↑ SV (up to a point). Why fluid challenge helps hypovolemic shock but not cardiogenic.
- **Post-MI patient with new murmur + acute pulmonary edema** → suspect papillary muscle rupture (acute MR) - surgical emergency.
- **S3 in adult** = volume overload (HF); **S4** = stiff ventricle (HTN, LVH).
- IV digoxin push: **slow over 5 min**, monitor for bradycardia; verify K⁺ before giving.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Pressure-volume loop pathologies:**
- **Aortic stenosis** → narrower loop with higher peak pressure; LV hypertrophy.
- **Aortic regurgitation** → wider loop (eccentric volume overload).
- **Mitral regurgitation** → loop opens earlier (no isovolumetric contraction phase); LV pumps backward into LA.
- **Mitral stenosis** → low EDV, low SV; LA dilates → AF + LA thrombus.
- **HFrEF** → ESV shifts right, narrow loop, ↓ SV.
- **HFpEF** → preserved EF but stiff LV; high filling pressure, normal SV.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Drugs by P-V loop effect:**
- **Preload reducers**: nitrates (venodilators), diuretics - shift loop **left**.
- **Afterload reducers**: ACE-I, ARBs, hydralazine, ARNI - narrow + shorten loop top.
- **Positive inotropes**: digoxin, dobutamine, milrinone, levosimendan - **taller** loop (↑ pressure generated), shifts ESPVR up.
- **Negative inotropes**: β blockers, non-DHP CCBs (verapamil, diltiazem) - opposite.
- **Pure vasodilators** (hydralazine + nitrates): mortality benefit in Black patients with HFrEF (BiDil trial).
</HighYield>
