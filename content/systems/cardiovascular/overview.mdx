The cardiovascular system is a closed loop of two pumps in series: the **right heart** drives the pulmonary circuit, the **left heart** drives the systemic circuit. Because the two pumps sit in one loop, whatever the right heart puts out must come back to the left heart - which is why their outputs have to match, beat for beat, over time.

## How the pages fit together

Everything in this system is one argument, built in order. Read it this way and each page supplies the vocabulary the next one assumes.

1. **[Heart](/cardiovascular/anatomy/heart)** - chambers, valves, great vessels, coronary supply. Establishes *where* blood goes and which structure fails when.
2. **[Conduction system](/cardiovascular/anatomy/conduction-system)** - the electrical trigger. Establishes *when* each chamber contracts, and gives you the ECG.
3. **[Cardiac cycle](/cardiovascular/physiology/cardiac-cycle)** - pressures, volumes, valve opening and closing, heart sounds. Turns anatomy plus timing into a mechanical cycle, and introduces **preload, afterload, contractility, stroke volume**.
4. **[Cardiac output regulation](/cardiovascular/physiology/cardiac-output-regulation)** - Frank-Starling, autonomic and hormonal control. Explains how stroke volume and rate are adjusted to demand.
5. **[Blood vessels & circulation](/cardiovascular/anatomy/vessels-and-circulation)** - the tubes the output flows into, and where resistance actually lives (the arterioles).
6. **[Hemodynamics & blood pressure](/cardiovascular/physiology/hemodynamics-and-bp)** - puts flow and resistance together as **BP = CO × TPR**, then the reflexes and hormones that defend it over seconds, minutes, and days.
7. **[Blood](/cardiovascular/anatomy/blood)** and **[blood gas & oxygen chemistry](/cardiovascular/physiology/blood-gas-and-oxygen-chemistry)** - what is being pumped, and how much oxygen it actually carries. The whole system exists to deliver oxygen: **DO₂ = CO × CaO₂**.
8. **[Hemostasis & coagulation](/cardiovascular/physiology/hemostasis-and-coagulation)** - how the loop seals itself when a vessel is breached, and what happens when it seals somewhere it shouldn't.

<Note title="The three numbers everything hangs off">
**CO = HR × SV**, **BP = CO × TPR**, and **DO₂ = CO × CaO₂**. Cardiac output appears in all three, which is why it is the variable the body defends hardest - and why shock can arise from a failure of any one term.
</Note>
