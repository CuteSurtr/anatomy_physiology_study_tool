The cardiac conduction system is a network of **specialized cardiomyocytes** (not nerves) that generate and propagate the electrical signal that triggers each heartbeat. Five components - SA node → AV node → bundle of His → bundle branches → Purkinje fibers - and a single rhythm command-line: **"go from the top, pause at AV, sweep the ventricles bottom-up."**

## The five components

<Figure name="cardiovascular/conduction-system" caption="The five components of the cardiac conduction system - SA node (pacemaker), atrial internodal pathways, AV node, bundle of His, right + left bundle branches, Purkinje fibers." />

| Component | Location | Intrinsic rate | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SA node** (sinoatrial) | Roof of right atrium, near SVC opening | **60–100 bpm** - dominant pacemaker | Spontaneously depolarizes (funny current I_f) → sets heart rate |
| **AV node** (atrioventricular) | Floor of right atrium, near coronary sinus + tricuspid annulus | 40–60 bpm | **Slows** conduction (~0.1 s delay) - allows atrial systole to complete before ventricles contract |
| **Bundle of His** | Penetrates fibrous skeleton into interventricular septum | 30–40 bpm | **Only** electrical bridge between atria and ventricles |
| **Bundle branches** (R + L) | Either side of interventricular septum | 30–40 bpm | Carry signal to apex; left further divides into anterior + posterior fascicles |
| **Purkinje fibers** | Subendocardial network of both ventricles | 20–40 bpm (last resort) | Rapid spread to ventricular myocardium - fastest conduction (4 m/s) |

<Mnemonic>
**"100, 75, 50, 25"** - approximate intrinsic firing rates of SA → AV → His → Purkinje. The fastest pacemaker dominates (**overdrive suppression**); if SA fails, AV takes over, and so on down the chain.
</Mnemonic>

## How a single beat propagates - step by step

1. **SA node fires** (autorhythmic) → wave spreads across both atria via internodal pathways + Bachmann's bundle (interatrial). → **P wave** on ECG.
2. **Atrial depolarization → atrial contraction** ("atrial kick" - adds final 20% to ventricular filling).
3. Wave reaches **AV node** → conduction slows (decremental conduction; ~100 ms). → **PR segment** on ECG.
4. **Bundle of His → R and L bundle branches** → ventricular septum depolarizes left-to-right first (Q wave).
5. **Purkinje fibers** sweep ventricles from **apex to base, endocardium to epicardium**. → **QRS complex**.
6. **Ventricular contraction** - ejection.
7. **Ventricular repolarization** (T wave) - epicardium repolarizes before endocardium (reason T is usually upright in same leads as QRS).

## Why the AV nodal delay?

<Figure name="cardiovascular/conduction-system" caption="" />


The 100 ms hesitation at the AV node is critical:
- **Atria empty into ventricles** before ventricles contract → optimal preload.
- **Filters atrial tachyarrhythmias**: in a-fib (350+ atrial impulses/min), the AV node blocks most of them. Ventricular rate stays survivable (~100–160).
- **Refractory period** > duration of impulse → can't propagate retrograde.

## Pacemaker action potential - different from contractile myocytes

<Figure name="cardiovascular/cardiac-action-potential" caption="Cardiac AP comparison. Ventricular AP has 5 phases with a plateau (Ca²⁺); pacemaker AP has phase 4 funny-current depolarization driving spontaneous firing." />

| Feature | Pacemaker (SA/AV) | Contractile myocyte |
|---|---|---|
| **Resting potential** | Unstable - drifts up (**funny current I_f** + slow Ca²⁺ leak) | Stable at –90 mV |
| **Upstroke (phase 0)** | **Ca²⁺** influx (slow, L-type) | **Na⁺** influx (fast) |
| **Plateau** | None | Phase 2 - Ca²⁺ in / K⁺ out balance |
| **Repolarization** | K⁺ efflux | K⁺ efflux |
| **Conduction speed** | Slow (0.05 m/s at AV) | Fast (Purkinje 4 m/s) |
| **Refractory period** | Long, decremental | Long (prevents tetany) |

The **funny current (I_f)** is a mixed Na⁺/K⁺ inward current activated at hyperpolarized voltages - paradoxically opens when the cell is "supposed" to rest. This is what makes pacemaker cells autorhythmic. **Ivabradine** blocks I_f → slows SA node → treats stable angina + heart failure without dropping contractility.

## Autonomic control of the heart

| Branch | Receptor | Effect on SA/AV | Effect on contractility |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sympathetic** (cardiac plexus, T1–T4) | β1 → Gs → ↑cAMP | ↑ rate (chronotropy +), ↑ AV conduction (dromotropy +) | ↑ force (inotropy +), ↑ relaxation (lusitropy +) |
| **Parasympathetic** (vagus, CN X) | M2 → Gi → ↓cAMP, ↑ K⁺ efflux | ↓ rate, ↓ AV conduction (right vagus mostly hits SA; left vagus mostly hits AV) | Minimal direct effect on ventricles |

At rest, **parasympathetic tone dominates** - a denervated transplanted heart runs at ~100 bpm (intrinsic SA rate) instead of 70.

## ECG correlates - anatomy to waveform

<Figure name="cardiovascular/cardiac-cycle-ecg" caption="Cardiac cycle ECG correlates. P = atrial depolarization (SA + atrial myocardium); QRS = ventricular depolarization (AV → His → bundles → Purkinje); T = ventricular repolarization." />

| ECG feature | What it represents | Normal duration |
|---|---|---|
| **P wave** | Atrial depolarization | &lt;0.12 s |
| **PR interval** | Atrial depo + AV nodal delay | 0.12–0.20 s |
| **QRS complex** | Ventricular depolarization | &lt;0.12 s |
| **ST segment** | Ventricles fully depolarized (plateau) | Isoelectric |
| **T wave** | Ventricular repolarization | - |
| **QT interval** | Total ventricular depolarization + repolarization | &lt;0.44 s (corrected for HR) |

The **PR interval > 0.20 s = 1st-degree AV block**. **QRS > 0.12 s = bundle branch block**. **QT > 0.5 s = high risk of torsades**.

## Blood supply to the conduction system - board-critical

<Figure name="cardiovascular/coronary-arteries-labeled" caption="Blood supply to conduction tissue. RCA supplies SA node (60%) and AV node (90%) in right-dominant circulation → inferior MI can disrupt conduction. LAD perforators supply bundle branches." />

| Structure | Artery (in 90% right-dominant hearts) |
|---|---|
| **SA node** | Right coronary artery → SA nodal branch (60%) or left circumflex (40%) |
| **AV node** | Right coronary artery → AV nodal branch (90%) - "right-dominant" |
| **Bundle of His** | LAD + posterior descending artery |
| **Right bundle branch** | LAD septal branches |
| **Left bundle branch** (anterior fascicle) | LAD septal branches |
| **Left bundle branch** (posterior fascicle) | Dual supply - LAD + RCA → most resistant to ischemia |

<Clinical>
**Inferior MI** (RCA occlusion) frequently causes **AV node ischemia → 2nd or 3rd degree heart block**. Usually transient (AV node has good collateral supply). Often responds to atropine.

**Anterior MI** (LAD occlusion) causes **bundle branch blocks** (His-Purkinje ischemia). New bifascicular block in the setting of anterior MI is an indication for **pacing** - high risk of complete heart block with no escape rhythm.

**Sick sinus syndrome** - SA node fibrosis (often age-related) → bradycardia + pauses + alternating brady-tachy. Treatment: **pacemaker**.
</Clinical>

## Function reference - every node and conduction structure

| Component | Cellular feature | Function | Clinical pearl |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SA node cells** | Express HCN4 channels (I_f); small, pale cytoplasm | Set heart rate; respond to β1/M2 | Ivabradine selectively blocks HCN4 → slows HR without lowering contractility |
| **Internodal tracts** | Bachmann (interatrial), Wenckebach (anterior), Thorel (posterior) | Carry depolarization from SA to AV + interatrial spread | Selective Bachmann's bundle pacing improves atrial sync |
| **AV node** | Slow Ca²⁺-mediated AP; decremental conduction; long refractory period | Critical gatekeeper - slows + filters atrial impulses | Target of AV-nodal blockers (β-blockers, non-DHP CCBs, digoxin, adenosine) in AVNRT/a-fib rate control |
| **Bundle of His** | Penetrates fibrous skeleton (the **only** atrioventricular electrical connection in a normal heart) | Single point of failure between atria + ventricles | Bypass via accessory pathway (Bundle of Kent) = **WPW syndrome** → delta wave |
| **Bundle branches** | Insulated fibers running on either side of IV septum | Rapid ventricular activation | LBBB widens QRS, hides ST changes; RBBB has classic RSR' in V1 |
| **Purkinje fibers** | Large pale cells with abundant glycogen; fastest conduction | Synchronous ventricular contraction; latent escape pacemaker (~30 bpm) | If both SA and AV fail, Purkinje escape rhythm keeps you alive - but slow + unreliable |
| **Fibrous skeleton** | Dense connective tissue around all four valves | **Electrical insulator** between atria and ventricles + anchor for valves + muscle origin | Without it, atrial impulses would directly excite the ventricles - no AV delay |

## Why this matters

<Figure name="cardiovascular/ecg-sinus" caption="" />


<HighYield variant="boards">
- **Adenosine** = transient AV nodal block (5–10 s asystole). Used to break AVNRT and to diagnose a-flutter (slows ventricular rate so the sawtooth pattern becomes visible).
- **Atropine** (M2 antagonist) → ↑ SA + AV conduction. First-line for symptomatic bradycardia.
- **β1-blockers, non-dihydropyridine CCBs (verapamil, diltiazem), digoxin** = AV-nodal blockers. Used for **rate control** in atrial fibrillation.
- **DON'T combine** β-blocker + non-DHP CCB IV - synergistic AV block → bradycardia/asystole.
- **WPW** - accessory bypass tract (Bundle of Kent) lets impulses skip the AV node → pre-excitation (delta wave). Avoid AV-nodal blockers in WPW + a-fib (atrial impulses race down the accessory pathway → V-fib).
- **3rd-degree (complete) AV block** = atria + ventricles independent. P waves march through. QRS narrow if escape pacemaker is junctional (~40 bpm); wide if ventricular (~30 bpm).
</HighYield>
