The heart is a four-chambered muscular pump sitting in the **middle mediastinum**, enclosed in the pericardium, oriented obliquely with its apex pointing inferiorly, anteriorly, and to the left. Roughly two-thirds of its mass lies left of the midline. The adult heart weighs **250–350 g in men and 200–300 g in women**, is approximately the size of a closed fist, and pumps roughly **7,500 L of blood per day** at rest.

## Position, surface anatomy, and relations

<Figure name="cardiovascular/heart-position" caption="Heart position in the thorax - in the middle mediastinum, apex pointing toward the left 5th intercostal space at the midclavicular line. About 2/3 of the heart lies left of midline. Enclosed by the pericardium between the two pleural cavities. (OpenStax)" />

The heart occupies the middle mediastinum between the two pleural cavities. Its boundaries:

- **Base** - directed posterosuperiorly toward the right shoulder; formed mainly by the left atrium plus a small contribution from the right atrium. Lies at the level of the third costal cartilage to T6–T9 vertebrae.
- **Apex** - directed anteroinferiorly toward the left, normally palpable as the **apex beat (point of maximal impulse, PMI)** in the **left fifth intercostal space, mid-clavicular line**. Displacement of the PMI suggests cardiomegaly or mediastinal shift.
- **Anterior (sternocostal) surface** - formed mainly by the **right ventricle**.
- **Inferior (diaphragmatic) surface** - formed mainly by the **left ventricle** + a strip of right ventricle. Rests on the central tendon of the diaphragm.
- **Right pulmonary surface** - formed by the right atrium.
- **Left pulmonary surface** - formed by the left ventricle; carves the **cardiac notch** in the left lung.

Auscultation areas (where each valve's sound is best heard) are **not over the valve itself** but downstream along the blood flow:

| Area | Location | Valve |
|---|---|---|
| Aortic | Right 2nd intercostal space, sternal border | Aortic |
| Pulmonary | Left 2nd intercostal space, sternal border | Pulmonary |
| Tricuspid | Left lower sternal border (4th–5th ICS) | Tricuspid |
| Mitral | Left 5th ICS, mid-clavicular line (= PMI) | Mitral |

<Mnemonic>
**"APT M"** for auscultation order, right→left, top→bottom: **A**ortic (R2), **P**ulmonary (L2), **T**ricuspid (L4 sternal), **M**itral (L5 MCL). Also **"All Physicians Take Money"**.
</Mnemonic>

## Embryologic origins (high-yield)

The heart begins as a **primitive heart tube** by week 3, derived from **splanchnic mesoderm** of the cardiogenic field. The tube folds and partitions into chambers between weeks 4–8. Definitive structures map back to embryologic precursors:

| Embryologic origin | Adult derivative |
|---|---|
| Truncus arteriosus | Aorta + pulmonary trunk |
| Bulbus cordis | Smooth parts of RV (conus arteriosus) + LV (aortic vestibule) |
| Primitive ventricle | Trabeculated parts of L and R ventricles |
| Primitive atrium | Trabeculated parts of L and R atria |
| Right horn of sinus venosus | Smooth part of RA (sinus venarum) |
| Left horn of sinus venosus | Coronary sinus |
| Common cardinal veins | SVC |

The **foramen ovale** allows right-to-left shunting in utero (bypassing fetal lungs); it closes functionally at birth and anatomically over the first year, becoming the **fossa ovalis** on the interatrial septum. A patent foramen ovale (PFO) is present in ~25% of adults.

## Fetal circulation - three shunts that close at birth

The fetus oxygenates blood in the **placenta**, not the lungs, so fetal circulation routes around the pulmonary circuit using **three shunts**. All three close after birth.

| Fetal structure | Connects | Adult remnant | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Umbilical vein** | Placenta → fetal liver | **Ligamentum teres** (in the falciform ligament) | Carries **oxygenated** blood from placenta to fetus |
| **Ductus venosus** | Umbilical vein → IVC (bypasses liver sinusoids) | **Ligamentum venosum** | Lets ~50% of placental blood skip the liver |
| **Foramen ovale** | Right atrium → left atrium (interatrial septum) | **Fossa ovalis** | Routes oxygenated IVC blood directly to LA (bypassing pulmonary circuit) |
| **Ductus arteriosus** | Pulmonary trunk → aortic arch (distal to left subclavian) | **Ligamentum arteriosum** | Routes RV output past the lungs into the systemic circulation |
| **Umbilical arteries (×2)** | Internal iliac arteries → placenta | **Medial umbilical ligaments** | Carries **deoxygenated** blood + waste from fetus to placenta |

### Changes at birth

1. **First breath** → lungs expand → ↓ pulmonary vascular resistance → blood flows through lungs for the first time.
2. **Cord clamping** → ↓ systemic venous return from placenta → ↓ RA pressure; concurrent ↑ pulmonary venous return → ↑ LA pressure. **LA > RA pressure** **functionally closes the foramen ovale** (the flap-valve septum primum is pressed against septum secundum).
3. **↑ Arterial PO₂** + **↓ prostaglandins** (placenta was the source) → **ductus arteriosus closes** within hours to days (functional) → anatomic closure in 2–3 weeks.
4. **Ductus venosus** closes passively as umbilical flow stops.

<Clinical>
**Patent ductus arteriosus (PDA)**: failure of DA to close after birth. Continuous "machinery" murmur best heard at left infraclavicular area. Left-to-right shunt (aorta → pulmonary trunk). Common in preemies + congenital rubella + high-altitude births.

- **Close it medically**: **indomethacin or ibuprofen** (NSAIDs block prostaglandin synthesis → closure). Use in symptomatic preemies.
- **Keep it open** (when needed for cyanotic heart disease where the systemic or pulmonary circuit depends on shunting): **alprostadil (PGE1)** infusion until surgical correction.

**Patent foramen ovale (PFO)**: present in ~25% of adults; usually asymptomatic. Risk of paradoxical embolism (DVT clot → R → L → systemic stroke). Closure considered after cryptogenic stroke in young patients.

**Coarctation of aorta**:
- **Preductal (infantile)**: narrowing proximal to DA. Symptoms in infancy if DA closes; PGE1 keeps it open temporarily. Associated with Turner syndrome.
- **Postductal (adult)**: narrowing distal to DA. Upper-extremity HTN + lower-extremity hypoperfusion + delayed femoral pulses + rib notching on CXR.

**Maternal NSAID use** in 3rd trimester → premature DA closure → fetal heart failure. Avoid NSAIDs after 30 weeks gestation.
</Clinical>

## Chambers in detail

### Right atrium (RA)

Receives deoxygenated blood from three sources:
- **Superior vena cava** (head, neck, upper limbs)
- **Inferior vena cava** (lower body); has a rudimentary **valve of the IVC** that directed blood toward the foramen ovale in the fetus
- **Coronary sinus** (cardiac veins); has the **Thebesian valve**

Internal surface has two regions divided by the **crista terminalis**: a posterior smooth-walled **sinus venarum** (sinus venosus derivative) and an anterior trabeculated portion bearing **pectinate muscles** that extend into the **right auricle**. The interatrial septum bears the **fossa ovalis**.

### Right ventricle (RV)

Triangular when viewed anteriorly. Internal surface is heavily trabeculated (**trabeculae carneae**) with three named papillary muscles (anterior, posterior, septal) anchoring the **chordae tendineae** to the tricuspid leaflets. The **moderator band** (septomarginal trabecula) carries the right bundle branch to the anterior papillary muscle. The smooth-walled superior outflow tract (**conus arteriosus / infundibulum**) leads to the pulmonary valve.

Wall thickness: **3–5 mm** (thin because the pulmonary circuit only needs ~15–25 mmHg systolic pressure).

### Left atrium (LA)

Receives **four pulmonary veins** (two on each side) without intervening valves. Smooth-walled mostly, with pectinate muscles only in the **left auricle**. The auricle is a common site of **thrombus formation in atrial fibrillation** - stasis here is the source of cardioembolic strokes.

### Left ventricle (LV)

Conical, with a circular cross-section. The single most clinically important chamber. Walls **8–12 mm thick** (3–4× the RV) - required to generate the ~120 mmHg peak pressure of systemic systole. Two papillary muscles (anterior, posterior) anchor chordae to the **two mitral leaflets** (anterior is larger, posterior is wider but shorter). The smooth **aortic vestibule** below the aortic valve has fibrous (not muscular) walls.

## The valves (and why they matter)

Two **AV valves** (between atria and ventricles) and two **semilunar valves** (between ventricles and great arteries). Each valve has a fibrous attachment ring - collectively the **cardiac skeleton** - that electrically insulates atria from ventricles and provides anchor points for valve cusps, atrial myocardium, and ventricular myocardium.

| Valve | Between | Cusps | Closure sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tricuspid | RA → RV | 3 (anterior, posterior, septal) | S1 |
| Mitral (bicuspid) | LA → LV | 2 (anterior, posterior) | S1 |
| Pulmonary | RV → pulmonary trunk | 3 (anterior, left, right) | S2 |
| Aortic | LV → aorta | 3 (right coronary, left coronary, non-coronary) | S2 |

### AV valve apparatus

The AV valves are not standalone leaflets - they're a **functional unit** of leaflets + chordae tendineae + papillary muscles + the annulus. Papillary muscles contract slightly *before* the rest of the ventricle, tensioning the chordae so the leaflets don't evert into the atrium. Failure of any part causes regurgitation:

- **Leaflet failure** - endocarditis vegetations, myxomatous degeneration (floppy mitral valve)
- **Chord rupture** - acute post-MI mitral regurgitation
- **Papillary muscle dysfunction/rupture** - inferior MI (posteromedial papillary muscle is supplied by the PDA only)
- **Annular dilation** - ventricular dilation stretches the ring (functional MR in HF)

### Semilunar valves

Three pocket-like cusps that fill with blood as it tries to backflow, closing the valve passively. The **sinuses of Valsalva** behind the aortic cusps prevent the cusps from sticking to the aortic wall when open; the **right and left coronary arteries** originate from the right and left sinuses respectively. The non-coronary (posterior) sinus has no artery.

<Clinical>
**Mitral regurgitation** lets blood backflow LA ← LV during systole. The murmur is **holosystolic at the apex, radiating to the axilla**. Over time, LA dilates → atrial fibrillation risk; LV dilates trying to compensate → eventual heart failure.
</Clinical>

## Wall layers - gross and microscopic

<Figure name="cardiovascular/heart-wall-layers" caption="Heart wall layers (OpenStax). Outer to inner: fibrous pericardium, parietal serous pericardium, pericardial cavity (with serous fluid), visceral serous pericardium = epicardium, myocardium (contracting cardiac muscle), endocardium." />

<Figure name="cardiovascular/heart-internal-anatomy" caption="Internal anatomy of the heart (OpenStax). Cutaway showing all four chambers, AV valves (mitral + tricuspid) with chordae tendineae anchored to papillary muscles, semilunar valves (aortic + pulmonary), and great vessels." />

From outside in:

1. **Fibrous pericardium** - tough outer sac, attached inferiorly to the central tendon of the diaphragm, anteriorly to the sternum (sternopericardial ligaments), and superiorly fused to the adventitia of the great vessels. Innervated by the **phrenic nerves** (C3–C5) - referred pain to the shoulder.
2. **Parietal pericardium** - serous lining of the fibrous pericardium.
3. **Pericardial cavity** - potential space with ~15–50 mL of serous fluid for lubrication.
4. **Visceral pericardium (epicardium)** - serous layer on the heart surface; contains coronary vessels, autonomic nerves, and variable amounts of fat.
5. **Myocardium** - the muscular middle layer doing the work. Composed of branched, striated, mononucleated cardiac myocytes connected at **intercalated discs** (gap junctions + desmosomes). Thickest in the LV.
6. **Endocardium** - single-layered endothelium lining all chambers and valves, continuous with vascular endothelium.

### Microscopic anatomy (histology)

- **Cardiomyocytes** are ~50–100 µm long, mostly mononucleated, with central nuclei (vs. peripheral in skeletal muscle). Show striations like skeletal muscle (same sarcomere architecture) but with **branching** and **intercalated discs**. T-tubules are larger and at the Z-line (not the A-I junction as in skeletal). One T-tubule per sarcomere ("dyads"), not two ("triads").
- **Purkinje fibers** are modified cardiomyocytes - larger, pale-staining, with sparse myofibrils and abundant glycogen.
- **Atrial myocytes** secrete **ANP** when stretched - appears as electron-dense granules near the Golgi.

## Pericardium pathology in one paragraph

The pericardium can become inflamed (**pericarditis** - chest pain worse on inspiration, relieved by leaning forward, often a friction rub on auscultation; ECG shows diffuse ST elevation + PR depression), accumulate excess fluid (**pericardial effusion** - silent if slow, tamponade if fast), become scarred (**constrictive pericarditis** - Kussmaul sign, pericardial knock, "square root sign" on cardiac catheterization), or harbor air (**pneumopericardium** - rare). Cardiac tamponade is the medical emergency - Beck's triad of hypotension, JVD, muffled heart sounds, with **pulsus paradoxus** (inspiratory drop in SBP > 10 mmHg).

## Cardiac conduction system

The heart's electrical signal originates intrinsically - even denervated transplanted hearts beat. Pathway:

**SA node** (right atrium, near SVC) → atrial conduction → **AV node** (interatrial septum, slows conduction ~0.1 s for atrial kick) → **Bundle of His** → **right and left bundle branches** → **Purkinje fibers** → ventricular myocardium (bottom-up activation produces the QRS).

Intrinsic rates: SA ~60–100/min, AV ~40–60/min, ventricular escape ~20–40/min. The fastest pacemaker wins.

## Coronary circulation - detailed

<Figure name="cardiovascular/human-heart-coronary" caption="Human heart with coronary arteries traced on the surface - epicardial vessels (RCA, LAD, LCx, marginal) running in atrioventricular and interventricular grooves." />

<Figure name="cardiovascular/coronary-arteries-labeled" caption="Coronary arteries labeled. RCA → marginal + posterior descending; left main → LAD + LCx → diagonals + obtuse marginals. The first branches off the aorta supply the myocardium itself." />

<Figure name="cardiovascular/coronary-arteries" caption="Coronary arteries with major branches labeled - anterior view of the heart. Note LAD in the anterior interventricular groove, LCX in the left AV groove, RCA in the right AV groove, and the PDA descending posteriorly." />

The first branches off the aorta (from the aortic sinuses, just above the aortic valve) are the **right coronary artery (RCA)** and the **left main coronary artery**. Coronary blood flow occurs predominantly in **diastole** because the contracted myocardium compresses intramural vessels during systole - this is why diastolic blood pressure is the key determinant of coronary perfusion, and why tachycardia (shortened diastole) precipitates ischemia in patients with coronary disease.

### Left coronary system

The **left main** is short (1–2 cm) and bifurcates into:

- **Left anterior descending (LAD)** - runs in the anterior interventricular groove to the apex; gives off **diagonal branches** to the anterior LV and **septal perforators** to the anterior interventricular septum.
- **Left circumflex (LCX)** - runs in the left atrioventricular groove; gives off **obtuse marginal branches** to the lateral LV wall.

### Right coronary system

The **RCA** runs in the right AV groove. Branches:

- **Sinoatrial nodal artery** (60% of people) - supplies the SA node
- **Right marginal branches** - supply the RV
- **Posterior descending artery (PDA)** - in 85% of people, this comes from the RCA (= **right-dominant** circulation). In 10% it comes from the LCX (left-dominant). In 5% it has a dual blood supply (codominant).
- **AV nodal artery** - typically arises from the RCA at the **crux of the heart** (junction of AV and interventricular grooves)

### Coronary infarct patterns

| Vessel occluded | Region infarcted | ECG leads | Clinical pearls |
|---|---|---|---|
| LAD | Anterior LV, anterior 2/3 of IV septum, apex | V1–V4 ("anterior MI") | Largest territory; "widow-maker" if proximal LAD |
| RCA | RV, inferior LV, posteromedial papillary muscle, SA + AV nodes (most) | II, III, aVF ("inferior MI") | Bradyarrhythmias, AV block, acute MR if papillary rupture; preload-sensitive (avoid nitrates if RV involved) |
| LCX | Lateral LV | I, aVL, V5–V6 ("lateral MI") | Less common in isolation |
| PDA | Posterior LV, posterior septum | ST depression V1–V3 (reciprocal) or posterior leads | Often part of an inferior MI |

### Venous drainage

Most cardiac venous blood returns via the **coronary sinus** in the posterior AV groove, draining into the **right atrium**. Tributaries:
- **Great cardiac vein** (with LAD)
- **Middle cardiac vein** (with PDA)
- **Small cardiac vein** (with right marginal)
- **Posterior vein of the LV**

A minority of blood drains via **anterior cardiac veins** directly into the RA, and via tiny **Thebesian veins** directly into all four chambers (a small physiologic right-to-left shunt).

## Innervation

The heart receives autonomic innervation only - there is no somatic motor supply.

- **Sympathetic** (T1–T5 spinal levels) → **cardiac plexus** → SA node, AV node, atrial + ventricular myocardium → **↑ rate, ↑ contractility, ↑ conduction velocity**. Sympathetic chain ganglia → cardiac nerves → cardiac plexus.
- **Parasympathetic** (CN X, vagus nerve) → cardiac plexus → mainly **SA node and AV node** → **↓ rate, ↓ AV conduction**. Vagal effect on ventricular myocardium is small.
- **Sensory** afferents travel back with sympathetic fibers; ischemic pain is referred to T1–T4 dermatomes (chest, left arm, jaw, neck).

The **cardiac plexus** sits at the base of the heart around the aortic arch and tracheal bifurcation. It receives sympathetic input from upper thoracic ganglia and parasympathetic from the vagi; postganglionic fibers run with the coronary arteries.

## Pericardium & layers

From outside in: **fibrous pericardium → parietal pericardium → pericardial cavity (serous fluid, 15–50 mL) → visceral pericardium (epicardium) → myocardium → endocardium**. Sudden 100 mL accumulation can cause **cardiac tamponade** (Beck's triad: hypotension, JVD, muffled heart sounds).

### Chambers and atrial features

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Right atrium** | Receives deoxygenated systemic venous blood (SVC, IVC, coronary sinus); pumps it ~5 mmHg into RV | First receiving chamber - its compliance buffers venous return; failure (RV failure backward) causes JVD, hepatomegaly, peripheral edema |
| **Right auricle** | Trabeculated outpouching of RA; increases atrial capacity | Site of pacing leads, occasional thrombi |
| **Crista terminalis** | Vertical muscular ridge separating smooth (sinus venarum) from trabeculated (pectinate) RA | Embryologic boundary between sinus venosus and primitive atrium; landmark for SA node location |
| **Sinus venarum** | Smooth posterior wall of RA receiving SVC and IVC | Low-resistance reservoir; from embryologic right horn of sinus venosus |
| **Pectinate muscles (RA)** | Trabeculated ridges in anterior RA and auricle | Increase contractility surface area without bulk |
| **Fossa ovalis** | Depression on interatrial septum - remnant of fetal foramen ovale | If patent (PFO ~25% of adults) → paradoxical embolism risk; closure target in cryptogenic stroke |
| **Valve of the IVC (Eustachian valve)** | Vestigial; in fetus directed IVC blood toward foramen ovale | Usually rudimentary in adults; sometimes prominent enough to be confused with a thrombus on echo |
| **Thebesian valve** | Guards opening of coronary sinus into RA | Prevents backflow of atrial blood into coronary sinus during atrial contraction |
| **Right ventricle** | Pumps blood to pulmonary circuit at ~25 mmHg systolic | Low-pressure pump - thin wall (3–5 mm) appropriate; doesn't tolerate sudden afterload increases (acute PE → RV failure) |
| **Conus arteriosus (infundibulum)** | Smooth-walled outflow tract of RV leading to pulmonary valve | Streamlines flow into pulmonary trunk; embryologically from bulbus cordis |
| **Trabeculae carneae (RV/LV)** | Ridges and bridges of muscle on inner ventricular wall | Increase contractility per gram; reduce wall stress per LaPlace |
| **Anterior, posterior, septal papillary muscles (RV)** | Anchor chordae tendineae of tricuspid valve | Tense chordae just before ventricular contraction → prevent leaflet eversion |
| **Moderator band (septomarginal trabecula)** | Carries the right bundle branch to anterior papillary muscle | Fast electrical bridge; prevents over-distension of RV |
| **Left atrium** | Receives oxygenated blood from 4 pulmonary veins; pumps into LV | Acts as filling reservoir; dilates first in mitral disease → atrial fibrillation |
| **Left auricle** | Trabeculated outpouching of LA | **#1 site of thrombus in atrial fibrillation** - source of cardioembolic strokes; closure devices (Watchman) target it |
| **Interatrial septum** | Wall separating RA and LA | Carries fossa ovalis; ASD here is a high-yield cardiac defect |
| **Left ventricle** | Pumps blood to systemic circuit at ~120 mmHg systolic | The dominant pump - its failure dictates "heart failure" prognosis; hypertrophies first in HTN, fails first in MI |
| **Aortic vestibule** | Smooth fibrous LV outflow below aortic valve | From bulbus cordis; site of subaortic stenosis |
| **Anterior and posterior papillary muscles (LV)** | Anchor mitral chordae | Posteromedial papillary muscle has single blood supply (PDA) → ruptures in inferior MI |
| **Interventricular septum** | Muscular and (small) membranous wall between ventricles | Membranous part: most common VSD location; muscular part: thicker, contracts with LV |

### Valves and their apparatus

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Tricuspid valve** | One-way RA → RV during diastole | Prevents reflux during RV systole; in IV drug users, the most common site of right-sided endocarditis |
| **Mitral (bicuspid) valve** | One-way LA → LV during diastole | Prevents reflux into low-pressure LA during LV systole (where SBP = 120 mmHg) - large pressure differential makes leaks dramatic |
| **Aortic valve** | One-way LV → aorta during systole | Closes to prevent backflow during diastole - diastolic coronary perfusion depends on this |
| **Pulmonary valve** | One-way RV → pulmonary trunk during systole | Closes during diastole; closure produces P2 of split S2 |
| **Chordae tendineae** | Tether AV valve leaflets to papillary muscles | Hold leaflets shut during ventricular contraction; rupture → acute MR |
| **Cardiac skeleton (fibrous annuli)** | Connective-tissue ring around each valve | (1) Electrical insulator between atria and ventricles - funnels conduction through AV node only; (2) Anchor for myocardial fibers; (3) Maintains valve geometry |
| **Sinuses of Valsalva** | Aortic root pockets behind each cusp | Prevent cusps from occluding coronary ostia; harbor RCA (right sinus), LCA (left sinus) |

### Great vessels

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Superior vena cava** | Returns blood from head, neck, upper limbs to RA | Obstruction (often by lung cancer) → SVC syndrome: facial swelling, JVD, headaches |
| **Inferior vena cava** | Returns blood from below diaphragm to RA | Largest vein; receives renal, hepatic, lumbar tributaries; passes through diaphragm at T8 |
| **Pulmonary trunk** | Carries deoxygenated blood from RV to lungs | Only artery in adult body carrying deoxygenated blood (mirrored by pulmonary veins) |
| **Pulmonary veins (4)** | Carry oxygenated blood from lungs to LA | Only veins carrying oxygenated blood; isolation procedure target in AF ablation |
| **Aorta (arch)** | Distributes oxygenated blood to systemic circulation | First branches: right + left coronary; arch branches: brachiocephalic, left common carotid, left subclavian (in 70%); coarctation site is just distal to left subclavian |

### Conduction system

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **SA node** | Primary pacemaker (60–100/min); spontaneously depolarizes via funny (If) current | Sets normal heart rate; sympathetic ↑ and parasympathetic ↓ it; sick sinus syndrome → bradycardia |
| **Atrial internodal tracts** | Conduct SA impulse across atria to AV node | Anterior, middle (Bachmann), and posterior pathways |
| **AV node** | Delays impulse ~0.1 s before passing to ventricles | The delay lets atria empty into ventricles ("atrial kick"); intrinsic rate 40–60/min - backup pacemaker |
| **Bundle of His** | Only electrical pathway through the fibrous cardiac skeleton | Without it, atria and ventricles would be electrically dissociated (complete heart block) |
| **Right and left bundle branches** | Distribute impulse down the interventricular septum | Block of one (RBBB, LBBB) creates characteristic wide-QRS ECG patterns |
| **Purkinje fibers** | Rapidly distribute the impulse through ventricular myocardium | Conduction velocity ~4 m/s - fastest in the heart; ensures near-simultaneous ventricular contraction (efficient ejection); intrinsic rate 20–40/min - last-ditch ventricular escape |
| **Moderator band** | (Listed above under RV) carries the right bundle to anterior papillary muscle | Coordinates papillary tension with RV contraction |

### Wall layers and supporting tissue

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Fibrous pericardium** | Tough outer sac anchoring heart to diaphragm + sternum | Prevents over-distension; phrenic innervation explains shoulder-tip referred pain |
| **Parietal pericardium (serous)** | Inner lining of fibrous pericardium | Continuous with visceral layer at great-vessel reflections |
| **Pericardial cavity** | Fluid-filled potential space (~15–50 mL) | Lubrication; rapid filling here causes tamponade |
| **Visceral pericardium (epicardium)** | Outer heart surface; contains coronary vessels and fat | Coronary arteries run within it before perforating into myocardium |
| **Myocardium** | Cardiac muscle layer - the work | Thickness scales with chamber pressure: LV >> RV >> atria |
| **Endocardium** | Endothelial lining of all chambers and valves | Continuous with vascular endothelium; site of endocarditis vegetations |
| **Intercalated discs** | Cell–cell junctions of cardiomyocytes: desmosomes + gap junctions + adherens junctions | Mechanically couple cells (desmosomes) and electrically couple them (gap junctions via connexin-43) - makes the heart a functional syncytium |
| **T-tubules** | Sarcolemmal invaginations carrying APs deep into the cell | One per sarcomere at the Z-line in cardiac muscle; enable rapid Ca²⁺-induced Ca²⁺ release |
| **Cardiomyocytes** | Branched, mononucleated, striated muscle cells | Auto-rhythmic in pacemaker tissue; depend on extracellular Ca²⁺ for contraction (unlike skeletal) |

### Coronary circulation

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Right coronary artery (RCA)** | Supplies RV, inferior LV, SA + AV nodes (most people) | Occlusion → inferior MI; can cause AV block + bradycardia |
| **Left main coronary** | Trunk dividing into LAD + LCX | Occlusion is "the widow-maker" - catastrophic LV territory loss |
| **LAD (anterior interventricular)** | Anterior LV, anterior 2/3 of septum, apex | Largest territory; supplies major papillary muscle and most of LV |
| **Diagonal branches (LAD)** | Lateral anterior LV | Lateral wall ischemia |
| **Septal perforators (LAD)** | Interventricular septum | Septal infarcts → VSD risk post-MI |
| **Left circumflex (LCX)** | Lateral LV (via obtuse marginals); left atrium | Occlusion → lateral MI; AV nodal artery in 10% (left-dominant) |
| **Posterior descending artery (PDA)** | Posterior 1/3 septum + inferior LV + posteromedial papillary muscle | Defines coronary dominance; supplies the AV nodal artery in right-dominant hearts |
| **Right marginal branches** | RV free wall | RV infarction (preload-dependent - don't give nitrates) |
| **Coronary sinus** | Main venous drainage to RA | Catheter route for biventricular pacing (CRT) |
| **Great cardiac vein** | Runs with LAD; drains anterior LV | Empties into coronary sinus |
| **Middle cardiac vein** | Runs with PDA; drains inferior LV | Empties into coronary sinus |
| **Thebesian veins** | Drain directly into all four chambers | Minor right-to-left shunt ("anatomic shunt") - contributes to physiologic A-a gradient |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX clinical priorities:**
- **MI (chest pain) priority sequence: MONA-B** - Morphine, Oxygen (if sat &lt;90%), Nitrates, Aspirin (chewed, 325 mg), Beta-blocker. **Aspirin first** in many algorithms.
- **Apical pulse** assessed at the **5th ICS, midclavicular line** for a full minute - required before digoxin administration; hold if &lt;60 bpm in adults, &lt;90 in infants.
- **JVD + peripheral edema + hepatomegaly** = right HF; **dyspnea + crackles + pink frothy sputum** = left HF.
- **Pulsus paradoxus** (&gt;10 mmHg inspiratory SBP drop) is a hallmark of cardiac tamponade - a nursing emergency.
- After cardiac catheterization: monitor groin site for bleeding/hematoma, distal pulses, and **keep extremity straight 4–6 h**.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**USMLE Step 1 patterns:**
- **PDA territory** = posterior 1/3 septum + inferior LV + posteromedial papillary muscle. PDA blood supply explains why inferior MIs cause posterior MR (papillary rupture).
- **Embryology gotchas**: foramen primum/secundum closure, ductus arteriosus → ligamentum arteriosum (between aorta and PA), ductus venosus → ligamentum venosum.
- **Coronary dominance** = which artery gives off the PDA. ~85% right-dominant.
- **Common congenital defects**: VSD &gt; ASD &gt; PDA; cyanotic = 5 T's (Tetralogy, Transposition, Truncus arteriosus, Tricuspid atresia, TAPVR).
- **Eisenmenger syndrome**: uncorrected L→R shunt → pulmonary HTN → shunt reverses R→L → cyanosis.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Cardiovascular pharmacology pearls:**
- **β1 blockers** (metoprolol, atenolol, bisoprolol) - cardioselective; preferred in HF and post-MI. Mortality benefit in HFrEF.
- **Non-selective β blockers** (propranolol) - also block β2 → bronchospasm; avoid in asthma.
- **ACE-I/ARB**: ↓ afterload + preload, anti-remodeling; **monitor K⁺ + creatinine** (can rise transiently); avoid in pregnancy + bilateral RAS.
- **Spironolactone** (aldosterone receptor antagonist): mortality benefit in HFrEF; watch K⁺ + gynecomastia (eplerenone avoids the gyno).
- **Digoxin**: narrow therapeutic window; toxicity → nausea, yellow-green halos, AV block; risk ↑ in hypokalemia (K⁺ and dig compete at Na/K ATPase).
- **Statins**: liver enzymes + myopathy (rhabdo with CYP3A4 inhibitors); take at night (HMG-CoA reductase peaks then).
</HighYield>
