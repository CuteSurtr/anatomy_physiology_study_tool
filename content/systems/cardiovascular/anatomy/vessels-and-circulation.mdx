Blood vessels are a continuous, branching, closed loop carrying blood from the heart to every cell and back. There are **three wall layers** (tunics), **five vessel classes** with distinct function, and **two parallel circuits** (pulmonary + systemic). Understanding vessel architecture is the foundation for hypertension, atherosclerosis, aneurysm, and every cardiovascular drug class.

## The three tunics - universal vessel architecture

| Tunic | Composition | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Tunica intima** (innermost) | Endothelium + thin basement membrane + internal elastic lamina | Smooth, non-thrombogenic surface; secretes NO + endothelin to control tone |
| **Tunica media** (middle) | Smooth muscle + elastic fibers + external elastic lamina | Vasoconstriction/dilation; recoil in elastic arteries |
| **Tunica externa / adventitia** (outer) | Collagen + vasa vasorum + sympathetic nerves | Anchors vessel; supplies the outer wall with its own blood |

The **tunica media is the largest in arteries** (thick muscular wall for high pressure), while **veins have a thicker tunica externa** (collagen scaffolding to resist pooling).

## Five vessel classes

<Figure name="cardiovascular/aorta-elastic-recoil" caption="Arterial elastic recoil (the Windkessel effect): in systole the aortic wall stretches to accommodate the ejected stroke volume; in diastole the wall recoils and keeps pushing blood forward, so flow stays continuous despite a pulsatile pump." />

| Vessel | Diameter | Key wall feature | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Elastic arteries** (aorta, pulmonary trunk, common carotid, subclavian, common iliac) | 1–2.5 cm | Thick media packed with **elastin** (sheets of laminae) | Pressure reservoir - recoil during diastole maintains forward flow (the **Windkessel effect**) |
| **Muscular arteries** (brachial, femoral, radial, splenic) | 0.1 mm – 1 cm | Thick **smooth muscle** media; prominent internal elastic lamina | Distribute blood; major site of sympathetic vasoconstriction |
| **Arterioles** | 10–100 μm | Few layers of smooth muscle | **Primary resistance vessels** - control of arteriolar tone sets total peripheral resistance (TPR) → systemic BP |
| **Capillaries** | 5–10 μm (one RBC wide) | Endothelium + basement membrane only - no media | Exchange of O₂, CO₂, nutrients, waste, and fluid |
| **Veins** (venules → small → large) | 0.1 mm – 3 cm | Thin walls; **valves** in extremities; large lumen | **Capacitance vessels** - hold ~64% of total blood volume |

### Capillary subtypes - three flavors

| Type | Wall | Where | Permeability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Continuous** | Tight junctions + intact basement membrane | Skin, muscle, lung, **CNS (blood-brain barrier)** | Most selective - only small lipid-soluble molecules + glucose (via GLUT) cross |
| **Fenestrated** | Pores (60–80 nm) closed by thin diaphragm | Kidney glomerulus, intestinal villi, endocrine glands | Allows passage of small proteins, hormones |
| **Sinusoidal** (discontinuous) | Large gaps; incomplete basement membrane | Liver, bone marrow, spleen | Cells and large proteins cross freely |

<Mnemonic>
**"BBB Sinks Slowly"** - Brain & CNS use the most restrictive **B**lood-**B**rain **B**arrier (continuous + tight junctions + astrocyte foot processes), while **S**inusoids in liver, bone marrow, **s**pleen are the leakiest.
</Mnemonic>

## Systemic vs pulmonary circulation

<Figure name="cardiovascular/circulation-overview" caption="Systemic vs pulmonary circulation - RV → pulmonary trunk → lungs → LA (pulmonary, low pressure); LV → aorta → body → RA (systemic, high pressure)." />

| Feature | Systemic | Pulmonary |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin** | Left ventricle → aorta | Right ventricle → pulmonary trunk |
| **Pressure** | High (~120/80) | Low (~25/8) |
| **Resistance** | High (TPR ~ 20× pulmonary) | Low |
| **Vessel walls** | Thick muscular | Thin |
| **Function** | Deliver oxygenated blood to all tissues | Bring deoxygenated blood to lungs for gas exchange |
| **Hypoxia response** | Vasodilation (more flow to hypoxic tissue) | **Vasoconstriction** (shunt blood away from poorly-ventilated alveoli) |

### Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction (HPV)
Unique to the lung. When alveolar PO₂ drops, pulmonary arterioles **constrict** - opposite of every other tissue. This shunts blood away from underventilated alveoli, optimizing V/Q matching. In chronic disease (COPD, high altitude) this becomes maladaptive → **pulmonary hypertension → cor pulmonale**.

## Major arteries - what supplies what

<Figure name="cardiovascular/major-systemic-arteries" caption="Major systemic arteries (OpenStax). Aorta → brachiocephalic + L common carotid + L subclavian; thoracic + abdominal aorta → celiac, SMA, renal, gonadal, IMA, common iliacs → femoral." />

### From the aortic arch (3 branches, left to right)
| Branch | Supplies |
|---|---|
| **Brachiocephalic trunk** → right common carotid + right subclavian | Right side of head/neck + right upper limb |
| **Left common carotid** | Left side of head/neck |
| **Left subclavian** | Left upper limb + posterior brain (via vertebrals) |

### Descending aorta - thoracic branches
- **Bronchial arteries** - nutrient supply to lung tissue itself (the pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood for gas exchange, not lung nutrition)
- **Esophageal arteries**
- **Intercostal arteries** - segmental, paired with intercostal nerves + veins (VAN: Vein → Artery → Nerve, superior to inferior)

### Abdominal aorta - three unpaired anterior visceral branches
| Branch | Level | Supplies |
|---|---|---|
| **Celiac trunk** | T12 | Foregut: stomach, liver, gallbladder, proximal duodenum, spleen, pancreas (partial) |
| **Superior mesenteric (SMA)** | L1 | Midgut: distal duodenum → proximal 2/3 transverse colon |
| **Inferior mesenteric (IMA)** | L3 | Hindgut: distal 1/3 transverse colon → upper rectum |

The aorta bifurcates at **L4** into the **common iliac arteries** (→ external + internal iliac).

## Venous return - three quirks

1. **No pumping**. Veins rely on (a) the **skeletal muscle pump** (contracting muscles squeeze deep veins, valves enforce one-way flow), (b) the **respiratory pump** (negative intrathoracic pressure on inspiration draws blood toward the heart), and (c) **residual arterial pressure** (vis a tergo).
2. **Valves** in extremity veins (especially the legs) prevent retrograde flow. Failure → **varicose veins**.
3. **Superficial vs deep**: superficial veins (great + small saphenous) drain through perforator veins into deep veins (femoral, popliteal). Clot in a deep vein = DVT. Clot in a superficial vein = superficial thrombophlebitis (less dangerous because no embolic risk).

### Major systemic veins
| Vein | Drains | Joins |
|---|---|---|
| **Superior vena cava (SVC)** | Head, neck, upper limbs, upper thorax | Right atrium |
| **Inferior vena cava (IVC)** | Everything below diaphragm | Right atrium |
| **Coronary sinus** | Heart wall itself | Right atrium (separately) |
| **Azygos system** | Posterior thoracic wall - backup if IVC is obstructed | SVC |
| **Hepatic portal vein** | GI tract + spleen + pancreas | Liver sinusoids (NOT IVC directly) - see [mesenteries](/digestive/anatomy/mesenteries) |

<Clinical>
**SVC syndrome** - obstruction (usually small-cell lung cancer or lymphoma) causes facial/upper-extremity edema, dilated chest wall collaterals, headache. The collateral pathway: azygos system → IVC. The **azygos = backup highway** for the SVC.

**Aortic aneurysm** - most commonly **abdominal**, infrarenal. Risk factors: smoking, hypertension, atherosclerosis, male, family history, Marfan/Ehlers-Danlos. Rupture is a surgical emergency (>5.5 cm = repair).
</Clinical>

## Function reference - every vessel layer

| Component | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Endothelium** | Selectively permeable; secretes NO (vasodilator) + endothelin-1 (vasoconstrictor) + tissue factor (clotting) + prostacyclin (anti-platelet) | Endothelial dysfunction = first step of atherosclerosis. NO loss in diabetes/HTN/smoking |
| **Internal elastic lamina** | Demarcates intima/media; allows recoil | Fragmented in aging arteries → arteriosclerosis |
| **Smooth muscle (media)** | Vasoconstriction (α1, ET-1) and dilation (β2, NO) | Target of every BP drug class |
| **External elastic lamina** | Outer boundary of media; only in arteries | Helps distinguish artery from vein histologically |
| **Vasa vasorum** | "Vessels of vessels" - arterioles in adventitia supplying the outer wall of large vessels | In syphilitic aortitis, vasa vasorum vasculitis weakens the aortic root → ascending aortic aneurysm |
| **Valves (in veins)** | Bicuspid folds of intima; prevent retrograde flow | Failure → varicose veins, venous stasis ulcers |
| **Pericytes** (capillary) | Contractile cells wrapping capillaries; regulate flow + permeability | Lost early in diabetic retinopathy |

## Why this matters - NCLEX/board frame

<HighYield variant="boards">
- **Resistance arterioles set BP**: TPR is the systemic arteriolar tone × blood viscosity (Poiseuille). Almost every antihypertensive targets these vessels or their inputs (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, CCBs, α-blockers, hydralazine).
- **Capillaries are the only exchange site** - everything else just plumbs. Edema = capillary hydrostatic ↑ (CHF, venous obstruction) or oncotic ↓ (nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis) or permeability ↑ (sepsis, burns).
- **Venous capacitance** holds 64% of blood. Venodilators (nitroglycerin, ACE inhibitors) drop preload → drop wall stress → angina relief.
- **HPV** is uniquely lung. Hypoxia → constriction (everywhere else dilates). Chronic hypoxia in COPD/altitude → pulmonary HTN → cor pulmonale.
- **Marfan syndrome** (fibrillin-1 mutation) weakens elastin scaffolding → aortic root dilation → dissection. Always look for tall stature, arachnodactyly, lens dislocation.
</HighYield>
