Blood is a **liquid connective tissue** - plasma (matrix) + formed elements (RBCs, WBCs, platelets) suspended in it. Adult blood volume is ~5 L (8% of body weight). Centrifuge a sample and you get **plasma (55%) on top, buffy coat (WBCs + platelets, &lt;1%) in the middle, and RBCs (45%) on the bottom** - that bottom number is the **hematocrit**.

## Composition at a glance

| Component | % volume | Composition | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plasma** | ~55% | 92% water + proteins + electrolytes + nutrients + waste + gases | Transport medium; oncotic pressure; clotting; immune defense |
| **Buffy coat** | &lt;1% | WBCs + platelets | Immunity + hemostasis |
| **Erythrocytes (RBCs)** | ~45% | Anucleate, biconcave; hemoglobin-packed | O₂ + CO₂ transport |

## Plasma proteins

| Protein | % of plasma protein | Made by | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Albumin** | 60% | Liver | Plasma **oncotic pressure** (75% of it); carrier for drugs, hormones, bilirubin, fatty acids |
| **Globulins** | 36% | Liver (α, β) + plasma cells (γ-immunoglobulins) | α + β = transport (transferrin, ceruloplasmin, lipoproteins); γ = antibodies |
| **Fibrinogen** | 4% | Liver | Cleaved to fibrin in the clotting cascade |
| **Regulatory/other** | &lt;1% | Various | Complement, clotting factors, hormones |

**Serum = plasma minus fibrinogen + clotting factors** (i.e., what's left after blood clots). Important: lab "serum chemistry" panels report on serum, not plasma.

<Clinical>
**Hypoalbuminemia** (cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome, malnutrition) → ↓ oncotic pressure → **edema** + **ascites** + drug levels harder to control (less protein binding → more free drug).

**Hypergammaglobulinemia** with a monoclonal spike on serum protein electrophoresis (SPEP) = **multiple myeloma** until proven otherwise.
</Clinical>

## Erythrocytes (RBCs)

<Figure name="cardiovascular/erythrocytes" caption="Erythrocytes - anucleate biconcave discs. Lack of nucleus + organelles maximizes room for hemoglobin and forces ATP from anaerobic glycolysis only." />

- **Diameter** 7–8 μm; **biconcave disc** → high surface area / volume for gas exchange + flexibility through capillaries (~5 μm).
- **Anucleate** + no organelles → can't divide or repair; carries hemoglobin only; lifespan **120 days** before being recycled in the spleen.
- **Hemoglobin (Hb)**: tetramer of 2α + 2β globin chains, each with a heme group containing Fe²⁺ that binds O₂. One Hb carries up to **4 O₂** molecules. Adult value: M 14–17, F 12–15 g/dL.

### Hemoglobin variants
| Hb | Composition | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **HbA** | 2α + 2β | Adult (~97%) |
| **HbA2** | 2α + 2δ | Adult (~2%) - elevated in β-thalassemia |
| **HbF** | 2α + 2γ | Fetal - higher O₂ affinity (left-shifted curve) lets fetal blood pull O₂ from maternal blood at the placenta |
| **HbS** | 2α + 2β^Glu→Val at position 6 | Sickle cell disease - polymerizes when deoxygenated |

### ABO + Rh blood groups
| Group | Antigens on RBC | Antibodies in plasma | Can receive |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | A | Anti-B | A, O |
| **B** | B | Anti-A | B, O |
| **AB** | A + B | None | **Universal recipient** |
| **O** | None | Anti-A + Anti-B | O only - **universal donor** (for RBCs) |
| **Rh+** | D antigen | None | Rh+ or Rh− |
| **Rh−** | None | None initially; develops after exposure | Rh− only |

**Hemolytic disease of the newborn (HDN)**: Rh− mother + Rh+ fetus. First pregnancy sensitizes mother (anti-D IgG). Second Rh+ pregnancy → maternal anti-D crosses placenta → fetal RBC hemolysis. Prevent with **RhoGAM** (anti-D Ig) at 28 weeks + delivery.

## Leukocytes (WBCs)

<Figure name="cardiovascular/wbc-types" caption="All five leukocyte classes, labelled. Granulocytes - neutrophil (multilobed nucleus, fine pale granules), eosinophil (bilobed nucleus, coarse red granules), basophil (granules so dense they obscure the nucleus). Agranulocytes - lymphocyte (large round nucleus filling the cell) and monocyte (large, kidney-bean nucleus). These are stylised 3D renderings, not a stained smear." />

<Figure name="cardiovascular/neutrophils" caption="Neutrophil granulocytes - multilobed nucleus (3-5 lobes) and faint pink granules; the dominant phagocyte of the acute innate response." />

<Figure name="cardiovascular/lymphocytes" caption="Lymphocytes - small mononuclear leukocytes with a single round nucleus and a thin rim of cytoplasm. T, B, and NK subtypes look similar on smear and are distinguished by surface markers." />

Five types - split by granule visibility:

| Cell | % WBCs | Lifespan | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Neutrophils** | 50–70% | Hours–days | Phagocytosis of bacteria; first responders; multi-lobed nucleus |
| **Lymphocytes** (B, T, NK) | 20–40% | Days–years | Adaptive immunity (B → antibodies; T → cell-mediated); NK kills virus-infected cells |
| **Monocytes** → macrophages in tissue | 2–8% | Months–years | Phagocytosis + antigen presentation; kidney-bean nucleus |
| **Eosinophils** | 1–4% | 8–12 days | Parasites + allergies; bi-lobed; red granules |
| **Basophils** / mast cells | &lt;1% | Days | Histamine + heparin release; allergy + inflammation |

<Mnemonic>
**"Never Let Monkeys Eat Bananas"** - order by abundance: **N**eutrophils > **L**ymphocytes > **M**onocytes > **E**osinophils > **B**asophils.
</Mnemonic>

### Differential clues
- **Neutrophilia + left shift** (band cells) → bacterial infection
- **Lymphocytosis** (atypical lymphs) → viral (esp. EBV, CMV)
- **Eosinophilia** → "NAACP" - **N**eoplasia, **A**llergy/asthma, **A**ddison disease, **C**ollagen vascular, **P**arasites
- **Monocytosis** → chronic infection (TB), some leukemias
- **Basophilia** → CML, hypothyroidism

## Platelets (thrombocytes)

<Figure name="cardiovascular/platelets" caption="Platelets - small anucleate cell fragments derived from megakaryocytes. Adhere to exposed collagen via vWF and GP1b, then aggregate via GP2b/3a and fibrinogen bridges to form the primary hemostatic plug." />

- Cell fragments shed from **megakaryocytes** in bone marrow.
- 150,000–400,000/μL; lifespan **8–10 days**.
- Anucleate; contain α + dense granules (ADP, serotonin, thromboxane, vWF, fibrinogen).
- Activated by collagen exposure → adhesion (via vWF + GP1b) → activation (TXA2, ADP release) → aggregation (GP2b/3a + fibrinogen bridges).

<Figure name="cardiovascular/coagulation" caption="Three stages of coagulation - vascular injury exposes collagen, platelets adhere + activate into a soft plug, then the coagulation cascade lays down a fibrin mesh that traps RBCs into a stable thrombus." />

## Hematopoiesis - where blood comes from

<Figure name="cardiovascular/hematopoiesis" caption="Human hematopoiesis - hematopoietic stem cell → myeloid progenitor (RBCs, platelets, granulocytes, monocytes) + lymphoid progenitor (B, T, NK cells). All from bone marrow except mature T cells (thymus)." />

All blood cells originate from a **pluripotent hematopoietic stem cell (HSC)** in bone marrow.

| Lineage | Differentiates to | Key growth factor |
|---|---|---|
| **Myeloid** | RBCs, platelets, neutrophils, monocytes, eosinophils, basophils | EPO (RBCs), TPO (platelets), G-CSF (neutrophils), GM-CSF, IL-3, IL-5 (eosinophils) |
| **Lymphoid** | B cells, T cells, NK cells | IL-2, IL-7 |

### Site of hematopoiesis by age
| Age | Site |
|---|---|
| **0–2 mo (fetal)** | Yolk sac |
| **2–7 mo (fetal)** | Liver + spleen |
| **>7 mo → birth** | Bone marrow (most bones) |
| **Adult** | Red marrow retreats to the **axial skeleton** (vertebrae, sternum, ribs, skull, pelvis) **plus the proximal epiphyses of the femur and humerus** |

In adults, peripheral long-bone marrow becomes **fatty (yellow marrow)** but can reconvert to red marrow under stress (chronic hypoxia, hemolysis).

<Clinical>
**Extramedullary hematopoiesis** - when bone marrow can't keep up (β-thalassemia major, myelofibrosis), the **liver and spleen** (and sometimes lymph nodes) restart RBC production → hepatosplenomegaly. The marrow itself also expands, widening the diploë of the skull and the facial bones ("chipmunk facies", "hair-on-end" skull on X-ray).

**Erythropoietin (EPO)** is made by **renal peritubular interstitial fibroblasts** in response to hypoxia. CKD → ↓ EPO → anemia. Treat with recombinant EPO. Athletes also abuse it (blood doping).
</Clinical>

## Iron metabolism - the critical limiting nutrient

<Figure name="cardiovascular/transferrin-cycle" caption="Transferrin cycle - Fe³⁺ binds plasma transferrin → binds TfR1 on cells → endocytosis → endosome acidifies → Fe released to cytoplasm via DMT1 → ferritin storage or use; apotransferrin recycled." />

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Absorption** | Duodenum + proximal jejunum, by two separate routes. **Non-heme iron** must be reduced from Fe³⁺ to **Fe²⁺** (by duodenal cytochrome b; dietary vitamin C helps) before **DMT1** carries it in. **Heme iron** is taken up intact by its own transporter and the iron is then released inside the enterocyte - which is why heme iron from meat is absorbed far more efficiently |
| **Transport** | Bound to **transferrin** in plasma |
| **Storage** | **Ferritin** (intracellular, soluble) + hemosiderin (insoluble aggregates) - mainly in liver, spleen, bone marrow |
| **Regulation** | **Hepcidin** (liver hormone) blocks ferroportin → traps iron in enterocytes + macrophages |

Iron studies (board favorite):
| Condition | Serum Fe | TIBC | Ferritin | % saturation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Iron deficiency** | ↓ | ↑ | ↓ | ↓ |
| **Anemia of chronic disease** | ↓ | ↓ | ↑ (or normal) | ↓ |
| **Hemochromatosis** | ↑ | ↓ | ↑↑ | ↑↑ |
| **Pregnancy / OCP** | normal | ↑ | normal | ↓ |

## Function reference - every blood component

| Component | Function | Disease association |
|---|---|---|
| **Albumin** | Oncotic pressure (75%); carrier protein | Cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome → edema |
| **Immunoglobulins** | Adaptive humoral immunity | Multiple myeloma, hyperIgM, CVID |
| **Fibrinogen** | Substrate for clotting (factor I) | Acute phase reactant; DIC depletes it |
| **Hemoglobin** | O₂/CO₂ transport; buffer | Sickle cell, thalassemia, methemoglobinemia |
| **Neutrophils** | Bacterial phagocytosis; oxidative burst | Chronic granulomatous disease (NADPH oxidase defect) |
| **Eosinophils** | Parasite defense; allergy | Hypereosinophilic syndromes |
| **Basophils / mast cells** | Histamine release; allergy | Anaphylaxis, mastocytosis |
| **Macrophages** | Phagocytosis + antigen presentation; remove senescent RBCs in spleen | Erythrophagocytosis in hemolytic anemias |
| **T lymphocytes** | Cell-mediated immunity (CD4 helper, CD8 cytotoxic) | HIV (CD4 depletion), DiGeorge (thymic aplasia) |
| **B lymphocytes** | Antibody production (after plasma cell differentiation) | Bruton agammaglobulinemia, CLL |
| **NK cells** | Kill virus-infected + tumor cells without prior sensitization | Chediak-Higashi (NK dysfunction) |
| **Platelets** | Primary hemostasis (plug); release growth factors | ITP, TTP, Glanzmann, Bernard-Soulier |
| **EPO** | Stimulates RBC production in bone marrow | CKD → anemia; renal cell carcinoma can secrete EPO (paraneoplastic) |
| **Hepcidin** | Iron regulator - blocks ferroportin | Hereditary hemochromatosis (low hepcidin) |

## Why this matters

<HighYield exam="boards">
- **Anemia classification by MCV**: microcytic (&lt;80) = iron deficiency, thalassemia, anemia of chronic disease, lead, sideroblastic; normocytic = acute blood loss, hemolysis, marrow failure; macrocytic (>100) = B12/folate, alcohol, hypothyroid, drugs (methotrexate, 5-FU).
- **Sickle cell**: HbS polymerizes when deoxygenated → vaso-occlusive crisis. Treat acute crisis with hydration + opioids + O₂; prevent with **hydroxyurea** (↑ HbF).
- **Hemolytic anemia labs**: ↑ LDH, ↑ indirect bilirubin, ↓ haptoglobin, schistocytes (mechanical) or spherocytes (immune).
- **DIC** - consumption of clotting factors + platelets → bleeding everywhere. ↑ D-dimer, ↑ PT/PTT, ↓ fibrinogen, ↓ platelets.
- **Polycythemia vera** - JAK2 mutation → ↑ RBC mass independent of EPO. Treat with phlebotomy + hydroxyurea + aspirin. Distinguished from secondary polycythemia by low EPO.
- **Universal donor** for **RBCs = O−** (no antigens); for **plasma = AB** (no anti-A or anti-B).
</HighYield>
