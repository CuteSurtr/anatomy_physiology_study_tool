The abdominopelvic cavity is a **tube within a tube**: outer abdominal wall, inner GI tract, peritoneum + mesenteries in between. Mesenteries are not passive draperies - they are the embryologic record of how each organ migrated, and they carry every artery, vein, nerve, and lymph vessel that supplies the gut. The **portal circulation** that runs through them is what makes "first-pass hepatic metabolism" a thing and gives the GI tract its unique pharmacology.

## Tube-within-a-tube architecture

The abdominal cavity is organized concentrically from outside in:

1. **Abdominal wall** (skin → muscle → transversalis fascia)
2. **Parietal peritoneum** lining the inner surface of the wall
3. **Peritoneal cavity** - a fluid-filled potential space (~50 mL serous fluid)
4. **Visceral peritoneum** covering the GI tract organs
5. **Alimentary canal** itself - open at mouth and anus

The peritoneal cavity is **sterile** in health. Any organism cultured from it = pathology.

## Intraperitoneal vs retroperitoneal vs infraperitoneal

<Figure name="digestive/retroperitoneal-spaces" caption="Retroperitoneal spaces. Primary retroperitoneal: kidneys, ureters, adrenals, aorta, IVC. Secondary retroperitoneal (started intraperitoneal but became retro): ascending + descending colon, duodenum (parts 2-4), pancreas." />

| Category | Means | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Intraperitoneal** | Suspended by mesentery; can move within cavity | Stomach, spleen, liver, gallbladder, jejunum, ileum, transverse colon, sigmoid colon, cecum |
| **Retroperitoneal** | Behind parietal peritoneum, immobile | (See SAD PUCKER below) |
| **Infraperitoneal / subperitoneal** | Below peritoneal reflection in the pelvis | Bladder, distal ureters, lower rectum, female internal genitalia, male prostate/seminal vesicles |
| **Secondary retroperitoneal** | Started intraperitoneal in embryo; mesentery fused to posterior wall | Pancreas (except tail), duodenum (parts 2–4), ascending + descending colon |

<Mnemonic>
**SAD PUCKER** for retroperitoneal: **S**uprarenals (adrenals), **A**orta + IVC, **D**uodenum (parts 2–4), **P**ancreas (except tail), **U**reters, **C**olon (ascending + descending), **K**idneys, **E**sophagus (lower thoracic), **R**ectum (mid).
</Mnemonic>

## The named mesenteries

<Figure name="digestive/stomach-detail" caption="" />


A "mesentery" is a double layer of peritoneum that suspends an intraperitoneal organ from the body wall and carries its vessels, nerves, and lymphatics. Multiple named ones:

| Mesentery | Connects | Contents / clinical |
|---|---|---|
| **Falciform ligament** | Anterior abdominal wall (at umbilicus) → liver | Only mesentery from anterior wall; contains the **ligamentum teres** (round ligament - remnant of fetal **umbilical vein**) |
| **Lesser omentum** (gastrohepatic + hepatoduodenal ligaments) | Liver → lesser curvature of stomach + first part of duodenum | Contains the **portal triad** in its free edge (hepatic artery + portal vein + common bile duct) - site of the **pringle maneuver** in liver hemorrhage |
| **Greater omentum** | Greater curvature of stomach → transverse colon (4 layers of peritoneum) | "Policeman of the abdomen" - wraps around injured or inflamed structures; major storage of visceral fat; isolates abscesses |
| **The mesentery (proper)** | Posterior wall → jejunum + ileum | Largest mesentery; carries SMA + SMV + autonomic nerves + lymphatics |
| **Transverse mesocolon** | Posterior wall → transverse colon | Carries middle colic vessels |
| **Sigmoid mesocolon** | Posterior wall → sigmoid colon | Carries sigmoid vessels; long mesentery → can twist (**sigmoid volvulus**) |
| **Mesoappendix** | Ileum → appendix | Carries appendicular artery |
| **Gastrosplenic ligament** | Greater curvature → spleen | Carries short gastric vessels |
| **Splenorenal ligament** | Spleen → posterior wall | Carries splenic vessels + tail of pancreas |
| **Coronary + triangular ligaments** | Liver → diaphragm | Define the bare area of the liver |

### Peritoneal pouches in the pelvis

The peritoneum reflects over the pelvic organs, creating the lowest points of the cavity - clinically critical because fluid (blood, pus, ascites) collects here:

- **Females**: **vesicouterine pouch** (between bladder and uterus) and **rectouterine pouch (of Douglas)** (between uterus and rectum - the lowest point in a female pelvis, accessed surgically through the posterior vaginal fornix).
- **Males**: **rectovesical pouch** (between bladder and rectum).

<Clinical>
**Culdocentesis**: needle insertion through posterior vaginal fornix into the **rectouterine pouch (of Douglas)** to sample peritoneal fluid - historically used to diagnose ruptured ectopic pregnancy (blood) or PID (pus).

**Surgical access** to retrieve oocytes for IVF, drain a pelvic abscess, or perform appendectomy can also use this route.
</Clinical>

## The three portal systems

<Figure name="digestive/hepatic-portal-system" caption="Hepatic portal vein system (OpenStax). Splenic + SMV unite behind pancreas to form portal vein → liver → hepatic veins → IVC. Inferior mesenteric drains into the splenic." />

A **portal system** has **capillaries → vessel → capillaries again** (instead of the normal capillaries → vein → heart). Three exist in the body:

| Portal system | First capillary bed | Portal vessel | Second capillary bed | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hepatic portal** | GI tract, spleen, pancreas | **Hepatic portal vein** | Liver sinusoids | Liver gets first crack at absorbed nutrients + drugs (**first-pass metabolism**) |
| **Hypothalamic-hypophyseal portal** | Hypothalamus median eminence | Hypophyseal portal veins | Anterior pituitary | Concentrated hypothalamic releasing hormones reach anterior pituitary |
| **Renal portal (juxta-glomerular)** | Glomerular capillary | Efferent arteriole | Peritubular capillaries (or vasa recta) | Lets the kidney reabsorb selectively after filtration |

## GI arterial supply - embryologic gut divisions

<Figure name="digestive/celiac-trunk" caption="Celiac trunk + branches - foregut blood supply. Three branches: left gastric, common hepatic, splenic. Supplies stomach, liver, spleen, pancreas head, proximal duodenum." />

The aorta sends three midline branches to the GI tract, each supplying one embryologic gut division:

| Aortic branch | Aortic level | Gut region supplied | Organs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Celiac trunk** | T12 | **Foregut** | Distal esophagus, stomach, liver, gallbladder, superior 1/2 of duodenum, pancreas (head/body), spleen |
| **Superior mesenteric artery (SMA)** | L1 | **Midgut** | Inferior 1/2 of duodenum, jejunum, ileum, cecum, appendix, ascending colon, proximal 2/3 of transverse colon |
| **Inferior mesenteric artery (IMA)** | L3 | **Hindgut** | Distal 1/3 of transverse colon, descending colon, sigmoid colon, upper rectum |

The **distal rectum** is supplied by **middle + inferior rectal arteries** off the **internal iliac** - not from the IMA, which is why hemorrhoidal venous drainage can bypass the portal system.

### Celiac trunk branches

Three branches: **left gastric**, **splenic**, **common hepatic**. Common hepatic gives off the **gastroduodenal** (→ stomach + duodenum + pancreas head) and continues as the **proper hepatic** (→ liver via right + left hepatic).

### SMA branches

**Inferior pancreaticoduodenal**, **middle colic**, **right colic**, **ileocolic**, multiple **jejunal + ileal** arteries.

### IMA branches

**Left colic**, **sigmoid arteries**, **superior rectal**.

## Portal venous return

Veins from the GI tract converge into the **hepatic portal vein** (formed behind the neck of the pancreas by the **SMV + splenic vein**, with IMV joining the splenic).

```
Capillaries of GI tract / spleen / pancreas
        ↓
  SMV + splenic vein (IMV joins splenic)
        ↓
   Hepatic portal vein
        ↓
   Liver sinusoids
        ↓
   Central veins
        ↓
   Hepatic veins
        ↓
   Inferior vena cava
        ↓
   Right atrium
```

## Portosystemic anastomoses - when the liver fails

<Figure name="digestive/portal-hypertension-caput-medusae" caption="Caput medusae - recanalization of the umbilical vein in portal hypertension. Tortuous abdominal-wall veins radiate from umbilicus. Other portosystemic anastomoses: esophageal varices, hemorrhoids." />

In **portal hypertension** (most commonly from cirrhosis), blood looks for routes around the liver back to the systemic circulation. Three anastomoses dilate, with characteristic clinical signs:

| Anastomosis site | Portal vessel | Systemic vessel | Clinical sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Distal esophagus** | Left gastric v. | Esophageal v. → azygos v. → SVC | **Esophageal varices** (life-threatening hematemesis) |
| **Anal canal** (above pectinate line) | Superior rectal v. | Middle + inferior rectal vv. → internal iliac v. | **Internal hemorrhoids** |
| **Umbilicus** | Paraumbilical vv. (in falciform ligament) | Superficial epigastric vv. → systemic | **Caput medusae** (dilated abdominal wall veins) |

<Note title="Pectinate line revisited">
The pectinate (dentate) line is the embryologic boundary in the anal canal between hindgut (endoderm) and ectodermal anal pit. Above the line: visceral, columnar epithelium, portal drainage, autonomic innervation (insensitive to pain - internal hemorrhoids don't hurt). Below the line: somatic, stratified squamous epithelium, systemic drainage, pudendal innervation (very pain-sensitive - external hemorrhoids hurt a lot).
</Note>

## Liver lobule histology (recap)

<Figure name="digestive/liver-lobule" caption="" />


Each hexagonal lobule has:
- **Central vein** in the middle (drains to hepatic vein)
- **Portal triad** at each corner: **hepatic artery branch + portal vein branch + bile duct**
- **Hepatocytes** arranged in plates radiating outward
- **Sinusoids** - fenestrated capillaries lined by **Kupffer cells** (resident macrophages)

Blood flows from portal triad → sinusoids → central vein. Bile flows the opposite direction in **canaliculi** between hepatocytes → bile ductules → portal triad ducts.

**Zonation** (peripheral to central):
- **Zone 1 (periportal)** - most oxygenated; gluconeogenesis, urea cycle; first hit by viral hepatitis + toxins absorbed from gut
- **Zone 3 (centrilobular)** - least oxygenated; CYP450 drug metabolism, alcohol; **first to die in ischemia** ("shock liver") and acetaminophen toxicity

## First-pass metabolism and how to bypass it

Drugs absorbed from the GI tract reach the liver before the rest of the body. The fraction reaching systemic circulation = **bioavailability (F)**.

| Route | Bypasses first-pass? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral (PO)** | No - full first-pass | Lowest F for high-extraction drugs |
| **Sublingual (SL)** | Yes - direct venous drainage to SVC | Fast onset (nitroglycerin, buprenorphine) |
| **Buccal (BUC)** | Yes | Slower than SL |
| **Rectal (PR)** | **Partial** - proximal rectum drains to portal vein, mid/distal rectum drains to systemic via middle/inferior rectal veins | Variable F depending on suppository position |
| **Intravenous (IV)** | Yes | 100% F by definition |
| **Intramuscular (IM)** | Yes | Slower than IV |
| **Subcutaneous (SC)** | Yes | Slowest parenteral |
| **Transdermal (TD)** | Yes | Slow + sustained |
| **Inhaled (INH)** | Yes (via pulmonary v. → left heart) | Fast for lipophilic drugs |
| **Intranasal (IN)** | Yes | Naloxone for opioid OD |

## Enterohepatic recirculation

<Figure name="digestive/enterohepatic-circulation" caption="Enterohepatic recirculation - liver secretes bile (and conjugated drugs) into duodenum; 95% of bile salts (and a subset of drugs) reabsorbed in terminal ileum → portal vein → liver, repeating ~6-8× per meal (Wikimedia)." />

Some drugs and endogenous compounds are excreted into bile, reach the gut, are reabsorbed in the terminal ileum, and return to the liver - and around again. Bile acids do this physiologically (95% reabsorbed). Drugs that undergo enterohepatic recirculation have long half-lives:
- **Phenytoin, valproic acid, carbamazepine** (antiepileptics)
- **Methotrexate**
- **NSAIDs**
- **Morphine glucuronide**
- **Mycophenolate**

Interrupting enterohepatic recirculation (cholestyramine, activated charcoal, bowel surgery removing terminal ileum) **drops drug levels**.

## Every mesenteric/vascular structure - function reference

| Structure | What it does | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Parietal peritoneum** | Lines abdominopelvic cavity | Somatic innervation → localized pain (peritoneal signs) |
| **Visceral peritoneum** | Covers intraperitoneal organs | Autonomic innervation → vague, referred pain |
| **Peritoneal cavity** | Potential space | Site of ascites in cirrhosis, peritoneal dialysis route |
| **Falciform ligament** | Anterior wall → liver | Carries ligamentum teres (fetal umbilical vein remnant) |
| **Lesser omentum** | Liver → stomach/duodenum | Free edge contains portal triad |
| **Greater omentum** | Stomach → transverse colon | "Policeman of abdomen"; visceral fat |
| **The mesentery proper** | Wall → jejunum/ileum | Carries SMA, SMV, lymphatics |
| **Celiac trunk** | Foregut blood supply | First aortic branch in abdomen (T12) |
| **SMA** | Midgut blood supply | "SMA syndrome": compression of duodenum part 3 |
| **IMA** | Hindgut blood supply | Watershed at splenic flexure (Griffiths' point) |
| **Portal vein** | GI venous return → liver | Formed by SMV + splenic vein; carries ~75% of liver blood flow |
| **Hepatic veins (3)** | Liver → IVC | Thrombosis = Budd-Chiari syndrome |
| **Portal triad** | Hepatic artery + portal vein + bile duct | In free edge of lesser omentum; Pringle maneuver clamps it |
| **Kupffer cells** | Sinusoidal macrophages | Filter gut bacteria from portal blood |
| **Hepatocyte** | Synthesis + metabolism | Lost in cirrhosis |
| **Pectinate line** | Endoderm/ectoderm boundary | Above: visceral, painless; below: somatic, painful |
| **Internal iliac artery** | Pelvic viscera | Branches: middle/inferior rectal, vesical, vaginal, uterine |
| **Thoracic duct** | Lipid absorption from intestine | Cisterna chyli origin → empties into left subclavian-IJ junction |
| **Lacteal** | Lymphatic vessel in villus | Picks up chylomicrons - bypasses liver first-pass |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Portal hypertension patterns:**
- **Esophageal varices** = portal blood backing up into left gastric → esophageal v. Risk factor: **hematemesis**. Treat acutely with octreotide (somatostatin analog - splanchnic vasoconstriction), endoscopic banding, TIPS.
- **Caput medusae** = portal blood backing up through paraumbilical vv. into abdominal-wall systemic veins. Visible serpentine veins radiating from umbilicus.
- **Hemorrhoids (internal)** = portal-side enlargement above pectinate line. **External hemorrhoids** = systemic-side (below pectinate); not portal HTN-related but painful (somatic).
- **Splenomegaly** in portal HTN comes from back pressure through splenic vein.

**Vascular watershed**: splenic flexure is at the border between SMA + IMA territories (Griffiths' point) → vulnerable to ischemic colitis in hypotension.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**TIPS (transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt)** decompresses portal pressure but eliminates first-pass - drug doses for hepatically-cleared meds must be adjusted (e.g., **phenytoin toxicity** can develop weeks after TIPS).

**Octreotide** = somatostatin analog → splanchnic vasoconstriction → ↓ portal pressure. First-line for variceal bleeding while waiting for endoscopy.

**Lactulose** for hepatic encephalopathy: traps NH₃ as NH₄⁺ in gut lumen → fecal excretion → ↓ blood ammonia → ↑ mentation.

**β-blockers (propranolol, nadolol)**: nonselective; ↓ portal pressure for **primary prophylaxis** of variceal bleeding.
</HighYield>
