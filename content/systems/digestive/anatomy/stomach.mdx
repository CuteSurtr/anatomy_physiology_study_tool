The stomach is a J-shaped reservoir between esophagus and duodenum that performs **mechanical churning, chemical digestion (acid + pepsin), and intrinsic factor secretion**.

<Figure name="digestive/stomach" caption="Gross anatomy of the stomach - esophagus entering at the cardia, then fundus, body, pyloric antrum, and pylorus (left); coronal section showing the rugae of the mucosa (right)." />

<Figure name="digestive/stomach-detail" caption="Stomach in detail (OpenStax). Regions: cardia, fundus, body, pyloric antrum, pylorus. Lesser curvature (right/superior) and greater curvature (left/inferior). Mucosa forms rugae when empty. Wall has a unique fourth oblique muscle layer (besides longitudinal + circular) for churning." />

## Regions

Four anatomical regions: **cardia, fundus, body, pyloric region (antrum + pylorus)**. The **lesser curvature** faces right/superior; the **greater curvature** faces left/inferior. The pylorus exits through the **pyloric sphincter** into the duodenum.

The mucosa is thrown into **rugae** that flatten when the stomach distends. A unique fourth muscle layer - the **oblique** layer (in addition to the GI tract's standard longitudinal and circular) - enables churning.

## Gastric glands & cell types

| Cell | Location | Secretion | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Parietal** | Body, fundus | HCl, intrinsic factor | Acidify; bind B12 for ileal absorption |
| **Chief** | Body, fundus | Pepsinogen, gastric lipase | Protein/fat digestion (activated at low pH) |
| **G cells** | Antrum | Gastrin | Stimulates HCl release |
| **Enterochromaffin-like (ECL)** | Body | Histamine | Drives parietal HCl via H₂ receptor |
| **D cells** | Antrum | Somatostatin | Inhibits gastrin, HCl |
| **Mucous neck** | Whole gland | Bicarbonate-rich mucus | Protects epithelium |

## Acid secretion machinery

Parietal cells contain the **H⁺/K⁺ ATPase** ("proton pump") on the apical membrane. Three stimuli converge: **gastrin** (G cells → CCK-B receptor), **histamine** (ECL → H₂ receptor), **ACh** (vagus → M3 receptor). All ultimately ↑ insertion of H⁺/K⁺ ATPase into the apical membrane.

<Clinical>
**Peptic ulcer disease (PUD)**: most often caused by **H. pylori** (urease neutralizes local acid, allowing bacterial colonization) or chronic NSAIDs (inhibit COX-1 → less protective prostaglandin → less mucus/bicarbonate). Treat *H. pylori* with **triple therapy**: PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin.

**Pernicious anemia**: autoimmune destruction of parietal cells → no intrinsic factor → no B12 absorption → megaloblastic anemia + neurologic deficits.
</Clinical>

## Every gastric structure - what it does and why it matters

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Cardia** | Region around the gastroesophageal junction | Site of Z-line; Barrett's metaplasia here |
| **Fundus** | Dome above the cardia, against the diaphragm | Stores swallowed air; gas bubble visible on upright X-ray |
| **Body (corpus)** | Largest region; contains most gastric glands | Site of HCl + pepsinogen + IF secretion |
| **Antrum (pyloric)** | Distal funnel | Houses G cells (gastrin); site of most peptic ulcers |
| **Pyloric sphincter** | Thickened circular muscle at gastric outlet | Controls gastric emptying; hypertrophied in pyloric stenosis (infants - projectile vomiting, "olive" mass) |
| **Lesser curvature** | Right (concave) border; faces liver | Site of left gastric artery; common ulcer location |
| **Greater curvature** | Left (convex) border; faces colon | Site of greater omentum origin |
| **Rugae** | Folds of mucosa + submucosa | Flatten on distension to ↑ capacity (~1.5 L) |
| **Gastric pits** | Invaginations leading to gastric glands | Surface area for secretion |
| **Surface mucous cells** | Secrete viscous alkaline mucus | Protect epithelium from acid/pepsin |
| **Mucous neck cells** | Secrete soluble mucus | Lubricate luminal contents |
| **Parietal (oxyntic) cells** | Secrete HCl (via H⁺/K⁺ ATPase) + **intrinsic factor** | HCl: digestion, kills microbes; IF: required for B12 absorption (loss → pernicious anemia) |
| **Chief (zymogen) cells** | Secrete **pepsinogen** + gastric lipase | Pepsinogen → pepsin in acid (autocatalytic); begins protein digestion |
| **G cells** | Secrete **gastrin** (in antrum) | Stimulates HCl release; gastrinoma → Zollinger-Ellison |
| **D cells** | Secrete **somatostatin** | Brakes on HCl + gastrin; basis of octreotide therapy |
| **Enterochromaffin-like (ECL) cells** | Secrete **histamine** | Drives parietal HCl via H₂ receptor (target of H₂ blockers) |
| **Enterochromaffin cells** | Secrete **serotonin** | Local motility |
| **Mucosa (epithelium + lamina propria + muscularis mucosae)** | Secretion + protection | Site of MALT lymphoma (H. pylori) |
| **Submucosa** | Contains **Meissner's plexus**, vessels, lymphatics | Regulates glandular secretion |
| **Muscularis externa (3 layers: oblique, circular, longitudinal)** | Mechanical churning | Unique 4th oblique layer enables stomach's complex mixing |
| **Myenteric (Auerbach's) plexus** | Between circular + longitudinal muscle | Coordinates peristalsis; absent in achalasia |
| **Serosa (visceral peritoneum)** | Outer covering | Continuous with greater and lesser omenta |
| **Left gastric artery** | Lesser curvature; from celiac trunk | Supplies cardia + body |
| **Right gastric artery** | Lesser curvature; from hepatic | Supplies lesser-curvature ulcer territory |
| **Right gastroepiploic + left gastroepiploic** | Greater curvature | Supply most of body |
| **Short gastric arteries** | Fundus; from splenic | Vulnerable in splenectomy |
| **Vagus nerve (CN X)** | Parasympathetic; **stimulates HCl, pepsinogen, motility** | Vagotomy used to treat refractory PUD; preserved selectively to retain motility |
| **Splanchnic nerves** | Sympathetic; reduce motility + secretion | Referred pain to T5–T9 dermatomes (epigastric) |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX GI patient priorities:**
- **NG tube**: confirm placement (pH &lt;5 = gastric, X-ray definitive); irrigate with NS, not water; HOB ↑.
- **PUD teaching**: avoid NSAIDs, smoking, alcohol; small frequent meals; antacids 1–3 h after meals.
- **GI bleed signs**: hematemesis (upper), melena (upper), hematochezia (lower); monitor H&amp;H, vitals, urine output.
- **Gastric vs duodenal ulcer pain**: gastric WORSE with food (gastric "gnawing on full"); duodenal BETTER with food (returns 2–3 h later).
- **Post-op gastric surgery dumping syndrome teaching**: small meals, low simple sugars, lie down after eating, fluids between (not with) meals.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Cell-and-disease patterns:**
- **Pernicious anemia**: autoimmune against parietal cells → no IF → B12 malabsorption → **megaloblastic anemia + neuro (subacute combined degeneration)**.
- **Atrophic gastritis** → ↑ gastric cancer risk; **MALT lymphoma** strongly H. pylori-associated.
- **Zollinger-Ellison**: gastrinoma → recurrent ulcers, often jejunal; ↑ fasting gastrin; secretin stimulation paradoxically ↑ gastrin in ZE.
- **Pyloric stenosis** (infant): nonbilious projectile vomiting, "olive" mass, **hypochloremic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis**.
- **Gastric cancer types**: intestinal (chronic gastritis, H. pylori), diffuse (signet-ring cells, linitis plastica).
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Acid-related drugs:**
- **PPIs** (omeprazole, pantoprazole): block H⁺/K⁺ ATPase irreversibly; take 30 min before meals; long-term → ↑ fracture, B12/Mg deficiency, C. diff, pneumonia.
- **H2 blockers** (famotidine, ranitidine): cimetidine has many CYP interactions + gynecomastia.
- **Antacids** (calcium, magnesium, aluminum hydroxide): immediate but short relief; Al → constipation; Mg → diarrhea.
- **Sucralfate**: forms protective barrier on ulcers; activated by acid (don't give with PPI).
- **Misoprostol**: PGE1 analog; protective in NSAID users; **contraindicated in pregnancy** (abortifacient).
- **Triple therapy for H. pylori**: PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin (or metronidazole) ×14 days.
</HighYield>

## Pancreas - gross anatomy (related accessory organ)

<Figure name="digestive/pancreas" caption="Pancreas gross anatomy - head (within the C-loop of duodenum), neck, body, and tail (extending to the spleen). The main pancreatic duct (Wirsung) joins the bile duct at the ampulla of Vater." />

The pancreas is a **mixed exocrine + endocrine gland** sitting transversely in the retroperitoneum at L1–L2:

| Region | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Head** | Sits within the C-loop of duodenum; uncinate process wraps behind SMV/SMA |
| **Neck** | Anterior to SMV/SMA |
| **Body** | Crosses anterior to aorta + L1–L2 vertebrae |
| **Tail** | Reaches the splenic hilum |

**Pancreatic duct (Wirsung)** runs the length and joins the common bile duct at the **ampulla of Vater (hepatopancreatic ampulla)**, opening into the 2nd part of the duodenum at the **major duodenal papilla** (controlled by the **sphincter of Oddi**). Some people have an accessory duct (**Santorini**) emptying at the minor papilla.

**Endocrine pancreas** (~2% of mass): **islets of Langerhans**, ~1 million islets. Cell types:
- **β cells (65%)** - insulin; central in islet
- **α cells (20%)** - glucagon; peripheral
- **δ cells (10%)** - somatostatin
- **PP cells** - pancreatic polypeptide
- **ε cells** - ghrelin

<Clinical>
**Acute pancreatitis** (causes: **GET SMASHED** - Gallstones, Ethanol, Trauma, Steroids, Mumps, Autoimmune, Scorpion sting, Hypercalcemia/triglycerides, ERCP, Drugs): epigastric pain radiating to back, ↑↑ lipase. **Gallstone pancreatitis** most common - stone obstructs ampulla.

**Pancreatic adenocarcinoma**: poor prognosis, head most common; **Courvoisier sign** - palpable non-tender gallbladder + jaundice; **migratory thrombophlebitis** (Trousseau).
</Clinical>

## Liver and biliary tree

<Figure name="digestive/liver" caption="Liver with four anatomical lobes (right, left, caudate, quadrate). Functionally divided into 8 Couinaud segments - each with independent vascular supply, lymphatic drainage, and biliary outflow, making segmental resections possible." />

### Liver - key facts

The liver is the **largest internal organ** (~1.5 kg in adults) and the metabolic command center. It has a **dual blood supply** unique among major organs:
- **Hepatic artery** (~25% of flow, ~50% of oxygen) - branch of celiac trunk
- **Portal vein** (~75% of flow, ~50% of oxygen) - carries nutrient-rich + bacteria-laden blood from the GI tract

Blood from both flows through **sinusoids** lined by Kupffer cells (macrophages) and converges in **central veins** → hepatic veins → IVC.

### Liver lobule architecture

Each lobule is a hexagonal unit centered on a **central vein**. At each corner is a **portal triad** = hepatic artery branch + portal vein branch + bile duct.

Hepatocyte zonation (relative to central vein):
- **Zone 1** (periportal, closest to triad) - most oxygenated; gluconeogenesis, β-oxidation; first hit by viruses + toxins absorbed from GI
- **Zone 2** - intermediate
- **Zone 3** (centrilobular, closest to central vein) - least oxygenated; drug metabolism (CYP450), alcohol; **first to die in ischemic injury** ("shock liver"); target of acetaminophen toxicity

### Liver functions (memorize)

| Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|
| **Synthesis** - albumin, clotting factors (II, VII, IX, X, fibrinogen), most plasma proteins | Liver failure → hypoalbuminemia (edema) + coagulopathy (↑ PT) |
| **Detoxification** - Phase I (CYP450 oxidation) + Phase II (conjugation) | Drug metabolism; bilirubin conjugation |
| **Glucose homeostasis** - glycogenesis, glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis | Fasting hypoglycemia in liver failure |
| **Bile synthesis** - from cholesterol via 7α-hydroxylase | Fat digestion + cholesterol excretion |
| **Urea cycle** | Detoxifies ammonia → urea; failure → hepatic encephalopathy (↑ NH₃) |
| **Storage** - glycogen, vit A/D/E/K/B12, iron, copper | Anemia (B12 def), bleeding (vit K def) |
| **Immune** - Kupffer cells filter portal blood | Block gut microbes from systemic circulation |
