The GI tract is the largest endocrine organ in the body and a chemical-mechanical coordinator: hormones, paracrine signals, neural circuits, and intrinsic pacemakers move ~9 L of fluid through 8 m of tube each day. A handful of **gut hormones** plus the **enteric nervous system** drive everything.

## The gut hormones

<Figure name="digestive/gi-hormones-summary" caption="Summary table of the four classical GI hormones: gastrin, secretin, CCK and GIP, with the source, stimulus, target organ and response of each. The remaining hormones in the table below (motilin, GLP-1, somatostatin, ghrelin, VIP) are not in this figure." />

| Hormone | Source | Released by | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gastrin** | G cells (antrum + duodenum) | Vagal input (GRP), peptides + amino acids in lumen, antral distension | ↑ HCl from parietal cells (via histamine from ECL cells); trophic to gastric mucosa |
| **Cholecystokinin (CCK)** | I cells (duodenum + jejunum) | Fatty acids + amino acids | Gallbladder contraction; sphincter of Oddi relaxation; ↑ pancreatic enzyme secretion; ↓ gastric emptying; satiety signal |
| **Secretin** | S cells (duodenum) | ↑ H⁺ + fatty acids in duodenum | ↑ pancreatic + biliary HCO₃⁻; ↓ gastric acid; ↑ secretion to neutralize acid |
| **GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide)** | K cells (duodenum + jejunum) | Glucose + AA + fat | **Incretin** - ↑ glucose-stimulated insulin release; ↓ gastric acid (minor) |
| **GLP-1** | L cells (ileum + colon) | Nutrients | **Incretin** - ↑ insulin, ↓ glucagon; ↓ gastric emptying; ↑ satiety. Target of semaglutide etc. |
| **Motilin** | M cells (small intestine) | Fasting state | Initiates **migrating motor complexes** (MMC) - sweep undigested residue toward colon every 90 min |
| **Somatostatin** | δ cells (pancreas + GI mucosa) | ↓ pH, ↑ AA + fat | Pan-inhibitor - suppresses gastrin, CCK, secretin, insulin, glucagon. Octreotide = synthetic analog |
| **Ghrelin** | P/D1 cells (stomach fundus) | Fasting + low blood glucose | ↑ appetite; ↑ GH release. "Hunger hormone" - falls after meals |
| **VIP** | Enteric ganglia | Distension, vagal | Smooth muscle relaxation; ↑ intestinal water + electrolyte secretion |

<Mnemonic>
**Two incretins, one brake, one appetite signal.** The **incretins** are **GIP** and **GLP-1** - both released by nutrients in the gut lumen, both amplifying insulin release before glucose even reaches the blood. The **brake** is **somatostatin**, which turns nearly every other GI secretion down. The **appetite signal** is **ghrelin**, the only one of the group that rises with fasting rather than feeding.
</Mnemonic>

## Gastric acid regulation - three phases of digestion

<Figure name="digestive/parietal-cell" caption="Inside the parietal cell: CO₂ + H₂O → carbonic acid → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. HCO₃⁻ leaves basolaterally in exchange for Cl⁻ (the post-meal alkaline tide) while the apical H⁺/K⁺ ATPase - marked here as the step PPIs block - pumps H⁺ into the lumen, with Cl⁻ following. The three receptors that switch the pump on are in the table below." />

| Phase | Trigger | Mechanism | % of total HCl |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cephalic** | Sight/smell/thought/chew of food | Vagal → ACh on parietal M3 + GRP on G cells → gastrin → histamine | 30% |
| **Gastric** | Food in stomach → distension + peptides | Antral G cells fire; vagovagal reflex amplifies | 60% |
| **Intestinal** | Chyme entering duodenum | Initially stimulatory; switches to inhibitory as acid + fat accumulate | 10% |

### Parietal cell - three pathways to acid

Three receptors converge on the **H⁺/K⁺-ATPase (proton pump)** in the canalicular membrane:

| Stimulus | Receptor | Source | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Histamine** | H2 (Gs → cAMP) | ECL cells (driven by gastrin + vagal GRP) | **The strongest single stimulus** - H2-blockers (cimetidine, famotidine) work here |
| **ACh** | M3 (Gq → IP3 → Ca²⁺) | Vagus | Cephalic + gastric phase |
| **Gastrin** | CCK-B (Gq → IP3) | G cells | Mostly indirect - gastrin → ECL → histamine |

**PPIs** (omeprazole, esomeprazole, pantoprazole) irreversibly bind the proton pump itself → block ALL three pathways → most powerful acid suppression.

<Clinical>
**Zollinger-Ellison syndrome** - gastrinoma (often duodenal, sometimes pancreatic; 25% MEN1). Recurrent ulcers + diarrhea (acid inactivates pancreatic enzymes). Fasting gastrin >1000 pg/mL; **secretin stimulation test** - paradoxical rise (normal gastrin falls with secretin).

**Pernicious anemia** - autoantibodies destroy parietal cells → ↓ intrinsic factor → B12 malabsorption. Also achlorhydria → ↑ gastrin (no feedback) → ECL hyperplasia → gastric carcinoid risk.
</Clinical>

## Motility - five movement patterns

<Figure name="digestive/peristalsis" caption="Peristalsis - the circular muscle contracts behind the bolus and relaxes ahead of it (the longitudinal layer does the opposite), a reflex organised by the myenteric (Auerbach) plexus." />


| Pattern | Where | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Peristalsis** | Whole tract | Coordinated contraction proximal + relaxation distal; ENS + vagal modulation |
| **Segmentation** | Small intestine | Stationary mixing contractions; alternating segments squeeze → mix chyme with secretions; main mode during digestion |
| **Mass movements** | Colon | 1-3× per day; large slow waves push contents toward rectum; triggered by gastrocolic reflex (meal) |
| **Tonic contractions** | Sphincters | Sustained closure between events (LES, pyloric, ileocecal, internal anal) |
| **Migrating motor complex (MMC)** | Small intestine | Fasting state; "housekeeper" - sweep residue every 90 min; **motilin-driven** |

### The pacemaker - interstitial cells of Cajal (ICC)
- Network of mesenchymal cells between muscle layers.
- Generate **slow waves** (basal electrical rhythm; 3/min stomach, 12/min duodenum, 8/min ileum, 3/min colon).
- Slow waves are subthreshold by themselves; need neural/hormonal input to bring myocyte voltage above threshold for Ca²⁺-driven contraction.
- Loss → gastroparesis, intestinal pseudo-obstruction.

## Reflexes - neural + hormonal coordination

| Reflex | Trigger | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Gastrocolic** | Stomach distension after meal | ↑ colonic motility → mass movements → urge to defecate |
| **Enterogastric** | Acid / fat in duodenum | ↓ gastric emptying (CCK, secretin, GIP + vagal) |
| **Ileogastric** | Distension of ileum | ↓ gastric motility |
| **Intestino-intestinal** | Local distension/irritation | Relaxation of surrounding bowel; ileus signal |
| **Defecation** | Rectal distension | Internal anal sphincter relaxes (reflex, S2-S4 parasympathetic); external sphincter under voluntary control (somatic, pudendal) |

## Enteric nervous system (ENS) - the "second brain"

<Figure name="digestive/digestive-detail" caption="The enteric nervous system runs the whole length of this tube - from the esophagus to the anal canal - as two plexuses inside the wall: myenteric (motility) and submucosal (secretion)." />

- ~500 million neurons in two plexuses:
  - **Myenteric (Auerbach) plexus** - between longitudinal and circular muscle; **motility**.
  - **Submucosal (Meissner) plexus** - submucosa; **secretion + local blood flow + sensory**.
- Can operate independently of CNS (transected gut still peristalses).
- Modulated by autonomic input:
  - **Parasympathetic** (vagus, pelvic splanchnics): ↑ secretion + motility
  - **Sympathetic** (T5-L2): ↓ motility + sphincter contraction
- Major neurotransmitters: ACh, NE, **VIP, NO, substance P, serotonin (5HT)**.

<Clinical>
**Hirschsprung disease**: failure of neural crest migration → no ganglion cells in distal colon → tonic constriction (no relaxation) → functional obstruction. Failure to pass meconium in 48 h; explosive expulsion on rectal exam.

**Achalasia**: degeneration of LES inhibitory neurons (VIP/NO releasing) → LES can't relax → dysphagia + bird-beak on barium swallow + dilated esophagus. Treat: pneumatic dilation, Heller myotomy, POEM.

**Gastroparesis**: delayed gastric emptying. Common in long-standing diabetes (autonomic neuropathy). Treat: metoclopramide (D2 antagonist + 5HT4 agonist) or erythromycin (motilin agonist).
</Clinical>

## Digestion - what enzymes break down what

<Figure name="digestive/villi-microvilli" caption="Digestion + absorption at the villus. Pancreatic enzymes in lumen (luminal digestion) → brush border enzymes in microvilli (membrane digestion) → enterocyte transporters." />

| Substrate | Enzyme(s) | Final form |
|---|---|---|
| **Starch** | Salivary + pancreatic amylase | Maltose, isomaltose, α-limit dextrin → brush-border maltase/isomaltase → glucose |
| **Sucrose** | Brush-border sucrase | Glucose + fructose |
| **Lactose** | Brush-border lactase | Glucose + galactose |
| **Protein** | Pepsin (stomach, pH 2), trypsin + chymotrypsin + elastase (pancreatic), brush-border peptidases | Amino acids + di/tripeptides |
| **Fat** | Lingual + gastric + pancreatic **lipase**; colipase, bile salts (emulsification) | 2-monoglyceride + fatty acids → enter enterocyte → re-esterified → chylomicron → lymph |
| **Nucleic acids** | Pancreatic + brush-border nucleases | Nucleotides + nucleosides + bases |

## Absorption - site by site

<Figure name="digestive/digestive-detail" caption="Orientation for the table below: duodenum and jejunum in the upper abdomen, ileum ending in the right lower quadrant, and the colon framing them." />

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| **Duodenum** | Iron, calcium, folate |
| **Jejunum** | Most digestion + absorption - sugars, amino acids, fat; most of the ~8 L of water taken up by the small intestine each day |
| **Terminal ileum** | **B12 (with IF)**, **bile salts** (95% reabsorbed for enterohepatic circulation) |
| **Colon** | Reclaims the last ~1.4 L of water and its electrolytes; SCFAs from microbial fermentation; vitamin K from gut flora |

## Function reference - every key regulator

| Component | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Parietal cell H⁺/K⁺-ATPase** | Final common pathway for HCl | Target of PPIs (most powerful acid suppression) |
| **G cell** | Gastrin secretion | Source of ZES; hyperplasia in PPI use, pernicious anemia |
| **ECL cell** | Histamine release → stimulates parietal H2 receptor | Hyperplasia in chronic hypergastrinemia → gastric carcinoid risk |
| **Chief cell** | Pepsinogen secretion | Activates to pepsin at pH &lt;3 (stomach acid required) |
| **D cell** | Somatostatin - local brake | Octreotide for variceal bleeding, carcinoid, VIPoma, acromegaly |
| **I cell (CCK)** | Times gallbladder emptying and pancreatic enzyme release to the arrival of fat and protein | CCK signalling survives cholecystectomy - it still relaxes the sphincter of Oddi; bile simply dribbles in continuously instead of arriving as a bolus |
| **Interstitial cells of Cajal** | GI pacemaker (slow waves) | Loss → gastroparesis, slow transit constipation |
| **Myenteric plexus** | Motility coordination | Absent in Hirschsprung (distal colon); damaged by Chagas (esophagus + colon) |
| **Submucosal plexus** | Glandular secretion, local blood flow, sensing the lumen | Drives the massive fluid secretion of cholera and VIPoma; sits in the submucosa, so it is spared by purely mucosal disease |
| **Migrating motor complex** | Inter-meal "housekeeper" | Disrupted in SIBO (small intestinal bacterial overgrowth) |
| **Vagus nerve** | Parasympathetic to all foregut + midgut | Vagotomy (rare now) eliminates cephalic phase; gastric emptying complications |
| **Pancreatic acinar cell** | Produces digestive enzymes in inactive form (zymogens) | Activation inside pancreas = pancreatitis |
| **Brush-border peptidases + disaccharidases** | Final-step digestion | Lactase deficiency → osmotic diarrhea; loss in mucosal injury (celiac, infection) |
| **Bile salts** | Emulsify fats; carrier of fat-soluble vitamins | Terminal ileum resection or Crohn → fat malabsorption, ADEK deficiency |

## Why this matters

<Figure name="digestive/gi-hormones-summary" caption="The four hormones every exam expects you to place: gastrin from the stomach, secretin and CCK from the duodenum, GIP from the upper small intestine." />


<HighYield exam="boards">
- **PPI complications**: ↓ Mg, ↓ B12, ↑ C. diff risk, ↑ pneumonia risk, ↑ hip fracture (osteoporosis). Use lowest effective dose for shortest duration.
- **Carcinoid syndrome**: serotonin-secreting tumor (often small bowel). Triad: flushing + diarrhea + wheezing. Right heart fibrosis (tricuspid/pulmonic). 5-HIAA urinary. Treat: octreotide.
- **Diabetic gastroparesis**: vagal neuropathy → ↓ MMC + slow emptying. N/V, early satiety, glucose fluctuations. Metoclopramide (D2 block + 5HT4 agonist), erythromycin acutely.
- **GLP-1 agonists** (semaglutide, liraglutide, tirzepatide): glucose + weight loss + CV benefit. Mechanism harnesses natural incretin. Side effects: GI (n/v, slow emptying), rare pancreatitis.
- **Vagotomy** (highly selective vs truncal): historically for ulcers; cuts cephalic-phase acid output. Selective preserves pyloric function.
- **Bariatric surgery** mechanisms: roux-en-Y bypass alters incretin profile (↑ GLP-1) - most of the weight loss isn't mechanical, it's hormonal.
- **Pancreatitis**: premature trypsinogen activation in acinar cells. Causes: **I GET SMASHED** - Idiopathic, Gallstones, Ethanol, Trauma, Steroids, Mumps, Autoimmune, Scorpion, Hypercalcemia/Hypertriglyceridemia, ERCP, Drugs.
- **VIPoma (Verner-Morrison)**: pancreatic VIP-secreting tumor → **WDHA** - Watery Diarrhea, Hypokalemia, Achlorhydria. Treat: octreotide + surgical resection.
- **Celiac**: villous atrophy → loss of brush border → secondary lactase + disaccharidase deficiency. Anti-TTG IgA serology. HLA-DQ2/DQ8.
</HighYield>
