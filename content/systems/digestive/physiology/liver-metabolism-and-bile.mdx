The liver is the body's central metabolic factory, detoxification plant, and bile producer. It receives **75% of its blood from the portal vein** (nutrient-rich, deoxygenated, from gut) and **25% from the hepatic artery** (oxygenated). Every absorbed nutrient passes through the liver first - the "**first pass effect**." Almost no drug or hormone escapes hepatic scrutiny.

<Figure name="digestive/liver" caption="The liver - largest internal organ, four lobes (right, left, caudate, quadrate). Dual blood supply: hepatic artery (oxygenated, 25%) and portal vein (nutrient-rich, 75%). Drains via hepatic veins into IVC. Bile leaves via the common hepatic duct; CBD merges with pancreatic duct at the ampulla of Vater." />

## Liver functions in one table

| Category | Functions |
|---|---|
| **Carbohydrate metabolism** | Gluconeogenesis (fasted state), glycogenolysis, glycogenesis, conversion of fructose/galactose to glucose, lactate clearance (Cori cycle) |
| **Protein metabolism** | Plasma protein synthesis (albumin, all coagulation factors except VIII, transferrin, ceruloplasmin, alpha-1 antitrypsin, complement, transthyretin), deamination of amino acids, **urea cycle**, gluconeogenesis from amino acids |
| **Lipid metabolism** | Cholesterol synthesis, bile acid synthesis, VLDL secretion, ketogenesis, fatty acid oxidation, lipoprotein clearance |
| **Bile production** | Bile salts, bilirubin conjugation + excretion, phospholipids, cholesterol |
| **Detoxification** | Phase I (CYP450 oxidation) + Phase II (conjugation with glucuronic acid, sulfate, glycine, glutathione) - then biliary or urinary excretion |
| **Storage** | Glycogen, fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), B12, iron (as ferritin), copper |
| **Hormone metabolism** | Inactivation of insulin, glucagon, thyroid hormones (T4→T3), steroid hormones; production of IGF-1 in response to GH |
| **Immune** | Kupffer cells (resident macrophages) phagocytose gut-derived bacteria and old RBCs |

## Bile - composition and function

<Figure name="digestive/gallbladder" caption="Gallbladder + biliary tree. Stores and concentrates bile 5-10×. CCK from duodenal I cells triggers contraction + sphincter of Oddi relaxation after a fatty meal." />

<Figure name="digestive/enterohepatic-circulation" caption="Enterohepatic circulation of bile salts - liver secretes bile → duodenum/jejunum emulsifies fat → 95% reabsorbed in the terminal ileum (ASBT) → portal vein → liver. Each bile salt recycles 6-8× per meal (Wikimedia)." />

**Bile** is an aqueous mixture made by hepatocytes (~600 mL/day) and modified by cholangiocytes. Stored and concentrated 5-10× in the gallbladder. Released after meals when **CCK** from duodenal I cells triggers gallbladder contraction + sphincter of Oddi relaxation.

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| **Bile salts (acids)** | Emulsify dietary fat, solubilize cholesterol; allow lipase access; form micelles for fat absorption |
| **Bilirubin (conjugated)** | Excretion of heme breakdown product |
| **Phospholipids (lecithin)** | Co-emulsifier; keeps cholesterol soluble |
| **Cholesterol** | Excretion route; precipitates if bile is supersaturated → cholesterol gallstones |
| **Water + electrolytes** | Bicarbonate-rich (cholangiocyte secretion); neutralizes duodenal acid |
| **IgA, drugs, metals** | Excreted via bile |

### Bile salt enterohepatic circulation

Bile salts are conjugated with glycine or taurine in the liver → secreted into bile → emulsify fats in the duodenum/jejunum → **95% reabsorbed in the terminal ileum** by the ASBT transporter → returned to the liver via portal vein → re-secreted. Each bile salt cycles 6-8× per meal.

The 5% lost in feces is replaced by new synthesis. Total pool ~3-4 g; total turnover ~25-30 g/day.

<Clinical>
**Clinical correlates:**
- **Ileal resection / Crohn disease** → bile salt malabsorption → fat malabsorption (steatorrhea), fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, oxalate kidney stones (fat binds Ca²⁺ in gut, leaving oxalate free to absorb).
- **Cholestyramine** binds bile salts in the gut → interrupts enterohepatic recycling → liver pulls cholesterol to make new bile salts → ↓ serum LDL.
- **Gallstones** - cholesterol (most common in Western countries, 80%); pigment (chronic hemolysis, cirrhosis). Risk factors: female, fertile, fat, forty, fair, family history.
</Clinical>

## Bilirubin metabolism

<Figure name="digestive/bilirubin-metabolism" caption="Bilirubin metabolism - heme breakdown in macrophages → biliverdin → unconjugated bilirubin (albumin-bound, water-insoluble) → liver UGT1A1 conjugates with glucuronic acid → water-soluble conjugated bilirubin → bile → gut bacteria → urobilinogen → stercobilin (stool) + urobilin (urine) (Wikimedia)." />

Bilirubin is the yellow product of heme catabolism. Pathway:

1. **Hb breakdown** in macrophages (spleen, liver Kupffer cells) → heme + globin (recycled).
2. **Heme oxygenase** opens the porphyrin ring → biliverdin + Fe²⁺ + CO. (CO is the only physiologic source; explains why HbCO has a baseline.)
3. **Biliverdin reductase** → **unconjugated bilirubin** (lipid-soluble, toxic, binds albumin for transport).
4. Liver takes up unconjugated bilirubin via OATP transporter.
5. **UDP-glucuronosyltransferase (UGT1A1)** conjugates bilirubin with 2 glucuronic acids → **conjugated bilirubin** (water-soluble).
6. Conjugated bilirubin is secreted into bile via **MRP2** transporter.
7. Gut bacteria deconjugate → urobilinogen → most converted to stercobilin (brown stool color), some reabsorbed and excreted in urine as urobilin (yellow urine color).

### Jaundice patterns

| Pattern | Unconjugated | Conjugated | Urine bilirubin | Causes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-hepatic / hemolytic** | ↑↑ | Normal | Absent (unconj is albumin-bound, can't filter) | Hemolysis, ineffective erythropoiesis |
| **Hepatic** | ↑ | ↑ | Present | Viral hepatitis, alcohol, cirrhosis, drug injury |
| **Post-hepatic / obstructive** | Normal-↑ | ↑↑ | Present (dark "tea-colored") | CBD stone, pancreatic head tumor, PSC, PBC |
| **Inherited - Gilbert** | ↑ (mild, fasting/illness) | Normal | Absent | UGT1A1 reduced activity; benign |
| **Inherited - Crigler-Najjar I** | ↑↑↑ | Normal | Absent | UGT1A1 absent; kernicterus; lethal without phototherapy/transplant |
| **Inherited - Dubin-Johnson** | Normal | ↑ | Present | MRP2 defect; black liver; benign |
| **Inherited - Rotor** | Normal | ↑ | Present | Defective hepatic uptake/storage; benign |

## Hepatic detoxification - phases I and II

| Phase | What it does | Enzyme family |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase I** | Oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis - introduces a reactive group (usually -OH) | **Cytochrome P450 (CYP)** - especially CYP3A4 (~50% of drugs), CYP2D6, CYP2C9, CYP1A2 |
| **Phase II** | Conjugation with glucuronic acid (UGT), sulfate, acetyl (NAT), methyl, glutathione (GST), or glycine - makes water-soluble | UGTs, GSTs, NATs, SULTs |
| **Phase III** | Active transport into bile/urine | MRP, MDR transporters |

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**CYP450 essentials:**
- **CYP3A4** metabolizes ~50% of clinical drugs. Inhibited by grapefruit juice → drug toxicity.
- **Inducers** (lower drug levels): rifampin, phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, St. John's wort, chronic alcohol, smoking
- **Inhibitors** (raise drug levels): macrolides (erythro, clarithro), azoles, protease inhibitors, grapefruit, acute alcohol, cimetidine, valproate, amiodarone, fluoxetine
- **CYP2D6** has slow/fast metabolizer polymorphisms - matters for codeine (→ morphine activation), tramadol, tamoxifen
- **Warfarin** is metabolized by CYP2C9; **clopidogrel** is *activated* by CYP2C19 (PPIs that inhibit 2C19 may blunt clopidogrel)
</HighYield>

### Acetaminophen and glutathione - the classic case

At therapeutic doses, most acetaminophen is conjugated with glucuronide or sulfate (safe). A small fraction goes through CYP2E1 → **NAPQI**, a toxic electrophile that is neutralized by glutathione conjugation.

In **overdose** (or chronic alcohol, fasting), NAPQI production rises and glutathione is depleted → NAPQI binds hepatocyte proteins → **centrilobular necrosis**.

**N-acetylcysteine (NAC)** restores glutathione → first-line antidote within ~8-10 hours (still useful up to 24 h). Use the Rumack-Matthew nomogram to decide if treatment is needed.

## Energy state - the fed vs fasted liver

<Figure name="digestive/pancreas-exo-endo" caption="Pancreas exocrine + endocrine - the liver's metabolic partner. Insulin (β cell, fed) and glucagon (α cell, fasting) reciprocally control hepatic glycogen synthesis/breakdown and gluconeogenesis." />

The liver's metabolic mode flips between two states based on the insulin:glucagon ratio:

| State | Insulin | Glucagon | Liver does… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fed** (postprandial, hours 0-4) | ↑ | ↓ | Stores glucose as glycogen; makes fat; ↑ protein synthesis; ↓ gluconeogenesis |
| **Fasting** (hours 4-18) | ↓ | ↑ | Breaks down glycogen (glycogenolysis); makes glucose from lactate/AAs/glycerol (gluconeogenesis) |
| **Starvation** (days 1-3) | ↓↓ | ↑↑ | Gluconeogenesis from protein; **ketogenesis** ramps up (β-hydroxybutyrate, acetoacetate fuel brain) |
| **Prolonged starvation** (week+) | ↓↓ | ↑↑ | Ketones become primary brain fuel; protein sparing; lipolysis dominates |

**Glycogen stores last ~24 hours.** Beyond that, the body must do gluconeogenesis (~80 g/day for the brain) - it spares muscle protein by switching the brain to ketones.

## Liver function tests - what they actually mean

<Figure name="digestive/biliary-tree" caption="Biliary tree - the anatomical context for LFTs. ALP, GGT, conjugated bilirubin rise in cholestatic processes (obstruction at any point); ALT/AST rise with hepatocyte injury upstream." />

| Test | Reflects |
|---|---|
| **ALT, AST** | Hepatocyte injury (cytoplasmic enzymes leaking into blood). AST > ALT 2:1 in alcoholic liver disease. ALT > AST in viral hepatitis, NAFLD |
| **Alkaline phosphatase (ALP)** | Cholestasis - bile duct epithelium and bone. GGT or 5'-NT helps distinguish liver from bone |
| **GGT** | Cholestasis, biliary disease, alcohol; very sensitive but nonspecific |
| **Bilirubin total / direct (conjugated) / indirect (unconjugated)** | Conjugation, transport, excretion |
| **Albumin** | Synthetic function (chronic - half-life ~20 days) |
| **PT / INR** | Synthetic function (acute - factor VII has 6-hour half-life) |
| **Ammonia** | Urea cycle failure → hepatic encephalopathy |

**True "liver function" tests** are albumin and INR (synthetic capacity). ALT/AST/ALP/GGT are *injury* markers. A patient can have very high ALT in acute hepatitis A but normal albumin and INR (liver function preserved).

## Cirrhosis - what fails

Chronic liver injury → fibrosis → cirrhosis → portal hypertension and synthetic failure.

| Complication | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Esophageal varices** | Portal HTN → portosystemic collateral at lower esophagus; rupture → massive UGI bleed |
| **Ascites** | Portal HTN + hypoalbuminemia + splanchnic vasodilation → renal Na⁺ retention |
| **Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP)** | Translocation of gut bacteria into ascites; **PMN > 250/mm³** is diagnostic |
| **Hepatic encephalopathy** | Failure to clear ammonia → cerebral edema, asterixis, confusion. Treat with lactulose + rifaximin |
| **Hepatorenal syndrome** | Splanchnic vasodilation → renal vasoconstriction → AKI without intrinsic renal damage |
| **Hepatopulmonary syndrome** | Pulmonary vasodilation → V/Q mismatch → hypoxia (platypnea-orthodeoxia) |
| **Coagulopathy** | ↓ factor synthesis (↑ INR), ↓ thrombopoietin (↓ platelets), splenic sequestration |
| **Estrogen excess** | Liver can't degrade estrogens → gynecomastia, spider angiomata, palmar erythema, testicular atrophy |

<HighYield exam="step2">
**Decompensated cirrhosis red flags:**
- New ascites → diagnostic paracentesis to rule out SBP
- New AMS → check ammonia, exclude SBP, give lactulose
- Variceal bleed → IV octreotide, ceftriaxone (prophylactic), endoscopic banding within 12 h
- HRS → midodrine + octreotide + albumin or terlipressin; transplant is definitive
- HCC screening - ultrasound + AFP every 6 months in cirrhotic patients
</HighYield>
