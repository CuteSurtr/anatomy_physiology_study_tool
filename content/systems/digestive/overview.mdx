The **alimentary canal** is a continuous tube: mouth → pharynx → esophagus → stomach → small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) → large intestine (cecum, colon, rectum, anal canal). The **accessory organs** sit outside the tube but empty secretions into it: salivary glands, liver (via gallbladder), and pancreas. Six core processes: ingestion, propulsion, mechanical digestion, chemical digestion, absorption, defecation.

## Mouth, saliva, and swallowing

<Figure name="digestive/salivary-glands" caption="The three pairs of major salivary glands: parotid (in front of and below the ear, duct crossing the cheek to the mouth), submandibular (below the body of the mandible), and sublingual (in the floor of the mouth)." />

The salivary glands produce **1-1.5 L of saliva a day**. Saliva is mostly water and mucin, plus bicarbonate (buffers acid), lysozyme and IgA (antimicrobial), **salivary alpha-amylase** (starts starch digestion) and a little **lingual lipase**. The **parotid** secretion is watery and enzyme-rich; the **submandibular** is mixed and supplies most saliva at rest; the **sublingual** is mostly mucus.

Salivary secretion is driven almost entirely by the **parasympathetic** system - CN VII (chorda tympani) to the submandibular and sublingual glands, CN IX to the parotid. Sympathetic activity leaves saliva scant and thick, which is why a dry mouth accompanies fear and anticholinergic drugs.

**Swallowing (deglutition)** runs in three phases: **voluntary (buccal)** - the tongue pushes the bolus into the oropharynx; **pharyngeal** - involuntary, the soft palate seals the nasopharynx, the larynx rises so the epiglottis covers it, and the upper esophageal sphincter opens; **esophageal** - a peristaltic wave carries the bolus down while the lower esophageal sphincter relaxes to admit it.

## The wall: the same four layers all the way down

From the esophagus to the anal canal the tube is built of four **tunics**. Read them from the lumen outward - almost every GI disease is easier to place once you can name the layer it damages.

| Layer | Contains | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Mucosa** | Epithelium + lamina propria (loose connective tissue, vessels, MALT) + **muscularis mucosae** (thin smooth muscle) | Secretion and absorption; the surface that faces food. Innermost layer |
| **Submucosa** | Dense connective tissue, larger vessels + lymphatics, **submucosal (Meissner) plexus** | Supplies the mucosa; the plexus controls secretion and local blood flow |
| **Muscularis externa** | Inner **circular** and outer **longitudinal** smooth muscle with the **myenteric (Auerbach) plexus** between them | Mixing and propulsion (peristalsis, segmentation); thickens into sphincters |
| **Serosa** (or **adventitia**) | Visceral peritoneum: thin connective tissue + mesothelium | Slippery covering for intraperitoneal organs. Retroperitoneal parts (most of the duodenum, ascending and descending colon) and the thoracic esophagus have fibrous **adventitia** instead, which binds them to the wall |

Regional variations on that plan: the esophagus and the anal canal below the pectinate line are lined by **stratified squamous** epithelium (abrasion) while the stomach and intestines are **simple columnar** (secretion and absorption); the stomach adds a third, **oblique** muscle layer for churning; the colon's longitudinal layer is condensed into three **teniae coli**.
