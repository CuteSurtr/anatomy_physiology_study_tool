The upper limb skeleton is built for **mobility over stability**, sacrificing structural support for a wide range of motion. It articulates with the axial skeleton only at the sternoclavicular joint - the rest is held on by muscle alone. **30 bones per limb**: clavicle + scapula + humerus + radius/ulna + 8 carpals + 5 metacarpals + 14 phalanges.

<Figure name="skeletal/upper-limb-hand-slide" caption="Lecture slide labeling the carpal bones, metacarpals, and phalanges of the hand." />

The lecture sequence moves from the **mobile shoulder girdle** down to the **precision joints of the hand**, which is the easiest way to remember the upper limb: proximal bones maximize reach, distal bones maximize manipulation.

## Pectoral girdle

<Figure name="skeletal/scapula-detail" caption="Scapula in anterior, posterior, and lateral views. Landmarks: glenoid cavity (articulates with humeral head), acromion (acromioclavicular joint with clavicle), coracoid process, scapular spine, supraspinous + infraspinous fossae, subscapular fossa." />

| Bone | Function | Clinical notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Clavicle** | Only bony link between upper limb + axial skeleton; strut keeping arm laterally; first bone to ossify (5th week); last to fuse (~25) | Most commonly fractured bone in body; midshaft = 80% of fractures; supraclavicular nerves often injured → sensory loss over the shoulder; rarely heals nonunion |
| **Scapula** | Floats on thoracic wall; provides glenoid for humerus + acromion for AC joint | Winging of scapula = long thoracic nerve (C5–C7) injury → serratus anterior palsy |

## Humerus

<Figure name="skeletal/humerus-elbow" caption="Humerus and its articulation at the elbow. Proximal humerus: head, greater and lesser tubercles, intertubercular sulcus, anatomical + surgical necks. Distal: trochlea (articulates with ulna), capitulum (articulates with radius), medial + lateral epicondyles, olecranon and coronoid fossae." />

- **Proximal**: head, anatomical neck (anchors capsule), surgical neck (common fracture site - **axillary nerve at risk**).
- **Greater + lesser tubercles**: muscle attachments (rotator cuff).
- **Bicipital (intertubercular) groove**: long head of biceps tendon.
- **Deltoid tuberosity**: deltoid insertion.
- **Spiral (radial) groove**: posterior shaft - **radial nerve** + profunda brachii artery (vulnerable in midshaft fracture).
- **Distal**: capitulum (radial head articulates), trochlea (ulnar trochlear notch articulates).
- **Medial epicondyle**: flexor origin + **ulnar nerve** runs behind ("funny bone").
- **Lateral epicondyle**: extensor origin.

### Humerus fractures - nerve at risk by location
| Fracture site | Nerve at risk | Deficit |
|---|---|---|
| **Surgical neck** | **Axillary** (C5–C6) | Deltoid weakness + lateral upper-arm sensory loss ("regimental badge") |
| **Midshaft / spiral groove** | **Radial** (C5–T1) | **Wrist drop**, weak elbow extension, sensory loss over dorsum of 1st web space |
| **Supracondylar** (children) | **Median** or **brachial artery** | Volkmann ischemic contracture; "ape hand" if median (lateral 2 lumbricals + thenar) |
| **Medial epicondyle** | **Ulnar** | "Claw hand" of medial 2 digits (4th + 5th) + loss of hypothenar |

<Mnemonic>
**"ARM"** - Axillary at the surgical neck, Radial in the radial groove, Median at supracondylar, Ulnar at medial epicondyle. Each fracture matches its named nerve.
</Mnemonic>

## Forearm - radius and ulna

<Figure name="skeletal/radius-ulna" caption="Radius (lateral) and ulna (medial) of the forearm. Ulna: olecranon, trochlear notch, coronoid + radial notches, head + styloid process distally. Radius: head + radial tuberosity proximally, styloid process distally. Interosseous membrane spans between them." />

| Bone | Side | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Radius** | Lateral (thumb side) | Pronates + supinates; carries weight to wrist (radiocarpal joint) |
| **Ulna** | Medial | Forms elbow (trochlear notch + olecranon); not at wrist |

- **Pronation/supination**: radius rotates over ulna; pivot at proximal radioulnar joint (annular ligament) + distal radioulnar joint + interosseous membrane.

### Common forearm fractures
| Pattern | Mechanism | Special name |
|---|---|---|
| **Distal radius (extra-articular)** with dorsal displacement | FOOSH in elderly | **Colles fracture** ("dinner fork" deformity) |
| **Distal radius** with volar displacement | Fall on flexed wrist | **Smith fracture** ("garden spade") |
| **Scaphoid** waist fracture | FOOSH | **Anatomic snuffbox tenderness**; AVN risk (retrograde blood supply); often missed on initial X-ray |
| **Radial head** | Falling on outstretched hand | Sail sign on lateral elbow X-ray (anterior fat pad displaced) |
| **Monteggia** | Proximal ulna fracture + radial head dislocation | "MUGR" = Monteggia Ulna, Galeazzi Radius |
| **Galeazzi** | Distal radius fracture + distal radioulnar dislocation | (see mnemonic above) |
| **Nightstick** | Isolated ulna shaft | Direct blow defending head |

## Wrist - 8 carpal bones in 2 rows

**Proximal row** (lateral → medial): **S**caphoid, **L**unate, **T**riquetrum, **P**isiform.
**Distal row** (lateral → medial): **T**rapezium, **T**rapezoid, **C**apitate, **H**amate.

<Mnemonic>
**"Some Lovers Try Positions That They Can't Handle"** - Scaphoid, Lunate, Triquetrum, Pisiform, Trapezium, Trapezoid, Capitate, Hamate.
</Mnemonic>

| Carpal | Clinical notes |
|---|---|
| **Scaphoid** | Most fractured carpal; **AVN of proximal pole** common (retrograde flow from distal vessels); snuffbox tenderness |
| **Lunate** | Most commonly dislocated; can cause **acute carpal tunnel syndrome** (median n. compression); "PILED PIE" on lateral X-ray |
| **Hamate** (hook) | Hook of hamate fracture in golf/baseball/tennis; ulnar nerve in Guyon's canal at risk |
| **Pisiform** | Sesamoid in flexor carpi ulnaris tendon |

## Carpal tunnel

<Figure name="skeletal/carpal-tunnel" caption="Carpal tunnel - cross-section. The flexor retinaculum (transverse carpal ligament) forms a fibrous roof over the carpal bones. The tunnel transmits the median nerve plus 9 flexor tendons (4 FDS, 4 FDP, 1 FPL). Median nerve compression here = carpal tunnel syndrome." />

- **Floor**: carpal bones.
- **Roof**: transverse carpal ligament (flexor retinaculum).
- **Contents**: 4 tendons of flexor digitorum superficialis, 4 of flexor digitorum profundus, 1 of flexor pollicis longus + **median nerve**.
- **Ulnar nerve + artery** pass through **Guyon's canal**, NOT carpal tunnel.

### Carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS)
- **Pathology**: median n. compression in carpal tunnel.
- **Symptoms**: pain, paresthesia in **lateral 3.5 digits** (thumb, index, middle, lateral half of ring); worse at night ("shake out the hand"); thenar atrophy late.
- **Causes**: idiopathic (women > men, 30–60), pregnancy, hypothyroidism, RA, diabetes, amyloidosis, acromegaly, repetitive use.
- **Tests**: Tinel (tap over nerve → tingling), Phalen (flexed wrists 60s → tingling).
- **Treatment**: night splint + NSAIDs → steroid injection → surgical release.

**Sparing**: palmar cutaneous branch of median n. (innervates skin over thenar eminence) leaves above the carpal tunnel → **thenar sensation preserved** in CTS but thumb opposition affected. This is a board favorite.

## Hand - intrinsic muscles + key dermatomes

### Median nerve hand muscles - **"LOAF"**
- **L**umbricals 1 + 2 (lateral)
- **O**pponens pollicis
- **A**bductor pollicis brevis
- **F**lexor pollicis brevis (superficial head)

### Ulnar nerve hand muscles - everything else
- Hypothenar group (opponens digiti minimi, abductor digiti minimi, flexor digiti minimi)
- Adductor pollicis
- Lumbricals 3 + 4
- Dorsal + palmar interossei

### Sensory territories
| Nerve | Skin |
|---|---|
| **Radial** | Dorsum of hand, lateral half (especially **dorsal 1st web space** - pure radial) |
| **Median** | Palmar lateral 3.5 fingers + fingertips dorsally |
| **Ulnar** | Medial 1.5 fingers (palmar + dorsal) |

## Brachial plexus injuries

The brachial plexus = ventral rami C5–T1. Branches at 5 levels: **Roots → Trunks → Divisions → Cords → Branches**. Mnemonic: **"Randy Travis Drinks Cold Beer"**.

| Injury | Mechanism | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Erb's palsy (C5–C6)** | Birth (shoulder dystocia) + motorcycle accidents | **"Waiter's tip"** posture: arm adducted, internally rotated, elbow extended, forearm pronated, wrist flexed |
| **Klumpke's palsy (C8–T1)** | Sudden upward pull (catching tree branch in a fall) | **Claw hand** + Horner's (T1 sympathetic) |
| **Thoracic outlet syndrome** | Compression at scalenes or cervical rib | Variable: vascular (subclavian a/v) + neurogenic (lower trunk C8–T1) |
| **Stinger / burner** | Football lateral neck flexion | Transient unilateral arm pain + paresthesia |
| **Long thoracic nerve (C5–C7)** | Iatrogenic (axillary node dissection), trauma | Winged scapula (serratus anterior palsy) |

## Function reference - every clinically critical structure

| Structure | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Clavicle** | Strut + protect brachial plexus + subclavian vessels | Fracture → first rib fracture indicates high-energy trauma + suspect great vessel injury |
| **Acromion + AC joint** | Roof for rotator cuff | Subacromial impingement; AC separation in football tackles |
| **Coracoid process** | Origin for short head biceps + coracobrachialis; pectoralis minor inserts here | Reference landmark in brachial plexus + axillary surgery |
| **Glenoid labrum** | Deepens shoulder socket | SLAP tear (superior labrum anterior-posterior), Bankart lesion in anterior dislocation |
| **Spiral groove** | Houses radial n. + profunda brachii a. | Midshaft humerus fracture → wrist drop |
| **Cubital tunnel** | Where ulnar n. passes behind medial epicondyle | "Tardy" ulnar palsy in old elbow fractures |
| **Interosseous membrane** | Force transmission radius ↔ ulna | Failure of force transfer in distal radius fracture → wrist pain |
| **Scaphoid** | Most-fractured carpal; retrograde blood supply | AVN of proximal pole; cast on suspicion even if X-ray negative |
| **Transverse carpal ligament** | Roof of carpal tunnel | Release in CTS surgery |
| **Guyon's canal** | Ulnar n. + a. between pisiform + hook of hamate | Cyclist's palsy; hamate fracture → ulnar n. injury |
| **Recurrent branch of median n.** | Motor to thenar (LOAF muscles) | Most superficial part of median n. → easily injured in shallow lacerations of thenar |
| **Snuffbox** | Bounded by extensor pollicis brevis + abductor pollicis longus laterally + extensor pollicis longus medially; contains scaphoid + radial a. | Tenderness = scaphoid fracture |

## Why this matters

<HighYield variant="boards">
- **Wrist drop = radial nerve**. "Saturday night palsy" = sleeping with arm over chair → compression at spiral groove.
- **Hand of benediction** (can't flex 2nd + 3rd) when asking patient to make a fist = high (proximal) median nerve lesion at elbow.
- **"OK sign" loss** (can't pinch tip-to-tip thumb + index) = **anterior interosseous nerve** (branch of median) injury.
- **Claw hand** (4th + 5th extended at MCP, flexed at IPs) = **ulnar nerve** injury at wrist.
- **"Ape hand"** (loss of thumb opposition) = **median nerve** injury at wrist (thenar atrophy).
- **Erb's palsy** = upper trunk (C5–C6) → waiter's tip; **Klumpke's** = lower trunk (C8–T1) → claw hand + Horner.
- **Lateral epicondylitis** ("tennis elbow") = extensor tendinopathy at common extensor origin; medial ("golfer's elbow") = flexor.
- **De Quervain tenosynovitis** - inflammation of APL + EPB tendons in 1st dorsal compartment of wrist; **Finkelstein test**.
- **Trigger finger** - stenosing tenosynovitis at A1 pulley → locking flexion.
- **Mallet finger** = forced flexion of extended DIP → avulsion of terminal extensor tendon.
- **Boxer's fracture** - 5th metacarpal neck fracture; punching injury.
</HighYield>
