The adult vertebral column has **33 vertebrae** in five regions, reducing to ~26 functional units once sacral and coccygeal fusion is counted.

<Figure name="skeletal/vertebral-column" caption="Vertebral column - anterior, posterior, and lateral views. The four alternating curvatures (cervical lordosis, thoracic kyphosis, lumbar lordosis, sacral kyphosis) are most obvious in the lateral view." />

<Figure name="skeletal/vertebra" caption="Three representative vertebra types in superior + lateral views: cervical (small body, transverse foramen for vertebral artery), thoracic (heart-shaped body with costal facets), lumbar (large weight-bearing body)." />

| Region | Count | Notable features |
|---|---|---|
| Cervical (C1–C7) | 7 | C1 (atlas - no body), C2 (axis - dens), C7 (vertebra prominens) |
| Thoracic (T1–T12) | 12 | Costal facets articulate with ribs |
| Lumbar (L1–L5) | 5 | Largest bodies; bear most weight |
| Sacral | 5 (fused) | Sacrum - articulates with iliac bones at SI joints |
| Coccygeal | 4 (fused) | Tailbone |

<Figure name="skeletal/cervical-vertebrae" caption="Cervical vertebrae C1-C7. The atlas (C1) is ring-shaped without a body and supports the skull; the axis (C2) has the dens that the atlas rotates around. C3-C6 are typical with bifid spinous processes and transverse foramina for the vertebral arteries; C7 is the vertebra prominens (long spinous process you can feel)." />

<Figure name="skeletal/lumbar-vertebrae" caption="Articulated lumbar vertebrae (lateral view) - the heaviest, most weight-bearing vertebrae with massive bodies, short pedicles, hatchet-shaped spinous processes, and the intervertebral discs between bodies. <Figure name="skeletal/sacrum-coccyx" caption="Sacrum (5 fused sacral vertebrae) and coccyx (4 small fused coccygeal vertebrae). Sacrum features: median + lateral sacral crests, anterior + posterior sacral foramina, sacral canal, sacral hiatus, ala, promontory. Articulates with the iliac bones at the sacroiliac joints." />

## Curvatures

Four normal curvatures alternate front-to-back: **cervical lordosis, thoracic kyphosis, lumbar lordosis, sacral kyphosis**. The thoracic and sacral curves are **primary** (present in the fetus); cervical and lumbar are **secondary** (develop when the infant lifts the head, then stands).

<Mnemonic>
Vertebrae per region: **7, 12, 5, 5, 4** - *breakfast at 7, lunch at 12, dinner at 5* (and the last two are dessert and a nightcap).
</Mnemonic>

<Clinical>
**Lumbar disc herniation** most commonly affects L4–L5 or L5–S1. A posterolateral herniation compresses the **traversing** nerve root, not the exiting one - at L4–L5, that's the L5 root, producing weak dorsiflexion (foot drop) and numbness over the dorsum of the foot.
</Clinical>

## Every vertebral structure - what it does and why it matters

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Vertebral body** | Cylinder of cancellous bone bearing axial load | Bears 80% of compressive load; collapses in osteoporotic compression fractures |
| **Pedicles** | Short stout pillars from body to transverse process | Connect anterior body to posterior arch; surgical anchor for pedicle screws |
| **Laminae** | Flat plates from transverse to spinous process | Roof the vertebral canal; removed in laminectomy to decompress cord |
| **Spinous process** | Posterior projection at the midline | Site of supraspinous + interspinous ligaments; palpable landmark (C7 = vertebra prominens) |
| **Transverse processes** | Lateral projections | Attachment for deep back muscles; ribs articulate at thoracic levels |
| **Articular processes (superior/inferior)** | Form facet (zygapophyseal) joints with adjacent vertebrae | Define spinal motion direction at each level; osteoarthritis here → facet pain |
| **Vertebral foramen** | Central hole through each vertebra | Collectively forms the vertebral canal that houses spinal cord and cauda equina |
| **Intervertebral foramen** | Lateral opening between adjacent vertebrae | Exit point for spinal nerve roots; narrowed in foraminal stenosis → radiculopathy |
| **Atlas (C1)** | Ring-shaped, no body, no spinous process | Articulates with occipital condyles (yes-nodding); cradles dens |
| **Axis (C2)** | Has the **dens (odontoid)** projecting upward | Allows head rotation around dens; dens fracture is dangerous (high cervical) |
| **Dens (odontoid)** | Peg of C2 inserting into C1 | Held by transverse ligament; rupture → C1-C2 dislocation, cord injury risk (RA, Down syndrome) |
| **Vertebra prominens (C7)** | Largest cervical spinous process | Surface landmark for counting vertebrae |
| **Costal facets (thoracic vertebrae)** | Articular surfaces on body + transverse process | Articulate with rib heads and tubercles - define thoracic cage motion |
| **Lumbar vertebrae bodies** | Largest, kidney-shaped | Bear most weight; site of most disc herniations (L4–L5, L5–S1) |
| **Sacrum (S1–S5 fused)** | Wedge transmitting weight to pelvis | Anchors sacroiliac joints; site of caudal anesthesia (sacral hiatus) |
| **Coccyx** | 3–4 fused rudimentary vertebrae | Attachment for pelvic floor muscles; coccydynia from falls |
| **Anterior longitudinal ligament (ALL)** | Broad band on anterior vertebral bodies | Resists hyperextension; thick (rarely fails) |
| **Posterior longitudinal ligament (PLL)** | Narrower band on posterior bodies inside the canal | Resists hyperflexion; narrow → most disc herniations escape laterally to compress nerve roots |
| **Ligamentum flavum** | Yellow elastic ligament between laminae | Maintains posture; hypertrophy → central spinal stenosis |
| **Supraspinous ligament** | Connects spinous-process tips | Limits flexion |
| **Interspinous + intertransverse ligaments** | Between processes | Minor stabilizers |
| **Intervertebral disc - anulus fibrosus** | Concentric layers of fibrocartilage | Tensile container; tears → nucleus herniation |
| **Intervertebral disc - nucleus pulposus** | Central gelatinous core (notochord remnant) | Distributes compressive load hydrostatically; herniates through anulus posterolaterally most often |
| **Spinal cord** | Houses motor, sensory, autonomic tracts | Continuous with medulla; ends at conus medullaris (L1–L2 in adults) |
| **Conus medullaris** | Tapered end of spinal cord | Surgical landmark for safe LP below it |
| **Cauda equina** | Bundle of lumbosacral nerve roots below conus | Compression → cauda equina syndrome (saddle anesthesia, incontinence - surgical emergency) |
| **Filum terminale** | Pial extension from conus to coccyx | Anchors cord; tethered cord syndrome in children |
| **Vertebral artery** | Branch of subclavian; ascends through C1–C6 transverse foramina | Posterior cerebral circulation; dissection causes posterior strokes (vertebrobasilar) |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX nursing actions:**
- **Suspect spinal injury** → immobilize (cervical collar + spine board); log-roll only; no flexion/extension until cleared.
- **Lumbar puncture position**: lateral decubitus, fetal/curled; insert below L3–L4 (in adults) to avoid cord; flat 4–6 h post-procedure to prevent spinal headache.
- **Disc herniation patient**: avoid Valsalva (no straining, lifting); proper body mechanics; bend at knees, not waist.
- **Cauda equina syndrome red flags**: saddle anesthesia, urinary retention/incontinence, bilateral leg weakness - surgical emergency.
- **Spinal precautions** post-laminectomy: log-roll, no twisting, no sitting &gt;30 min initially.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**USMLE patterns:**
- **Dermatomes to memorize**: C4 collar, T4 nipple, T10 umbilicus, L1 inguinal, L4 knee/medial leg, S2–S4 perineum.
- **Disc herniation rule**: posterolateral herniation hits the **traversing** nerve root (L4–L5 → L5 root → foot drop, weak dorsiflexion).
- **Spina bifida types**: occulta (vertebral arch defect, often asymptomatic), meningocele (meninges only), myelomeningocele (meninges + cord - Chiari II association); **maternal folate prevents**.
- **AAFOL** for atlanto-axial subluxation risk: Achondroplasia, Atlanto-occipital instability in Down's, Friedreich ataxia, Os odontoideum, Lateral mass fracture - important pre-anesthesia.
</HighYield>

The skull has **22 bones** total: 8 cranial + 14 facial. Sutures (immovable fibrous joints) connect adjacent bones - **coronal** (frontal-parietal), **sagittal** (between parietals), **lambdoid** (parietal-occipital), **squamous** (parietal-temporal). The **pterion** is a thin H-shaped junction (frontal-parietal-temporal-sphenoid) where the **middle meningeal artery** lies just deep - classic site of **epidural hematoma** after a blow to the temple.

### Long bone anatomy

<Figure name="skeletal/long-bone" caption="Structure of a long bone (femur shown). Each long bone has a diaphysis (shaft) with a medullary cavity, two epiphyses (ends) covered by articular cartilage, and a metaphysis between them containing the epiphyseal plate in growing bones." />

Key regions of a long bone, each with a specific role:

| Region | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Diaphysis** | Hollow shaft with thick compact bone and central medullary cavity | Mechanical strength; medullary cavity holds yellow marrow (adipose) in adults |
| **Epiphysis** | Bulbous ends with spongy bone + articular cartilage | Articulates with adjacent bones; site of red marrow in some bones (proximal femur, humerus) |
| **Metaphysis** | Junction between diaphysis + epiphysis | Contains epiphyseal plate in growing bone; site of growth-plate fractures (Salter-Harris) |
| **Epiphyseal (growth) plate** | Hyaline cartilage where longitudinal growth happens | Active in children; ossifies at puberty → epiphyseal line in adults |
| **Articular cartilage** | Hyaline cartilage capping epiphyses | Frictionless joint surface; no perichondrium, limited self-repair |
| **Periosteum** | Fibrous outer covering with osteoprogenitor cells | Source of bone width growth; site of muscle/tendon attachment |
| **Endosteum** | Thin membrane lining medullary cavity + canals | Site of remodeling; contains osteoclasts + osteoprogenitors |
| **Medullary cavity** | Central canal of diaphysis | Holds yellow (adult) or red (child) marrow |
| **Compact bone** | Dense outer layer with osteons (Haversian systems) | Resists bending and compression |
| **Spongy (cancellous) bone** | Trabecular meshwork in epiphyses | Resists multidirectional forces; light; houses red marrow |
| **Volkmann's canals** | Horizontal channels connecting osteons | Carry vessels between Haversian systems and periosteum |
| **Haversian canal** | Central canal of each osteon | Carries blood vessels + nerves along the long axis |
