The lower limb is built for **stability over mobility** - opposite design philosophy from the upper limb. The pelvic girdle locks rigidly to the axial skeleton at the sacroiliac joints. The femur is the largest, strongest bone in the body. Three weight-bearing arches in the foot distribute force. **30 bones per limb**: hip + femur + patella + tibia/fibula + 7 tarsals + 5 metatarsals + 14 phalanges.

## Pelvic girdle

The **hip bone (os coxae)** has three fused parts (fusion completes by ~25):
- **Ilium** - large flared wing; ASIS, PSIS, iliac crest.
- **Ischium** - posterior + inferior; ischial tuberosity ("sit bones"), ischial spine (greater + lesser sciatic notches).
- **Pubis** - anterior; pubic symphysis joins the two; superior + inferior pubic rami.

All three meet at the **acetabulum** (the hip socket).

### Pelvic ligaments
- **Sacrospinous** (sacrum → ischial spine) + **sacrotuberous** (sacrum → ischial tuberosity) - convert greater + lesser sciatic notches into foramina.
- **Iliolumbar, sacroiliac** - stabilize SI joint.

### Greater + lesser sciatic foramina - pelvic exits
| Foramen | Above piriformis | Below piriformis |
|---|---|---|
| **Greater sciatic** | Superior gluteal n. + vessels | Sciatic n., inferior gluteal n. + vessels, pudendal n. (re-enters via lesser), posterior femoral cutaneous, internal pudendal vessels, nerve to obturator internus + quadratus femoris |
| **Lesser sciatic** | - | Tendon of obturator internus, pudendal n. + internal pudendal vessels (re-entering perineum), nerve to obturator internus |

The pudendal nerve loops around the ischial spine - **pudendal nerve block** is performed by palpating this landmark transvaginally.

## Femur

- **Proximal**: head (articulates with acetabulum; ligamentum teres carries foveolar artery), **neck** (common fracture site in elderly), greater + lesser trochanter (muscle attachments), intertrochanteric line + crest.
- **Shaft**: linea aspera (posterior - muscle attachments).
- **Distal**: medial + lateral condyles, intercondylar fossa (cruciates), epicondyles, adductor tubercle.

### Femoral neck fractures
| Location | Blood supply at risk | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Subcapital / intracapsular** | Medial circumflex femoral disrupted → **AVN risk** | Hemiarthroplasty (or total hip) |
| **Intertrochanteric** | Extracapsular, blood supply intact | ORIF with sliding hip screw |
| **Subtrochanteric** | Extracapsular | ORIF |

Femoral neck fracture in elderly is high-mortality (~25% at 1 year). Operate within 48 h.

## Patella + knee

- **Patella** = largest sesamoid in the body; in quadriceps tendon.
- Functions: ↑ moment arm of quadriceps (improves extension force ~50%); protects knee anteriorly; ↓ friction.
- **Patellar dislocation** - usually lateral (Q-angle pulls); recurrent in young women.

## Tibia + fibula

| Bone | Function | Clinical |
|---|---|---|
| **Tibia** | Weight-bearing; medial + larger; tibial plateau articulates with femur; medial malleolus at ankle | Shin splints (medial tibial stress); Salter-Harris fractures in children |
| **Fibula** | Non-weight-bearing; lateral; provides muscle attachments + ankle stability; lateral malleolus | Can be harvested for free flap; common peroneal n. wraps neck |

**Common peroneal (fibular) nerve at fibular neck** - superficial, easily compressed (tight cast, crossing legs) → **foot drop** + sensory loss over dorsum of foot.

## Foot - tarsals, metatarsals, phalanges

### 7 tarsal bones
- Posterior: **calcaneus** (heel), **talus** (on top of calcaneus, articulates with tibia/fibula).
- Middle: **navicular** (medial), **cuboid** (lateral), 3 **cuneiforms** (medial, intermediate, lateral).
- 5 **metatarsals** + 14 **phalanges**.

<Figure name="skeletal/lower-limb-foot-slide" caption="Lecture slide labeling the tarsal bones, metatarsals, and phalanges of the foot." />

### Arches of the foot
| Arch | Components | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Medial longitudinal** | Calcaneus → talus → navicular → 3 cuneiforms → MT 1–3 | Tallest; main weight-bearing arch |
| **Lateral longitudinal** | Calcaneus → cuboid → MT 4–5 | Lower; static |
| **Transverse** | Cuneiforms + cuboid + MT bases | Distributes weight across forefoot |

Supported by **plantar fascia** + **spring ligament** + **deltoid ligament** + intrinsic muscles. **Pes planus** (flat foot): collapse of medial longitudinal arch; **pes cavus** (high arch): excessive arching (Charcot-Marie-Tooth).

### Ankle joint - stability ligaments
| Ligament | Function | Injury |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior talofibular (ATFL)** | Anterior + lateral ankle | **Most common ankle sprain** (inversion injury) |
| **Calcaneofibular (CFL)** | Lateral | Sprained with severe inversion |
| **Posterior talofibular (PTFL)** | Posterior + lateral | Rarely injured |
| **Deltoid (medial) ligament** | Strong; resists eversion | Rarely sprained - usually fractures medial malleolus instead |

## Major lower-limb nerves and lesions

| Nerve | Roots | Motor | Sensory | Injury → |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Femoral** | L2–L4 | Quadriceps, sartorius, iliacus | Anterior thigh + medial leg (saphenous) | Pelvic surgery/hematoma → weak knee extension + ↓ patellar reflex |
| **Obturator** | L2–L4 | Hip adductors | Medial thigh | Anterior hip surgery → weak adduction; can refer pain to knee (Howship-Romberg sign) |
| **Sciatic** | L4–S3 | Hamstrings + (via branches) all leg + foot | Posterior thigh, lateral leg, foot | Improper IM injection (always inject upper outer quadrant of gluteus); piriformis syndrome; herniated disc |
| **Tibial** (sciatic branch) | L4–S3 | Posterior leg, intrinsic foot muscles, plantarflexion + inversion | Sole of foot | Tarsal tunnel syndrome (rarer); proximal injury → can't plantarflex/invert (heel walking only) |
| **Common peroneal (fibular)** (sciatic branch) | L4–S2 | Dorsiflexion + eversion (anterior + lateral compartments) | Dorsum of foot + lateral leg | **Fibular neck injury → foot drop** (loss of dorsiflexion) |
| **Superior gluteal** | L4–S1 | Gluteus medius + minimus | None | Hip abductor weakness → **Trendelenburg gait** (contralateral hip drops) |
| **Inferior gluteal** | L5–S2 | Gluteus maximus | None | Difficulty climbing stairs, rising from chair |
| **Pudendal** | S2–S4 | External anal + urethral sphincters, perineal muscles | Perineum, scrotum/labia, clitoris/penis | Sensory loss after prolonged cycling; pudendal nerve entrapment |

<Mnemonic>
**"P.E.D."** - **P**iriformis exits the greater sciatic foramen, **E**verything **E**lse goes **D**eep to it. The piriformis is the key landmark for everything passing through the greater sciatic foramen.
</Mnemonic>

## Compartments of the leg

| Compartment | Muscles | Innervation | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anterior** | Tibialis anterior, EHL, EDL, peroneus tertius | **Deep fibular** | Dorsiflexion + toe extension; site of **anterior compartment syndrome** in tibial fractures |
| **Lateral** | Peroneus longus, brevis | **Superficial fibular** | Eversion |
| **Posterior superficial** | Gastrocnemius, soleus, plantaris | **Tibial** | Plantarflexion |
| **Posterior deep** | Tibialis posterior, FDL, FHL | **Tibial** | Inversion + toe flexion |

**Anterior compartment syndrome** - most common compartment syndrome in the leg, after tibia fracture. 6 Ps: **P**ain (out of proportion, ↑ with passive stretch), **P**aresthesia, **P**allor, **P**ulselessness (late), **P**aralysis (late), **P**oikilothermia. **Fasciotomy is the treatment**, not waiting on imaging.

## Function reference - every clinically critical structure

| Structure | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Ligamentum teres** | Carries foveolar artery to femoral head (in children) | Adults: minor; femoral head AVN if neck fracture cuts medial circumflex |
| **Greater trochanter** | Insertion for gluteus medius + minimus | Trendelenburg test landmark; trochanteric bursitis |
| **Iliotibial band** | Tendon of TFL + gluteus maximus | ITB syndrome - lateral knee pain in runners |
| **Linea aspera** | Posterior femoral ridge - adductor + biceps femoris attachments | Stable for plating in distal femur fractures |
| **Patella** | Sesamoid in quad tendon | Improves extension; sliding chondromalacia in runners |
| **Tibial tuberosity** | Patellar ligament insertion | Osgood-Schlatter (apophysitis in adolescents) |
| **Pes anserinus** | "Goose foot" - sartorius + gracilis + semitendinosus on medial tibia | Bursitis here mimics MCL injury |
| **Anterior talofibular ligament** | Resists inversion + plantarflexion | Most common ankle sprain |
| **Plantar fascia** | Supports medial longitudinal arch | Plantar fasciitis - first-step heel pain |
| **Sustentaculum tali** | Medial calcaneus shelf supporting talus | Site for FHL pulley; spring ligament attachment |
| **Femoral triangle** | Bordered by inguinal ligament, sartorius, adductor longus; contains **NAVeL** (Nerve, Artery, Vein, empty space, Lymphatics - lateral to medial) | Femoral hernia site; pulse exam |
| **Adductor (Hunter's) canal** | From femoral triangle to adductor hiatus; femoral a/v transition to popliteal | Femoral block; vascular access |
| **Popliteal fossa** | Knee back; tibial n., popliteal a + v, common peroneal n. | Baker cyst, popliteal aneurysm, DVT propagation |
| **Tarsal tunnel** | Behind medial malleolus; tibial n., posterior tibial a/v, FDL/FHL/TP | Tarsal tunnel syndrome; pulse exam |

## Why this matters

<HighYield variant="boards">
- **Foot drop = common peroneal nerve** (fibular neck injury or L5 radiculopathy).
- **Trendelenburg sign = superior gluteal nerve** (or L5 radiculopathy) - opposite hip drops on weight-bearing because gluteus medius can't hold it.
- **Femoral neck fracture in elderly** → 25% 1-year mortality. AVN risk → arthroplasty.
- **DVT** classically in lower limb - venous stasis (immobility), endothelial injury (trauma), hypercoagulability (Virchow triad).
- **Achilles tendon rupture** - sudden pain + palpable gap + positive **Thompson test** (squeeze calf → no plantarflexion); fluoroquinolones predispose.
- **Hip dislocation**: posterior (90%) - knee flexed, hip flexed/adducted/internally rotated (dashboard injury); risk **sciatic n. injury + AVN**. Reduce within 6 h to ↓ AVN.
- **Anterior cruciate ligament tear** - see joints page; female athletes 2–8× higher risk.
- **Salter-Harris fractures** - physis fractures in growing bones; types I-V; type II most common; types III–V increase growth disturbance risk.
- **Compartment syndrome** - clinical diagnosis. Don't wait for imaging.
- **L5 radiculopathy** = weak EHL (toe extension) + foot dorsiflexion ± numbness over dorsum/web space between great + 2nd toes. Differentiates from common peroneal palsy by hip abduction weakness (gluteus medius is also L5).
- **S1 radiculopathy** = weak gastrocnemius (toe walking), absent Achilles reflex, sensory loss over lateral foot.
</HighYield>
