A joint (articulation) is any contact between two bones (or bone + cartilage). Joints come in three structural types - **fibrous, cartilaginous, synovial** - with very different mobility, blood supply, and disease patterns. The **synovial joint** is the workhorse: freely movable, lubricated by synovial fluid, the target of arthritis.

<Figure name="skeletal/synovial-joint-slide" caption="Lecture-slide model of a synovial joint showing the capsule, synovial membrane, synovial fluid, and articular cartilage." />

## Classification

### By structure (what's between the bones)

| Type | Material | Mobility | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fibrous** | Dense connective tissue | None to slight | Skull sutures (synarthrosis); gomphoses (tooth-socket); syndesmoses (tibiofibular, radioulnar membranes) |
| **Cartilaginous - primary (synchondrosis)** | Hyaline cartilage | None | Epiphyseal plate; first sternocostal joint |
| **Cartilaginous - secondary (symphysis)** | Fibrocartilage | Slight | Pubic symphysis; intervertebral discs; manubriosternal |
| **Synovial** | Synovial cavity + fluid | Free | Knee, hip, shoulder, elbow, etc. |

<Figure name="skeletal/cartilaginous-joints" caption="Two types of cartilaginous joints - synchondroses (hyaline cartilage, like the epiphyseal plate or first sternocostal joint) and symphyses (fibrocartilage, like the pubic symphysis or intervertebral discs)." />

<Figure name="skeletal/synovial-joint" caption="Anatomy of a generic synovial joint - articular hyaline cartilage on each bone end, joint cavity filled with synovial fluid, joint capsule (fibrous outer + synovial inner membrane), and reinforcing ligaments." />

### By function (how much they move)
- **Synarthrosis** - immobile (most fibrous)
- **Amphiarthrosis** - slightly mobile (cartilaginous secondary)
- **Diarthrosis** - freely mobile (all synovial)

## Synovial joint - the standard model

<Figure name="skeletal/synovial-joint" caption="" />


Components present in every synovial joint:

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| **Articular (hyaline) cartilage** | Smooth low-friction surface; spreads load; avascular + aneural → poor healing |
| **Joint cavity** | Potential space filled with synovial fluid |
| **Synovial membrane (synovium)** | 2 cell types: A (macrophage-like, debris removal) + B (fibroblast-like, hyaluronic acid + lubricin secretion) |
| **Synovial fluid** | Viscous, hyaluronic-acid rich filtrate of plasma; lubricates + nourishes cartilage |
| **Fibrous capsule** | Outer layer; continuous with periosteum; richly innervated (proprioception + pain) |
| **Ligaments** | Bone ↔ bone; restrain motion; intracapsular (ACL, PCL) or extracapsular (collaterals) |
| **Menisci / labra** (some joints) | Fibrocartilage cushions; deepen socket (glenoid, acetabulum); shock absorbers (knee) |
| **Bursae** | Closed sacs of synovial fluid; reduce friction over bony prominences or tendons |

## Synovial joint subtypes (by shape - board favorite)

<Figure name="skeletal/synovial-joint-types" caption="The six types of synovial joints with example locations - (a) pivot (atlantoaxial C1-C2), (b) hinge (elbow), (c) saddle (1st carpometacarpal of thumb), (d) plane (intertarsal), (e) condyloid (radiocarpal wrist), (f) ball-and-socket (hip)." />

| Type | Movement | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Plane (gliding)** | Sliding | Intercarpal, intertarsal, acromioclavicular |
| **Hinge** | 1 axis (flex/extend) | Elbow (humeroulnar), knee (modified), ankle, IP joints |
| **Pivot** | 1 axis (rotation) | Atlantoaxial (C1-C2, dens of C2); proximal radioulnar |
| **Condyloid (ellipsoid)** | 2 axes (no rotation) | Radiocarpal (wrist); MCP joints |
| **Saddle** | 2 axes + some rotation | First carpometacarpal (thumb) - gives opposition |
| **Ball-and-socket** | 3 axes | Hip, shoulder (glenohumeral) |

### TMJ: the oddball synovial joint

<Figure name="skeletal/tmj-slide" caption="The temporomandibular joint is a specialized synovial joint with a fibrocartilaginous disc, so it behaves like both a hinge and a gliding articulation." />

<Figure name="skeletal/tmj-detail" caption="TMJ in detail - mandibular condyle articulating with the mandibular fossa of the temporal bone, fibrocartilaginous articular disc dividing the joint cavity into superior and inferior compartments, and the joint capsule + lateral ligament reinforcing it." />

<Figure name="skeletal/atlantoaxial-joint" caption="Atlantoaxial joint - the pivot joint between atlas (C1) and axis (C2). The dens (odontoid process) of C2 projects up through the anterior arch of C1 and is held there by the transverse ligament; rotation of the head occurs at this joint." />

The **temporomandibular joint (TMJ)** is worth calling out separately because it does not behave like a simple elbow-style hinge:

- It combines **hinge** and **gliding** movements.
- It uses an **articular disc of fibrocartilage**, not standard hyaline articular cartilage.
- Dysfunction produces jaw pain, clicking, and limited excursion rather than classic peripheral-joint arthritis symptoms.

## Three biggest joints - detailed

<Figure name="skeletal/knee-joint-openstax" caption="Knee joint - largest synovial joint, modified hinge. Cruciate ligaments inside, collaterals outside, menisci as shock absorbers." />

### Shoulder (glenohumeral)
- **Ball-and-socket**, ball = humeral head, socket = shallow glenoid fossa.
- **Most mobile** joint in the body → **most commonly dislocated** (95% anterior-inferior, axillary nerve at risk).
- **Glenoid labrum** deepens socket.
- **Rotator cuff** stabilizers: **S**upraspinatus, **I**nfraspinatus, **T**eres minor, **S**ubscapularis (**"SITS"**). Supraspinatus most commonly torn.
- **Long head of biceps** tendon runs through the joint.

### Hip
- **Ball-and-socket**, ball = femoral head, socket = deep acetabulum.
- Far less mobile than shoulder but far more stable.
- **Acetabular labrum** + **transverse acetabular ligament** + thick capsule.
- **Ligaments**: iliofemoral ("Y of Bigelow" - strongest in body), pubofemoral, ischiofemoral.
- **Ligamentum teres** carries the artery to the head of the femur (foveolar branch of obturator); main supply otherwise from medial circumflex femoral.
- **Vulnerable**: femoral head AVN after femoral neck fracture (especially elderly).

### Knee
- **Modified hinge** (some rotation in flexion).
- Three articulations in one capsule: medial + lateral tibiofemoral + patellofemoral.
- **Menisci** (medial + lateral fibrocartilage C-shapes) deepen tibial plateau; medial meniscus attached to MCL (vulnerable).
- **Cruciate ligaments** (intracapsular but extrasynovial):
  - **ACL** prevents anterior tibial translation (positive Lachman, anterior drawer)
  - **PCL** prevents posterior tibial translation (posterior drawer, dashboard injury)
- **Collateral ligaments**:
  - **MCL** (medial) - taut in extension; valgus stress
  - **LCL** (lateral) - taut in extension; varus stress
- **"Unhappy triad"** of O'Donoghue: clipping injury (lateral force on extended knee) tears **ACL + MCL + medial meniscus**.
- **Bursae**: prepatellar (housemaid's knee), infrapatellar (clergyman's knee), pes anserinus.

<Clinical>
**Anterior shoulder dislocation** - common in young athletes. Axillary nerve at risk → deltoid weakness + regimental badge sensory loss. Reduce promptly; sling 2–3 weeks; rehab.

**ACL tear** - non-contact pivot injury; "pop" + immediate effusion + instability. Lachman (most sensitive) > anterior drawer. MRI confirms. Reconstruction in young/active.

**Septic arthritis** - surgical emergency. Hot, swollen, immobile joint with fever. **Joint aspiration**: WBC >50,000 (often >100,000), Gram stain ± organism, culture. Common agents: S. aureus (most), Neisseria gonorrhoeae (sexually active young adult, with rash + tenosynovitis triad), Streptococcus.
</Clinical>

## Synovial fluid analysis - interpreting the aspirate

<Figure name="skeletal/synovial-joint-slide" caption="" />


| Parameter | Normal | Non-inflammatory (OA) | Inflammatory (RA, crystal) | Septic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Color** | Clear, straw | Clear | Yellow, cloudy | Purulent |
| **Viscosity** | High | High | Low | Variable |
| **WBC** | &lt;200 | &lt;2,000 | 2,000–50,000 | >50,000 (often >100k) |
| **% PMNs** | &lt;25% | &lt;25% | >50% | >75% |
| **Crystals** | None | None | Gout: needle-shaped, neg birefringent monosodium urate; Pseudogout: rhomboid, pos birefringent CPPD | None |
| **Gram stain / culture** | Neg | Neg | Neg | **Positive** |

<Mnemonic>
**"You're a NEG to gout"** - gout crystals are **negatively** birefringent and **needle**-shaped. Pseudogout (CPPD) is positively birefringent and rhomboid.
</Mnemonic>

## Major arthritis patterns

<Figure name="skeletal/atlantoaxial-joint" caption="" />


| Disease | Mechanism | Pattern | Hallmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Osteoarthritis (OA)** | "Wear and tear" - cartilage erosion | Asymmetric; large weight-bearing (hip, knee) + DIPs/PIPs of hands | Heberden (DIP) + Bouchard (PIP) nodes; joint space narrowing + osteophytes on X-ray; morning stiffness &lt;30 min |
| **Rheumatoid arthritis (RA)** | Autoimmune synovitis; pannus formation | **Symmetric** small joints (MCPs, PIPs, wrists); spares DIPs | Morning stiffness >1 h; ulnar deviation; swan neck + boutonniere; RF + anti-CCP; ↑ ESR/CRP |
| **Psoriatic arthritis** | Spondyloarthropathy | Asymmetric; DIPs; "pencil-in-cup" deformity | Nail pitting + onycholysis; sausage digit (dactylitis) |
| **Ankylosing spondylitis** | HLA-B27 spondyloarthropathy | Sacroiliac + axial spine | "Bamboo spine" on X-ray; uveitis; aortitis |
| **Gout** | Monosodium urate crystals | Asymmetric; classically **first MTP (podagra)** | Negatively birefringent needles; tophi; renal stones |
| **Pseudogout (CPPD)** | Calcium pyrophosphate crystals | Knee, wrist | Positively birefringent rhomboid; chondrocalcinosis on X-ray |
| **Septic arthritis** | Bacterial invasion | Acute, monoarticular, hot, fever | WBC >50k in fluid; Gram stain + culture |

## Function reference - every joint structure

| Component | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Articular cartilage** | Low-friction smooth surface; type II collagen + aggrecan | Avascular → poor healing; first lost in OA |
| **Synoviocyte type A** | Macrophage-like; phagocytose debris | Inflammation hub in RA |
| **Synoviocyte type B** | Fibroblast-like; secrete hyaluronan + lubricin | Reduced lubricin in some arthritides |
| **Synovial fluid hyaluronan** | Major lubricant + nutrient carrier | Loss in OA → friction injury |
| **Meniscus** | Distributes load; shock absorbs | Avascular inner 2/3 → poor healing; outer 1/3 vascularized = repairable |
| **Anterior cruciate ligament** | Prevents anterior tibial translation | Female > male tear rate (anatomic + hormonal) |
| **Rotator cuff** | Dynamic shoulder stabilizer + initiates abduction | Supraspinatus impingement most common; degenerative tear |
| **Glenoid labrum** | Deepens shoulder socket; Bankart lesion in dislocation | SLAP tears from biceps overuse |
| **Bursa** | Friction reducer | Bursitis (prepatellar, olecranon) - local infection or repetitive trauma |
| **Ligamentum teres (head of femur)** | Carries foveolar artery in children | Femoral head AVN if neck fracture cuts off medial circumflex supply in adults |
| **Subchondral bone** | Bone underneath cartilage; transmits load | Sclerosis + cysts in OA |
| **Joint capsule** | Mechanical envelope + proprioception | Source of pain in arthritis; capsulitis ("frozen shoulder") |

## Why this matters

<Figure name="skeletal/cartilaginous-joints" caption="" />


<HighYield variant="boards">
- **Anterior shoulder dislocation** - **axillary nerve** at risk (sensory: lateral upper arm "regimental badge"; motor: deltoid + teres minor).
- **Posterior shoulder dislocation** - classic in seizures, electrocution. Often missed; **light bulb sign** on AP X-ray.
- **Subluxation of the radial head** ("nursemaid's elbow") - pull on extended pronated arm of toddler; reduce by **supination + flexion**.
- **OA management**: weight loss + NSAIDs + intra-articular steroids → joint replacement when severe.
- **RA management**: methotrexate first-line DMARD; biologics (anti-TNF, anti-IL-6, rituximab, JAK inhibitors) for refractory; bridge with low-dose steroids.
- **Hip fracture in elderly** - femoral neck fracture risks **AVN** of femoral head; intertrochanteric fractures do not. ORIF vs hemi-arthroplasty depends on location.
- **Joint aspiration before steroid injection** - always exclude septic arthritis first.
- **Disseminated gonococcal infection (DGI)** - triad: migratory polyarthralgia + tenosynovitis + pustular rash; or monoarticular purulent arthritis. Treat ceftriaxone + azithromycin (for chlamydia coverage).
</HighYield>
