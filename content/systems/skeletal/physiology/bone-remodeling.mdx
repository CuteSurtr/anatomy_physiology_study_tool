Bone is dynamic tissue - the entire adult skeleton turns over roughly every **10 years**. Remodeling is the coordinated activity of three cell types in a **basic multicellular unit (BMU)**:

- **Osteoclasts** (from monocyte lineage) - resorb bone via H⁺ and lysosomal enzymes in a sealed "ruffled border" against the matrix.
- **Osteoblasts** (from mesenchymal stem cells) - secrete osteoid (Type I collagen + ground substance), which then mineralizes with hydroxyapatite.
- **Osteocytes** - entombed osteoblasts; sense mechanical strain and signal remodeling.

Read the cycle left-to-right: **resorption first, formation second**. Most bone disease is not a total failure of both processes, but a shift in their balance or timing.

## Ossification - how bone is made

Both routes to bone replace an earlier tissue; neither builds bone from nothing.

| Route | Model tissue | Bones formed | Sequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Intramembranous** | Mesenchymal (fibrous) membrane | Flat bones of the cranial vault, most of the mandible, and the clavicle | Mesenchyme condenses → osteoblasts secrete osteoid → woven-bone spicules → remodelled into compact + spongy bone |
| **Endochondral** | Hyaline cartilage model | Everything else - all long bones, vertebrae, pelvis, skull base | Cartilage model → periosteal bone collar → **primary** ossification centre in the diaphysis → **secondary** centres in the epiphyses → cartilage left only at the growth plate and articular surface |

Inside the **epiphyseal (growth) plate**, cartilage is produced on the epiphyseal side and destroyed on the diaphyseal side, so the plate stays roughly constant in thickness while the bone lengthens. Its zones, epiphysis → diaphysis, are **resting → proliferation → hypertrophy → calcification → ossification**.

- **Interstitial growth** (length) happens only at the epiphyseal plate. The plate closes in late adolescence - **oestrogen drives closure in both sexes** - leaving the epiphyseal line.
- **Appositional growth** (width) is lifelong: periosteal osteoblasts add bone to the outer surface while endosteal osteoclasts widen the medullary cavity.

Because the growth plate is cartilage, it is a mechanical weak point: in children a force that would sprain an adult ligament instead fractures the physis (**Salter-Harris** injuries).

## The calcium balancing act

Bone is the body's calcium reservoir (~99% of total). Two hormones dominate:

- **Parathyroid hormone (PTH)** ↑ when serum Ca²⁺ ↓ → stimulates osteoclasts (indirectly, via osteoblast RANKL), ↑ renal Ca²⁺ reabsorption, ↑ activation of vitamin D.
- **Calcitonin** (thyroid C cells) ↑ when serum Ca²⁺ ↑ → inhibits osteoclasts. Minor role in adults.

**Calcitriol** (active vitamin D) ↑ intestinal Ca²⁺ absorption.

<Clinical>
**Osteoporosis**: net resorption > deposition. Postmenopausal estrogen loss removes a brake on osteoclasts. **Bisphosphonates** (alendronate) bind hydroxyapatite and poison osteoclasts when they try to resorb. **Denosumab** is a monoclonal antibody against RANKL - same target, different mechanism.
</Clinical>

<Note title="Wolff's law">
Bone remodels in response to mechanical load. Astronauts lose ~1–2% bone mass per month in microgravity; weight-bearing exercise is the single most evidence-based intervention for osteoporosis prevention.
</Note>

## Fracture repair - remodeling under emergency conditions

1. **Haematoma** (hours) - torn periosteal and medullary vessels bleed; osteocytes near the break die from loss of perfusion.
2. **Soft (fibrocartilaginous) callus** (days to ~3 weeks) - fibroblasts and chondroblasts bridge the gap with collagen and cartilage.
3. **Hard (bony) callus** (~3-12 weeks) - the soft callus is converted to woven bone by endochondral ossification.
4. **Remodeling** (months to years) - the same BMU machinery replaces woven bone with lamellar bone aligned to load, so the bulky callus is trimmed back (Wolff's law).

## Every bone-remodeling player - function reference

### Cells

| Cell | Origin | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Osteoblast** | Mesenchymal stem cell | Secretes osteoid (Type I collagen + GAGs) → mineralizes with hydroxyapatite | Builder; expresses RANKL + OPG, regulates osteoclast activity indirectly |
| **Osteocyte** | Entombed osteoblast in lacunae | Senses mechanical strain; signals through canaliculi via gap junctions | Mechanosensor; if killed (avascular necrosis), bone resorbs |
| **Osteoclast** | Hematopoietic monocyte lineage | Multinucleated; resorbs bone via H⁺ (vacuolar ATPase) + cathepsin K | Breaker; target of bisphosphonates (poisons it after uptake) and denosumab (anti-RANKL) |
| **Osteoprogenitor cell** | Periosteum, endosteum | Stem-cell pool → osteoblasts | Renewable source for repair |
| **Bone-lining cell** | Quiescent osteoblast | Covers inactive bone surfaces | Activated to begin remodeling cycle |

### Bone matrix components

| Component | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Type I collagen** | 90% of organic matrix | Tensile strength; mutations → osteogenesis imperfecta |
| **Hydroxyapatite [Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂]** | Mineral phase (~65% of bone weight) | Compressive strength; calcium reservoir |
| **Osteocalcin** | Non-collagenous protein from osteoblasts | Binds Ca²⁺ in matrix; serum marker of bone formation |
| **Osteonectin (SPARC)** | Glycoprotein from osteoblasts | Mineral-matrix linker |
| **Osteopontin** | Anchors osteoclasts to bone | Mediates resorption |
| **Sialoprotein, GAGs** | Ground substance | Hydration, structure |

### Signaling molecules

| Signal | Source | Action | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RANKL** | Osteoblast | Binds RANK on osteoclast precursor → osteoclast differentiation | Master switch for resorption; denosumab blocks |
| **OPG (osteoprotegerin)** | Osteoblast | Decoy receptor for RANKL → ↓ osteoclast activation | Natural brake; estrogen boosts OPG (lost in menopause) |
| **M-CSF** | Osteoblasts + stromal | Required for osteoclast precursor proliferation | Synergizes with RANKL |
| **Wnt / β-catenin** | Osteoblasts | Stimulates bone formation | Sclerostin (from osteocytes) inhibits Wnt → ↓ formation; romosozumab blocks sclerostin |
| **BMPs** | Osteoblasts, growth plate | Osteoinduction | Used in spinal fusion (BMP-2, BMP-7) |
| **TGF-β** | Bone matrix, platelets | Couples resorption to formation | Released by osteoclast activity |

### Hormonal regulation of calcium

| Hormone | Source | Effect on bone | Net effect on serum Ca²⁺ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PTH** | Parathyroid chief cells | Indirectly stimulates osteoclasts (via osteoblast RANKL) | ↑ Ca²⁺; ↓ phosphate (renal) |
| **Calcitriol** | Kidney (1α-hydroxylase) | Promotes mineralization at physiologic doses; mobilizes Ca²⁺ at high | ↑ Ca²⁺ (mainly via intestinal absorption) |
| **Calcitonin** | Thyroid C cells | Directly inhibits osteoclasts | ↓ Ca²⁺ (small effect in adults) |
| **Estrogen** | Ovaries (until menopause) | ↑ OPG, ↓ RANKL; protects bone | Postmenopausal loss → osteoporosis |
| **GH / IGF-1** | Pituitary / liver | ↑ bone formation | Acromegaly → thickened bones; deficiency → short stature |
| **Cortisol** | Adrenal cortex | Inhibits osteoblasts, ↑ resorption | Chronic excess (Cushing, steroids) → osteoporosis |
| **Thyroid hormone** | Thyroid follicular cells | ↑ remodeling | Hyperthyroidism → osteoporosis |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX patient priorities:**
- **Osteoporosis teaching**: weight-bearing exercise, **1200 mg/d calcium + 800 IU vitamin D**, fall prevention, smoking/alcohol cessation.
- **Bisphosphonates**: take with full glass of water, **stay upright 30 min** to prevent esophagitis; take on empty stomach.
- **Hyperparathyroidism labs**: ↑ Ca²⁺, ↓ phosphate, ↑ PTH; symptoms = "**stones, bones, groans, psychic moans**" (kidney stones, bone pain, abdominal pain, depression).
- **Cast care**: check 5 P's (pain, pallor, paresthesia, pulselessness, paralysis) - compartment syndrome.
- **Post-op total hip**: maintain abduction with pillow; no hip flexion &gt;90°; no crossing legs.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Bone pharmacology:**
- **Bisphosphonates** (alendronate, zoledronic acid) - bind hydroxyapatite, poison osteoclasts; rare ONJ + atypical femoral fractures with prolonged use.
- **Denosumab** - anti-RANKL antibody; SC q6mo; doesn't accumulate in bone (vs. bisphosphonates).
- **Teriparatide** (PTH 1–34) - anabolic; daily SC for severe osteoporosis; black-box: osteosarcoma in rats.
- **Romosozumab** - anti-sclerostin; both ↑ formation + ↓ resorption; cardiovascular warning.
- **Raloxifene** - SERM; bone agonist + breast antagonist; ↑ VTE risk.
- **Calcitonin** - nasal spray; less effective; analgesic in vertebral fractures.
</HighYield>
