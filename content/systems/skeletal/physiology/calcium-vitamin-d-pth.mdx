Plasma calcium is held within ~1% of its setpoint (~10 mg/dL ionized + total) by a three-hormone axis: **PTH** (immediate buffer, bone + kidney), **calcitriol** (slow, gut + bone), and **calcitonin** (minor in humans). Every step of bone remodeling, neuromuscular transmission, muscle contraction, and coagulation depends on this regulation.

## Why calcium matters

<Figure name="skeletal/calcium-homeostasis-openstax" caption="Calcium homeostasis overview - 99% in bone, 1% in cells, 0.1% in plasma. Ionized Ca²⁺ is physiologically active; tightly regulated 8.5-10.5 mg/dL." />

| Function | Why Ca²⁺ matters |
|---|---|
| **Neuromuscular excitability** | Stabilizes resting potential by blocking voltage-gated Na⁺ channels at rest. ↓ Ca²⁺ → tetany (Trousseau, Chvostek). ↑ Ca²⁺ → weakness, drowsiness. |
| **Muscle contraction** | Trigger for troponin C in striated muscle; calmodulin → MLCK in smooth |
| **Cardiac action potential** | Plateau (phase 2) driven by L-type Ca²⁺ |
| **Synaptic NT release** | Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ entry → SNARE-vesicle fusion |
| **Coagulation** | Factor IV; multiple steps need free ionized Ca²⁺ |
| **Bone** | Hydroxyapatite mineral matrix |
| **Hormone release** | Insulin (β cell stimulus-secretion coupling), epi (chromaffin) |
| **Second messenger** | IP3-released Ca²⁺ inside the cell |

## The three calcium pools

<Figure name="skeletal/compact-bone-histology" caption="Bone histology - calcium's main reservoir. 99% of body calcium is stored in hydroxyapatite, exchanged with plasma via osteoclast resorption + osteoblast deposition." />

| Pool | % | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Bone** | 99% | Mineral reservoir; readily exchangeable surface Ca²⁺ on hydroxyapatite + deeper "stable" stores |
| **Intracellular** | &lt;1% | Mostly in ER/SR; cytosol kept very low (~100 nM) by SERCA + PMCA pumps |
| **Plasma + ECF** | ~0.1% | The pool the body regulates |

Of plasma calcium (normal ~8.5-10.5 mg/dL total):
- **Ionized (50%)** - biologically active; the regulated variable
- **Protein-bound (40%)** - mostly albumin
- **Complexed (10%)** - citrate, phosphate, bicarbonate

**Correction for albumin**: corrected Ca = measured + 0.8 × (4 - albumin). Or just measure ionized Ca².

**Alkalosis ↑ albumin binding** → ↓ ionized Ca → tetany even at "normal" total Ca (e.g., panic-attack hyperventilation).

## The three hormones

<Figure name="skeletal/vitamin-d-pathway" caption="Vitamin D₃ synthesis pathway - skin (UVB on 7-dehydrocholesterol) → liver (25-hydroxylase) → kidney (1α-hydroxylase) → 1,25-(OH)₂ vitamin D = calcitriol, the active hormone." />

<Figure name="skeletal/calcium-homeostasis-openstax" caption="Calcium homeostasis (OpenStax). Low Ca²⁺ → PTH (parathyroid) → ↑ bone resorption + renal Ca²⁺ reabsorption + calcitriol → ↑ intestinal Ca²⁺ absorption. High Ca²⁺ → calcitonin (thyroid C cells)." />

### PTH - minute-to-minute regulator
**Source**: parathyroid chief cells, responsive to ionized Ca²⁺ via the **calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR)**.

| Target | Action |
|---|---|
| **Bone** | Stimulates osteoblasts → ↑ RANK-L → osteoclast activation → bone resorption → release Ca²⁺ + PO₄³⁻ + alkaline phosphatase to blood |
| **Kidney (distal tubule)** | ↑ Ca²⁺ reabsorption (TRPV5) |
| **Kidney (proximal tubule)** | ↓ PO₄³⁻ reabsorption (phosphaturic - internalizes Na/Pi cotransporter) |
| **Kidney** | ↑ 1α-hydroxylase → activates 25-OH vitamin D → calcitriol |

Net: ↑ serum Ca²⁺, ↓ serum PO₄³⁻ (kidney phosphate loss outpaces gut phosphate absorption).

**CaSR** is a Gq-coupled GPCR. ↑ Ca²⁺ → CaSR activation → ↓ PTH release. **Cinacalcet** is a calcimimetic (allosteric CaSR activator) - suppresses PTH in secondary hyperparathyroidism + parathyroid carcinoma.

### Calcitriol (1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D₃) - daily-to-weekly regulator
**Synthesis pathway** (key board pattern):
```
7-dehydrocholesterol (skin)
  + UV-B light
  ↓
Cholecalciferol (vitamin D₃)
  ↓ in liver: 25-hydroxylase
25-(OH)-D₃ (calcidiol)           ← THE STORAGE FORM; measured to assess vitamin D status
  ↓ in kidney: 1α-hydroxylase (PTH-stimulated, suppressed by FGF23)
1,25-(OH)₂-D₃ (calcitriol)       ← THE ACTIVE FORM
```

**Sources**:
- Skin synthesis (UV-B, 7-dehydrocholesterol)
- Diet: D₂ (ergocalciferol, plants) + D₃ (cholecalciferol, fatty fish, fortified milk)

**Calcitriol actions** (mostly via VDR → nuclear transcription):
| Target | Action |
|---|---|
| **Small intestine** | ↑ Ca²⁺ + PO₄³⁻ absorption (calbindin, TRPV6) |
| **Bone** | Synergizes with PTH on osteoclasts; also needed for proper osteoblast function + mineralization |
| **Kidney** | ↑ Ca²⁺ + PO₄³⁻ reabsorption (minor compared to PTH) |
| **Parathyroid** | Negative feedback on PTH transcription |
| **Immune** | Modulates T-cell function; ↓ AS in MS, ↓ severity of some autoimmune diseases |

Net: ↑ serum Ca²⁺ **AND** ↑ serum PO₄³⁻ (both go up - unlike PTH).

### Calcitonin - minor in humans
**Source**: parafollicular C cells of thyroid (neural crest origin).

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| ↑ Ca²⁺ | ↓ osteoclast activity → ↓ bone resorption → ↓ Ca²⁺ |

In humans, thyroidectomy doesn't cause hypercalcemia → calcitonin's physiologic role is minimal. **Clinical uses**:
- Tumor marker for medullary thyroid carcinoma (the cells that make it)
- Therapy for Paget's disease + hypercalcemia of malignancy (short-term)

### Newer: FGF23 - phosphate regulator
- Secreted by **osteocytes** when serum PO₄³⁻ ↑.
- Acts on kidney → ↓ PO₄³⁻ reabsorption (phosphaturic) AND ↓ 1α-hydroxylase → ↓ calcitriol → ↓ gut PO₄³⁻ absorption.
- Net: lowers PO₄³⁻.
- Pathologically elevated in **CKD** (↑ FGF23 is early; PTH rises later) - drives bone loss + cardiovascular calcification.
- **Oncogenic osteomalacia**: phosphaturic mesenchymal tumors secrete FGF23 → hypophosphatemia + osteomalacia.

## Putting the three together

<Figure name="skeletal/pth-feedback-loop" caption="PTH negative feedback - low serum Ca²⁺ → parathyroid chief cells secrete PTH → ↑ bone resorption + ↑ renal Ca²⁺ reabsorption + ↑ renal 1α-hydroxylase → calcitriol → ↑ intestinal Ca²⁺ absorption → serum Ca²⁺ rises → suppresses further PTH (Wikimedia)." />

| Stimulus | PTH | Calcitriol | Net effect on Ca | Net effect on PO₄ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **↓ ionized Ca²⁺** | ↑ | ↑ (PTH stimulates 1α-OHase) | Rises | Falls (PTH wins phosphate handling) |
| **↑ ionized Ca²⁺** | ↓ | ↓ | Falls | Rises slightly |
| **↓ PO₄³⁻** | minimal | ↑ (low PO₄ activates 1α-OHase) | Slightly rises | Rises (calcitriol pushes gut + reab) |
| **↑ PO₄³⁻ (chronic, e.g., CKD)** | ↑ (secondary hyperPT) | ↓ (FGF23 suppresses) | Often falls | Stays high (kidney failure) |

## Pathologies of the axis

| Disease | Mechanism | Ca | PO₄ | PTH | Vit D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary hyperparathyroidism** | Parathyroid adenoma (85%) → autonomous PTH | ↑ | ↓ | ↑↑ | normal/↑ |
| **Secondary hyperparathyroidism (CKD)** | ↓ active vit D + ↑ phosphate → ↓ ionized Ca → ↑ PTH | ↓ or normal | ↑ | ↑↑ | ↓ |
| **Tertiary hyperparathyroidism** | Long-standing secondary → autonomous PT hyperplasia | ↑ | ↑ | ↑↑↑ | ↓ |
| **Hypoparathyroidism** | Post-thyroidectomy, DiGeorge, autoimmune | ↓ | ↑ | ↓ | low active |
| **Pseudohypoparathyroidism (Albright)** | Tissue resistance to PTH (Gs defect) | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ | ↓ |
| **Vitamin D deficiency (osteomalacia / rickets)** | Low sun + low intake; malabsorption; CKD | ↓ or normal | ↓ | ↑ | ↓↓ |
| **Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia (FHH)** | Loss-of-function CaSR mutation | ↑ | normal | normal/↑ | normal - KEY: LOW urinary Ca (&lt;100 mg/d, FECa &lt;1%) |
| **Humoral hypercalcemia of malignancy** | PTH-related peptide (PTHrP) from tumor | ↑ | ↓ | ↓ (suppressed by ↑Ca) | normal/↓ |
| **Sarcoidosis / granulomatous** | Macrophages express 1α-hydroxylase | ↑ | ↑ | ↓ | ↑ active form |

<Mnemonic>
**"Stones, Bones, Groans, Moans, Psychiatric overtones"** - symptoms of hypercalcemia: nephrolithiasis (Ca + PO₄ + oxalate stones), bone pain + fractures, abdominal pain + constipation, fatigue/depression, confusion/psychosis. Above 14 mg/dL = emergency.
</Mnemonic>

## Bone remodeling - the cellular layer

<Figure name="skeletal/long-bone" caption="Related figure for context — Bone remodeling - the cellular layer." />

| Cell | Origin | Function | Regulators |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Osteoblast** | Mesenchymal stem cell | Bone formation (osteoid + mineralization); has PTH + vitamin D receptors | Wnt, IGF, BMP, mechanical load |
| **Osteoclast** | Hematopoietic (monocyte lineage) | Bone resorption | RANK-L (from osteoblasts) drives differentiation; OPG (decoy receptor) inhibits |
| **Osteocyte** | Trapped osteoblast in matrix | Mechanosensor; signals via canaliculi; secretes sclerostin (inhibits osteoblasts) + FGF23 | Mechanical strain, PTH |
| **Bone-lining cell** | Quiescent osteoblast | Cover bone surfaces; gateway for remodeling | - |

### RANK / RANK-L / OPG axis
- **RANK-L** on osteoblast surface → binds **RANK** on osteoclast precursor → activates → osteoclast.
- **OPG** (osteoprotegerin) is a decoy receptor that binds RANK-L and prevents activation.
- **Denosumab** is a monoclonal anti-RANK-L (mimics OPG) → potent osteoclast suppression → osteoporosis + bone metastases treatment.
- **Bisphosphonates** (alendronate, zoledronate) - bind hydroxyapatite + inhibit osteoclast activity (induce apoptosis via FPP synthase inhibition).
- **Teriparatide** (PTH 1-34): paradoxically **anabolic** when given as intermittent pulses (continuous PTH = resorptive). Sole anabolic therapy for severe osteoporosis.
- **Romosozumab**: anti-sclerostin → unleashes osteoblasts → builds bone.

## Function reference - every key player

| Component | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **PTH** | Master Ca regulator | Primary hyperparathyroidism = adenoma (85%) > hyperplasia; calcium + PTH inappropriately elevated |
| **CaSR** | Ca sensor on parathyroid + thick ascending limb | Loss-of-function = FHH (mild ↑Ca, low urine Ca); gain-of-function = autosomal dominant hypocalcemia (low Ca, low PTH) |
| **1α-hydroxylase** | Renal activation of 25-OH vit D | Lost in CKD → ↓ active vit D → ↓ gut Ca absorption → ↑ PTH |
| **25-hydroxylase** | Hepatic activation step | Rarely the bottleneck except severe liver dz |
| **VDR (vitamin D receptor)** | Nuclear receptor for calcitriol | Mutation → vitamin D-resistant rickets type II |
| **TRPV5/6** | Ca channels in DCT (TRPV5) + gut (TRPV6) | Regulated by calcitriol + estrogen (menopause → loss → osteoporosis) |
| **Calbindin** | Intracellular Ca shuttle in enterocyte + DCT | Calcitriol-induced |
| **Sclerostin** | Osteocyte secretion that inhibits Wnt → blocks osteoblasts | Target of romosozumab |
| **RANK-L / RANK / OPG** | Osteoclast activation switch | Denosumab blocks RANK-L; central in tumor osteolysis |
| **FGF23** | Phosphate-lowering hormone from osteocytes | Drives CKD-MBD; X-linked hypophosphatemia (PHEX mutation → ↑ FGF23); tumor-induced osteomalacia |
| **Alkaline phosphatase** | Mineralization enzyme; in osteoblasts | ↑ in bone turnover (Paget's, mets, healing fracture, growth) |
| **Carboxyglutamate residues** (Gla) | Vitamin K-dependent; osteocalcin needs them | Warfarin can affect bone - minor effect |

## Why this matters

<HighYield variant="boards">
- **Primary hyperparathyroidism** workup: ↑ Ca + ↑ PTH = adenoma (85%) > 4-gland hyperplasia (15%, often MEN1). Sestamibi scan + neck ultrasound localize. Minimally invasive parathyroidectomy.
- **Hypercalcemia of malignancy** - PTHrP from squamous cell tumors (lung, head/neck, esophagus, cervix), breast, RCC. Cytokine-mediated in multiple myeloma. Treat: aggressive saline + furosemide + bisphosphonate (zoledronate) + calcitonin (acute) + steroids (lymphoma, granulomatous).
- **CKD-MBD** (CKD mineral and bone disorder): ↓ Vit D activation → ↓ Ca absorption → secondary hyperPT → bone disease + vascular calcification. Treat: phosphate binders, calcitriol or analogs (paricalcitol), cinacalcet.
- **DEXA scan T-scores**: -1 to -2.5 = osteopenia; ≤ -2.5 = osteoporosis; ≤ -2.5 + fragility fracture = severe osteoporosis. First-line: bisphosphonate. Refractory: denosumab, teriparatide, romosozumab.
- **Rickets**: childhood vitamin D deficiency → bowing of legs, bossing of skull, rachitic rosary. Osteomalacia is the adult form.
- **Renal osteodystrophy**: combination of osteomalacia + hyperparathyroid bone disease + adynamic bone disease (low PTH from over-treatment) in CKD patients.
- **Tetany** (low ionized Ca²⁺): Trousseau (BP cuff → carpal spasm), Chvostek (facial tap → twitch). Treat: IV calcium gluconate (acute); oral Ca + activated vit D long-term.
- **Calcium oxalate vs calcium phosphate stones**: oxalate is most common (often idiopathic hypercalciuria); phosphate stones suggest distal RTA. Both: ↑ urinary Ca = risk.
- **Hungry bone syndrome**: after parathyroidectomy, the chronically demineralized skeleton rapidly absorbs Ca + PO₄ + Mg → severe hypocalcemia. Pre-treat with calcium + Vit D.
</HighYield>
