Plasma pH is maintained at **7.35–7.45** through a coordinated buffer + respiratory + renal response. Three lines of defense, with three different speeds:

1. **Chemical buffers** (instant) - bicarbonate, phosphate, hemoglobin, proteins
2. **Respiratory compensation** (minutes) - change ventilation to alter PaCO₂
3. **Renal compensation** (hours to days) - change H⁺ secretion + HCO₃⁻ reabsorption + new HCO₃⁻ generation

Understanding the **Henderson-Hasselbalch** equation and the **anion gap** lets you read any blood gas.

## The fundamental equation

```
pH = 6.1 + log([HCO₃⁻] / (0.03 × PaCO₂))
```

For a quick bedside check:
```
pH ↑ when HCO₃⁻ ↑ or PaCO₂ ↓
pH ↓ when HCO₃⁻ ↓ or PaCO₂ ↑
```

Normal values:
| Parameter | Range |
|---|---|
| pH | 7.35–7.45 |
| PaCO₂ | 35–45 mmHg |
| HCO₃⁻ | 22–26 mEq/L |
| Anion gap | 8–12 mEq/L |
| PaO₂ | 75–100 mmHg (room air) |

## Four primary disorders

<Figure name="urinary/davenport-diagram" caption="Davenport diagram - [HCO₃⁻] plotted against pH, with isobars for PCO₂. Each primary acid-base disorder shifts the operating point in a characteristic direction; renal + respiratory compensation moves it along a buffer line." />

| Disorder | Primary problem | pH | PaCO₂ | HCO₃⁻ | Compensation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Metabolic acidosis** | ↓ HCO₃⁻ (loss or consumption) | ↓ | ↓ (Kussmaul) | ↓↓ | Hyperventilation → drives PaCO₂ down |
| **Metabolic alkalosis** | ↑ HCO₃⁻ (loss of H⁺ or HCO₃⁻ gain) | ↑ | ↑ | ↑↑ | Hypoventilation → drives PaCO₂ up |
| **Respiratory acidosis** | ↑ PaCO₂ (hypoventilation) | ↓ | ↑↑ | ↑ (renal) | Kidney makes new HCO₃⁻ over 3–5 days |
| **Respiratory alkalosis** | ↓ PaCO₂ (hyperventilation) | ↑ | ↓↓ | ↓ (renal) | Kidney dumps HCO₃⁻ over 3–5 days |

**Acute respiratory disturbances** have minimal renal compensation. **Chronic** ones show a much bigger HCO₃⁻ shift - that's how you tell duration on a gas.

## Compensation formulas (Winter's rules)

<Figure name="urinary/anion-gap-mudpiles" caption="MUDPILES — causes of high anion-gap metabolic acidosis. Methanol, Uremia, DKA, Propylene glycol, Iron/Isoniazid, Lactic acidosis, Ethylene glycol, Salicylates. Winter's formula predicts compensation." />

| Disorder | Expected compensation |
|---|---|
| **Metabolic acidosis** | PaCO₂ = 1.5 × HCO₃⁻ + 8 (± 2) - Winter's formula |
| **Metabolic alkalosis** | PaCO₂ = 0.7 × HCO₃⁻ + 20 |
| **Acute resp acidosis** | HCO₃⁻ ↑ 1 for every 10 ↑ in PaCO₂ |
| **Chronic resp acidosis** | HCO₃⁻ ↑ 3.5 for every 10 ↑ in PaCO₂ |
| **Acute resp alkalosis** | HCO₃⁻ ↓ 2 for every 10 ↓ in PaCO₂ |
| **Chronic resp alkalosis** | HCO₃⁻ ↓ 5 for every 10 ↓ in PaCO₂ |

If the measured value deviates from expected → a **second** disorder is present (mixed disturbance).

## Anion gap

<Figure name="urinary/anion-gap-mudpiles" caption="Causes of high anion-gap metabolic acidosis - MUDPILES (CAT-MUDPILES): Methanol, Uremia, DKA, Propylene glycol, Iron/INH, Lactic acidosis, Ethylene glycol, Salicylates." />

```
AG = Na⁺ – (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻)
```

Normal 8–12 (or 3–10 with newer assays). Albumin contributes ~3 to the gap - adjust:
```
Adjusted AG = AG + 2.5 × (4 – albumin)
```

### Anion-gap metabolic acidosis - **MUDPILES**
| Letter | Cause |
|---|---|
| **M** | Methanol (→ formic acid) |
| **U** | Uremia (sulfates, phosphates) |
| **D** | DKA (ketoacids) - also alcoholic + starvation ketoacidosis |
| **P** | Propylene glycol (lorazepam infusions) |
| **I** | Iron / Isoniazid (lactic acidosis) |
| **L** | Lactate (shock, sepsis, metformin in renal failure) |
| **E** | Ethylene glycol (→ oxalic + glycolic acids) |
| **S** | Salicylates (mixed AG metabolic acidosis + resp alkalosis) |

### Non-anion-gap metabolic acidosis - **HARDASS**
| Letter | Cause |
|---|---|
| **H** | Hyperalimentation / Hyperchloremia |
| **A** | Addison disease (mineralocorticoid deficiency) |
| **R** | Renal tubular acidosis (RTA) |
| **D** | Diarrhea (loss of HCO₃⁻) |
| **A** | Acetazolamide (CA inhibitor → renal HCO₃⁻ wasting) |
| **S** | Spironolactone (mineralocorticoid antagonism) |
| **S** | Saline infusion (dilutional / hyperchloremic) |

## Renal tubular acidosis (RTA) - the three types

| Type | Defect | Urine pH | Serum K⁺ | Classic associations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Type 1 (distal)** | Distal H⁺ secretion fails | >5.5 | ↓↓ (severe hypokalemia) | Stones (calcium phosphate), Sjögren, lithium, amphotericin |
| **Type 2 (proximal)** | HCO₃⁻ reabsorption defective | &lt;5.5 (eventually) | ↓ | Fanconi syndrome, multiple myeloma, acetazolamide |
| **Type 4 (hyperaldosteronism-mimic)** | ↓ aldosterone or resistance → hyperkalemia → ↓ NH₃ synthesis | &lt;5.5 | **↑** (hyperkalemia!) | Diabetic neuropathy, ACE inhibitors, K-sparing diuretics, NSAIDs |

Type 4 is the only RTA with hyperkalemia - high K⁺ inhibits proximal tubule NH₃ synthesis → ↓ NH₄⁺ excretion → can't excrete H⁺.

## Metabolic alkalosis

### Causes
- **Vomiting / NG suction** - loss of HCl from stomach → ↑ HCO₃⁻ generation
- **Loop or thiazide diuretics** - volume depletion + ↑ aldosterone + H⁺/K⁺ loss
- **Mineralocorticoid excess** - Conn, Cushing, Liddle, licorice
- **Bartter, Gitelman syndromes** - congenital tubular defects mimicking diuretic effect
- **Milk-alkali syndrome** - calcium carbonate excess

### Saline-responsive vs saline-resistant
| Type | Mechanism | Urine Cl⁻ | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Saline-responsive** (volume contraction maintains alkalosis) | Vomiting, diuretics | &lt;20 mEq/L | Volume resuscitation with NaCl |
| **Saline-resistant** | Mineralocorticoid excess | >20 mEq/L | Treat underlying cause (spironolactone for Conn) |

## Respiratory acidosis (hypoventilation)

| Acute (mins) | Chronic (days–weeks) |
|---|---|
| Opioid overdose | COPD |
| Brainstem injury | Obesity hypoventilation |
| Acute neuromuscular (Guillain-Barré, MG crisis) | Chronic neuromuscular (ALS, muscular dystrophy) |
| Pneumothorax / large effusion | Severe kyphoscoliosis |
| Severe asthma exacerbation | OSA |

## Respiratory alkalosis (hyperventilation)

- **Anxiety / panic attack** (classic)
- **Salicylate toxicity** - early; followed by metabolic acidosis (AG)
- **Pulmonary embolism**, pneumonia, ARDS (hypoxic drive)
- **High altitude** (chronic)
- **Pregnancy** (progesterone → respiratory drive)
- **Cirrhosis** (↑ ammonia)
- **Sepsis** (early)

## Renal handling of acid

<Figure name="urinary/nephron-three-processes" caption="Renal acid handling - PCT reclaims filtered HCO3- via NHE3/CA; α-intercalated cells of CD secrete H+ (titratable acidity + NH4+); β-intercalated cells secrete HCO3- in alkalosis." />

### Bicarbonate reabsorption (PCT)
Filtered HCO₃⁻ is reabsorbed indirectly: tubular H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ → H₂CO₃ → H₂O + CO₂ (via apical CA) → CO₂ diffuses into cell → reverses to HCO₃⁻ (intracellular CA) → basolateral exit via Na/3HCO₃⁻ cotransporter. **80–90% in PCT**.

### New bicarbonate generation
The kidney is the **only** organ that can excrete acid. Two mechanisms:

1. **Titratable acid** - H⁺ secretion into tubule combines with HPO₄²⁻ → H₂PO₄⁻. Limited by filtered phosphate; ~30 mEq/day.
2. **NH₄⁺ excretion** (the major mechanism) - proximal tubule cells deaminate glutamine → α-ketoglutarate + 2 NH₄⁺. NH₄⁺ trapped in tubule + α-KG metabolized to HCO₃⁻ (new alkali). Up-regulated in chronic acidosis. **Limit: ~300 mEq/day**.

### α-intercalated cells (collecting duct)
- Apical **H⁺-ATPase** + **H⁺/K⁺-ATPase** secrete H⁺
- Basolateral Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger (band 3) delivers new HCO₃⁻ to blood
- Aldosterone stimulates → metabolic alkalosis in primary hyperaldosteronism

## Function reference - every regulator

| Component | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **HCO₃⁻ / CO₂ buffer pair** | Major extracellular buffer (open system - CO₂ blown off) | Bedside ABG variables |
| **Phosphate buffer** | Tubular fluid + intracellular | Source of titratable acid |
| **Hemoglobin** | Major intracellular buffer in RBCs | Haldane effect - deoxy-Hb is a better buffer |
| **Plasma proteins** | Intravascular buffer | Albumin contributes ~3 to anion gap |
| **Bone** | Calcium phosphate buffers chronic acidosis | Chronic acidosis → bone loss (CKD, RTA) |
| **PCT brush border CA IV** | Catalyzes luminal H₂CO₃ ↔ H₂O + CO₂ | Acetazolamide inhibits → proximal RTA-like effect |
| **Cytosolic CA II** | Generates HCO₃⁻ inside tubule cells | Inhibition → can't generate HCO₃⁻ |
| **α-intercalated cells** | H⁺ secretion via H+-ATPase | Failure = type 1 RTA |
| **β-intercalated cells** | HCO₃⁻ secretion in alkalosis (reverse polarity) | Symmetric mirror of α cells |
| **Principal cells** | Aldosterone-regulated Na/K exchange | Indirectly affects H⁺ excretion via ENaC-driven electrical gradient |
| **Macula densa / RAAS** | Volume sensing → aldosterone | "Contraction alkalosis" mechanism - volume depletion drives alkalosis |
| **Glutamine metabolism (PCT)** | Source of NH₄⁺ for acid excretion | Up-regulated in chronic acidosis; suppressed by hyperkalemia (Type 4 RTA) |

## Stepwise reading of an ABG (the 5-step method)

1. **Look at pH** - acidemic (&lt;7.35) or alkalemic (>7.45)?
2. **Look at PaCO₂ + HCO₃⁻** - which is moving with the pH (= primary disorder)?
3. **Calculate anion gap** if metabolic acidosis is in the differential.
4. **Compute expected compensation** - if measured ≠ expected, second disorder.
5. **Delta-delta** (in AG metabolic acidosis): Δ AG / Δ HCO₃⁻. If &lt;1, there's also a non-AG acidosis. If >2, there's also a metabolic alkalosis.

## Why this matters

<HighYield variant="boards">
- **Salicylate overdose**: early respiratory alkalosis (direct medullary stimulation) → later AG metabolic acidosis (uncouples oxidative phosphorylation + lactate). Treatment: urinary alkalinization with HCO₃⁻ (ion-trap salicylate in urine).
- **Methanol vs ethylene glycol**: both AG acidosis. Methanol → blindness (formate damages optic nerve). EG → calcium oxalate crystals in urine + renal failure. Both: **fomepizole** (blocks alcohol dehydrogenase) ± hemodialysis.
- **DKA** treatment: fluids → insulin → K⁺ (insulin shifts K intracellularly). Don't give HCO₃⁻ unless pH &lt;6.9.
- **Loop diuretic alkalosis**: hypokalemic, hypochloremic, hyperaldosteronemic. Replete K⁺ and Cl⁻.
- **Acetazolamide** - CA inhibitor → ↓ HCO₃⁻ reabsorption → metabolic acidosis. Used for altitude sickness, glaucoma, pseudotumor cerebri.
- **Chronic compensation maxes out** - pH still abnormal. Normal pH with abnormal PaCO₂ + HCO₃⁻ = mixed disorder, not "fully compensated."
- **Permissive hypercapnia** in ventilated ARDS: accept ↑ PaCO₂ to avoid volutrauma; renal compensation handles HCO₃⁻.
</HighYield>
