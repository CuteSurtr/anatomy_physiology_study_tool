Paired bean-shaped organs in the **retroperitoneum** at vertebral levels **T12–L3**. The right kidney sits ~1.5 cm lower than the left because of the liver. Each adult kidney measures approximately **11 cm long × 6 cm wide × 3 cm thick** and weighs **~150 g**. Combined, the kidneys receive **~20% of cardiac output** (~1.2 L/min of blood flow) - the highest blood flow per gram of tissue of any organ in the body.

## Gross anatomy and surface relations

Each kidney has:
- **Two surfaces** - anterior (covered by peritoneum partially) and posterior (against psoas, quadratus lumborum, diaphragm)
- **Two borders** - lateral (convex) and medial (concave, bearing the hilum)
- **Two poles** - superior (capped by the adrenal gland) and inferior

The **hilum** transmits, from anterior to posterior, **vein → artery → ureter** (**VAU** - "V before A before U"). The hilum also transmits lymphatics, nerves, and adipose tissue.

### Coverings (outside in)

1. **Renal fascia (of Gerota)** - fibrous outer envelope; anchors kidney to surrounding structures.
2. **Perinephric fat** - between renal fascia and capsule; cushions the kidney.
3. **Renal capsule** - thin fibrous covering directly on the kidney; strips cleanly in a healthy kidney, adheres in chronic inflammation.

### Anterior relations

The right and left kidneys have **asymmetric** anterior relations - easy to mix up.

| Right kidney | Left kidney |
|---|---|
| Liver (right lobe) | Spleen |
| Right colic flexure | Stomach (lesser sac) |
| Second part of duodenum | Pancreas (body + tail) |
| Right adrenal | Left adrenal |
| Small intestine | Jejunum, left colic flexure, descending colon |

### Posterior relations (both kidneys)

Diaphragm, 12th rib (the kidney crosses the rib obliquely - important for surgical access via the loin), psoas major, quadratus lumborum, transversus abdominis. Nerves at risk during nephrectomy: **subcostal, iliohypogastric, ilioinguinal**.

## Internal gross structure

Cut a kidney sagittally and you see:

- **Cortex** (outer, granular) - contains the renal corpuscles, PCT, DCT, and cortical collecting ducts. Extends into the medulla as **renal columns of Bertin** between pyramids.
- **Medulla** (inner, striated) - contains **8–18 renal pyramids**, each pointing toward the hilum with its **apex = papilla**. The striations are the loops of Henle and collecting ducts.
- **Renal sinus** - fat-filled cavity at the hilum containing the calyces, pelvis, and segmental vessels.
- **Minor calyces** (8–18, one per papilla) → **major calyces** (2–3) → **renal pelvis** → **ureter**.

A **lobe** of the kidney = one pyramid + its overlying cortex. Adult human kidneys are smooth on the surface (vs. multilobulated in cows, bears). Fetal lobulation is normally absent by age 5.

### Embryology

The definitive kidney develops from the **metanephros** (week 5+). Two components meet:
- **Ureteric bud** (from mesonephric duct) → ureter, pelvis, calyces, collecting ducts.
- **Metanephric mesenchyme** → glomeruli, tubules (PCT, loop, DCT).

Failure of the bud to induce the mesenchyme → **renal agenesis**. Bilateral agenesis → **Potter sequence** (oligohydramnios → pulmonary hypoplasia, limb deformities, characteristic facies). Failure of kidney ascent from the pelvis → **pelvic kidney** or **horseshoe kidney** (caught under the inferior mesenteric artery during ascent).

## The nephron - anatomy

About **1 million nephrons per kidney**. Two populations:

- **Cortical** (~85%): short loops of Henle, entirely in the cortex.
- **Juxtamedullary** (~15%): long loops descending deep into the medulla - these create and maintain the medullary osmotic gradient required for urine concentration.

Segments in order:

1. **Renal corpuscle** = glomerulus + Bowman's capsule (filtration).
2. **Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)** - bulk reabsorption (~65% of filtrate).
3. **Loop of Henle** - countercurrent multiplier; descending limb permeable to water, ascending limb permeable to solute.
4. **Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)** - fine-tuning, regulated by aldosterone (later parts) and PTH (Ca²⁺ reabsorption).
5. **Collecting duct** - final concentration via ADH (water permeability) and aldosterone (Na⁺/K⁺).

## Blood supply - detailed

Renal arteries are direct lateral branches of the **abdominal aorta** at the L1–L2 level. The **right renal artery** is longer and passes posterior to the IVC. Each renal artery divides into **5 segmental arteries** (one to each segment); these are end-arteries - occlusion produces wedge-shaped infarcts.

<Figure name="urinary/renal-portal-circulation-slide" caption="Lecture slide emphasizing the kidney's portal arrangement: afferent arteriole to glomerulus to efferent arteriole to peritubular capillaries." />

<Figure name="urinary/renal-blood-flow" caption="Renal blood flow path (OpenStax) - renal artery → segmental → interlobar (between pyramids) → arcuate (cortex/medulla junction) → cortical radiate (interlobular) → afferent arteriole → glomerulus → efferent arteriole → peritubular capillaries (cortical nephrons) or vasa recta (juxtamedullary) → veins back along the same path → renal vein." />

Arterial cascade:

```
Renal artery → segmental → interlobar (between pyramids in renal columns)
  → arcuate (arch over base of pyramid at corticomedullary junction)
  → cortical radiate (interlobular, into cortex)
  → afferent arteriole
  → glomerulus (capillary tuft)
  → efferent arteriole (unique - second arteriole in series)
  → peritubular capillaries (cortical nephrons) OR vasa recta (juxtamedullary nephrons)
  → cortical radiate vein → arcuate v. → interlobar v. → renal v. → IVC
```

The **two capillary beds in series** (glomerular then peritubular) are the anatomical basis for glomerular filtration. The afferent arteriole is normally larger and shorter than the efferent - when efferent constricts (e.g., angiotensin II), glomerular hydrostatic pressure rises and GFR is preserved. ACE inhibitors block this and selectively dilate the efferent → useful in proteinuric disease, but precipitate AKI in bilateral renal artery stenosis.

The **left renal vein** is longer than the right and crosses anterior to the aorta and posterior to the SMA - the **"nutcracker" anatomical location**, where it can be compressed (nutcracker syndrome: left flank pain, hematuria, varicocele in men since the left gonadal vein drains into it).

## Histology of the nephron

Microscopic features for high-yield identification:

| Segment | Epithelium | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **Bowman's capsule (parietal)** | Simple squamous | Outer wall |
| **Bowman's capsule (visceral)** | Podocytes with foot processes | Form filtration slits |
| **Proximal convoluted tubule** | Simple cuboidal with prominent **brush border** | Eosinophilic, blurred lumen - densest sodium reabsorption |
| **Thin descending limb** | Simple squamous | Permeable to water; thin walls in the medulla |
| **Thick ascending limb** | Simple cuboidal | No brush border; clear lumen; NKCC2 pumps |
| **Distal convoluted tubule** | Simple cuboidal, no brush border | Lumen clearer than PCT; specialized macula densa where contacts afferent arteriole |
| **Collecting duct (cortical)** | Simple cuboidal | Distinct cell borders; **principal** (light, water + Na⁺/K⁺) and **intercalated** (dark, H⁺/HCO₃⁻) cells |
| **Collecting duct (medullary)** | Simple columnar | Larger, ADH-responsive, terminates at papilla |

## Lymphatics, innervation, and nerve supply

- **Lymphatics** drain to **lateral aortic (lumbar) nodes**.
- **Innervation** is from the **renal plexus** (sympathetic from T10–L1 via celiac and aorticorenal ganglia; parasympathetic from vagus). Sympathetic activation → afferent arteriole vasoconstriction, ↑ renin release.
- **Referred pain** from the kidneys/ureters is felt in the **flank, radiating to the groin** (T10–L1 dermatomes - "loin to groin" with ureteric colic).

<Clinical>
**Renal artery stenosis** → ↓ perfusion → ↑ renin → ↑ angiotensin II → secondary **hypertension** that is often resistant to standard therapy. Listen for an abdominal bruit; image with renal Doppler or CTA.
</Clinical>

## Juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA)

<Figure name="urinary/renal-corpuscle" caption="" />


Three structures meet here:

- **Macula densa** - specialized DCT epithelial cells; sense distal NaCl.
- **Juxtaglomerular (granular) cells** - modified smooth-muscle cells in the afferent arteriole wall; release **renin** when sensing low pressure.
- **Mesangial cells** (extraglomerular) - structural support and signaling.

The JGA is the control center for **renin-angiotensin-aldosterone (RAAS)** activation and **tubuloglomerular feedback** (high distal NaCl → adenosine → afferent constriction → ↓ GFR).

## Every renal structure - what it does and why it matters

### Coverings and gross structure

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Renal fascia (of Gerota)** | Fibrous envelope anchoring kidney to retroperitoneum | Surgical plane; contains perinephric abscesses |
| **Perinephric (perirenal) fat** | Cushions kidney within fascia | Anchorage; rapid weight loss → kidneys can become ptotic |
| **Renal capsule** | Tough fibrous covering directly on kidney parenchyma | Inelastic - swelling (pyelonephritis, acute obstruction) causes pain via capsular stretch |
| **Renal cortex** | Outer ~1 cm; contains glomeruli, PCT, DCT, cortical collecting ducts | Site of all filtration; first to atrophy in chronic ischemia |
| **Renal medulla (pyramids)** | Inner zone; loops of Henle and collecting ducts | Maintains osmotic gradient (countercurrent multiplication) required to concentrate urine |
| **Renal columns of Bertin** | Cortex extending between pyramids | Conduct interlobar vessels to corticomedullary junction |
| **Renal papilla (apex of pyramid)** | Where collecting ducts open into calyx | Site of papillary necrosis (sickle cell, NSAIDs, DM) |
| **Minor calyx** | Cup around each papilla | Drains one pyramid's urine |
| **Major calyx** | Confluence of 3–4 minor calyces | Funnels into pelvis |
| **Renal pelvis** | Funnel-shaped urine collection within the hilum | Site of transitional cell carcinoma; pyelonephritis "pyelo-" = pelvis |
| **Renal hilum** | Entry/exit of vein, artery, ureter (anterior → posterior: V-A-U) | Surgical landmark; also lymphatics + nerves pass here |

### Vascular tree

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Renal artery** | Lateral branch of abdominal aorta (L1–L2) | Stenosis → secondary HTN (renovascular); right is longer (passes behind IVC) |
| **Segmental arteries (×5)** | End-arteries to each kidney segment | Occlusion → wedge-shaped infarct (no collateral) |
| **Interlobar arteries** | Pass between pyramids in renal columns | Connect segmental to arcuate |
| **Arcuate arteries** | Arch over base of pyramid at corticomedullary junction | Give off cortical radiate branches |
| **Cortical radiate (interlobular) arteries** | Penetrate cortex; give off afferent arterioles | Each supplies many afferent arterioles |
| **Afferent arteriole** | Carries blood to glomerulus | Autoregulated by myogenic + tubuloglomerular feedback; NSAIDs constrict via blocking PGE2 |
| **Glomerulus** | Capillary tuft inside Bowman's capsule | Site of filtration (~120 mL/min normally) |
| **Efferent arteriole** | Drains glomerulus, gives rise to peritubular capillaries / vasa recta | ACE inhibitors dilate it preferentially → ↓ glomerular pressure (helpful or harmful depending on setting) |
| **Peritubular capillaries** | Surround tubules of cortical nephrons | Reabsorb solutes and water back into circulation |
| **Vasa recta** | Hairpin capillaries paralleling loops of Henle | Preserve medullary osmotic gradient (countercurrent exchange) |
| **Renal vein** | Drains kidney to IVC | Left renal vein crosses anterior to aorta, posterior to SMA → nutcracker compression risk |

### Filtration apparatus

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Bowman's (glomerular) capsule** | Cup-shaped epithelial covering of glomerulus | Receives filtrate; visceral layer = podocytes |
| **Podocytes** | Cells with foot processes (pedicels) forming filtration slits | Slit diaphragms (nephrin protein) prevent protein filtration - defects → nephrotic syndrome (e.g., minimal change disease) |
| **Glomerular basement membrane (GBM)** | Negatively charged matrix between endothelium and podocytes | Size + charge selectivity; anti-GBM antibodies → Goodpasture |
| **Fenestrated endothelium** | Capillary endothelium with 70–100 nm pores | Allows water and small solutes through; cells stay in lumen |
| **Mesangial cells** | Pericyte-like cells in glomerular tuft | Structural support; contract to regulate filtration surface; phagocytic; proliferate in glomerulonephritis |

### Nephron segments (functional table)

<Figure name="urinary/podocytes-openstax" caption="Podocytes (OpenStax) - foot processes wrap glomerular capillaries; slits between foot processes bridged by slit diaphragms form the size-selective filter (proteins > ~60 kDa retained)." />

<Figure name="urinary/renal-corpuscle" caption="Renal corpuscle - glomerulus + Bowman's capsule. Afferent arteriole in, efferent arteriole out; podocytes wrap capillary loops; mesangial cells in the center; juxtaglomerular apparatus (JG cells + macula densa) at the vascular pole." />

| Segment | What it does | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)** | Bulk reabsorption: ~65% of Na⁺, water, and HCO₃⁻; 100% of glucose, amino acids; secretes H⁺, organic acids/bases | Sets baseline output; brush border + many mitochondria; site of carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (acetazolamide) and SGLT2 inhibitors |
| **Thin descending limb of Henle** | Passive water reabsorption (osmotic) | Concentrates urine on the way down; impermeable to solute |
| **Thin ascending limb** | Passive NaCl efflux | Impermeable to water |
| **Thick ascending limb (TAL)** | Active NaCl reabsorption via NKCC2; impermeable to water | Builds the medullary gradient; **loop diuretic target** (furosemide blocks NKCC2) |
| **Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)** | Fine-tuning: Na⁺/Cl⁻ reabsorption via NCC; Ca²⁺ reabsorption under PTH | **Thiazide diuretic target** (blocks NCC); PTH-stimulated Ca²⁺ reabsorption here |
| **Connecting tubule + collecting duct (principal cells)** | Na⁺ reabsorption via ENaC (aldosterone-driven); K⁺ secretion; H₂O via aquaporin-2 (ADH-driven) | **Aldosterone target** (spironolactone blocks the receptor); **ADH target** (V2 receptor); fine adjustment of final urine |
| **Collecting duct (α-intercalated cells)** | H⁺ secretion (H⁺ ATPase, H⁺/K⁺ ATPase); HCO₃⁻ reabsorption | Acid-base regulation in acidemia |
| **Collecting duct (β-intercalated cells)** | HCO₃⁻ secretion; H⁺ reabsorption | Active in alkalemia |

### Juxtaglomerular apparatus

| Cell type | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Macula densa** | Specialized DCT cells sensing distal NaCl | High distal NaCl → release adenosine → afferent constriction (tubuloglomerular feedback); low NaCl → ↑ renin |
| **Juxtaglomerular (granular) cells** | Modified afferent arteriole smooth muscle; secrete renin | β1-receptor stimulation, low perfusion, and low NaCl → renin release → RAAS activation |
| **Extraglomerular mesangial cells** | Structural and signaling between macula densa and arterioles | Coordinate the feedback loop |

### Endocrine functions of the kidney

| Hormone | Source within kidney | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Renin** | JG cells | Cleaves angiotensinogen → angiotensin I; starts RAAS |
| **Erythropoietin (EPO)** | Peritubular interstitial fibroblasts of the cortex | Stimulates RBC production; deficient in CKD → anemia |
| **Calcitriol (active vitamin D)** | PCT (1α-hydroxylase) | ↑ intestinal Ca²⁺ absorption; deficient in CKD → renal osteodystrophy |
| **Prostaglandins** | Various tubular and interstitial cells | Vasodilate afferent arteriole; protective in volume depletion; NSAIDs block → AKI in vulnerable patients |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX renal priorities:**
- **Daily weight** is the single best fluid-status indicator - 1 kg = 1 L.
- **Strict I/O** in renal patients; calorie/protein/K⁺ restrictions per provider.
- **AV fistula care**: assess **thrill (palpate) + bruit (auscultate)**; no BP, no blood draw, no IV in that arm.
- **Foley insertion**: sterile technique; secure to thigh; remove ASAP to prevent CAUTI.
- **Renal biopsy post-procedure**: bed rest 24 h, monitor for flank pain + hematuria, vital signs.
- **Nephrolithiasis**: strain urine; encourage fluids; pain control.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Renal anatomy pearls:**
- **Right kidney lower than left** (because of liver).
- **Hilum order**: anterior → posterior = **V-A-U** (vein, artery, ureter).
- **Left renal vein crosses anterior to aorta + posterior to SMA** → nutcracker compression; receives left gonadal, left adrenal, left lumbar veins.
- **Renal cell carcinoma** invades the renal vein and IVC; may extend to right atrium.
- **Wilms tumor (kids)**: most common solid renal tumor in children; WT1 gene; can be part of WAGR or Denys-Drash syndromes.
- **Horseshoe kidney**: gets caught under IMA during ascent; associated with Turner syndrome.
</HighYield>

## Urinary bladder and outflow

<Figure name="urinary/bladder" caption="Urinary bladder anatomy - note the trigone (smooth triangle between ureteric orifices and internal urethral orifice), detrusor muscle, internal urethral sphincter (involuntary, smooth muscle), and external urethral sphincter (voluntary, skeletal muscle)." />

The bladder is a hollow muscular organ in the pelvis, retropubic when empty, rising into the abdomen when full (~500 mL capacity). Key features:

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Detrusor muscle** | Three layers of smooth muscle (longitudinal, circular, longitudinal) | Contracts during micturition; targeted by anticholinergics (oxybutynin) for overactive bladder |
| **Trigone** | Smooth triangle on posterior wall between 2 ureteric orifices + internal urethral orifice | Mesonephric origin (vs rest of bladder = urogenital sinus); doesn't expand; tumor-prone |
| **Ureteric orifices** | Where ureters enter bladder; oblique entry through wall acts as flap valve | Prevents reflux during bladder contraction; failure → VUR (vesicoureteric reflux) → recurrent UTI + scarring |
| **Internal urethral sphincter** | Smooth muscle, involuntary (sympathetic) | Holds urine reflexively; relaxed during micturition reflex |
| **External urethral sphincter** | Skeletal muscle, voluntary (pudendal n. - S2–S4) | Voluntary continence; damaged in childbirth, prostatectomy |
| **Urachus (median umbilical ligament)** | Fibrous remnant of allantois from fetal bladder to umbilicus | Patent urachus → urine leak from umbilicus in newborn |

### Micturition reflex

**Filling phase**: sympathetic (T11–L2) keeps detrusor relaxed + internal sphincter tight via β3 + α1 receptors.
**Voiding phase**: parasympathetic (S2–S4) contracts detrusor (M3) + reflexively relaxes internal sphincter. Voluntary cortical override on external sphincter (pudendal).

<Clinical>
**Neurogenic bladder**: above pontine micturition center → spastic (reflex); SCI below pons but above sacral → spastic with detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia; sacral cord/cauda equina → flaccid + retention; diabetic neuropathy → hypotonic + retention.

**BPH** (older men): prostatic enlargement → urethral obstruction → hesitancy, weak stream, nocturia; treat with α1-blockers (tamsulosin) and 5α-reductase inhibitors (finasteride).

**Stress incontinence** (laughing, sneezing): weak pelvic floor / external sphincter; pelvic-floor exercises (Kegels), surgery.
**Urge incontinence**: overactive detrusor; anticholinergics or β3 agonists (mirabegron).
</Clinical>
