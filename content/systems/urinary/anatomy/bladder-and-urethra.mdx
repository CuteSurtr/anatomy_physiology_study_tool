After urine leaves the renal pelvis, it travels through the **ureter → bladder → urethra** for storage and timed elimination. This conduit has three jobs: protect the upper urinary tract, store urine continently, and empty it under voluntary command. Each step has a distinctive anatomy, a different nerve supply, and characteristic clinical syndromes.

## Ureters

- **Length**: 25–30 cm; retroperitoneal throughout their course.
- **Three constrictions** - three classic sites of stone impaction:
  1. **Ureteropelvic junction (UPJ)** - kidney → ureter
  2. **Crossing the pelvic brim** (over common iliac vessels)
  3. **Ureterovesical junction (UVJ)** - entry into bladder wall

- **Course**:
  - Run on **psoas major**, crossed by the **gonadal vessels** ("water under the bridge" - ureter passes posterior).
  - Enter the bladder obliquely → creates a valve effect (prevents reflux).
  - In females, the ureter passes **under the uterine artery** ("water under the bridge" - surgical hazard in hysterectomy).

- **Wall**: three layers - inner mucosa (transitional epithelium / urothelium), middle smooth muscle (peristaltic), outer adventitia.

- **Blood supply**: segmental, from whatever artery is nearby (renal, gonadal, common iliac, internal iliac, uterine/vesical). Important - ureters can be devascularized in surgery if dissected aggressively.

<Clinical>
**Renal colic** - kidney stone passing through ureter. Classic: severe **colicky flank pain** radiating to **groin/labia/scrotum** (referred via T11–L2), hematuria, restless patient. Stones &lt;5 mm usually pass; 5–10 mm sometimes; >10 mm need intervention. CT without contrast is gold standard.

**Iatrogenic ureteral injury** - most common during hysterectomy (uterine artery–ureter crossing) or colectomy. Recognized late = urinoma, fistula. Always identify the ureters during pelvic surgery.
</Clinical>

## Bladder

- **Location**: subperitoneal in the lesser pelvis, behind the pubic symphysis. When full it rises into the abdomen, lifting the parietal peritoneum off the anterior abdominal wall - that's why **suprapubic catheterization** is possible without entering the peritoneum.
- **Shape**: pyramidal when empty, ovoid when full. Has an **apex** (anterior, anchored to umbilicus by median umbilical ligament = obliterated urachus), **body**, **fundus/base** (posterior), and **neck** (inferior, leads to urethra).

### Internal landmarks
| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigone** | Smooth triangular area at base, bounded by 2 ureteric orifices + internal urethral orifice. Embryologically derived from mesoderm (rest of bladder is endoderm). **Site of urothelial carcinoma origin** in many cases |
| **Rugae** | Mucosal folds in the rest of the bladder - disappear with distension |
| **Detrusor muscle** | Smooth muscle wall, three poorly-defined layers, parasympathetic contraction |
| **Internal urethral sphincter** | At neck; smooth muscle, **involuntary**, sympathetic α1 control (NE → contraction → continence) |

### Bladder relations
| Direction | Adjacent structure |
|---|---|
| **Anterior** | Pubic symphysis (separated by retropubic space of Retzius - fat-filled, ideal for retropubic prostatectomy) |
| **Superior** | Peritoneum (covers dome) + small intestine / sigmoid |
| **Posterior (male)** | Rectum (separated by rectovesical pouch + seminal vesicles + ductus deferens) |
| **Posterior (female)** | Cervix + anterior vagina |
| **Inferior (male)** | Prostate gland sits on bladder neck |
| **Inferior (female)** | Pelvic floor; urethra short |

## Urethra

<Figure name="urinary/urethra-slide" caption="Lecture slide highlighting the urethra as the final outflow channel, with its internal and external sphincters and the added clinical importance of the male prostatic segment." />

### Male - 4 segments, 18–20 cm
| Segment | Length | Surrounding | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-prostatic** | 1 cm | Bladder neck | Internal urethral sphincter |
| **Prostatic** | 3–4 cm | Prostate | Widest + most dilatable; receives ejaculatory ducts + prostatic ducts; verumontanum (utricle = remnant of müllerian) |
| **Membranous** | 1–2 cm | External urethral sphincter (skeletal, voluntary, somatic - pudendal n.) | **Narrowest + least distensible** - at risk in straddle injuries / pelvic fracture |
| **Spongy (penile)** | 15 cm | Corpus spongiosum | Bulbar segment dilates posteriorly; receives bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands; opens at external urethral meatus |

### Female - 4 cm
Single straight tube, passes through the urogenital diaphragm + perineal membrane; opens between clitoris and vaginal orifice. Short length = ↑ UTI risk.

<Clinical>
**Straddle injury** in males injures the **bulbar urethra** (forced against pubic ramus); pelvic fracture injures the **membranous urethra** (avulsion above the urogenital diaphragm). Suspect with blood at meatus, scrotal hematoma, high-riding prostate. **Do NOT insert Foley** until retrograde urethrogram clears the urethra.

**Hypospadias** - urethral meatus on the ventral surface of the penis; failure of urethral folds to fuse. Most common penile congenital anomaly. **Epispadias** (dorsal opening) is much rarer and associated with bladder exstrophy.
</Clinical>

## Innervation - the micturition reflex

| Function | Nerve | Receptor | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Detrusor contraction** (void) | **Parasympathetic** S2–S4 (pelvic splanchnics) | **M3** muscarinic | Contraction → void |
| **Internal sphincter contraction** (storage) | **Sympathetic** L1–L2 (hypogastric n.) | **α1** | Tightens neck → continence; also β3 → detrusor relaxation |
| **External sphincter** (voluntary) | **Somatic** S2–S4 (pudendal n.) | Nicotinic | Voluntary continence |
| **Sensory afferents** | Pelvic splanchnic (fullness) + hypogastric (pain) | - | Spinal cord → pontine micturition center |

<Mnemonic>
**"S2–4 keeps the urine off the floor"** - both parasympathetic (detrusor) and somatic (external sphincter) come from S2–S4. Sympathetic (T11–L2, storage) is up higher.
</Mnemonic>

### Storage vs voiding - switching between modes

| Phase | Spinal level | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Storage** | Sympathetic (T11–L2) dominant | Detrusor relaxed (β3); internal sphincter tight (α1); external sphincter tight (voluntary) |
| **Voiding** | Parasympathetic (S2–S4) dominant + voluntary release of external sphincter | Detrusor contracts (M3); internal sphincter relaxes; pelvic floor relaxes |

The **pontine micturition center (PMC)** in the brainstem coordinates this switch. Cortical input from the prefrontal cortex provides voluntary control over when to allow PMC activation.

## Bladder pharmacology - drug classes that change continence

| Class | Mechanism | Use | Side effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Muscarinic antagonists** (oxybutynin, tolterodine, solifenacin) | Block M3 → relax detrusor | Overactive bladder | Dry mouth, blurred vision, confusion in elderly |
| **β3 agonists** (mirabegron) | Activate β3 → relax detrusor | OAB; fewer anticholinergic effects | Mild HTN |
| **α1 antagonists** (tamsulosin, doxazosin) | Relax internal sphincter + prostate | BPH outflow obstruction | Orthostatic hypotension |
| **5α-reductase inhibitors** (finasteride) | ↓ DHT → ↓ prostate volume | BPH (months to effect) | Sexual side effects |
| **Cholinergic agonists** (bethanechol) | Stimulate detrusor M3 | Post-op urinary retention | Cholinergic crisis |
| **Loop diuretics** | ↑ urine volume | Volume overload | Nocturia worsens UI |

## Neurogenic bladder - when innervation fails

| Type | Lesion | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Spastic (upper motor neuron)** | Above S2 - stroke, MS, suprasacral cord injury | Small, hyperreflexic bladder; detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia; urge incontinence |
| **Flaccid (lower motor neuron)** | At or below S2 - cauda equina, sacral cord injury, DM autonomic neuropathy | Large atonic bladder; overflow incontinence |
| **Stress incontinence** | Pelvic floor + urethral support weakness (multiparity, post-menopause) | Leak with cough, sneeze, exercise. Treat: pelvic floor exercises, urethral sling |
| **Urge incontinence** | Detrusor overactivity (idiopathic, brain lesion, infection) | "Gotta go!" Treat: bladder training + antimuscarinic ± β3 agonist |
| **Overflow** | Outflow obstruction (BPH) or atonic bladder | Constant dribbling; large post-void residual |

## Function reference - every component

| Component | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Ureteric peristalsis** | Drives urine downward; 1–5 contractions/min | Lost after transplant (causes initial reflux); reflux nephropathy |
| **UVJ oblique entry** | Functional valve against reflux | Failure = **vesicoureteral reflux (VUR)** → recurrent UTI + renal scarring in children |
| **Trigone** | Fixed bladder base; ureteric orifices | Common site of urothelial CA; sensitive to bladder fullness |
| **Detrusor** | Bladder wall smooth muscle | Hypertrophies in BPH ("trabeculated" bladder); fails in long-standing obstruction |
| **Internal urethral sphincter** | Involuntary continence | Damaged in TURP → retrograde ejaculation (semen → bladder) |
| **External urethral sphincter** | Voluntary continence | Damaged in radical prostatectomy → stress incontinence |
| **Membranous urethra** | Shortest, narrowest male segment | Site of pelvic-fracture rupture; "high-riding" prostate |
| **Urachus** | Embryologic remnant from allantois → median umbilical ligament | Patent urachus = umbilical urine; urachal cyst can develop |
| **Cowper's (bulbourethral) glands** | Pre-ejaculate mucus | Site of trauma in pelvic fractures; rarely abscess |
| **Pudendal nerve (S2–S4)** | Sensory + motor to external sphincter + perineum + clitoris/penis | Block useful for childbirth, hemorrhoids; injury → fecal + urinary incontinence |
| **Pelvic splanchnics (S2–S4)** | Parasympathetic to detrusor | Loss → atonic bladder |
| **Hypogastric n. (T11–L2)** | Sympathetic to internal sphincter | Damaged in retroperitoneal surgery → retrograde ejaculation in males |

## Why this matters

<HighYield variant="boards">
- **UTI risk female > male** - short urethra (4 cm vs 18 cm); E. coli most common; complicated UTI = pregnancy, men, indwelling catheter, immunocompromised.
- **Acute prostatitis** vs **cystitis**: prostate exam in prostatitis = exquisitely tender (don't vigorously massage - bacteremia). Cystitis = no prostate involvement.
- **BPH** (transition zone) vs **prostate CA** (peripheral zone). PSA elevated in both; DRE: BPH smooth/symmetric, CA hard/nodular.
- **TURP syndrome** - hyponatremia from absorption of irrigation fluid (glycine/sorbitol) during transurethral resection of prostate. Confusion, seizures.
- **Bladder cancer**: painless hematuria + smoking history; urothelial (transitional cell) >> squamous (Schistosoma haematobium endemic areas). Cystoscopy + cytology.
- **Cauda equina syndrome**: bilateral leg pain, saddle anesthesia, **urinary retention** + **fecal incontinence**. Surgical emergency.
- **Anticholinergic toxidrome** in elderly given oxybutynin: "blind as a bat, mad as a hatter, red as a beet, hot as a hare, dry as a bone." Use safer alternatives (mirabegron).
</HighYield>
