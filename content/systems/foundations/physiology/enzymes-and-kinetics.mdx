Almost every physiologic process is enzymatic. **Enzymes** are catalytic proteins (rarely RNA) that lower the activation energy of a reaction without being consumed. They give the body **specificity** (one enzyme, one reaction), **speed** (10⁶ to 10¹² rate acceleration), and **control points** (every regulated pathway has at least one rate-limiting enzyme).

## Michaelis-Menten kinetics

Most enzymes follow a hyperbolic substrate-velocity curve:

`V = (Vmax × [S]) / (Km + [S])`

| Parameter | What it represents | Clinical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Vmax** | Maximum reaction velocity when enzyme is saturated with substrate | Reflects amount + turnover of enzyme |
| **Km** | Substrate concentration giving ½ Vmax | Inverse of affinity - small Km = high affinity |
| **kcat** | Turnover number (substrates converted per enzyme per second) | Catalytic efficiency = kcat / Km |

**Lineweaver-Burk plot** (1/V vs 1/[S]) - linearizes the equation. The x-intercept = −1/Km, the y-intercept = 1/Vmax.

### Inhibitor types - distinguishable on Lineweaver-Burk

| Inhibitor | Effect on Km | Effect on Vmax | Effect on lines | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Competitive** | ↑ (lower apparent affinity) | Unchanged | Same y-intercept, different x | Methotrexate competes with folate for DHFR |
| **Non-competitive** | Unchanged | ↓ | Same x-intercept, different y | Allosteric inhibitors |
| **Uncompetitive** | ↓ | ↓ | Parallel lines | Lithium on inositol monophosphatase |
| **Mixed** | Changes | Changes | Lines cross outside axes | Many natural feedback inhibitors |

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Competitive vs non-competitive** is the most common exam framework:
- Competitive → can be overcome by ↑ substrate. Methanol poisoning treated with fomepizole (competitive inhibitor of alcohol dehydrogenase) or ethanol (competing substrate).
- Non-competitive → cannot be overcome by ↑ substrate. Some allosteric inhibitors and irreversible inhibitors (organophosphate AChE inhibitors) fit this pattern functionally.
</HighYield>

## Enzyme regulation - five levers

The body controls enzymes via:

1. **Substrate availability** - feed enzyme, get product
2. **Allosteric modulation** - effectors bind elsewhere, change activity
3. **Covalent modification** - usually phosphorylation/dephosphorylation
4. **Proteolytic activation** - zymogens (trypsinogen → trypsin) - typical in GI + clotting
5. **Gene expression** - transcription rate determines enzyme abundance

|---|---|---|
| **B1 (Thiamine)** | TPP | Pyruvate dehydrogenase, α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, transketolase, branched-chain ketoacid dehydrogenase |
| **B2 (Riboflavin)** | FAD, FMN | Oxidoreductases, ETC complex II |
| **B3 (Niacin)** | NAD⁺, NADP⁺ | Dehydrogenases, redox |
| **B5 (Pantothenate)** | CoA | Acyl group transfer (acetyl-CoA, succinyl-CoA) |
| **B6 (Pyridoxine)** | PLP | Transaminases, decarboxylases, ALA synthase, GABA synthesis |
| **B7 (Biotin)** | Biotin | **Carboxylation** - pyruvate carboxylase, acetyl-CoA carboxylase, propionyl-CoA carboxylase |
| **B9 (Folate)** | THF | One-carbon transfer, purine + thymidine synthesis |
| **B12 (Cobalamin)** | Methylcobalamin, adenosylcobalamin | Methionine synthase, methylmalonyl-CoA mutase |
| **C (Ascorbate)** | Ascorbate | Collagen prolyl + lysyl hydroxylase, dopamine β-hydroxylase, Fe absorption |

<Mnemonic title="Vitamin deficiencies you must spot">
- **B1 (thiamine)** - **Wernicke encephalopathy** (alcoholic): ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, confusion. Korsakoff = chronic. Beriberi (cardiac + neurologic).
- **B3 (niacin)** - **Pellagra**: dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia, death.
- **B6 (pyridoxine)** - peripheral neuropathy (isoniazid co-administer to prevent).
- **B7 (biotin)** - rare; alopecia, dermatitis, ketolactic acidosis (raw egg whites - avidin).
- **B9 (folate)** - macrocytic anemia + neural tube defects in pregnancy; NO neurological deficit (vs B12).
- **B12 (cobalamin)** - macrocytic anemia + subacute combined degeneration of spinal cord (dorsal columns + corticospinal tracts).
- **C (ascorbate)** - **Scurvy**: bleeding gums, perifollicular hemorrhage, poor wound healing, corkscrew hairs.
- **Vitamin K** - bleeding, prolonged PT. Newborns get IM vitamin K.
- **Vitamin A** - night blindness, xerophthalmia, follicular hyperkeratosis.
- **Vitamin D** - rickets/osteomalacia (see integumentary).
- **Vitamin E** - hemolysis, neuropathy, ataxia.
</Mnemonic>

## Acid-base buffering chemistry

Body pH is defended in narrow range 7.35-7.45. Three lines of defense:

1. **Chemical buffers** (seconds)
2. **Respiratory compensation** (minutes)
3. **Renal compensation** (days)

### The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation

`pH = pKa + log([A⁻] / [HA])`

For the main extracellular buffer (bicarbonate / carbonic acid):
`pH = 6.1 + log([HCO₃⁻] / (0.03 × PaCO₂))`

The system works because:
- CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ (catalyzed by carbonic anhydrase)
- Lungs control PaCO₂ (volatile acid - 15,000 mEq/day cleared)
- Kidneys control HCO₃⁻ (regenerate + reabsorb) and excrete fixed acid (NH₄⁺, titratable acidity)

### Major body buffers

| Buffer | Where | pKa | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻/H₂CO₃)** | Extracellular | 6.1 | Dominant ECF buffer; open system - lungs can blow off CO₂ |
| **Phosphate (HPO₄²⁻/H₂PO₄⁻)** | Intracellular + urine | 6.8 | Major intracellular buffer + urinary titratable acid |
| **Hemoglobin** | RBCs | Multiple histidine residues | Buffers CO₂ at tissue (Bohr/Haldane) |
| **Plasma proteins** | ECF | ~7 | Histidines and N-termini |
| **Ammonia/Ammonium (NH₃/NH₄⁺)** | Urine | 9.2 | Renal generated; principal mechanism of new HCO₃⁻ |

<Clinical>
**The 4-step acid-base approach to any ABG:**

1. **pH** - acidemic (<7.35) or alkalemic (>7.45)?
2. **PaCO₂ + HCO₃⁻** - which is the primary disturbance?
   - ↓ pH + ↑ PaCO₂ → respiratory acidosis
   - ↓ pH + ↓ HCO₃⁻ → metabolic acidosis
   - ↑ pH + ↓ PaCO₂ → respiratory alkalosis
   - ↑ pH + ↑ HCO₃⁻ → metabolic alkalosis
3. **Compensation appropriate?**
   - Metabolic acidosis: Winter's formula PaCO₂ = 1.5 × HCO₃⁻ + 8 ± 2
   - Acute respiratory: HCO₃⁻ rises 1 mEq per 10 mmHg PaCO₂ rise
   - Chronic respiratory: HCO₃⁻ rises 3-4 mEq per 10 mmHg
4. **Anion gap (Na⁺ − Cl⁻ − HCO₃⁻, normal ~10±2)?**
   - HAGMA causes: **MUDPILES** - Methanol, Uremia, DKA, Propylene glycol, Iron/Isoniazid/Inhalants, Lactic acidosis, Ethylene glycol, Salicylates
   - NAGMA causes: **HARDASS** - Hyperalimentation, Addison disease, Renal tubular acidosis, Diarrhea, Acetazolamide, Spironolactone, Saline infusion
</Clinical>

## Inborn errors of metabolism - enzyme deficiencies that hit physiology

| Disease | Enzyme deficient | Major feature |
|---|---|---|
| **PKU** | Phenylalanine hydroxylase | Intellectual disability, musty body odor; treat with low-Phe diet, supplement Tyr |
| **Galactosemia** | GALT | Failure to thrive, jaundice, cataracts; avoid lactose |
| **Hereditary fructose intolerance** | Aldolase B | Hypoglycemia after fructose, avoid fructose/sucrose |
| **Maple syrup urine disease** | Branched-chain α-ketoacid dehydrogenase | Burnt sugar urine, neurologic decompensation |
| **G6PD deficiency** | G6PD (PPP) | Hemolytic anemia with oxidants (sulfa, antimalarials, fava); X-linked |
| **Tay-Sachs** | Hexosaminidase A | Lysosomal GM2 ganglioside buildup → cherry-red spot, neurologic decline |
| **Pompe** | Acid α-glucosidase | Glycogen in lysosomes; **cardiomegaly + hypotonia in infants** |
| **McArdle** | Muscle phosphorylase | Exercise intolerance, second wind |
| **von Gierke (GSD-I)** | Glucose-6-phosphatase | Severe fasting hypoglycemia, hepatomegaly, lactic acidosis |
| **OTC deficiency** | Ornithine transcarbamylase | Hyperammonemia (urea cycle); X-linked |
| **Lesch-Nyhan** | HGPRT | Hyperuricemia, self-mutilation; X-linked |
