Every hormone, neurotransmitter, growth factor, and cytokine works through a small number of conserved molecular pathways. Master these **six pathways** and the chemistry of every endocrine and pharmacology lecture clicks into place: GPCRs with Gs/Gi/Gq, receptor tyrosine kinases, steroid hormone receptors, ligand-gated ion channels, JAK-STAT, and guanylyl cyclase.

## The four classes of cell-surface and intracellular receptors

<Figure name="foundations/cell-membrane" caption="Receptor types in the cell — membrane (GPCRs, RTKs, ligand-gated channels) and intracellular (nuclear receptors for steroids + thyroid + vitamin D)." />

| Class | Where the receptor is | Signal type | Speed | Example ligands |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ligand-gated ion channels (ionotropic)** | Plasma membrane | Direct ion flow | Milliseconds | Nicotinic ACh, GABA-A, NMDA glutamate |
| **G-protein coupled receptors (GPCR, metabotropic)** | Plasma membrane | Second messenger via G protein | Seconds | β-adrenergic, muscarinic, most peptide hormones |
| **Enzyme-linked / catalytic receptors** | Plasma membrane | Receptor itself is enzyme (tyrosine kinase, serine/threonine kinase, guanylyl cyclase) | Minutes | Insulin, IGF, EGF, ANP, TGF-β |
| **Nuclear / intracellular receptors** | Cytoplasm or nucleus | Direct transcription factor | Hours | Steroid hormones, thyroid, vitamin D, retinoic acid |

## GPCRs - the workhorse pathway

<Figure name="foundations/gpcr-pathway" caption="G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) - 7-pass transmembrane receptor. Ligand binding triggers GDP→GTP exchange on Gα; the activated α subunit dissociates and activates downstream effectors (Wikimedia)." />

A GPCR is a 7-pass transmembrane protein. Ligand binding → conformational change → exchange of GDP for GTP on the α subunit of a heterotrimeric **G protein (αβγ)** → α subunit dissociates and activates downstream effectors.

Three major G-protein subtypes determine the downstream signal:

| G protein | Effector | Second messenger | Net effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gs** | Activates **adenylyl cyclase (AC)** | **↑ cAMP** → activates PKA | ↑ phosphorylation of many proteins |
| **Gi** | Inhibits **AC** | **↓ cAMP** | Opposite of Gs |
| **Gq** | Activates **phospholipase C (PLC-β)** | **IP₃ + DAG** → ↑ intracellular Ca²⁺ + activate PKC | Smooth muscle contraction, secretion |

### The cAMP-PKA pathway in detail

1. Ligand binds Gs-coupled GPCR (e.g., β₁ adrenergic on cardiomyocyte, glucagon receptor on hepatocyte).
2. Gαs swaps GDP for GTP, dissociates from βγ.
3. Gαs activates **adenylyl cyclase** → ATP → **cAMP** (cyclic AMP).
4. cAMP binds the regulatory subunits of **protein kinase A (PKA)**, releasing the catalytic subunits.
5. PKA phosphorylates serines/threonines on target proteins. Examples:
   - Cardiac L-type Ca²⁺ channels → ↑ Ca²⁺ entry → ↑ contractility
   - Glycogen phosphorylase kinase → ↑ glycogenolysis
   - Hormone-sensitive lipase → ↑ lipolysis
   - CREB transcription factor → new gene expression
6. cAMP is degraded by **phosphodiesterases (PDEs)**. Methylxanthines (caffeine, theophylline) and PDE inhibitors (milrinone, sildenafil) prolong cAMP/cGMP signaling.

### The Gq-PLC-IP₃/DAG pathway

1. Ligand binds Gq-coupled GPCR (α₁ adrenergic, M₁/M₃ muscarinic, H₁ histamine, angiotensin II AT1, vasopressin V₁).
2. Gαq activates **phospholipase C-β (PLC-β)**.
3. PLC-β cleaves **PIP₂ (phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate)** → **IP₃ + DAG**.
4. IP₃ → ER **IP₃ receptor** → Ca²⁺ release from ER.
5. DAG remains in the membrane and activates **protein kinase C (PKC)** with Ca²⁺ as cofactor.
6. PKC phosphorylates downstream targets.

Net effect: smooth muscle contraction (α₁ vasoconstriction), gland secretion, exocrine activity, vasopressin V₁ vascular effects.

## Receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) - growth and metabolism

<Figure name="foundations/signal-transduction" caption="Signal transduction overview - RTKs (insulin receptor, EGFR) activate RAS/MAPK or PI3K/AKT cascades. Mutations are common oncogenic drivers." />

<Figure name="foundations/cell-membrane" caption="Cell plasma membrane - phospholipid bilayer with embedded receptors (RTKs, GPCRs, ligand-gated channels) and intracellular signaling machinery." />

<Figure name="foundations/animal-cell" caption="Cell-level context for signaling - signals from the plasma membrane converge on the cytoplasm and nucleus, where transcription factors and second messengers act." />

RTKs include the **insulin receptor**, IGF-1 receptor, EGF/ErbB family, FGF family, PDGF, VEGF.

Mechanism:
1. Ligand binds extracellular domain → receptor dimerization.
2. Dimerized receptors **trans-autophosphorylate** their intracellular tyrosine residues.
3. Phospho-tyrosines recruit adaptor proteins via **SH2 domains** (e.g., GRB2, IRS-1, PI3K p85).
4. Downstream cascades:
   - **PI3K → AKT → mTOR** - growth, anabolism, GLUT4 translocation (insulin), protein synthesis
   - **Ras → Raf → MEK → ERK (MAPK)** - proliferation, differentiation, gene expression
   - **STAT** activation in some

**Insulin's** unique RTK pathway: insulin binds → IRS-1 phosphorylated → PI3K → AKT → GLUT4 vesicles fuse with membrane → glucose enters muscle/fat. This is why type 2 DM, with AKT pathway resistance, has impaired glucose uptake.

## Nuclear receptors - steroid + thyroid pathway

<Figure name="endocrine/steroidogenesis-detail" caption="Steroid hormone synthesis — all derive from cholesterol; bind intracellular receptors → translocate to nucleus → transcription factors. Slow (hours) but powerful." />

Lipid-soluble hormones (steroids, thyroid hormone, vitamin D, retinoic acid) diffuse through the membrane and bind intracellular receptors. The receptor-ligand complex translocates to the nucleus, dimerizes, and binds DNA at **hormone response elements**, regulating transcription.

Effects manifest over hours (transcription, translation, protein appearance).

| Hormone | Receptor location |
|---|---|
| **Cortisol, aldosterone** | Cytoplasm → nucleus |
| **Estrogen, progesterone, androgens** | Nucleus |
| **Thyroid hormone (T₃)** | Nucleus, bound to DNA even without ligand |
| **Vitamin D, retinoic acid** | Nucleus, RXR heterodimers |

## Guanylyl cyclase pathway - the NO/ANP signal

<Figure name="cardiovascular/raas" caption="Guanylyl cyclase pathway is the counterweight to RAAS - NO (vasodilator) + ANP/BNP (natriuretic) both signal via cGMP. PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil) prolong this signal." />

Two forms:
- **Particulate (membrane) GC** - activated by **ANP, BNP, CNP** → ↑ cGMP → PKG → smooth muscle relaxation, natriuresis
- **Soluble (cytoplasmic) GC** - activated by **nitric oxide (NO)** → ↑ cGMP → vascular smooth muscle relaxation

cGMP is hydrolyzed by **PDE5** in vascular smooth muscle. **Sildenafil/tadalafil** inhibit PDE5 → prolonged cGMP → vasodilation in cavernosal arteries (erection) and pulmonary vessels (pulmonary HTN therapy).

## JAK-STAT - the cytokine pathway

Used by interferons, GH, prolactin, erythropoietin, thrombopoietin, leptin, many interleukins.

1. Ligand binds → receptor dimerizes
2. Associated **JAK kinases** trans-phosphorylate each other + receptor
3. Phospho-receptors recruit **STAT** proteins via SH2 domains
4. STATs are phosphorylated, dimerize, translocate to nucleus, activate transcription

JAK inhibitors (**tofacitinib, baricitinib, ruxolitinib**) are used in RA, psoriasis, ulcerative colitis, polycythemia vera.

## TGF-β and serine/threonine kinase receptors

TGF-β superfamily (including BMPs, activin, inhibin) → serine/threonine kinase receptors → **SMAD** transcription factors → growth inhibition, ECM production. Mutations underlie cancers (loss of growth suppression) and skeletal disorders (FGFR mutations in achondroplasia, BMP defects in fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva).

## Second messengers - the chemical vocabulary

<Figure name="foundations/camp-pathway" caption="Second messengers - cAMP (PKA), cGMP (PKG), IP3 (Ca²⁺ from ER), DAG (PKC), Ca²⁺ itself. Amplify the original ligand signal 10⁵-10⁶× via cascade." />

| Second messenger | Source | Major downstream targets |
|---|---|---|
| **cAMP** | AC from ATP | PKA, Epac, CNG channels |
| **cGMP** | GC from GTP | PKG, CNG channels in photoreceptors |
| **IP₃** | PLC from PIP₂ | IP₃R on ER → Ca²⁺ release |
| **DAG** | PLC from PIP₂ | PKC |
| **Ca²⁺** | ER (IP₃R, RyR), extracellular | Calmodulin, CaMK, calcineurin, troponin C, synaptotagmin |
| **NO** | NOS (eNOS, nNOS, iNOS) | sGC → cGMP |
| **PIP₃** | PI3K from PIP₂ | AKT (PKB) |

## Pharmacology - drug-receptor concepts

<Figure name="foundations/signal-transduction" caption="Related figure for context — Pharmacology - drug-receptor concepts." />

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Agonist** | Binds + activates receptor (full or partial) |
| **Antagonist** | Binds but does not activate; blocks agonist |
| **Inverse agonist** | Binds, stabilizes inactive state; reduces basal activity |
| **Competitive antagonist** | Binds same site as agonist; surmountable by ↑ agonist |
| **Non-competitive antagonist** | Binds allosteric site; not surmountable |
| **Affinity** (Kd) | Concentration at which 50% of receptors are bound (smaller Kd = tighter) |
| **Efficacy** | Maximum effect a drug can produce |
| **Potency** (EC50) | Concentration producing 50% of maximum effect |
| **Tachyphylaxis** | Rapid loss of response with repeated dosing (receptor desensitization / internalization) |

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Classic GPCR exam patterns:**
- A patient overdoses on cocaine - which receptor effect predominates? Cocaine blocks NE reuptake → ↑ NE at adrenergic receptors → tachycardia + vasoconstriction (β1 + α1) + mydriasis (α1).
- Why does sildenafil cause vasodilation? PDE5 inhibition → cGMP accumulates → vascular smooth muscle relaxation. Avoid with nitrates (NO donors also raise cGMP - additive hypotension).
- Why does milrinone increase contractility? PDE3 inhibition in cardiomyocytes → cAMP accumulates → ↑ PKA → ↑ L-type Ca²⁺ → ↑ contractility.
- Why does pertussis toxin cause whooping cough symptoms? ADP-ribosylates Gαi → permanent inhibition of Gi → cAMP cannot be turned off → mucous hypersecretion, lymphocytosis.
- Why does cholera toxin cause profuse watery diarrhea? ADP-ribosylates Gαs → permanent activation of Gs → ↑ cAMP in enterocytes → CFTR Cl⁻ channels stay open → massive secretion.
</HighYield>

## Receptor desensitization

Repeated agonist exposure → receptor:
1. **Phosphorylation** by GRKs (GPCR kinases) → recruits **β-arrestin** → uncouples G protein
2. **Internalization** via clathrin-coated pits → endosomes → either recycled or degraded
3. **Downregulation** - chronic signaling reduces receptor gene transcription

This explains **tolerance** to opioids, β-agonists in asthma, nitrate tachyphylaxis, and benzodiazepine tolerance.
