Every physiological process - muscle contraction, ion pumping, neurotransmission, biosynthesis - is paid for in **ATP**. The cell makes ATP by breaking carbon-hydrogen bonds and capturing the released energy in phosphate bonds. Glucose oxidation is the cleanest example: **C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + ~30 ATP**.

The pathway has three stages: **glycolysis** (cytosol), the **TCA / Krebs cycle** (mitochondrial matrix), and **oxidative phosphorylation** (inner mitochondrial membrane).

## Stage 1 - Glycolysis (cytosol, no oxygen needed)

Glucose (6 C) → 2 pyruvate (3 C each). 10 enzymatic steps. Investment of 2 ATP up front, payoff of 4 ATP at the end → **net +2 ATP** and **+2 NADH** per glucose.

**Key regulatory enzymes:**
- **Hexokinase / glucokinase** - traps glucose in the cell by phosphorylating it. Glucokinase (liver, β cell) has low affinity → only active at high glucose; not feedback-inhibited by G6P.
- **Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1)** - the **rate-limiting** step. Activated by AMP, fructose-2,6-bisphosphate; inhibited by ATP, citrate.
- **Pyruvate kinase** - generates the second ATP. Activated by F1,6BP (feedforward); inhibited by ATP and alanine.

**Fates of pyruvate:**
| Conditions | Fate |
|---|---|
| **Aerobic** (O₂ available) | Pyruvate dehydrogenase → acetyl-CoA → TCA cycle |
| **Anaerobic** (no O₂; exercising muscle, RBCs) | Lactate dehydrogenase → lactate; regenerates NAD⁺ to keep glycolysis going |
| **Alcoholic fermentation** (yeast, not human) | Ethanol + CO₂ |

## Stage 2 - Pyruvate dehydrogenase + TCA cycle (mitochondrial matrix)

### Pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH)

Pyruvate enters the mitochondrion via the pyruvate carrier and is converted to acetyl-CoA + CO₂ + NADH by the **PDH complex** - a giant complex of three enzymes (E1, E2, E3) using **5 cofactors**:

<Mnemonic title="PDH and α-KGDH cofactors - 'Tender Loving Care For Nancy'">
- **T**PP (thiamine pyrophosphate, vit B1)
- **L**ipoic acid
- **C**oA (pantothenic acid, vit B5)
- **F**AD (riboflavin, vit B2)
- **N**AD⁺ (niacin, vit B3)

Thiamine deficiency (alcoholism, malnutrition) impairs PDH and α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase → **Wernicke encephalopathy** (ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, confusion).
</Mnemonic>

### TCA cycle

Each acetyl-CoA (2 C) enters and is fully oxidized to **2 CO₂**, producing **3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP** per turn. Since glucose makes 2 pyruvate → 2 acetyl-CoA → 2 TCA turns.

**Per acetyl-CoA: 3 NADH + 1 FADH₂ + 1 GTP**
**Per glucose (2 turns): 6 NADH + 2 FADH₂ + 2 GTP**
**Plus PDH: 2 NADH**
**Plus glycolysis: 2 NADH + 2 ATP**

## Stage 3 - Electron transport chain + oxidative phosphorylation

The NADH and FADH₂ collected above carry **high-energy electrons** to the inner mitochondrial membrane, where four protein complexes (I, II, III, IV) pump H⁺ from matrix to intermembrane space, building a proton gradient. **ATP synthase (Complex V)** lets H⁺ flow back through it, using the energy to phosphorylate ADP → ATP.

### The four ETC complexes

| Complex | What enters | What exits | H⁺ pumped |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** (NADH dehydrogenase) | NADH | NAD⁺, e⁻ to CoQ | Yes |
| **II** (Succinate dehydrogenase) - the only TCA enzyme in the membrane | FADH₂ (from succinate → fumarate) | FAD, e⁻ to CoQ | No |
| **III** (Cytochrome bc1) | CoQH₂ | CoQ, e⁻ to cytochrome c | Yes |
| **IV** (Cytochrome c oxidase) | cytochrome c | Reduces O₂ → H₂O | Yes |
| **V** (ATP synthase) | H⁺ down gradient | ATP from ADP + Pi | (no, ↓ gradient) |

**O₂ is the final electron acceptor.** Without O₂, NADH and FADH₂ pile up, NAD⁺/FAD aren't regenerated, the TCA cycle stalls, and the cell must rely on glycolysis + lactate fermentation alone.

### ATP yield per glucose (rounded)

| Step | NADH | FADH₂ | GTP/ATP | Subtotal ATP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glycolysis | 2 (cytosolic; counted as 1.5 each = 3) | - | 2 (net) | 5 |
| 2× PDH | 2 (mitochondrial) | - | - | 5 |
| 2× TCA | 6 | 2 | 2 | 20 |
| **Total** | | | | **~30 ATP** (some sources say 32, depending on shuttle and stoichiometry) |

**One NADH → ~2.5 ATP. One FADH₂ → ~1.5 ATP.** The exact number depends on which shuttle is used to move cytosolic NADH into mitochondria (malate-aspartate gives 2.5; glycerol-3-phosphate gives 1.5).

## Inhibitors and uncouplers - poisons at every step

| Site | Inhibitor | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Complex I | Rotenone, MPP+ | Block NADH entry; MPTP causes parkinsonism by selective dopaminergic neuron damage |
| Complex II | Malonate | Competitive inhibitor of succinate dehydrogenase (research) |
| Complex III | Antimycin A | Blocks e⁻ flow |
| Complex IV | **CN⁻ (cyanide)**, **CO**, **H₂S**, sodium azide | Halt e⁻ transfer to O₂ → metabolic acidosis, hypoxia despite normal PaO₂ |
| ATP synthase | Oligomycin | Blocks H⁺ flow through ATP synthase |
| Uncouplers | **2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP)**, thermogenin (UCP1 in brown fat), aspirin (high dose) | H⁺ leaks back across the membrane bypassing ATP synthase → energy released as **heat**; ETC speeds up to maintain gradient; ATP ↓ |

<Clinical>
**Cyanide toxicity** - smoke inhalation (fire), industrial exposure, amygdalin (apricot pits, laetrile). Blocks complex IV → cells can't use O₂. Lactic acidosis with normal PaO₂; venous blood looks arterial (high SvO₂ because tissues can't extract O₂). Treat with **hydroxocobalamin** (binds CN to form cyanocobalamin) or **sodium nitrite + sodium thiosulfate** (nitrite induces methemoglobinemia which binds CN; thiosulfate provides sulfur for rhodanese to make thiocyanate).

**2,4-DNP** - 1930s diet drug; uncouples ETC → fat-burning, weight loss, **fatal hyperthermia**. Still illegally sold; lethal.

**Brown adipose tissue** uses **UCP1 (thermogenin)** for non-shivering thermogenesis in newborns and cold-acclimatized adults.
</Clinical>

## Fed and fasted state - what burns what

### Fed (postprandial, hours 0-4)

Insulin ↑. Liver:
- Uptakes glucose (GLUT2) → glycogen synthesis
- ↑ Glycolysis, ↑ pentose phosphate pathway
- ↑ Lipogenesis (acetyl-CoA → fatty acids → triglycerides → VLDL)
- Muscle uptakes glucose (GLUT4 to membrane) for glycogen + immediate use
- Adipose uptakes glucose + free fatty acids → triglyceride storage

### Fasting (hours 4-24)

Insulin ↓, glucagon ↑. Liver:
- Glycogen → glucose (glycogenolysis); ~24-hour supply
- Gluconeogenesis from lactate (Cori cycle), glycerol, amino acids (especially alanine - the glucose-alanine cycle)
- Adipose lipolysis → FFA → muscle uses FFA (β-oxidation)
- Brain continues to use glucose

### Starvation (days)

- Glycogen exhausted by day 1
- Maximal gluconeogenesis (limit ~80 g/day for brain)
- **Ketogenesis** from acetyl-CoA: β-hydroxybutyrate, acetoacetate, acetone. Brain shifts to ketones as primary fuel after ~3 days → spares muscle protein.
- Heart and skeletal muscle use FFA + ketones; brain on ketones; only RBCs and renal medulla still need glucose (no mitochondria; anaerobic)

## β-Oxidation - fat burning

Long-chain FFAs are activated to fatty acyl-CoA in cytoplasm, transported into mitochondria via the **carnitine shuttle** (CPT-I, CPT-II), then cycled through β-oxidation: each turn cleaves 2 carbons → acetyl-CoA + 1 NADH + 1 FADH₂.

**Palmitate (16 C) → 8 acetyl-CoA + 7 NADH + 7 FADH₂** in 7 cycles. Each acetyl-CoA then enters TCA. Net per palmitate ≈ **106 ATP** - vastly more than glucose. Fat is the most energy-dense fuel.

**Disorders:**
- **CPT-II deficiency** → muscle weakness, rhabdomyolysis during exercise/fasting (can't use fat)
- **MCAD deficiency** (medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase) → fasting hypoketotic hypoglycemia in infants
