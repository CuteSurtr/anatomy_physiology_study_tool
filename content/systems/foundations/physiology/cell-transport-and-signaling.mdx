Every physiological process relies on **moving stuff across membranes** (transport) and **passing information** (signaling). Five transport categories, four major signaling cascades, dozens of disease implications. Mastering these explains nearly every drug class.

<Figure name="foundations/transport" caption="Facilitated diffusion via a membrane channel protein - passive but selective." />

## Membrane structure recap

- **Phospholipid bilayer** - hydrophobic interior + polar surfaces.
- **Cholesterol** - controls fluidity (more = stiffer membrane); ~30% of plasma membrane mass.
- **Integral membrane proteins** - span the bilayer; transporters, channels, receptors.
- **Peripheral membrane proteins** - attached on one side.
- **Glycocalyx** - sugar chains on extracellular surface; recognition + adhesion.

Bilayer is **freely permeable** to: O₂, CO₂, N₂, small uncharged molecules (water - slowly), steroid hormones (rapidly).
**Impermeable** to: ions, charged molecules, large polar molecules → need transporters.

## Five modes of membrane transport

| Mode | Energy | Direction | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple diffusion** | None (entropy) | Down gradient | O₂, CO₂, urea, steroids, NH₃, ethanol |
| **Facilitated diffusion** | None | Down gradient via specific protein | Glucose via GLUT, water via aquaporins, ions via channels |
| **Primary active transport** | ATP directly | Against gradient | Na/K-ATPase, Ca-ATPase (SERCA, PMCA), H/K-ATPase (stomach), H-ATPase (lysosome) |
| **Secondary active transport** | Gradient (usually Na⁺) | Against own gradient using another's | SGLT (glucose + Na), Na/Ca exchanger, NKCC2 (kidney) |
| **Vesicular** | ATP | Bulk | Endocytosis (receptor-mediated, phagocytosis, pinocytosis), exocytosis |

### Key pumps
| Pump | Move | Stoichiometry | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase** | 3 Na out, 2 K in | 1 ATP | The most important pump; sets electrochemical gradient. **Digoxin inhibits → ↑ intracellular Na → ↓ Na/Ca exchange → ↑ intracellular Ca → ↑ cardiac contractility** |
| **Ca²⁺-ATPase (SERCA)** | Ca²⁺ into SR | 1 ATP / 2 Ca²⁺ | Phospholamban inhibition removed by PKA phosphorylation → faster relaxation (lusitropy) in cardiac β1 stim |
| **Ca²⁺-ATPase (PMCA)** | Ca²⁺ out across plasma membrane | 1 ATP / Ca²⁺ | Maintains cytosolic Ca low |
| **H⁺/K⁺-ATPase** | H⁺ out, K⁺ in (parietal cell) | 1 ATP / H⁺ | Target of PPIs |
| **V-type H⁺-ATPase** | H⁺ into lysosome | 1 ATP / H⁺ | Maintains lysosomal pH ~4.5 |
| **F-type ATP synthase (mitochondria)** | Backwards - uses H⁺ gradient to MAKE ATP | - | Final step of oxidative phosphorylation |

### Ion channels - gated
| Gating | Triggered by | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Voltage-gated** | Membrane depolarization | Na⁺ (action potential upstroke), K⁺ (repolarization), Ca²⁺ (NT release, contraction) |
| **Ligand-gated** | Extracellular ligand | Nicotinic ACh, AMPA/NMDA glutamate, GABA-A, glycine |
| **Mechanosensitive** | Stretch | Cochlear hair cells (Piezo channels), baroreceptor mechanosensation |
| **Light-gated** | Photon | Retinal rhodopsin (technically Gt-coupled → cGMP-gated) |

### Carrier kinetics
- Show **saturation** (have Km + Vmax) unlike simple diffusion.
- Carrier-mediated transport demonstrates **transport maximum (Tm)** - e.g., glucose appears in urine when filtered load exceeds renal proximal tubule SGLT Tm (~300 mg/min).
- Some carriers have **competitive inhibition** (similar substrate competes).

## Water + osmosis

- Net water flow driven by **osmotic gradient** (toward higher solute concentration).
- **Aquaporins** speed water permeability ~10-100×; AQP2 in collecting duct is the ADH-regulated channel (vasopressin → insertion → water reabsorption).
- **Tonicity** depends only on solutes that DON'T cross the membrane (effective osmoles):
  - **Hypertonic**: cell shrinks
  - **Isotonic**: no change
  - **Hypotonic**: cell swells
- IV fluids:
  - 0.9% saline = isotonic
  - 5% dextrose in water = isotonic IV but becomes hypotonic intracellularly (sugar consumed)
  - 3% saline = hypertonic (osmotherapy for ICP)

<Clinical>
**SIADH**: inappropriate ADH → aquaporins always open → water retention → dilutional hyponatremia. Treat: fluid restriction; tolvaptan (V2 antagonist); severe → 3% saline cautiously.

**Diabetes insipidus**: lack of ADH (central) or kidney resistance (nephrogenic) → can't reabsorb water → dilute urine + thirst. Treat central with desmopressin; nephrogenic with thiazide diuretic (paradoxical - induces hypovolemia → ↑ PCT Na/water reabsorption).
</Clinical>

## Signaling - four major pathways

### 1. Ion channels (ionotropic)
- Fastest (microseconds-ms).
- Ligand binding → channel opens → ion flux → membrane potential change.
- Examples: nicotinic AChR, glutamate (NMDA/AMPA), GABA-A.

### 2. G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs) - most drug targets
**Mechanism**: ligand binds → receptor conformational change → GDP/GTP exchange on Gα → Gα-GTP detaches + activates effectors.

| G-protein | Effector | 2nd messenger | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gs** | Adenylate cyclase ↑ | ↑ cAMP → PKA | Heart β1 (↑ contraction), kidney V2 (water), liver glucagon |
| **Gi** | Adenylate cyclase ↓ | ↓ cAMP | Muscarinic M2 in heart (↓ HR), α2 (↓ NE release), opioid μ |
| **Gq** | Phospholipase C | IP3 + DAG → ↑ Ca²⁺, PKC | α1, M1, M3, H1, V1 |
| **G12/13** | Rho GEFs | Cytoskeletal rearrangement | Some thrombin effects |

The same NT (e.g., NE) acts via different G-proteins at different receptors (α1 → Gq vasoconstriction; α2 → Gi presynaptic inhibition; β1 → Gs heart; β2 → Gs bronchodilation).

**Cholera toxin** locks Gs in ON state → ↑↑ cAMP in gut → massive Cl⁻ secretion → secretory diarrhea.
**Pertussis toxin** locks Gi OFF → ↑ cAMP → multiple effects (whooping cough biology).

### 3. Receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs)
- Slower (seconds-minutes); growth + metabolism.
- Ligand binds → receptor dimerizes → autophosphorylation → recruits adapter proteins → cascade.

| Pathway | Receptor → cascade |
|---|---|
| **Insulin** | IR → IRS-1 → PI3K → Akt → GLUT4 translocation; also IRS-1 → MAPK → growth |
| **Growth factors (EGF, PDGF, FGF, VEGF)** | RTK → Ras → Raf → MEK → ERK → transcription |
| **Cytokines (JAK/STAT)** | Receptor + JAK kinase → phosphorylates STAT → STAT dimerizes → nucleus |

**JAK inhibitors** (tofacitinib, ruxolitinib) treat RA, polycythemia vera, atopic dermatitis.
**Imatinib** (BCR-ABL kinase) - first targeted cancer therapy; CML.

### 4. Nuclear receptors (intracellular)
- Slowest (hours-days); steroid + thyroid + lipid hormones.
- Hormone diffuses across membrane → binds cytoplasmic or nuclear receptor → conformational change → translocates to nucleus → binds DNA response element → ↑/↓ transcription.

| Hormone | Receptor | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| **Cortisol** | GR (cytoplasm) | Anti-inflammatory, gluconeogenesis, immunosuppression |
| **Aldosterone** | MR (cytoplasm) | ENaC + Na/K-ATPase up in collecting duct |
| **Estrogen / testosterone** | ER, AR (nuclear or cytoplasm) | Sex steroids' classical effects |
| **Thyroid hormone (T3)** | TR (nuclear) | Metabolic rate, growth |
| **Vitamin D (calcitriol)** | VDR (nuclear) | Calcium + phosphate absorption + immune |
| **Retinoic acid** | RAR / RXR | Development, differentiation |

## Endocytosis modes

| Mode | Selectivity | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Phagocytosis** | Whole particles | Macrophages eating bacteria |
| **Pinocytosis** ("cell drinking") | Bulk fluid | Constitutive uptake |
| **Receptor-mediated** (clathrin-coated pits) | Specific ligands | LDL receptor - Brown + Goldstein won Nobel for this |
| **Caveolae-mediated** | Lipid raft components | Some toxins; VEGF signaling |

## Second messengers - the toolkit

| 2nd messenger | Source | Targets |
|---|---|---|
| **cAMP** | Adenylate cyclase | PKA → phosphorylates many proteins |
| **cGMP** | Guanylate cyclase (sGC = NO target; pGC = ANP target) | PKG → vasodilation, retinal photoreceptors |
| **IP3** | PIP2 by PLC | Releases Ca²⁺ from ER |
| **DAG** | PIP2 by PLC | Activates PKC |
| **Ca²⁺** | Channels + IP3R + RyR + leak | Calmodulin, troponin C, calcineurin |
| **NO** | NOS family | Activates sGC → cGMP; major vasodilator |

**Caffeine + theophylline**: inhibit phosphodiesterase → cAMP stays elevated → β-like effects (bronchodilation, ↑ HR, alertness).
**Sildenafil (Viagra)**: PDE5 inhibitor → ↑ cGMP in corpus cavernosum → smooth muscle relaxation → erection.
**Milrinone**: PDE3 inhibitor → ↑ cAMP in heart → inotropy; cardiogenic shock.

## Function reference - every key player

| Component | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Na/K-ATPase** | Sets membrane potential + drives Na-coupled cotransport | Digoxin target; loss → swelling, death |
| **Aquaporins** | Water channels | AQP2 in collecting duct = ADH target; loss = nephrogenic DI |
| **Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels** | Action potential upstroke | Local anesthetics block; Brugada syndrome (cardiac); periodic paralysis |
| **Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels** | NT release + cardiac plateau + smooth muscle contraction | DHPs (amlodipine) block L-type; benzodiazepines + gabapentinoids affect α2δ subunit |
| **Voltage-gated K⁺ channels** | Repolarization; pacemaker | Class III antiarrhythmics (amiodarone, sotalol); long QT mutations |
| **Nicotinic AChR** | Ionotropic (Na/K) | NMJ + autonomic ganglia + CNS; varenicline (smoking cessation) |
| **GABA-A** | Ionotropic Cl⁻ | Benzo + barbiturate + alcohol target |
| **GPCRs** | Most drug targets in pharmacology | ~30% of all marketed drugs |
| **Receptor tyrosine kinases** | Growth + metabolism | Tumor drivers (HER2, EGFR); targets of small-molecule + monoclonal cancer drugs |
| **JAK/STAT** | Cytokine signaling | Polycythemia vera (JAK2 V617F), JAK inhibitor drugs |
| **Nuclear receptors** | Steroid/thyroid receptors | Tamoxifen (ER), spironolactone (MR), bicalutamide (AR), levothyroxine (TR replacement) |
| **PIP2** | Lipid precursor for IP3 + DAG | Cleaved by PLC; site of Gq signaling |
| **Cholera toxin** | Locks Gs ON → ↑↑ cAMP in gut | Massive secretory diarrhea |
| **Pertussis toxin** | Locks Gi OFF | Whooping cough toxin |
| **Anthrax edema factor** | Bacterial adenylate cyclase → ↑ cAMP | Anthrax pathophysiology |
| **Diphtheria toxin** | ADP-ribosylates EF-2 → blocks protein synthesis | - |

## Why this matters

<HighYield variant="boards">
- **Digoxin** mechanism: Na/K-ATPase inhibition → ↑ intracellular Na → ↓ Na/Ca exchanger gradient → ↑ intracellular Ca → ↑ contractility. Toxicity: anorexia, nausea, vision changes (yellow), arrhythmias. Hypokalemia + hypomagnesemia worsen toxicity.
- **GLUT family**:
  - GLUT1 (RBC, brain, BBB) - basal
  - GLUT2 (β cells, liver, kidney, gut) - high Km, glucose sensor
  - GLUT3 (neurons) - constitutive high affinity
  - GLUT4 (muscle, fat) - insulin-regulated
  - GLUT5 (gut, sperm) - fructose
- **Loop diuretics** inhibit NKCC2 in thick ascending limb → loses Na/K/Cl + indirectly Ca/Mg (paracellular).
- **Thiazides** inhibit NCC in DCT → loses Na/Cl + indirectly retains Ca (helpful for stones).
- **Cardiac glycoside toxicity** treated with: digoxin-specific Fab fragments, correct K⁺ (controversial - high K⁺ worsens, low K⁺ also worsens; target slightly high), atropine if bradyarrhythmia.
- **Insulin signaling defect (T2DM)**: chronic inflammation → TNF-α → IKK activation → phosphoserine on IRS-1 → IRS-1 degradation → blunted PI3K/Akt → ↓ GLUT4 translocation → muscle resistance.
- **Cystic fibrosis**: CFTR (cAMP-gated Cl⁻ channel) defect → reduced Cl⁻ + water secretion onto epithelial surfaces → thick mucus everywhere. ΔF508 (~70%) misfolds + is degraded.
- **Familial hypercholesterolemia**: defective LDL receptor → impaired receptor-mediated endocytosis of LDL → ↑↑ LDL. Statins help by ↑ LDL receptor expression.
- **GPCR desensitization**: continuous agonist → β-arrestin recruitment → receptor internalization → tachyphylaxis. Explains why pulsatile GnRH is needed; continuous → axis suppression.
</HighYield>
