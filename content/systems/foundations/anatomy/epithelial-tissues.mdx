Epithelial tissue is the body's **lining and covering system**. It forms the skin surface, the luminal lining of hollow organs, and the secretory portions of glands. The lecture slides emphasize four organizing ideas: **shape, layering, polarity, and whether the epithelium is proper or glandular**.

<Figure name="histology/epithelial-types-slide" caption="Lecture-slide overview of epithelial classification by shape (squamous, cuboidal, columnar) and layering (simple vs stratified)." />

## Classification by shape

| Shape | Key visual cue | Main job | Classic examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Squamous** | Flat, scale-like cells | Diffusion or abrasion resistance | Alveoli, capillaries, skin, esophagus |
| **Cuboidal** | Cells as tall as they are wide | Secretion and absorption | Kidney tubules, small ducts |
| **Columnar** | Tall cells with elongated nuclei | Absorption, secretion, transport | Intestine, stomach, uterine tube |

### Squamous: protective vs exchange surfaces

<Figure name="histology/squamous-cells-slide" caption="The slides pair two uses of squamous epithelium: multilayered protection in stratified squamous mucosa and thin exchange surfaces in simple squamous alveoli." />

- **Simple squamous** is ideal where diffusion must be fast, so it lines **alveoli**, **capillaries**, and other thin membranes.
- **Stratified squamous** is ideal where friction is constant, so it lines the **skin**, **oral cavity**, **esophagus**, and **anus**.

## Classification by layering

| Layering pattern | Meaning | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | One layer of cells | Best for diffusion, absorption, secretion |
| **Stratified** | Multiple layers | Best for protection from abrasion |
| **Pseudostratified** | One layer that only looks multilayered | Common in the respiratory tract; often ciliated |
| **Transitional** | Shape changes with stretch | Specialized for the urinary tract |

## Proper epithelium vs glandular epithelium

<Figure name="histology/glandular-epithelium-slide" caption="The lecture slides separate surface-lining proper epithelium from glandular epithelium, which is specialized for secretion." />

- **Proper epithelium** covers external or internal surfaces.
- **Glandular epithelium** invaginates and specializes for secretion.
- **Endocrine glands** release products into blood.
- **Exocrine glands** release products into ducts or directly onto a surface.

## Polarity and basement membrane

<Figure name="histology/epithelial-polarity-slide" caption="Epithelial cells are polarized: the apical surface faces the lumen or exterior, while the basal surface anchors to the basement membrane." />

The lecture's polarity slide matters because epithelial cells are not symmetric:

- The **apical surface** faces the lumen or body exterior and may carry **cilia**, **microvilli**, or a **brush border**.
- The **basal surface** rests on the **basement membrane**, which anchors the tissue to underlying connective tissue.
- Epithelium itself is **avascular**, so nutrients diffuse in from the connective tissue below.

## High-yield examples from the slides

| Site | Epithelium | Why this fit makes sense |
|---|---|---|
| **Alveoli** | Simple squamous | Fast gas diffusion |
| **Kidney tubules** | Simple cuboidal | Reabsorption and secretion |
| **Intestine** | Simple columnar | Absorption plus mucus secretion |
| **Trachea / bronchi** | Pseudostratified ciliated columnar | Mucociliary clearance |
| **Skin / esophagus / anus** | Stratified squamous | Mechanical protection |
| **Bladder** | Transitional | Expands and recoils with filling |
