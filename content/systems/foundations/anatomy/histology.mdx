Every organ in the body is built from combinations of just **four major tissue types**: nervous, muscle, epithelial, and connective. Knowing the cellular features of each is what lets you identify a tissue under the microscope - and explains why some heal in days (epithelium) while others heal in months or never (nervous, cartilage).

## The four tissue types at a glance

| Tissue | Function | Cellularity | Vascularity | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nervous** | Receives, processes, transmits electrical signals | High | Avascular (lives off CSF + nearby capillaries) | Brain, spinal cord, peripheral nerves |
| **Muscle** | Contraction → movement, propulsion, pumping | Very high | Variable (cardiac > skeletal > smooth) | Biceps, heart, GI wall |
| **Epithelial** | Lining, covering, secretion, absorption | Very high | **Avascular** - relies on underlying CT capillaries | Skin epidermis, gut lining, glands |
| **Connective** | Support, protection, energy + fluid storage, transport, movement | Low (mostly extracellular matrix) | Variable (high in bone, low in cartilage) | Bone, tendon, blood, fat, dermis |

## 1. Nervous tissue

Built from **neurons** (signal carriers) + **glial cells** (support).

### Neuron

- **Dendrites** receive signals from other cells or sensory stimuli.
- **Cell body (soma)** contains the nucleus and most organelles.
- **Axon** transmits action potentials to other neurons, muscle cells, or glands.

### Glial cells

| Glial cell | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Astrocyte** | CNS | Maintains blood-brain barrier, nutrient/waste handling |
| **Oligodendrocyte** | CNS | Myelinates multiple CNS axons (one cell, several wraps) |
| **Microglia** | CNS | Resident phagocyte/macrophage of CNS |
| **Ependymal cells** | CNS ventricles | Line ventricles, form choroid plexus (CSF production) |
| **Schwann cell** | PNS | Myelinates **a single PNS axon segment**; wraps unmyelinated axons too |
| **Satellite cell** | PNS ganglia | Supports neuronal cell bodies in ganglia |

<Clinical>
**Why MS affects only the CNS**: MS is autoimmune attack on oligodendrocytes (CNS myelin). **Guillain-Barré** is the PNS analog - autoimmune attack on Schwann cell myelin.
</Clinical>

## 2. Muscle tissue

Three types, distinguished histologically and functionally:

| Feature | Skeletal | Cardiac | Smooth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Striations** | Yes | Yes | No (filaments disorganized) |
| **Cell shape** | Long, parallel fibers | Branched | Spindle/tapered |
| **Nuclei per cell** | Multiple, peripheral | 1 central | 1 central |
| **Special junctions** | None | **Intercalated discs** (gap junctions + desmosomes) | Gap junctions |
| **Control** | Voluntary (somatic) | Involuntary (autonomic + intrinsic pacemaker) | Involuntary (autonomic) |
| **Action** | Pulls bone or skin | Pumps blood | Squeezes hollow-organ contents |
| **Regeneration** | Limited (satellite cells) | Essentially none (post-MI scar) | Good (proliferates) |

Striations come from the regular overlap of **actin (thin)** and **myosin (thick)** filaments organized into sarcomeres. Smooth muscle has the same proteins but disorganized - hence no striations.

## 3. Epithelial tissue

Lines, covers, and creates selective membranes. Two important classifications:

### By shape

| Shape | Function | Typical location |
|---|---|---|
| **Squamous** (flat) | Fast diffusion, protection in thin sheets | Alveoli, capillary endothelium, mesothelium |
| **Cuboidal** (square) | Absorption, secretion | Kidney tubules, gland ducts, ovary surface |
| **Columnar** (tall) | Absorption, secretion, can bear cilia/microvilli | Stomach, intestine, uterine tube, bronchi |

### By layering

| Layering | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Simple** | One layer - fast diffusion + secretion + absorption |
| **Stratified** | Multiple layers - abrasion resistance |
| **Pseudostratified** | One layer, but nuclei at different heights makes it *look* stratified - characteristically with cilia (respiratory tract) |
| **Transitional** | Surface cells change shape (round when relaxed, flat when stretched) - only in urinary tract |

### Proper vs glandular

- **Proper epithelium** - covers surfaces (skin, lumens)
- **Glandular epithelium** - invaginated to form glands:
  - **Endocrine** - ductless, secretes hormones into the bloodstream
  - **Exocrine** - secretes via ducts onto a surface or into a lumen

### Polarity

- **Apical surface** - faces the outside / lumen (where microvilli, cilia, brush borders are)
- **Basal surface** - attached to a **basement membrane** that anchors the epithelium to the underlying connective tissue

Epithelium has **no blood supply of its own** - it relies on capillaries in the connective tissue below.

If you want the lecture-slide version of this topic with more visuals, see [Epithelial Tissues](/foundations/anatomy/epithelial-tissues).

## 4. Connective tissue

The most diverse and abundant tissue type.

### Shared features

- All develop from **mesenchyme** (embryonic mesoderm-derived connective tissue)
- Cells have no fixed polarity
- Cells produce and are surrounded by **extracellular matrix (ECM)** - this is what makes connective tissue connective tissue.
- Cells exist in two phases:
  - **Immature "–blast"** (osteoblast, chondroblast, fibroblast) - still dividing, actively secreting matrix
  - **Mature "–cyte"** (osteocyte, chondrocyte, fibrocyte) - quieter, maintaining matrix

### The extracellular matrix

| Component | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Ground substance** | Water + glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) + proteoglycans |
| **Fibers** | **Collagen** (strongest, most abundant), **Elastic** (stretch + recoil), **Reticular** (delicate sponge-like; bone marrow, lymph nodes, spleen) |

### Subtypes

| Subtype | Examples | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Loose, areolar** | Just deep to skin, around organs | Lots of open space + blood vessels; site of edema |
| **Loose, adipose** | Subcutaneous fat | Mostly adipocytes with large lipid vacuoles |
| **Loose, reticular** | Spleen, lymph nodes, bone marrow internal scaffolding | Reticular fibers supporting cells |
| **Dense regular** | Tendons (muscle → bone), ligaments (bone → bone) | Collagen unidirectional - high tensile strength one way |
| **Dense irregular** | Dermis | Collagen multidirectional - strength in many directions |
| **Dense elastic** | Aorta, great vessels | Elastic fibers dominate - recoil |
| **Cartilage - hyaline** | Nose, ribs, articular surfaces | Smooth, glassy; no nerves or vessels |
| **Cartilage - fibro** | Vertebral discs, menisci, pubic symphysis | Lots of collagen; stiffer |
| **Cartilage - elastic** | External ear, epiglottis | Elastic fibers; flexible |
| **Bone - compact** | Outer dense layer | Osteons (Haversian systems) |
| **Bone - spongy** | Inside epiphyses, in the medullary cavity | Trabecular network; houses red marrow |
| **Blood** | In vessels | Ground substance = plasma; fibers form only on clotting (fibrin) |

## Embryologic origins - who comes from where

| Germ layer | Tissues |
|---|---|
| **Ectoderm** | Integument (epidermis, hair, nails), melanocytes, **all nervous tissue** (CNS + PNS), lens of eye, retina, tooth enamel, anterior pituitary (oral ectoderm), adrenal medulla (neural crest) |
| **Mesoderm** | All connective tissue (bone, cartilage, blood, dermis), all muscle (skeletal, cardiac, smooth), cardiovascular system, kidneys, gonads, adrenal cortex, spleen, serous membrane mesothelium |
| **Endoderm** | Inner lining of GI + respiratory + most of GU tracts; liver, pancreas, thyroid, parathyroids, thymus, auditory tube + middle ear epithelium |

## Every tissue feature - function reference

| Feature | What it does | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Neuron dendrite** | Receives signals | Site of synaptic input |
| **Neuron axon** | Transmits AP | Conducts toward target; site of myelination |
| **Astrocyte foot processes** | Form blood-brain barrier | Selectively gate CNS entry |
| **Oligodendrocyte** | Myelinates **multiple** CNS axons | Saltatory conduction; lost in MS |
| **Schwann cell** | Myelinates **one** PNS axon segment | Lost in Guillain-Barré |
| **Microglia** | CNS macrophage | Phagocytosis + inflammation; activated in injury, infection, Alzheimer |
| **Skeletal muscle fiber** | Voluntary contraction | Pulls on bone via tendon |
| **Cardiac myocyte** | Coordinated contraction | Intercalated discs allow functional syncytium |
| **Smooth muscle cell** | Squeezes hollow organs | Autonomic control; lacks striations |
| **Intercalated disc** | Cardiac cell-to-cell junction | Gap junctions for electrical, desmosomes for mechanical |
| **Sarcomere** | Contractile unit | Z disc → M line in striated muscle |
| **Squamous epithelium** | Thin diffusion barrier | Alveoli, capillaries, mesothelium |
| **Cuboidal epithelium** | Secretion + absorption | Tubules, glands |
| **Columnar epithelium** | Tall absorption/secretion + cilia/microvilli | Gut, respiratory tract |
| **Pseudostratified ciliated columnar** | Mucociliary escalator | Trachea, bronchi |
| **Stratified squamous** | Abrasion resistance | Skin epidermis, esophagus, mouth, anus |
| **Basement membrane** | Anchors epithelium to CT | Filtration barrier in glomerulus; target of anti-GBM disease |
| **Apical brush border** | Microvilli increase surface area | Small intestine, PCT |
| **Goblet cell** | Secretes mucus | GI + respiratory protection |
| **Fibroblast** | Produces ECM | Wound healing; scar formation |
| **Osteoblast → osteocyte** | Build then maintain bone | Mineralization cycle |
| **Chondroblast → chondrocyte** | Build then maintain cartilage | Avascular tissue - slow healing |
| **Adipocyte** | Stores triglycerides | Energy reserve + insulation + endocrine (leptin, adiponectin) |
| **Mast cell** | Releases histamine on IgE crosslinking | Allergy + parasites |
| **Macrophage** | Phagocytosis + antigen presentation | Innate + adaptive bridge |
| **Plasma cell** | Antibody secretion | Differentiated B cell |
| **Collagen fiber** | Tensile strength | Strongest fiber; bone, tendon, dermis |
| **Elastic fiber** | Stretch + recoil | Aorta, lung, dermis |
| **Reticular fiber** | Delicate sponge-network | Lymphoid organ + bone marrow scaffolding |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Histology pattern recognition for boards:**
- **Stratified squamous keratinized** = thick-skin epidermis (palms, soles). Non-keratinized = oral, esophageal, vaginal, anal canal mucosa.
- **Pseudostratified ciliated columnar with goblet cells** = respiratory tract from nose down to terminal bronchioles. Loss of cilia in smokers → squamous metaplasia → dysplasia → carcinoma.
- **Simple columnar with goblet cells + brush border** = stomach to anal canal.
- **Transitional epithelium** = urinary tract only (renal calyces → bladder → proximal urethra).
- **Reed-Sternberg cell** = Hodgkin lymphoma (CD15+, CD30+).
- **Russell bodies** = plasma cells filled with Ig (multiple myeloma).
- **Mallory bodies** = hyaline inclusions in alcoholic liver disease.
- **Lewy bodies** = α-synuclein aggregates (Parkinson, Lewy body dementia).
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX implications of tissue type:**
- **Epithelial heals fast** - superficial burns and abrasions re-epithelialize quickly if dermis intact.
- **Connective tissue (esp. cartilage)** heals slowly - knee cartilage damage is often permanent without surgery.
- **Cardiac muscle** doesn't regenerate - MI leaves a non-contractile scar; remodeling drives heart failure.
- **CNS neurons** don't regenerate - stroke damage is permanent; rehab works through plasticity, not new cells.
- **Edema** is fluid accumulation in **loose areolar connective tissue** - elevate, compression stockings, treat underlying cause.
</HighYield>
