## Internal organs

- **Ovaries** - almond-shaped, lie in the ovarian fossa on the lateral pelvic wall. House the follicle population (~1–2 million at birth, ~400 ovulated in a lifetime). Produce oocytes + estrogen + progesterone.
- **Uterine (fallopian) tubes** - ~10 cm. Four parts: infundibulum (with fimbriae) → ampulla (**most fertilizations occur here**) → isthmus → intramural.
- **Uterus** - pear-shaped muscular organ. Regions: fundus, body, isthmus, cervix. Walls: **perimetrium, myometrium, endometrium** (functional + basal layers - only functional is shed).
- **Vagina** - fibromuscular tube, 8–10 cm. pH ~3.5–4.5 from lactobacilli converting glycogen to lactic acid.

## External genitalia (vulva)

Mons pubis, labia majora, labia minora, clitoris, vestibule with vaginal and urethral openings, Bartholin glands, hymen.

## Blood supply & innervation

- **Ovarian arteries** from abdominal aorta (gonads are retroperitoneal in origin)
- **Uterine arteries** from internal iliac
- Lymphatics drain along the same vessels - important for cancer staging:
  - Ovary, fundus → **para-aortic** nodes
  - Body, cervix → **internal iliac, external iliac, sacral** nodes
  - Vulva → **superficial inguinal** nodes

## Supporting ligaments

| Ligament | Connects |
|---|---|
| Broad ligament | Uterus to lateral pelvic wall (drape of peritoneum) |
| Round ligament | Uterine horn → labia majora through inguinal canal |
| Suspensory ligament of ovary | Ovary to pelvic wall (carries ovarian vessels) |
| Ovarian ligament | Ovary → uterus |
| Cardinal (transverse cervical) | Cervix → lateral pelvic wall (main support) |
| Uterosacral | Cervix → sacrum |

<Clinical>
**Ectopic pregnancy** most commonly implants in the **ampulla** of the uterine tube. Presents with unilateral pelvic pain + amenorrhea + positive β-hCG; ruptured ectopic is a surgical emergency (hypovolemic shock).

**Cervical cancer** spreads to internal iliac nodes; vulvar cancer to superficial inguinal - these nodal pathways are a high-yield difference.
</Clinical>

## Every female reproductive structure - what it does and why it matters

### Internal organs

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Ovary** | Houses follicles; produces oocytes + estrogen + progesterone | The "gonad"; ovarian reserve declines with age - primary cause of female infertility after 35 |
| **Ovarian cortex** | Contains follicles at various stages | Histologic site of follicle counting (AMH correlates) |
| **Ovarian medulla** | Vessels + nerves + connective tissue | Hilum cells can become androgen-secreting tumors |
| **Primordial follicle** | Resting; oocyte + single squamous layer | The reserve - finite at birth (~1 million), down to ~400 ovulated over a lifetime |
| **Primary follicle** | Recruited; oocyte + single cuboidal granulosa layer | First step toward ovulation |
| **Secondary (antral) follicle** | Has fluid-filled antrum; multilayered | FSH-sensitive; selects dominant follicle each cycle |
| **Graafian (mature) follicle** | Pre-ovulatory; ~2 cm | Ruptures at ovulation; releases oocyte + cumulus cells |
| **Corpus luteum** | Post-ovulatory follicle remnant | Secretes progesterone for 14 days; rescued by hCG if pregnancy |
| **Corpus albicans** | Scar from regressed corpus luteum | Permanent fibrous remnant |
| **Uterine (fallopian) tubes** | Convey oocyte from ovary toward uterus; site of fertilization | ~10 cm; ectopic pregnancy if implantation here (most often in ampulla) |
| **Infundibulum** | Funnel-shaped distal portion of tube | Bears fimbriae |
| **Fimbriae** | Finger-like projections at infundibulum | Sweep oocyte from ovary into tube |
| **Ampulla** | Widest, longest part of uterine tube | **Most common site of fertilization** and ectopic pregnancy |
| **Isthmus** | Narrow part near uterus | Tubal ligation often done here |
| **Intramural (interstitial) portion** | Passes through uterine wall | Joins uterine cavity |
| **Uterus - fundus** | Domed top above tube entries | Where placenta usually implants |
| **Uterus - body (corpus)** | Main muscular region | Houses developing fetus |
| **Uterus - isthmus** | Narrow region between body and cervix | Becomes lower uterine segment in pregnancy |
| **Cervix** | Lower neck; opens into vagina | Cervical cancer screening site (Pap smear) |
| **External os** | Cervical opening to vagina | Source of mucus changes with cycle |
| **Internal os** | Cervical opening to uterine cavity | Incompetent → preterm labor |
| **Endometrium - functional layer** | Outer layer responsive to hormones | **Shed in menstruation**; receptive site for implantation |
| **Endometrium - basal layer** | Stem-cell layer; regenerates functional | Preserved through menstruation |
| **Myometrium** | Smooth muscle layer | Contractions during labor + dysmenorrhea; site of leiomyomas (fibroids) |
| **Perimetrium (serosa)** | Outer peritoneal covering | Source of endometriosis if implants on it |
| **Vagina** | Fibromuscular birth canal | Stratified squamous epithelium; pH 3.5–4.5 from lactobacilli |
| **Fornices (anterior, posterior, lateral)** | Recesses around cervix in vagina | Posterior fornix → access to pouch of Douglas (culdocentesis) |

### External genitalia (vulva)

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Mons pubis** | Fatty pad over pubic symphysis | Cushion |
| **Labia majora** | Outer hair-bearing folds | Embryologic homolog of scrotum |
| **Labia minora** | Inner hairless folds | Homolog of penile shaft skin; enclose vestibule |
| **Clitoris** | Erectile tissue (homolog of penis); rich sensory innervation | Mediates orgasm; pudendal nerve |
| **Vestibule** | Region between labia minora | Contains urethral + vaginal openings |
| **Hymen** | Mucosal fold partially covering vaginal opening | Variably present |
| **Bartholin (greater vestibular) glands** | Secrete lubricating mucus into vestibule | Can become obstructed → Bartholin cyst/abscess |
| **Skene (paraurethral) glands** | Drain into urethra; homolog of male prostate | Source of some "female ejaculate"; site of paraurethral cysts |

### Supporting ligaments

| Ligament | Connects | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Broad ligament** | Uterus → lateral pelvic wall | Drape of peritoneum carrying tubes + vessels | Houses tubes, round + ovarian ligaments |
| **Round ligament** | Uterine horn → labia majora (through inguinal canal) | Remnant of gubernaculum | Round ligament pain in pregnancy |
| **Suspensory ligament (infundibulopelvic)** | Ovary → pelvic wall | Carries ovarian a. + v. | Ligated in oophorectomy |
| **Ovarian ligament** | Ovary → uterus | Remnant of gubernaculum (cranial) | Holds ovary near uterus |
| **Cardinal (transverse cervical) ligament** | Cervix → lateral pelvic wall | **Main support of uterus** | Carries uterine a. |
| **Uterosacral ligament** | Cervix → sacrum | Resists anterior displacement | Posterior cul-de-sac landmark |

### Vasculature + lymphatics

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Ovarian artery** | Direct branch of abdominal aorta at L2 | Long course because of gonadal descent in embryology |
| **Uterine artery** | Branch of internal iliac | Crosses over ureter (**water under the bridge** - surgical hazard) |
| **Vaginal artery** | Branch of uterine or internal iliac | Supplies vagina |
| **Internal pudendal artery** | Branch of internal iliac | Supplies external genitalia |
| **Ovarian + fundal lymphatics → para-aortic nodes** | Ovary, fundus, fallopian tube drainage | Why ovarian cancer spreads to abdomen |
| **Body + cervix lymphatics → internal iliac, sacral nodes** | Body of uterus and cervix | Cervical cancer staging path |
| **Vulvar lymphatics → superficial inguinal nodes** | Vulva, lower vagina | Vulvar cancer staging path |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX women's health priorities:**
- **Pregnancy danger signs**: vaginal bleeding, severe headache + visual changes (preeclampsia), abdominal pain, decreased fetal movement, ruptured membranes, severe vomiting.
- **Naegele's rule**: EDD = LMP + 7 days − 3 months + 1 year.
- **Leopold maneuvers**: assess fetal position; bladder empty first, supine briefly OK.
- **Fundal height** in cm ≈ weeks gestation (20–36 wk).
- **Post-partum hemorrhage** (#1 cause: uterine atony): firm fundus, massage if boggy; bladder may displace.
- **Mastitis teaching**: continue breastfeeding/pumping; antibiotics; warm compresses.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Female anatomy gotchas:**
- **Ectopic pregnancy**: 95% tubal; **ampulla most common**; presents with unilateral pelvic pain + amenorrhea + ↑ β-hCG.
- **Ovarian cancer**: vague symptoms, late presentation; CA-125 monitoring; BRCA1/2 association.
- **Cervical cancer**: HPV 16/18; transformation zone; screening with Pap + HPV co-test.
- **Endometriosis**: ectopic endometrial tissue; dysmenorrhea, dyspareunia, infertility; chocolate cysts on ovaries.
- **Uterine fibroids (leiomyomas)**: most common pelvic tumor; estrogen-dependent; shrink after menopause.
- **Ureter crosses under uterine artery** ("water under the bridge") - must identify in hysterectomy to avoid injury.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Women's health pharm:**
- **Combined OCPs**: estrogen + progestin; risks include VTE, MI/stroke (especially smokers &gt;35), HTN; protective against ovarian + endometrial cancer.
- **Progestin-only (mini-pill, IUD)**: safer if VTE history, breastfeeding; less reliable if missed.
- **Mifepristone**: progesterone receptor antagonist; medication abortion (with misoprostol).
- **Oxytocin**: induces/augments labor; can cause uterine hyperstimulation, water intoxication.
- **Tocolytics** (terbutaline, mag sulfate, nifedipine): delay preterm labor; mag also for preeclampsia (watch DTRs, respiratory rate).
- **Methotrexate**: ectopic pregnancy (small, unruptured); folate antagonist.
- **Magnesium sulfate**: preeclampsia/eclampsia seizure prevention; toxicity → loss of DTRs, respiratory depression; **antidote = calcium gluconate**.
</HighYield>

## Male reproductive system (companion)

<Figure name="reproductive/male-reproductive" caption="Male reproductive system - sagittal view showing testis, epididymis, vas (ductus) deferens, ejaculatory duct, seminal vesicle, prostate, bulbourethral (Cowper's) gland, urethra, penis." />

The male reproductive system mirrors many female structures. Sperm travel: **seminiferous tubules → rete testis → efferent ductules → epididymis (storage + maturation) → vas deferens → ejaculatory duct → urethra**.

### Male reproductive structures

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Testis** | Sperm + testosterone production | Inside scrotum (cooler 2°C); descends through inguinal canal in fetal life |
| **Seminiferous tubules** | Site of spermatogenesis | Sertoli cells (FSH target, support sperm); spermatogenic cells |
| **Sertoli cells** | Form blood-testis barrier; nourish developing sperm; secrete inhibin | FSH target; protects developing germ cells from autoimmune attack |
| **Leydig cells (interstitial)** | Synthesize testosterone | LH target |
| **Epididymis** | Stores + matures sperm (~20 days) | Sperm acquire motility here |
| **Vas (ductus) deferens** | Transports sperm to ejaculatory duct | Site of vasectomy; passes through inguinal canal |
| **Seminal vesicles** | Secrete ~70% of semen volume (fructose, prostaglandins, fibrinogen) | Energy + initial coagulation of semen |
| **Prostate gland** | Secretes ~25% of semen (alkaline, PSA, citrate, zinc) | Alkaline pH protects sperm from vaginal acidity; PSA used as cancer marker; BPH commonly affects central zone |
| **Bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands** | Pre-ejaculate lubrication | Small contribution to semen |
| **Urethra (male)** | Carries semen + urine | Prostatic, membranous, spongy (penile) segments |
| **Penis (corpora cavernosa, corpus spongiosum)** | Erectile tissue + urethra | NO-mediated vasodilation → erection; PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil) prolong cGMP |
| **Scrotum** | Houses testes outside body cavity | Maintains testis at ~2°C below body temp via cremaster + dartos muscles |

<Clinical>
**Cryptorchidism**: undescended testis; ↑ infertility + testicular cancer risk; surgery (orchiopexy) by age 1.
**Varicocele**: dilated pampiniform plexus, almost always **left side** (left gonadal vein → left renal vein, right angle entry). "Bag of worms" feel.
**Testicular torsion**: surgical emergency; cremasteric reflex absent; high-riding testis. Detorse within 6 h to save testis.
**Erectile dysfunction**: vascular (most common in older men) or psychogenic; PDE5 inhibitors first-line.
**BPH** vs **prostate cancer**: BPH = central zone, urethral compression; prostate Ca = peripheral zone, palpable on DRE.
</Clinical>

## Spermatogenesis (male gamete production)

<Figure name="reproductive/spermatogenesis" caption="Spermatogenesis: spermatogonium (46) → primary spermatocyte (46, 4n after DNA replication) → meiosis I → secondary spermatocytes (23, 2n) → meiosis II → spermatids (23, n) → spermatozoa." />

Sperm production takes ~64 days, occurring continuously from puberty in the seminiferous tubules. Steps:

| Stage | Chromosome | DNA content | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spermatogonium** | 46 (diploid) | 2n | Stem-cell pool; some self-renew, others enter mitosis |
| **Primary spermatocyte** | 46 (diploid) | 4n after S phase | Enters meiosis I |
| **Secondary spermatocyte** | 23 (haploid) | 2n | Produced by meiosis I; very short-lived |
| **Spermatid** | 23 (haploid) | n | Produced by meiosis II; undergoes **spermiogenesis** (cytoplasm loss, tail formation, acrosome) |
| **Spermatozoon** | 23 (haploid) | n | Mature sperm; head (nucleus + acrosome), midpiece (mitochondria), tail (flagellum) |

**Sertoli cells** (FSH target) form the **blood-testis barrier**, nurse developing germ cells, secrete inhibin, and phagocytose excess cytoplasm. **Leydig cells** (LH target, interstitial) synthesize testosterone.

### Oogenesis comparison (key contrasts)

| Feature | Spermatogenesis | Oogenesis |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | Continuous from puberty | Begins in fetal life, arrests + resumes in cycles |
| Output per cycle | 4 functional sperm per primary | 1 functional ovum + 3 polar bodies |
| Meiosis I arrest | None | Prophase I until ovulation (could be decades) |
| Meiosis II arrest | None | Metaphase II until fertilization |
| Cell loss | Minimal | Massive (atresia) |
| Hormonal cycling | Tonic (continuous) | Cyclic (28 days) |

<Clinical>
**Down syndrome (trisomy 21)** risk rises with maternal age because oocytes have been arrested in meiosis I since fetal life - older oocytes have older spindle proteins, higher risk of nondisjunction.

**Klinefelter syndrome (47, XXY)**: tall, gynecomastia, small testes, ↑ FSH/LH, ↓ testosterone, infertile.
**Cryptorchidism**: undescended testis → spermatogenesis fails (needs cooler temp); ↑ testicular cancer risk.
</Clinical>
