The pelvis is the bony **basin** at the bottom of the trunk and the floor of the abdominal cavity. It supports body weight, anchors lower-limb muscles, and houses the reproductive + urinary + lower GI viscera. The **perineum** is the diamond-shaped region below the pelvic floor and the territory of obstetric, urologic, and colorectal surgery.

The classical plates used below are female because the layers are easier to see, but the **same bony borders, levator ani sling, and perineal triangles apply in both sexes**. What changes most is which organs pass through the floor.

## Bony pelvis

The bony pelvis is built from:

- **Two hip (innominate / coxal) bones** - each fused from three childhood bones: **ilium + ischium + pubis**
- **Sacrum** (5 fused vertebrae)
- **Coccyx** (3–4 fused rudimentary vertebrae)

These connect at:
- **Sacroiliac joints** (paired, posterior - strong synovial joints)
- **Pubic symphysis** (anterior - cartilaginous joint)

### Pelvic inlet vs outlet

- **Pelvic inlet** (pelvic brim) = boundary between the **false pelvis** (above, contains abdominal viscera) and **true pelvis** (below, contains pelvic viscera). Defined by sacral promontory, iliopectineal lines, pubic crest.
- **Pelvic outlet** = lower opening, bounded by pubic arch, ischial tuberosities, sacrotuberous ligaments, coccyx.

### Male vs female pelvis (high-yield)

| Feature | Male | Female |
|---|---|---|
| Inlet | Heart-shaped | **Round/oval** (wider) |
| Sub-pubic angle | < 70° (narrow) | **> 80°** (wide) - accommodates childbirth |
| Sacrum | Long, narrow, curved | Short, wide, less curved |
| Ischial spines | Prominent inward | Less prominent |
| Pelvic outlet | Narrower | Wider |
| Pelvic basin | Funnel-shaped | Shallow, broad |

## Pelvic diaphragm - the floor

<Figure name="reproductive/pelvic-floor-openstax" caption="Muscles of the pelvic floor (OpenStax). Pelvic diaphragm = levator ani (puborectalis + pubococcygeus + iliococcygeus) + coccygeus, supporting all pelvic viscera against gravity and intra-abdominal pressure." />

The pelvic outlet has a hole. Without a muscular floor, abdominopelvic organs would fall out. That floor is the **pelvic diaphragm**, formed by paired muscles:

| Muscle | Components | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Levator ani** | **Puborectalis + pubococcygeus + iliococcygeus** | Supports pelvic viscera; maintains continence; contributes to lifting/coughing/Valsalva |
| **Coccygeus** | Sacrum/coccyx attachment | Minor support; flexes coccyx |

The **puborectalis** is particularly important - it forms a sling around the anorectal junction, maintaining the **anorectal angle** that's key for fecal continence. Relaxation during defecation straightens this angle.

The pelvic diaphragm is innervated by **direct branches from S2–S4** (and pudendal n. for some superficial fibers).

<Clinical>
**Pelvic organ prolapse** in women: weakened levator ani (after childbirth, aging) → **cystocele** (bladder bulges into anterior vaginal wall), **rectocele** (rectum bulges into posterior wall), **uterine prolapse** (uterus descends into vagina). Manage with pelvic floor exercises (Kegels), pessary, or surgical repair.

**Pelvic floor dysfunction** in men: can occur post-prostatectomy → incontinence; pelvic floor PT can help.

**Urinary stress incontinence**: with cough/laugh/sneeze. Weak external urethral sphincter + pelvic floor. Treat with Kegels, mid-urethral sling.

**Urge incontinence**: detrusor overactivity. Treat with antimuscarinics (oxybutynin), β3 agonist (mirabegron).
</Clinical>

## Perineum - the diamond

<Figure name="reproductive/perineum-male" caption="Male perineum (OpenStax) - urogenital triangle (anterior) with bulbospongiosus + ischiocavernosus, deep + superficial transverse perineal muscles, external urethral sphincter; anal triangle (posterior) with external anal sphincter." />

<Figure name="reproductive/perineum-female" caption="Female perineum (OpenStax) - urogenital triangle with bulbospongiosus surrounding the vaginal opening, ischiocavernosus, deep + superficial transverse perineal muscles, external urethral sphincter; anal triangle with external anal sphincter." />

The perineum is bounded by the **pubic symphysis (anterior)**, **ischial tuberosities (lateral)**, and **coccyx (posterior)**. A line between the two ischial tuberosities divides it into two triangles:

### Urogenital (UG) triangle (anterior)

Contains:
- **External genitalia** (penis + scrotum in male; vulva in female)
- **Urethral opening** (and vaginal opening in female)
- **Perineal muscles** (superficial: bulbospongiosus, ischiocavernosus, superficial transverse perineal; deep: external urethral sphincter, deep transverse perineal)
- **UG diaphragm / perineal membrane** - a fibromuscular sheet

### Anal triangle (posterior)

Contains:
- **Anus** + external anal sphincter
- **Ischioanal (ischiorectal) fossae** - fat-filled spaces lateral to anal canal, allow distension during defecation; site of ischioanal abscess

### Perineal body

A fibromuscular node at the center of the perineum (between vaginal/urethral and anal openings). Attachment site for **9 muscles** - the keystone of pelvic floor integrity. **Tears in childbirth** can damage this and cause long-term incontinence + prolapse.

<Clinical>
**Episiotomy** in childbirth: surgical incision of perineum to enlarge vaginal opening. **Mediolateral** preferred over midline because midline cuts can extend into the anal sphincter.

**Perineal lacerations** in childbirth graded 1–4:
- 1°: skin only
- 2°: perineal muscles
- 3°: external anal sphincter
- 4°: through external + internal anal sphincters into rectal mucosa

3rd + 4th degree cause fecal incontinence if not properly repaired.

**Perineal abscess** (e.g., perirectal, ischiorectal): typically polymicrobial; surgical drainage. Crohn's disease commonly causes fistulizing perirectal disease.
</Clinical>

## Pelvic ligaments

<Figure name="reproductive/pelvic-ligaments" caption="Pelvic ligaments from above. Sacrospinous + sacrotuberous ligaments convert greater/lesser sciatic notches into foramina. Iliolumbar + sacroiliac ligaments stabilize the SI joint." />

Major suspensory + supportive ligaments (in women):

| Ligament | Connects | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Broad ligament** | Lateral pelvic wall → uterus (drape) | Carries uterine tube, round ligament, ovary; subdivided into mesometrium, mesosalpinx, mesovarium |
| **Round ligament** | Uterine cornu → labia majora through inguinal canal | Remnant of gubernaculum; pain in pregnancy when stretched |
| **Suspensory ligament of the ovary** (infundibulopelvic) | Ovary → pelvic wall | Carries ovarian a. + v. - ligated in oophorectomy |
| **Ovarian ligament** | Ovary → uterus | Other gubernaculum remnant |
| **Cardinal (transverse cervical) ligament** | Cervix → lateral pelvic wall | **Primary support of uterus**; carries uterine a. |
| **Uterosacral ligament** | Cervix → sacrum | Suspends + lifts cervix |
| **Pubocervical ligament** | Pubis → cervix | Anterior support |

In males, the analogous gubernaculum-derived structure is the **scrotal ligament**.

## Pelvic blood supply

<Figure name="reproductive/internal-iliac-branches" caption="Internal iliac artery branches - anterior trunk (uterine, vaginal, vesical, middle rectal, obturator, internal pudendal, inferior gluteal) and posterior trunk (iliolumbar, lateral sacral, superior gluteal). Supplies pelvic viscera + walls + perineum." />

The **internal iliac artery** is the main supplier of pelvic viscera. Its branches:

| Branch | Supplies |
|---|---|
| **Umbilical → superior vesical** | Bladder; the umbilical itself becomes the medial umbilical ligament after birth |
| **Inferior vesical** (male) / **Vaginal a.** (female) | Bladder base, prostate (male) / vagina (female) |
| **Middle rectal** | Rectum |
| **Internal pudendal** | Exits pelvis through greater sciatic foramen, re-enters via lesser sciatic; supplies perineum |
| **Uterine a.** (female) | Uterus; **crosses anterior to the ureter** ("water [ureter] under the bridge [uterine artery]") - surgical hazard in hysterectomy |
| **Obturator a.** | Medial thigh adductors |
| **Inferior gluteal a.** | Gluteal region |
| **Superior gluteal a.** | Gluteal muscles |

<Clinical>
**"Water under the bridge"**: the ureter passes posterior to (under) the uterine artery near the cervix. In hysterectomy, this is where the ureter is most vulnerable to injury. Identify and protect before ligating uterine vessels.
</Clinical>

## Pelvic innervation

<Figure name="reproductive/pudendal-nerve" caption="Pudendal nerve (S2-S4) - exits through greater sciatic foramen → wraps around ischial spine → re-enters via lesser sciatic foramen → pudendal (Alcock) canal → branches: inferior rectal, perineal, dorsal nerve of penis/clitoris. Motor + sensory to perineum + external sphincters." />

| Nerve | Origin | Distribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Pudendal nerve** | S2–S4 (sacral plexus) | Sensory + motor to perineum; exits pelvis via greater sciatic foramen, re-enters via lesser sciatic, runs in pudendal canal (Alcock's) |
| **Pelvic splanchnic nerves** | S2–S4 | Parasympathetic to hindgut + bladder + reproductive organs |
| **Sacral sympathetic chain** | T11–L2 (preganglionic) | Sympathetic to pelvic viscera + lower limb |
| **Inferior hypogastric plexus** | Sympathetic + parasympathetic + visceral afferent | The mixed autonomic plexus to pelvic viscera |

<Mnemonic>
**"S2, 3, 4 keep the penis off the floor"** - pudendal n. roots, essential for erection (well, parasympathetic) and external sphincter tone.
</Mnemonic>

<Clinical>
**Pudendal nerve block** for childbirth: inject local anesthetic at the ischial spine, blocking afferents from the perineum. Allows painless episiotomy + delivery without affecting uterine contraction.

**Cauda equina syndrome**: bilateral lower-limb weakness + **saddle anesthesia** (S2–S4 distribution) + urinary retention + fecal incontinence + loss of erectile function. Surgical emergency.

**Pudendal neuralgia**: chronic perineal pain. Compression in Alcock's canal between sacrotuberous + sacrospinous ligaments. Triggered by sitting.
</Clinical>

## Peritoneal pouches in the pelvis (recap)

The peritoneum drapes over pelvic organs from above, creating dependent fluid-collection sites:

| Pouch | Between |
|---|---|
| **Vesicouterine pouch** (female) | Bladder ↔ uterus |
| **Rectouterine pouch of Douglas** (female) | Uterus ↔ rectum - **lowest point in female pelvis** |
| **Rectovesical pouch** (male) | Bladder ↔ rectum - **lowest point in male pelvis** |

**Surgical access via posterior vaginal fornix** reaches the pouch of Douglas - used for culdocentesis, IVF oocyte retrieval, and selected laparoscopic procedures.

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Pelvic exam-board essentials:**
- **"Water under the bridge"** - ureter passes posterior to uterine artery. Identify before clamping during hysterectomy.
- **Pudendal block at ischial spine** - landmark for anesthesia, fetal station 0.
- **Female vs male pelvis** - sub-pubic angle > 80° in female (obstetric adaptation); narrow heart-shape in male.
- **Cardinal ligament** = primary uterine support (carries uterine artery; involved in uterine prolapse).
- **Saddle anesthesia** (S2–S4 distribution) = cauda equina syndrome - surgical emergency.
- **Pouch of Douglas** = lowest point in female pelvis = where free abdominal fluid (blood, pus, ascites) collects.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX women's health priorities:**
- **Post-childbirth perineal care**: ice packs first 24 h, then warm sitz baths; pat dry (don't wipe); peri-bottle for cleaning after voiding; assess for hematoma, infection, dehiscence; pelvic floor exercises after 1st-degree healing.
- **Pelvic organ prolapse teaching**: pessary cleaning + reinsertion; Kegel exercises (hold 10 s, repeat 10×, do 3 sets/day); weight loss; treat chronic cough/constipation.
- **Post-prostatectomy**: continence + erectile function may take months to recover; Kegels for men help; nerve-sparing technique improves outcomes.
- **Pudendal block during labor**: anesthetizes perineum without affecting contractions; useful for forceps/vacuum delivery.
</HighYield>
