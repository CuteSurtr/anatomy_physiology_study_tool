The male reproductive system produces, matures, stores, and delivers sperm; secretes testosterone; and houses the urethra for both urine and semen. Its anatomy is shaped by an embryologic quirk: the **testes develop in the abdomen and descend through the inguinal canal** into the scrotum, dragging vessels, nerves, and the vas deferens with them.

## Sperm pathway - testis to urethra

Memorize this in order:

1. **Seminiferous tubules** (in testis) - spermatogenesis happens here
2. **Rete testis** - converging tubule network
3. **Efferent ductules** - short tubes leaving testis
4. **Epididymis** - head → body → tail; **sperm mature here over ~20 days**, acquiring motility
5. **Vas (ductus) deferens** - peristaltic propulsion up the spermatic cord
6. **Ampulla of vas deferens** - terminal dilation, joins seminal vesicle duct
7. **Ejaculatory duct** - passes through prostate
8. **Prostatic urethra** - receives ejaculatory duct
9. **Membranous urethra** - through pelvic diaphragm
10. **Spongy (penile) urethra** - through corpus spongiosum
11. **External urethral orifice**

## Testis and scrotum

The testes are paired ovoid organs **outside the body cavity** because spermatogenesis requires a temperature **~2 °C below core body temp**.

Coverings (outside in):
- **Skin** of scrotum (thin, pigmented)
- **Dartos fascia** (smooth muscle - wrinkles scrotum to retain heat)
- **External spermatic fascia**
- **Cremaster muscle** (from internal oblique - voluntary lift in cold or cremasteric reflex)
- **Internal spermatic fascia**
- **Tunica vaginalis** (parietal then visceral) - peritoneal remnant from processus vaginalis
- **Tunica albuginea** - fibrous capsule of testis

### Inside the testis

<Figure name="reproductive/testicle" caption="Testicle - external (left) and sagittal section (right) showing seminiferous tubules, rete testis, efferent ductules to the epididymis (head, body, tail), and continuation as the vas deferens." />

| Structure | Function |
|---|---|
| **Seminiferous tubules** (~1 km combined length) | Spermatogenesis - Sertoli cells + germ cells |
| **Sertoli cells** | Nurse cells; form **blood-testis barrier**; secrete **inhibin B** (negative feedback on FSH); **FSH-responsive** |
| **Leydig (interstitial) cells** | Between tubules; secrete **testosterone**; **LH-responsive** |
| **Rete testis** | Anastomosing tubule network in mediastinum testis |
| **Efferent ductules** | ~12 tubules → epididymis head |

<Note title="Spermatogenesis ladder">
Spermatogonium (2n) → primary spermatocyte (2n, 4d DNA after S phase) → **meiosis I** → secondary spermatocytes (1n, 2d) → **meiosis II** → spermatids (1n, 1d) → **spermiogenesis** (cytoplasm loss, tail formation, acrosome) → spermatozoa.

Total time: **~64 days** (vs oogenesis which arrests for decades).
</Note>

## Inguinal canal - descent of the testes

In utero, the testis migrates from the posterior abdominal wall (T10 level) down through the **inguinal canal** into the scrotum. It drags its **spermatic cord** with it.

### Spermatic cord contents

| Contents | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Vas deferens** | The conduit |
| **Testicular artery** (from abdominal aorta at L2) | Long course because of embryologic origin |
| **Pampiniform venous plexus** | Network of veins around the testicular artery - countercurrent heat exchange to cool arterial blood |
| **Lymphatics** | Drain to **para-aortic nodes** (NOT inguinal) - important for cancer staging |
| **Autonomic nerves** | Sympathetic |
| **Genital branch of genitofemoral nerve** | Innervates cremaster muscle |
| **Ilioinguinal nerve** | Hitchhikes through canal; sensory to anterior scrotum |

### Inguinal canal openings

- **Deep (internal) inguinal ring**: opening in transversalis fascia
- **Superficial (external) inguinal ring**: opening in external oblique aponeurosis

<Clinical>
**Inguinal hernias** - abdominal contents protrude into the canal:
- **Indirect** (more common): through deep ring → follows spermatic cord path; **lateral to inferior epigastric vessels**. Congenital - patent processus vaginalis.
- **Direct**: through Hesselbach's triangle (medial to inferior epigastric, lateral to rectus, above inguinal ligament). Acquired - weak abdominal wall.

**Cryptorchidism**: failure of testicular descent. ↑ Risk of infertility + testicular cancer. Surgical correction (orchiopexy) by age 1.

**Varicocele**: dilated pampiniform plexus. Almost always **left-sided** because the **left testicular vein drains into the left renal vein at a right angle** (vs right drains directly into IVC). "Bag of worms" feel. Can cause infertility. Sudden left varicocele in an older man - consider renal cell carcinoma compressing the left renal vein.

**Hydrocele**: fluid in tunica vaginalis. Transilluminates.

**Testicular torsion**: twisting of spermatic cord cuts off blood supply. Sudden severe scrotal pain, high-riding testis, absent cremasteric reflex. **Surgical emergency** - detorse within 6 h to save testis.
</Clinical>

## Accessory glands

Three glands contribute to seminal fluid:

| Gland | Contribution | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Seminal vesicles (paired)** | **~70%** of ejaculate volume | Fructose (energy for sperm), prostaglandins, fibrinogen (initial coagulation), alkaline pH |
| **Prostate gland** | **~25%** of ejaculate | Citrate, zinc, PSA (re-liquefies coagulated semen), proteolytic enzymes; alkaline pH (protects sperm in acidic vagina) |
| **Bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands** | Small contribution - **pre-ejaculate** | Lubrication + neutralization of urethra |

### Prostate gland - anatomy + zones

The prostate sits inferior to the bladder, surrounding the prostatic urethra. About the size of a walnut (~20 g) in young men; can enlarge to apple-sized in BPH.

| Zone | Location | Pathology |
|---|---|---|
| **Peripheral zone** | Posterior (~70% of gland) | **Prostate adenocarcinoma** - palpable on DRE |
| **Central zone** | Around ejaculatory ducts (~25%) | Less common pathology |
| **Transition zone** | Around urethra (~5%) | **BPH** - narrows urethra → urinary obstruction |

**PSA (prostate-specific antigen)** - protease secreted by all prostatic epithelium (both normal and cancerous). Elevated in BPH, prostatitis, **prostate cancer**, recent ejaculation/DRE. Free vs total PSA ratio helps distinguish cancer (low free%) from BPH.

<Clinical>
**BPH**: nodular hyperplasia of transition zone → narrows prostatic urethra → urinary hesitancy, weak stream, nocturia, retention.
- Treat with **α1-blockers** (tamsulosin - relaxes bladder neck + prostate smooth muscle) and/or **5α-reductase inhibitors** (finasteride - blocks testosterone → DHT conversion; shrinks gland over months).
- Surgical: TURP, laser enucleation.

**Prostate cancer**: peripheral zone (palpable as hard nodule on DRE). PSA screening controversial. Treatment: surveillance for low-risk; surgery, radiation, androgen deprivation (GnRH agonists like leuprolide - continuous dosing suppresses; anti-androgens like bicalutamide) for higher-risk.
</Clinical>

## Penis - anatomy + physiology of erection

The penis has three cylindrical erectile bodies:

| Body | Position | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Corpus cavernosum (paired)** | Dorsal | Primary erectile tissue; engorged on erection |
| **Corpus spongiosum** | Ventral; surrounds urethra | Distal expansion forms **glans penis**; remains soft to keep urethra patent during ejaculation |

Each corpus is wrapped in a thick fibrous **tunica albuginea**.

### Erection physiology - "Point and shoot"

**Erection = parasympathetic** (CN S2–S4, pelvic splanchnics → cavernous nerves):

1. NO released from parasympathetic nerve endings + endothelium
2. NO → ↑ cGMP in cavernosal smooth muscle
3. Smooth muscle relaxes → ↑ arterial inflow
4. Engorged sinusoids compress veins against tunica albuginea → ↓ venous outflow
5. Result: rigid erection

**Ejaculation = sympathetic** (hypogastric plexus, L1–L2):
- Contracts vas deferens + seminal vesicles + prostate
- Closes internal urethral sphincter (prevents retrograde ejaculation)

**Orgasm = somatic** (pudendal n., S2–S4): rhythmic bulbospongiosus + ischiocavernosus contractions propel semen.

<Mnemonic>
**Point and Shoot**: **P**arasympathetic = erection (point); **S**ympathetic = ejaculation (shoot).
</Mnemonic>

<Clinical>
**Erectile dysfunction**: vascular (most common, atherosclerosis), neurogenic (diabetes, post-prostatectomy), or psychogenic.
- **PDE5 inhibitors** (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil): block breakdown of cGMP → prolong erection. Contraindicated with **nitrates** (synergistic vasodilation → severe hypotension).
- **Intracavernosal injection**: alprostadil (PGE1 → vasodilation).

**Priapism**: sustained erection >4 h without arousal. Causes: sickle cell, leukemia, drugs (trazodone, intracavernosal injections). Aspiration + injection of **phenylephrine** (α1 agonist → vasoconstriction) to detumesce.

**Peyronie disease**: scarring of tunica albuginea → curved erection.
</Clinical>

## Lymphatic drainage - high-yield surgical fact

| Region | Drains to |
|---|---|
| **Testis** | **Para-aortic (lumbar) lymph nodes** at the level of L2 - follows the testicular vessels back to their embryologic origin |
| **Scrotum (skin)** | **Superficial inguinal nodes** |
| **Penis (skin + glans)** | Mostly superficial inguinal; some deep inguinal |
| **Prostate** | Internal iliac, sacral nodes |

This is why **testicular cancer never starts with inguinal lymphadenopathy** - it spreads to retroperitoneum first. A "lump in the groin" from testicular cancer = late disease.

## Vasectomy - sterilization

Cut + ligate (or cauterize) the **vas deferens** through a small scrotal incision. Sperm continue to be produced + reabsorbed in the epididymis. **Hormones unchanged** (still LH/testosterone). Effective ~3 months later (need 2 negative semen analyses to confirm).

## Every male reproductive structure - function reference

| Structure | What it does | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Testis** | Spermatogenesis + testosterone | Descent from abdomen - site of multiple congenital anomalies |
| **Seminiferous tubules** | Site of sperm production | Sertoli + germ cells |
| **Sertoli cells** | Nurse germ cells; blood-testis barrier; inhibin B | FSH target |
| **Leydig cells** | Testosterone production | LH target |
| **Rete testis** | Tubule network | Sperm convergence |
| **Epididymis** | Sperm storage + maturation (~20 days) | Sperm acquire motility here |
| **Vas deferens** | Conduit; smooth muscle peristalsis | Vasectomy target |
| **Spermatic cord** | Vas + vessels + nerves | Through inguinal canal |
| **Pampiniform plexus** | Countercurrent heat exchange | Cools arterial blood; varicocele if dilated |
| **Seminal vesicles** | 70% of ejaculate; fructose + prostaglandins | Joined by vas at ejaculatory duct |
| **Ejaculatory duct** | Vas + seminal vesicle → urethra | Passes through prostate |
| **Prostate** | 25% of ejaculate; PSA; protective alkaline secretion | BPH (transition zone), cancer (peripheral zone) |
| **Bulbourethral glands** | Pre-ejaculate lubrication | Small contribution |
| **Prostatic urethra** | Receives ejaculatory + prostatic ducts | Surrounded by prostate |
| **Membranous urethra** | Through pelvic diaphragm | Site of external sphincter - voluntary continence (pudendal n.) |
| **Spongy urethra** | Through corpus spongiosum + penis | Longest segment |
| **Corpus cavernosum** | Dorsal erectile tissue | Engorged in erection |
| **Corpus spongiosum** | Ventral erectile tissue; surrounds urethra | Forms glans distally |
| **Glans penis** | Distal corpus spongiosum | Densely innervated (pudendal n.) |
| **Prepuce (foreskin)** | Covers glans | Removed in circumcision |

| **Inguinal canal** | Conduit for spermatic cord | Hernia site |
| **Pudendal nerve (S2–S4)** | Somatic sensation to perineum + motor to external sphincter + bulbospongiosus/ischiocavernosus | "S2, 3, 4 keep the penis off the floor" |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Male repro pearls:**
- **Testicular cancer** drains to **para-aortic LNs** (not inguinal). Tumor markers: AFP (yolk sac), β-hCG (choriocarcinoma), LDH (general). Most curable solid malignancy in young men.
- **BPH** - transition zone; treat with α1-blockers + 5α-reductase inhibitors.
- **Prostate cancer** - peripheral zone; PSA + DRE for screening (debated); androgen deprivation.
- **Cryptorchidism** → ↑ testicular cancer risk + infertility even after correction.
- **Klinefelter (47, XXY)**: tall, gynecomastia, small testes, ↑ FSH/LH, ↓ testosterone, infertile.
- **Kallmann syndrome**: hypogonadotropic hypogonadism + anosmia (failed migration of GnRH neurons along terminal nerve).
- **5α-reductase deficiency**: XY genotype, female external genitalia at birth, virilizes at puberty.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Erection + reproductive pharm:**
- **PDE5 inhibitors** (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil): for ED. Contraindicated with **nitrates** - fatal hypotension.
- **Finasteride / dutasteride**: 5α-reductase inhibitors; BPH, also male-pattern baldness; teratogen - women shouldn't handle crushed tablets.
- **Tamsulosin / alfuzosin**: selective α1A-blockers; less postural hypotension than non-selective (doxazosin); also for ureteral stones.
- **Leuprolide**: GnRH agonist; continuous → suppresses LH/FSH → ↓ testosterone (prostate Ca treatment). Pulsatile → stimulates (fertility treatment).
- **Flutamide, bicalutamide, enzalutamide**: androgen receptor antagonists.
- **Spironolactone**: aldosterone antagonist with anti-androgen side effect - gynecomastia.
- **Cimetidine, ketoconazole**: anti-androgen side effect → gynecomastia.
</HighYield>
