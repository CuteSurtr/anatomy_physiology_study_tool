Sperm and egg both start from primordial germ cells (PGCs) that migrate from the yolk sac to the gonadal ridges by week 5–6 of embryogenesis. From there, **spermatogenesis** runs continuously from puberty onward (~80 days per sperm); **oogenesis** begins in fetal life, pauses for 12–50 years, and finishes only at fertilization.

## Comparison at a glance

<Figure name="reproductive/spermatogenesis-stages" caption="" />


| Feature | Spermatogenesis | Oogenesis |
|---|---|---|
| **When it starts** | Puberty (continuous afterward) | **Fetal life** (all primary oocytes formed before birth) |
| **When it ends** | Lifelong | Menopause (~ age 50) |
| **Where** | Seminiferous tubules (testis) | Ovarian cortex (follicles) |
| **Per cycle yield** | 4 sperm per spermatogonium | 1 ovum + 3 polar bodies per oogonium |
| **Cells per round** | Millions/day | 1 mature oocyte per cycle (~400 in lifetime) |
| **Length** | ~64-74 days end to end | Months to decades |
| **Meiosis I arrested?** | No | Yes - prophase I (dictyotene) from fetal life until ovulation |
| **Meiosis II arrested?** | No | **Yes - metaphase II** until fertilization |
| **Gametes are…** | 23, X **or** 23, Y | Always 23, X |
| **Cytoplasm sharing** | Spermatogonia → cytoplasmic bridges | Polar body sheds - egg keeps everything |

## Spermatogenesis - the testis line

<Figure name="reproductive/spermatogenesis-stages" caption="Spermatogenesis stages - spermatogonia (2n) → primary spermatocytes (2n) → meiosis I → secondary spermatocytes (n) → meiosis II → spermatids (n) → spermatozoa (n, via spermiogenesis). 64-day process inside seminiferous tubules supported by Sertoli cells." />


### Steps (each spermatogonium → 4 sperm)
```
Spermatogonium (2n, 46 chr, 2C DNA)        ← stem cell, mitosis
    ↓ mitosis (one daughter stays stem)
Primary spermatocyte (2n, 46, 4C)          ← starts meiosis I (DNA already doubled)
    ↓ Meiosis I
2× Secondary spermatocyte (1n, 23, 2C)     ← arrived at haploid; sister chromatids still attached
    ↓ Meiosis II
4× Spermatid (1n, 23, 1C)                  ← haploid
    ↓ Spermiogenesis (no division)
4× Spermatozoon (1n, 23, 1C)               ← motile mature sperm
```

### Spermiogenesis - assembling the sperm
| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Acrosome** forms from Golgi | Contains hyaluronidase + acrosin for zona pellucida penetration |
| **Tail (flagellum)** grows | 9+2 microtubule axoneme + dynein |
| **Cytoplasm shed** | Residual body engulfed by Sertoli cells |
| **Nuclear compaction** | DNA tightly wound, histones largely replaced by protamines |
| **Mitochondria** spiral around midpiece | Energy for flagellar motion |

### Sertoli cells - "nurse cells"
| Function | Why |
|---|---|
| **Form blood-testis barrier** (tight junctions between Sertoli cells) | Protects haploid germ cells from systemic immune surveillance |
| **Nourish developing sperm** | Direct contact + secretions |
| **Secrete androgen-binding protein (ABP)** | Keeps testosterone high in tubule lumen |
| **Secrete inhibin** | Negative feedback on FSH (anterior pituitary) |
| **Convert testosterone → estradiol** (aromatase) | Local + circulating estrogen for spermatogenesis + bone density |
| **Phagocytose residual bodies** | Cleanup during spermiogenesis |

### Leydig cells (interstitial)
- Outside the tubules, in the interstitium.
- **Make testosterone** under LH stimulation.
- Local concentration is ~100× systemic - required for spermatogenesis.

### HPG axis in males
```
Hypothalamus (GnRH, pulsatile)
  ↓
Anterior pituitary
  ├── LH → Leydig cell → Testosterone → negative feedback on hypothalamus + pituitary
  └── FSH → Sertoli cell → spermatogenesis + Inhibin → negative feedback on FSH
```

GnRH must be **pulsatile** - continuous administration shuts down the axis (basis of leuprolide for prostate cancer).

## Oogenesis - the ovary line

<Figure name="reproductive/follicle-stages" caption="Ovarian follicle development - primordial → primary → secondary → tertiary (antral) → mature Graafian → ovulation → corpus luteum → corpus albicans. One oocyte completes meiosis I each cycle at ovulation; meiosis II completes only on fertilization." />

### Steps (each oogonium → 1 ovum + 3 polar bodies)
```
Oogonium (2n, 46, 2C)                       ← fetal, before week 12
    ↓ mitosis (only in fetal life)
Primary oocyte (2n, 46, 4C)                 ← arrested in prophase I (dictyotene)
                                              [paused until ovulation, months-decades]
    ↓ Meiosis I (resumes just before ovulation under LH surge)
Secondary oocyte (1n, 23, 2C) + 1st polar body
    ↓ enters Meiosis II
Arrested at metaphase II                    ← THIS IS WHAT IS OVULATED
                                              [paused until fertilization]
    ↓ Meiosis II completes only if sperm penetrates
Mature ovum (1n, 23, 1C) + 2nd polar body
```

### Why pause twice?
- **Prophase I arrest** (dictyotene) preserves all 1-2 million primary oocytes throughout childhood. Ages of mom = age of oocytes → ↑ aneuploidy risk with maternal age (40+ years for some).
- **Metaphase II arrest** until fertilization keeps the egg primed to immediately complete meiosis the moment sperm enters - prevents parthenogenesis.

### Follicle stages (around each oocyte)
| Stage | Cell layers + key features |
|---|---|
| **Primordial follicle** | Single layer flat granulosa cells; resting pool from before birth |
| **Primary follicle** | Cuboidal granulosa cells + zona pellucida appears |
| **Secondary follicle** | Multiple granulosa layers + theca interna develops (LH receptors) |
| **Antral (Graafian) follicle** | Antrum (fluid-filled space) + cumulus oophorus around the oocyte + outer theca externa |
| **Mature/preovulatory** | One dominant follicle per cycle reaches ~20 mm; the rest atrese |
| **Corpus luteum** | Post-ovulation, granulosa + theca become large luteal cells → progesterone + estrogen |
| **Corpus albicans** | Scar tissue replacing corpus luteum after ~14 days if no implantation |

### Two-cell, two-gonadotropin theory
```
LH → Theca cells: cholesterol → androstenedione → diffuses to granulosa
FSH → Granulosa cells: androstenedione + aromatase → estradiol
```
This explains why **both LH and FSH** are needed for estrogen synthesis. Theca = androgen factory; granulosa = aromatization.

<Mnemonic>
**"Theca produces, granulosa converts"** - theca makes androgens (LH-driven); granulosa aromatizes to estrogen (FSH-driven). PCOS = ↑ LH → ↑ thecal androgens → hirsutism + acne; insulin resistance amplifies the LH effect.
</Mnemonic>

## Fertilization - what actually happens

1. **Capacitation** in female tract - sperm membrane is modified by enzymes + Ca²⁺, making it competent for the acrosome reaction.
2. Sperm penetrates corona radiata (cumulus cells).
3. **Acrosome reaction**: sperm contacts ZP3 on zona pellucida → acrosomal enzymes (acrosin, hyaluronidase) released → digest a path.
4. Sperm fuses with oocyte plasma membrane → cortical granules release → **zona reaction** prevents polyspermy (modifies ZP3 so other sperm can't bind).
5. **Oocyte completes meiosis II** → produces second polar body.
6. **Pronuclei** (one from each) form, then fuse → **zygote (2n)**.
7. Cleavage divisions → morula (day 4) → blastocyst (day 5-6) → implantation (day 6-7).
8. Syncytiotrophoblast secretes **hCG** → maintains corpus luteum → progesterone → maintains endometrium.

## Menopause - the ovarian endgame

<Figure name="reproductive/follicle-stages" caption="" />


| Phase | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Reserve** | Maximum ~7 million PGCs at month 5 of fetal life → 1-2 million at birth → 400k at puberty → 1k by 50s |
| **Mechanism** | Apoptosis ("atresia") of follicles is constant; rate is independent of cycle |
| **Onset** | Average ~51 years (range 45-55) |
| **Hormonal signature** | ↑↑ FSH (lost inhibin + estrogen negative feedback), ↑ LH; ↓↓ estradiol; ↓ inhibin |
| **Symptoms** | Hot flashes, vaginal atrophy, mood changes, sleep disturbance, accelerated osteoporosis |
| **Treatment** | HRT (estrogen ± progestin if uterus); SSRIs for hot flashes; vaginal estrogen for atrophy |

<Clinical>
**Klinefelter (XXY)**: most common cause of primary testicular failure. Tall, gynecomastia, small firm testes, infertile, ↓ testosterone, ↑↑ LH/FSH. Often diagnosed at infertility workup.

**Turner (X0)**: ovarian dysgenesis. Short stature, webbed neck, primary amenorrhea, ↑↑ FSH, ↓ estrogen, streak ovaries, coarctation, horseshoe kidney.

**PCOS**: hyperandrogenism + oligomenorrhea + polycystic ovaries on US (Rotterdam: 2 of 3). LH/FSH ratio often >2:1. Insulin resistance is central. Treat: lifestyle, metformin, OCPs, spironolactone, letrozole/clomiphene for fertility.

**Premature ovarian insufficiency** (POI): menopause before 40. Causes: autoimmune (look for adrenal, thyroid), fragile X premutation, chemo/radiation, idiopathic. ↑↑ FSH.
</Clinical>

## Function reference - every key player

| Component | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Sertoli cell** | Blood-testis barrier, nourish sperm, inhibin, ABP, aromatase | Damaged by heat (varicocele), radiation, chemo, mumps |
| **Leydig cell** | Testosterone synthesis under LH | Loss → hypogonadism |
| **Theca cell** | Androgen precursor synthesis under LH | Source of androgens in PCOS |
| **Granulosa cell** | Aromatize androgens → estrogen; FSH receptor | Tumor → estrogen excess; Call-Exner bodies on histology |
| **Corpus luteum** | Progesterone (+ estrogen) for ~14 days post-ovulation | Maintained by hCG if pregnancy; otherwise involutes |
| **Zona pellucida** | Glycoprotein coat (ZP1-4) | ZP3 = sperm receptor; modified by cortical reaction (blocks polyspermy) |
| **Acrosome** | Apical enzyme cap of sperm | Loss = infertility (globozoospermia) |
| **Inhibin** | Sertoli + granulosa product; ↓ FSH | Tumor marker for granulosa cell tumors |
| **hCG** | Trophoblast → maintains corpus luteum | Pregnancy test; ↑ also in choriocarcinoma, hydatidiform mole |
| **GnRH** | Hypothalamic pulses every 60-90 min | Continuous infusion shuts the axis off (leuprolide) |
| **Aromatase** | Androgen → estrogen | Inhibited by anastrozole, letrozole (breast cancer, infertility) |
| **5α-reductase** | Testosterone → DHT | Inhibited by finasteride, dutasteride (BPH, alopecia, transgender care) |
| **Androgen receptor** | Nuclear; mediates T and DHT effects | Complete loss = androgen insensitivity syndrome (46,XY phenotypic female) |

## Why this matters

<Figure name="reproductive/spermatogenesis" caption="" />


<HighYield variant="boards">
- **Maternal age + Down syndrome**: rate of trisomy 21 rises steeply after age 35 because of the long prophase I arrest. Recommend offer NIPT to all pregnancies.
- **Cryptorchidism**: undescended testis. Risk: germ cell cancer (4-7×), infertility. Surgical orchiopexy by age 6-12 months.
- **Varicocele**: dilation of pampiniform plexus, usually **left** (left gonadal vein drains into left renal vein at 90°). Causes infertility via ↑ scrotal temperature.
- **Androgen insensitivity (CAIS)**: 46,XY with non-functional AR → phenotypic female (testosterone has no effect, but testicular AMH still regresses Müllerian ducts). Primary amenorrhea + blind vaginal pouch + absent uterus + breasts develop normally (testosterone aromatized to estrogen).
- **Kallmann syndrome**: anosmia + hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (no GnRH neuron migration). Treat with exogenous GnRH or testosterone.
- **OCPs**: estrogen + progestin → suppress LH/FSH → no ovulation. Side effects: VTE (estrogen), HTN, breast tenderness, breakthrough bleeding.
- **Clomiphene**: SERM that antagonizes hypothalamic ER → ↑ GnRH → ↑ FSH/LH → ovulation induction.
- **Letrozole**: aromatase inhibitor; first-line for PCOS infertility (better than clomiphene per PPCOS II trial).
- **GnRH agonist** (leuprolide) given continuously: paradoxical suppression after initial flare. Used for prostate CA, endometriosis, precocious puberty.
- **Spermatogenesis-killing exposures**: heat (hot tubs, varicocele), radiation, chemo (especially alkylators), excess testosterone (negative feedback shuts FSH), opioids (suppress GnRH), anti-androgens.
</HighYield>
