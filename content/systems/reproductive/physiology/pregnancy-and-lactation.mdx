Pregnancy reroutes nearly every physiologic system. Plasma volume rises 50%, GFR rises 50%, cardiac output rises 30-50%, tidal volume rises 40%. Hormone profiles are unique. Then parturition - a single positive feedback loop driven by oxytocin and prostaglandins - ends nine months of remodeling in a few hours. Lactation begins as estrogen and progesterone fall and prolactin rises.

## Fertilization and implantation - the first two weeks

<Figure name="reproductive/placenta-structure" caption="Placenta structure - maternal side (decidua basalis) with maternal lacunae; fetal side (chorionic villi) carrying fetal capillaries. Maternal blood bathes the villi; chorionic villus epithelium mediates exchange." />

| Day | Event |
|---|---|
| **0** | Ovulation; oocyte released from a Graafian follicle into the fallopian tube |
| **0-1** | **Fertilization** in the ampulla. Sperm penetrates zona pellucida, fuses with oocyte → completion of meiosis II → zygote |
| **1-3** | Cleavage divisions (2-, 4-, 8-cell) - morula by day 3 |
| **4-5** | Blastocyst forms - inner cell mass (→ embryo) + trophoblast (→ placenta), blastocoel cavity |
| **5-6** | Blastocyst arrives in the uterus, hatches from zona pellucida |
| **6-12** | **Implantation** in the endometrium (now decidualized under progesterone). Syncytiotrophoblast invades; **hCG production begins** |

**hCG** (human chorionic gonadotropin) is the signal that "rescues" the corpus luteum from regression. The corpus luteum keeps producing **progesterone** until the placenta takes over (~10 weeks). hCG also drives β-hCG home pregnancy tests - detectable in urine ~10 days post-fertilization.

<Note title="hCG pearls">
- hCG **doubles every ~48 h** in early viable pregnancy. Slow rise → suspect ectopic or impending miscarriage.
- hCG cross-reacts with **TSH receptor** → mild ↑ free T₄ and ↓ TSH in first trimester (physiologic).
- Very high hCG (> 100,000) suggests **multiple gestation** or **gestational trophoblastic disease** (molar pregnancy, choriocarcinoma).
- hCG also stimulates fetal Leydig cells in male fetuses → testosterone synthesis.
- Detectable on dipstick at hCG > 25 IU/L, on quantitative serum at > 5 IU/L.
</Note>

## The placenta - a temporary endocrine and gas-exchange organ

<Figure name="reproductive/placenta-photo" caption="Placenta - the discoid organ formed by the chorionic villi (fetal) and decidua basalis (maternal). Two umbilical arteries + one umbilical vein run through the cord." />

<Figure name="foundations/foetal-circulation" caption="Fetal circulation - oxygenated blood enters via umbilical vein → ductus venosus → IVC → RA → foramen ovale → LA → systemic. Shunts (DA, FO, ductus venosus) bypass the non-functional fetal lungs and liver." />

The placenta is the interface between maternal and fetal circulation. Functions:

| Function | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Gas exchange** | Maternal blood bathes chorionic villi; O₂/CO₂ diffuse across syncytiotrophoblast. Fetal Hb (HbF) has higher O₂ affinity than maternal → favors O₂ transfer |
| **Nutrient transfer** | Glucose (GLUT1/3 facilitated diffusion), amino acids (active), fatty acids (passive) |
| **Waste removal** | CO₂, urea, creatinine, bilirubin → maternal circulation |
| **Hormone production** | hCG (early), progesterone, estriol, hPL (human placental lactogen), CRH |
| **Immune barrier** | Limits fetal antigen exposure; allows passage of IgG (passive immunity) |
| **Drug barrier (imperfect)** | Many drugs cross; teratogens including warfarin, ACE-Is, valproate, isotretinoin, methotrexate, thalidomide |

### Placental hormones

| Hormone | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **hCG** | Syncytiotrophoblast | Maintains corpus luteum (→ progesterone) until ~10 wk |
| **Progesterone** | Corpus luteum → placenta (~10 wk) | Maintains endometrium; suppresses uterine contraction; mammary alveolar growth |
| **Estriol (E3)** | Placenta (uses DHEA-S from fetal adrenal as substrate) | Uterine growth, blood flow; mammary duct growth |
| **hPL (human placental lactogen)** | Syncytiotrophoblast | "Insulin antagonist" - ↑ maternal insulin resistance to spare glucose for fetus. Drives **gestational diabetes**. ↑ lipolysis. Stimulates mammary growth |
| **Placental CRH** | Placenta (mainly late gestation) | Drives fetal cortisol production → lung maturation; may set timing of parturition |
| **Relaxin** | Corpus luteum + placenta | Loosens pelvic ligaments, softens cervix |

## Maternal physiologic adaptations

<Figure name="reproductive/placenta-structure" caption="" />


| System | Change | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Cardiovascular** | CO ↑ 30-50% (HR ↑ 10-15 bpm, SV ↑); MAP ↓ slightly (estrogen vasodilation, ↓ SVR); plasma volume ↑ 50%, RBC mass ↑ 30% → **dilutional anemia** | Avoid supine position in T3 - IVC compression by uterus → ↓ venous return |
| **Hematology** | Hypercoagulable (↑ fibrinogen, ↑ factors VII, VIII, IX, X; ↓ protein S; ↓ fibrinolysis) | DVT/PE risk 4-5× higher; LMWH if high risk |
| **Respiratory** | TV ↑ 30-40%, minute ventilation ↑ 40% (progesterone drive); chronic mild respiratory alkalosis (PaCO₂ ~30); FRC ↓ (uterus pushes diaphragm) | Dyspnea common; rapid desaturation with apnea |
| **Renal** | GFR ↑ 50%; creatinine drops to ~0.5 (a normal Cr in pregnancy is abnormal); glucosuria common (filtered load exceeds reabsorptive maximum); RAAS activated but → physiologic | Drug doses may need adjustment |
| **Endocrine** | TBG ↑ → total T₄ ↑ (free T₄ normal); insulin resistance ↑ from hPL; prolactin rises; pituitary enlarges 30% | Gestational diabetes; need ↑ levothyroxine dose; risk of Sheehan if peripartum hemorrhage |
| **GI** | Progesterone slows motility → constipation, GERD, gallstones; nausea/vomiting peak T1 from hCG | Hyperemesis gravidarum if severe |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Lumbar lordosis ↑; pelvic ligaments loosen | Low back pain; sacroiliac pain |
| **Skin** | Linea nigra, melasma (chloasma), striae gravidarum, spider angiomas, palmar erythema | Hormonal (MSH, estrogen) |

## Parturition - the birth cascade

The trigger of labor is not fully understood but involves:
- **↓ Progesterone:estrogen ratio** locally at the uterus (estrogen rises as fetal adrenal makes DHEA-S → placental estriol)
- **↑ Oxytocin receptors** on myometrium (estrogen-driven)
- **↑ Prostaglandins** (PGE₂, PGF₂α) from membranes and decidua → cervical ripening + myometrial contraction
- **Cervical stretch** → reflex oxytocin release (Ferguson reflex)
- **Mechanical stretch** of myometrium → ↑ gap junctions (connexin-43) → coordinated contraction
- **Placental CRH** → fetal cortisol → lung maturation + may signal parturition

### The positive feedback loop

<Figure name="foundations/positive-feedback-pregnancy" caption="Positive feedback in childbirth - cervical stretch activates oxytocin release from the posterior pituitary, which intensifies uterine contraction, which further stretches the cervix. The loop only terminates when the baby is delivered." />

Once labor begins, **cervical stretch → oxytocin release → myometrial contraction → more cervical stretch**. The loop is self-amplifying and only terminates with delivery.

### The three stages of labor

| Stage | What happens | Typical duration (primip) |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1: Dilation** | Onset of regular contractions → 10 cm dilation. Latent (0-6 cm, slow) and active (6-10 cm, ~1 cm/h) phases | 12-20 h |
| **Stage 2: Expulsion** | Full dilation → birth of baby. Maternal pushing + uterine contractions | Up to 3 h |
| **Stage 3: Placental** | Birth of baby → delivery of placenta | 5-30 min |

<Clinical>
**Drugs in obstetric practice:**
- **Oxytocin (Pitocin)** - induction/augmentation of labor; postpartum hemorrhage prevention
- **Methylergonovine** - postpartum hemorrhage (avoid in HTN, preeclampsia)
- **Carboprost (PGF₂α)** - postpartum hemorrhage (avoid in asthma)
- **Misoprostol (PGE₁ analog)** - cervical ripening, hemorrhage
- **Magnesium sulfate** - eclampsia/preeclampsia seizure prophylaxis; tocolysis (off-label); neuroprotection in preterm
- **Betamethasone** - antenatal fetal lung maturation (between 24-34 wk)
- **Nifedipine, terbutaline** - tocolytics for preterm labor
- **RhoGAM (anti-D immunoglobulin)** - given at 28 wk and within 72 h of delivery in Rh-negative mothers carrying Rh-positive babies → prevents alloimmunization and erythroblastosis fetalis in future pregnancies
</Clinical>

## Lactation

<Figure name="reproductive/breast-lobules-ducts" caption="" />


### Mammary gland preparation

During pregnancy:
- **Estrogen** stimulates duct proliferation
- **Progesterone** stimulates alveolar (lobuloalveolar) growth
- **hPL and prolactin** drive secretory cell differentiation
- **Insulin, cortisol, thyroid hormone** are permissive

Prolactin (from anterior pituitary lactotrophs) rises throughout pregnancy but milk *production* is **inhibited** by high estrogen and progesterone.

### Lactogenesis

| Stage | Trigger | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Lactogenesis I** | During pregnancy (mid-late) | Colostrum (small amounts, IgA-rich) |
| **Lactogenesis II** | Withdrawal of placental progesterone after delivery + continued prolactin | "Milk coming in" within 2-3 days |
| **Lactogenesis III** | Continued suckling-driven prolactin release | Mature milk; supply matches demand |

### Two-hormone reflex loop

| Reflex | Stimulus | Hormone | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Milk production** | Suckling → afferent → hypothalamus → ↓ dopamine → anterior pituitary | **Prolactin** (anterior pituitary) | Synthesis of milk in alveolar cells |
| **Milk ejection (let-down)** | Suckling, infant cry, even thinking of baby | **Oxytocin** (posterior pituitary) | Myoepithelial cell contraction around alveoli → milk into ducts |

**Both reflexes can be inhibited by stress** (sympathetic activation, cortisol) - which is why anxiety can disrupt breastfeeding.

### Milk composition

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| **Lactose** | Energy; promotes Ca²⁺ absorption |
| **Lipid (3-4%)** | Energy; fatty acids for membranes; long-chain PUFAs for brain |
| **Whey + casein protein** | Amino acids; immune factors |
| **Secretory IgA** | Passive mucosal immunity - one of the most important benefits of breastfeeding |
| **Lysozyme, lactoferrin** | Antimicrobial |
| **Maternal lymphocytes** | Trophic, immune |
| **Vitamins** (low D, low K) | Supplement vitamin D and give vitamin K at birth |

### Lactational amenorrhea

Prolactin suppresses **GnRH** → suppresses LH/FSH → no ovulation. Exclusive, frequent breastfeeding can provide 98% contraceptive efficacy in the first 6 months postpartum (LAM - lactational amenorrhea method). Not reliable beyond 6 months or if supplementation is added.

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Classic exam vignettes:**
- **Preeclampsia** - new HTN + proteinuria after 20 wk. Severe features: BP ≥ 160/110, end-organ damage (↑ LFTs, ↓ platelets, AKI, headache, vision changes, pulmonary edema). Treat with delivery (definitive), magnesium for seizure prophylaxis, labetalol/hydralazine/nifedipine for BP.
- **Eclampsia** - preeclampsia + seizures.
- **HELLP** - Hemolysis, Elevated LFTs, Low Platelets. Variant of severe preeclampsia.
- **Gestational diabetes** - insulin resistance from hPL. Screen 24-28 wk. Treat with diet, exercise, insulin if needed.
- **Sheehan syndrome** - postpartum pituitary necrosis from peripartum hemorrhage. Cannot lactate (no prolactin); amenorrhea; fatigue.
- **Postpartum thyroiditis** - hyperthyroid → hypothyroid → recovery; positive TPO antibodies. May progress to permanent Hashimoto.
- **Mastitis** - lactating breast erythema + fever + pain. **Continue breastfeeding**; antibiotics if no improvement (S. aureus most common).
- **Galactorrhea + amenorrhea** in a non-pregnant non-lactating woman → check prolactin → prolactinoma if elevated.
</HighYield>
