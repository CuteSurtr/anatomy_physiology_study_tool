Skeletal muscle contraction is **thin filaments sliding past thick filaments**, shortening the sarcomere - the filaments themselves don't shorten. The cycle is calcium-gated and ATP-powered. First proposed independently by **A.F. Huxley & R. Niedergerke** and **H.E. Huxley & J. Hanson** in 1954.

## Molecular cast of characters

### Thick filament (myosin)

A bundle of ~300 **myosin II** molecules. Each myosin is a hexamer: two **heavy chains** (each with a globular head, neck, and rod tail) + two pairs of **light chains** (essential + regulatory). Heads project outward in a helical pattern. Functions of the head:

- ATPase activity (hydrolyzes ATP to ADP + Pi)
- Actin binding
- Conformational change → "power stroke"

The **bare zone** in the middle of each thick filament (where no heads project) corresponds to the **H zone** of the sarcomere.

<Figure name="muscular/actin-myosin" caption="Actin/myosin filaments. Thin filament: actin backbone with tropomyosin coiled along it and troponin (with TnC/TnT/TnI subunits) at intervals. Thick filament: myosin tail bundle with paired motor heads projecting outward." />

### Thin filament (actin + tropomyosin + troponin)

- **G-actin** monomers polymerize into a double-helical **F-actin** strand. Has structural polarity: plus (+) end at the Z disc, minus (−) end at the M line.
- **Tropomyosin** - a rope-like coiled-coil dimer that wraps along seven actin monomers, lying in the groove of the F-actin helix. At rest it physically blocks the myosin-binding sites on actin.
- **Troponin complex** (three subunits per regulatory unit):
  - **Troponin T (TnT)** - binds tropomyosin (anchor)
  - **Troponin I (TnI)** - binds actin, **inhibits** actomyosin interaction at rest
  - **Troponin C (TnC)** - binds Ca²⁺ (four sites; two regulatory, two structural)

### Other key proteins

- **Titin** - the largest known protein (~3 MDa). Spans Z disc to M line; gives passive elastic recoil. Limits sarcomere length.
- **Nebulin** - runs along the thin filament; thought to act as a "ruler" for thin filament length.
- **Dystrophin** - links sarcomere cytoskeleton to the sarcolemma via the dystroglycan complex. Lost in **Duchenne muscular dystrophy** (X-linked frameshift), reduced in **Becker** (in-frame deletion).
- **Myosin-binding protein C, α-actinin (Z disc anchor), desmin (intermediate filament around Z discs)** - structural.

## The cross-bridge cycle

1. **ATP binds myosin head** → myosin releases actin.
2. **ATP hydrolysis** → head cocks into high-energy position (still holding ADP + P_i).
3. **Ca²⁺ binds troponin C** → tropomyosin shifts, exposing actin binding sites.
4. **Cross-bridge forms** → myosin head binds actin.
5. **Power stroke** → P_i released, head pivots, thin filament slides toward M-line. ADP released.
6. Cycle repeats as long as Ca²⁺ and ATP are present.

## Excitation–contraction coupling

The signal from motor neuron to muscle contraction is a chain of seven steps. Each one is a potential failure point with known clinical correlates.

1. **AP arrives at the motor neuron terminal** → voltage-gated **Ca²⁺ channels** open → Ca²⁺ enters terminal.
2. **ACh vesicles fuse with the membrane** (SNARE-mediated) → ACh released into the synaptic cleft.
3. **ACh binds nicotinic AChR (NM)** on the motor end-plate of the sarcolemma → ion channel opens → Na⁺ in, K⁺ out → **end-plate potential**.
4. **End-plate potential triggers a muscle AP** that propagates along the sarcolemma and **down the T-tubules** (deep invaginations of the sarcolemma at the A-I junction).
5. **T-tubule depolarization activates DHPRs** (L-type Ca²⁺ channels acting as voltage sensors). DHPRs are **mechanically coupled** to **ryanodine receptors (RyR1)** on the SR membrane.
6. **RyR1 opens** → Ca²⁺ released from the SR into the cytosol (Ca²⁺-induced Ca²⁺ release amplifies this in cardiac muscle but is less prominent in skeletal).
7. **Ca²⁺ binds TnC** → cross-bridge cycle begins.

Relaxation requires **SERCA** (sarcoplasmic/ER Ca²⁺ ATPase) on the SR membrane to pump cytosolic Ca²⁺ back into the SR - an active process consuming ~30% of muscle ATP at rest. The **Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger** on the sarcolemma extrudes a smaller fraction of Ca²⁺ to the extracellular space.

### The T-tubule triad

<Figure name="muscular/sarcoplasmic-reticulum" caption="Sarcoplasmic reticulum + T-tubules forming triads around each sarcomere. T-tubule depolarization activates DHPRs (voltage sensors), which mechanically open the adjacent ryanodine receptors (RyR1) on the SR, releasing Ca2+ into the cytosol." />

In skeletal muscle, each T-tubule is flanked by **two terminal cisternae** of SR, forming a **triad** at the A-I band junction. Cardiac muscle has **dyads** (one T-tubule + one SR cisterna) located at the Z line - and uses extracellular Ca²⁺ influx (rather than purely SR release) to trigger contraction.

### Where it can go wrong (high-yield NMJ and EC-coupling disorders)

| Disease | Mechanism | Clinical pearl |
|---|---|---|
| **Myasthenia gravis** | Auto-Ab against postsynaptic nicotinic AChR | Ptosis, fatigable weakness; **improves** with edrophonium/pyridostigmine |
| **Lambert-Eaton** | Auto-Ab against presynaptic VGCC (Ca²⁺ channel) | Often paraneoplastic (small-cell lung Ca); **improves** with repeated use |
| **Botulism** | Botulinum toxin cleaves SNARE proteins → no ACh release | Descending flaccid paralysis, "floppy baby"; pupils dilated |
| **Tetanus** | Tetanospasmin cleaves SNAREs in inhibitory spinal interneurons → unopposed motor | Spastic paralysis, lockjaw, opisthotonos |
| **Curare** | Competitive nicotinic AChR block | Used historically as paralytic in anesthesia |
| **Succinylcholine** | Depolarizing NMJ blocker (sustained agonist) | Brief paralysis for intubation; risk of hyperkalemia and **malignant hyperthermia** |
| **Malignant hyperthermia** | Mutated RyR1 → uncontrolled Ca²⁺ release from SR | Hyperthermia, rigidity, hypercarbia under volatile anesthesia + succinylcholine; treat with **dantrolene** (RyR1 blocker) |
| **Duchenne muscular dystrophy** | Dystrophin gene frameshift | Boys, calf pseudohypertrophy, Gowers sign, ↑↑↑ CK |

<Note title="Why rigor mortis happens">
After death, ATP runs out. Myosin heads cannot release actin (step 1 fails) and Ca²⁺ leaks from the SR with no SERCA to pump it back. Muscle stays contracted until proteolysis breaks the cross-bridges (~24–48 h).
</Note>

<Clinical>
**Myasthenia gravis**: autoantibodies against the nicotinic ACh receptor at the NMJ. Fewer functional receptors → fewer end-plate potentials → muscle weakness that worsens with use. Improves transiently with **acetylcholinesterase inhibitors** (pyridostigmine), which prolong ACh in the synaptic cleft.
</Clinical>

## Sarcomere anatomy review

The contractile unit is the **sarcomere**, from one **Z disc** to the next. Within it:

| Band/zone | What's there | Behavior on contraction |
|---|---|---|
| **Z disc** | Anchors thin filaments | Z discs pulled toward M line |
| **I band** | Thin filaments only (around the Z) | **Shortens** |
| **A band** | Full length of thick filaments | **Stays the same length** (myosin doesn't shrink) |
| **H zone** | Thick filaments only (no overlap) | **Shortens** |
| **M line** | Center of A band; anchors thick filaments | Reference point - does not move |

## Muscle fiber types

| Type | Contraction | Fatigue | Color | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Type I (slow oxidative)** | Slow | Resistant | Red (myoglobin-rich, many mitochondria) | Postural, marathon |
| **Type IIa (fast oxidative)** | Fast | Moderate | Pink | Sustained sprinting |
| **Type IIx/b (fast glycolytic)** | Fast | Quickly | White | Sprints, jumps |

## Every sarcomere structure and EC-coupling component - function table

### Sarcomere structural components

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Z disc** | Boundary of one sarcomere; anchors thin filaments via α-actinin | Defines the sarcomere unit; transmits force between sarcomeres; in series for whole-fiber shortening |
| **M line** | Midline of sarcomere; anchors thick filaments via myomesin | Keeps thick filaments registered and aligned; reference point in A band |
| **A band** | Length of thick filaments (myosin) | Stays the **same length during contraction** - myosin doesn't shorten; useful EM landmark |
| **I band** | Region of thin filament only, around the Z disc | **Shortens during contraction** as thin filaments slide inward |
| **H zone** | Center of A band - thick filament without thin overlap | **Shortens during contraction** as thin filaments encroach |
| **Bare zone (middle of thick filament)** | Region of myosin tails with no projecting heads | Marks the actual center; corresponds to M line region |
| **Sarcomere** | Z-disc-to-Z-disc unit | Fundamental contractile unit; thousands in series per myofibril |

### Myosin (thick filament)

| Component | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Myosin heavy chain (×2 per molecule)** | Globular head + neck + α-helical rod tail | Heads do work; tails self-assemble into the bipolar thick filament |
| **Myosin head** | ATPase activity + actin binding + power stroke | Each cycle pulls thin filament ~10 nm toward M line; hydrolyzes 1 ATP per cycle |
| **Essential + regulatory light chains** | Wrap the neck (lever arm); modulate head movement | Phosphorylation of regulatory light chain in smooth muscle is the contraction switch (unlike skeletal) |
| **Myosin-binding protein C (MyBP-C)** | Stabilizes thick filament; modulates cross-bridge cycling | Mutations cause hypertrophic cardiomyopathy in heart |

### Thin filament

| Component | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **G-actin** | Globular monomer | Polymerizes into F-actin |
| **F-actin** | Polymer (double helix) | Substrate for myosin binding; polarized (+ end at Z, − end at M) |
| **Tropomyosin** | Coiled-coil dimer covering 7 actin monomers | At rest, **physically blocks myosin-binding sites**; shifts when Ca²⁺ binds troponin |
| **Troponin T (TnT)** | Binds tropomyosin | Anchors the troponin complex along the thin filament |
| **Troponin I (TnI)** | Binds actin; **inhibits** actin-myosin interaction at rest | Released when TnC binds Ca²⁺ - uncovers binding sites |
| **Troponin C (TnC)** | Binds 4 Ca²⁺ (2 regulatory + 2 structural) | The Ca²⁺ sensor of contraction; cardiac TnT and TnI are used as **biomarkers of MI** |

### Scaffolding and cytoskeleton

| Component | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Titin** | Largest known protein; Z disc → M line | Passive elastic recoil; limits sarcomere length; bidirectional spring during contraction/relaxation |
| **Nebulin** | Long protein along the thin filament | Acts as a "ruler" specifying thin filament length |
| **α-actinin** | Z disc protein; crosslinks thin filaments | Maintains Z-disc structure |
| **Desmin** | Intermediate filament wrapping each Z disc, linking adjacent myofibrils | Keeps Z discs in register across the muscle cell; mutations → desminopathies |
| **Dystrophin** | Links sarcomere cytoskeleton (actin) to dystroglycan complex in the sarcolemma | Transmits force from sarcomere to extracellular matrix; loss → **Duchenne MD**; reduced → Becker MD |
| **Dystroglycan complex** | Spans sarcolemma; binds dystrophin (inside) and laminin (outside) | Mechanically anchors muscle cell to basement membrane |

### Excitation–contraction coupling machinery

| Component | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Sarcolemma** | Muscle cell plasma membrane | Carries AP from end-plate along the fiber |
| **Motor end-plate / NMJ** | Specialized synapse between motor neuron and muscle | Site of MG, LEMS, botulism, curare action |
| **Nicotinic AChR (NM type)** | Ligand-gated cation channel on motor end-plate | Opens on ACh binding → end-plate potential triggers muscle AP |
| **T-tubule** | Invagination of sarcolemma at the A-I band junction in skeletal muscle (one per sarcomere) | Carries AP into the cell interior - without it, deep myofibrils wouldn't get the signal |
| **Triad (skeletal)** | T-tubule flanked by two SR cisternae | Structural site of DHPR-RyR1 coupling |
| **Dyad (cardiac)** | T-tubule + one SR cisterna at the Z line | Cardiac version - uses Ca²⁺-induced Ca²⁺ release more prominently |
| **Dihydropyridine receptor (DHPR)** | L-type Ca²⁺ channel + voltage sensor on T-tubule | **Mechanically gates RyR1 in skeletal muscle** (direct mechanical coupling); in cardiac, allows Ca²⁺ influx that triggers RyR2 |
| **Ryanodine receptor (RyR1 skeletal, RyR2 cardiac)** | Massive Ca²⁺-release channel on SR membrane | Opens to flood cytosol with Ca²⁺; **RyR1 mutations → malignant hyperthermia**; **dantrolene blocks it** |
| **Sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR)** | Specialized smooth ER that stores Ca²⁺ at ~10,000× cytosolic concentration | Source of Ca²⁺ for contraction; volume + Ca²⁺ content set how strong/fast the contraction can be |
| **Terminal cisternae** | Dilated SR portions abutting T-tubules | Site of RyR clusters; the actual Ca²⁺ release sites |
| **SERCA** | Sarcoplasmic Ca²⁺ ATPase | Pumps cytosolic Ca²⁺ back into SR - required for relaxation; consumes ~30% of resting muscle ATP |
| **Calsequestrin** | High-capacity Ca²⁺-buffering protein in SR lumen | Stores Ca²⁺ at high concentration without raising free Ca²⁺ (keeps SERCA gradient manageable) |
| **Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger (NCX)** | Sarcolemmal exchanger | Extrudes Ca²⁺ to extracellular space; important in cardiac myocytes |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX nursing priorities for NMJ disorders:**
- **Myasthenia gravis**: weakness worsens with use; **edrophonium (Tensilon) test** historically; now anti-AChR antibodies. Crisis = respiratory failure → ICU.
- **Pyridostigmine** dosing on schedule (don't miss - risk of myasthenic crisis); over-dose → cholinergic crisis (SLUDGE-M).
- **Plasmapheresis or IVIG** for myasthenic/Guillain-Barré crisis.
- **Malignant hyperthermia**: hyperthermia, rigidity, hypercarbia after volatile anesthetics or succinylcholine → **stop anesthetic, give dantrolene IV**.
- **Botulism** infant feeding precaution: no honey before 1 year; "floppy baby" with descending paralysis.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**NMJ and muscle pharmacology:**
- **Succinylcholine** (depolarizing NMJ blocker): rapid intubation; risks hyperkalemia (avoid in burns &gt;24 h, crush injury, denervation), MH.
- **Rocuronium / vecuronium** (non-depolarizing): reversed by **sugammadex** (rocuronium) or neostigmine + glycopyrrolate.
- **Pyridostigmine, neostigmine, edrophonium** - AChE inhibitors; treat MG.
- **Atropine** - muscarinic blocker, used with neostigmine to avoid bradycardia.
- **Dantrolene** - blocks RyR1 → treats MH and neuroleptic malignant syndrome.
- **Botulinum toxin (Botox)** - therapeutic: dystonia, spasticity, migraine, cosmetic; blocks SNARE-mediated ACh release.
</HighYield>
