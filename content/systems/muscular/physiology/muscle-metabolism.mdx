A contracting muscle needs **ATP** - for myosin head detachment, Ca²⁺ pumping back into the SR (SERCA), and Na/K-ATPase activity. Muscle has **three energy systems** running on different timescales, and **fatigue** has multiple proven mechanisms beyond "running out of ATP."

## Three ATP-supply systems

| System | Timescale | Mechanism | ATP yield | Limited by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Phosphocreatine (PCr) system** | 0-10 s | PCr + ADP → ATP + Cr (creatine kinase) | Instant; ~1 ATP per PCr | PCr stores (~80 mmol/kg dry muscle) |
| **Anaerobic glycolysis** | 10 s - 2 min | Glucose/glycogen → 2 pyruvate → 2 lactate; no O₂ | 2 ATP/glucose | Lactate + H⁺ accumulation, glycogen stores |
| **Oxidative phosphorylation** | >2 min | Pyruvate + FFAs + AAs → CO₂ + H₂O via TCA + ETC | 30-32 ATP/glucose; ~106 ATP/palmitate | O₂ + substrate delivery; mitochondrial capacity |

### Switching between systems
- **100m sprint**: ~80% PCr + glycolysis, ~20% oxidative.
- **400m run**: ~50/50 glycolytic + oxidative.
- **Marathon**: ~99% oxidative - fat is the dominant fuel after first hour.

### The "lactate threshold"
- During exercise, blood lactate stays near baseline up to ~60% VO₂max, then rises sharply.
- This isn't because oxygen runs out - it's because **glycolytic flux exceeds pyruvate's clearance via the mitochondria**, so excess pyruvate is shunted to lactate.
- Lactate is then exported, reused by other muscle/heart/liver (Cori cycle to glucose in liver).

<Mnemonic>
**"Lactate is fuel, not waste"** - heart, slow-twitch muscle, and brain happily oxidize lactate. The Cori cycle uses 6 ATP in liver to regenerate 2 ATP in muscle (net loss); but it offloads acidosis from the working muscle.
</Mnemonic>

## Energy sources by intensity

```
Effort   PCr   Glyco   Oxid
─────────────────────────────
Max     ████   ███      █
High     ██    ███     ██
Mod       █    ██      ███
Easy      ·    █       ███
Rest      ·    ·       ███ (FFA)
```

At rest, **fatty acid oxidation** dominates (>70% of energy). FFAs liberated by adipose lipolysis (sensitive to insulin - high insulin shuts off FFA delivery, hence rebound hunger after sugar).

## Fuel storage in muscle

| Substrate | Stored as | Mass |
|---|---|---|
| **Glycogen** | Glycogen granules with glycogenin core | ~400 g total body (300 g muscle + 100 g liver) |
| **PCr** | Phosphate-creatine pool | ~120 g |
| **Triglycerides** | Intramuscular droplets near mitochondria | ~300 g |
| **Free amino acids** | Glutamine, alanine pool | Small |

Muscle glycogen is **locally trapped** - no glucose-6-phosphatase, so it can't release glucose to the bloodstream like liver does. It's just for the muscle that made it.

## The Cori and Cahill cycles - moving energy around

| Cycle | Origin | Pathway |
|---|---|---|
| **Cori cycle** | Anaerobic muscle | Muscle: glucose → 2 lactate. Lactate → liver. Liver: 2 lactate → glucose (gluconeogenesis, 6 ATP). Glucose → blood → muscle |
| **Cahill (alanine) cycle** | Working/fasting muscle | Muscle: pyruvate + glutamate → alanine + α-KG. Alanine → liver. Liver: alanine → pyruvate → glucose. Carries N to urea cycle |

Both move carbon back to muscle as glucose at a net energy cost - paid by liver.

## Fatigue - five proven mechanisms

| Type | Mechanism | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Central fatigue** | Decreased CNS drive (motor cortex, motivation, NT depletion) | Long-duration, hot, hypoglycemic |
| **Substrate depletion** | Glycogen exhaustion (the "wall" at ~2-3 h of moderate exercise) | Endurance |
| **Metabolite accumulation** | ↑ Pi (from PCr breakdown) blocks myosin cycling; ↓ pH affects troponin C; reactive O species damage | Intense |
| **Ion balance** | K⁺ leaks out of cell → loss of resting potential → AP failure; Na/K-ATPase keeps up but lags | High frequency contraction |
| **Excitation-contraction uncoupling** | DHPR-RyR1 coupling becomes unreliable; SR Ca²⁺ release drops | Extended high-intensity |

Note: **lactate per se is NOT the cause of fatigue** - that's an old myth. The H⁺ from glycolysis is more relevant (acidosis), and even that contributes less than Pi accumulation.

## EPOC - "afterburn"

**Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption** - O₂ consumption stays elevated for minutes to hours after exercise:
- Restoring PCr stores (~3 min)
- Re-saturating myoglobin/hemoglobin
- Lactate clearance (~1 h)
- Body temperature normalization
- Restoring ATP cycling
- Catecholamine clearance

The "afterburn" is real but small (~10-15% of exercise calorie cost). Not the path to weight loss.

## Muscle adaptation - what training changes

| Type | Adaptations |
|---|---|
| **Endurance training** | ↑ mitochondrial density (PGC-1α), ↑ capillarization, ↑ oxidative enzymes, ↑ myoglobin, **shift toward type I fibers (slow oxidative)** |
| **Resistance training** | ↑ myofibrillar proteins (hypertrophy via mTOR), ↑ neural drive (early gains are neural), ↑ PCr stores, **type II fibers grow** (especially IIa) |
| **Detraining** | All of the above reverse in weeks to months; faster than they were gained |

### Why elite sprinters and marathoners look different
- **Sprinters**: high type IIx % + large cross-section + neuromuscular efficiency.
- **Marathoners**: high type I % + dense capillary network + low body fat + high VO₂max.
- Fiber-type % is largely genetically determined (training shifts ~5-10%).

<Clinical>
**Rhabdomyolysis** - myocyte necrosis releases CK + myoglobin + K⁺ + phosphate into blood.
- Causes: crush injury, severe exercise, **statins** (especially with fibrates, macrolides, fibrates, grapefruit), seizures, cocaine, hyperthermia, drugs that disrupt SR Ca²⁺.
- Triad: tea-colored urine + ↑↑ CK (often >5000) + ↑ K⁺.
- AKI from heme pigment toxicity + Ca²⁺ deposition.
- Treat: aggressive IV fluids (~ 1.5 L/h until clear urine), monitor K⁺, fix electrolytes.

**Malignant hyperthermia**: RyR1 mutation (autosomal dominant) → halogenated anesthetics + succinylcholine trigger sustained SR Ca²⁺ release → tetanic contraction + heat (>40°C) + acidosis + hyperkalemia + rhabdo. Treatment: **dantrolene** (blocks RyR1).

**McArdle disease (glycogen storage type V)**: muscle phosphorylase deficiency → can't break down muscle glycogen. Exercise intolerance + cramps + myoglobinuria after intense exercise; **"second wind" phenomenon** as FFAs take over.

**Pompe disease (type II)**: acid maltase deficiency → glycogen accumulates in lysosomes → cardiomyopathy + hypotonia (infantile fatal). Enzyme replacement available.
</Clinical>

## Function reference - every metabolic component

| Component | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Creatine kinase (CK)** | PCr + ADP ↔ ATP + Cr | Plasma CK is the biomarker of muscle damage; MB isoform specific to cardiac |
| **Hexokinase II** | Glucose → G6P in muscle (insulin-sensitive isoform) | First step of muscle glucose use; failed insulin signaling = T2DM |
| **Glycogen phosphorylase** (myo isoform) | Glycogen → G1P | McArdle disease |
| **PFK-1** | Rate-limiting glycolysis | Tarui disease (rare); fluoride competitive inhibitor (lab tube preservative) |
| **Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)** | Pyruvate ↔ lactate | Reversible; allows muscle to "store" pyruvate as lactate for export |
| **Pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH)** | Pyruvate → acetyl-CoA | Gateway to TCA; requires thiamine + lipoate + CoA + FAD + NAD; deficiency → lactic acidosis |
| **CPT-1** | Carnitine-bound FA into mitochondria | Rate-limiting for FA oxidation; inhibited by insulin (high-carb state) |
| **β-oxidation** | FA → acetyl-CoA repeatedly | Long-chain FAs need carnitine shuttle; medium-chain (MCT) don't |
| **Citrate synthase** | First TCA step (oxaloacetate + acetyl-CoA → citrate) | Mitochondrial density marker |
| **Cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV)** | Final ETC enzyme | Inhibited by CN⁻, CO, H₂S (cellular asphyxiants) |
| **GLUT4** | Insulin-regulated glucose uptake (muscle + adipose) | Recruited by exercise via AMPK independent of insulin - why exercise improves glycemic control |
| **AMPK** | "Energy sensor" - activated by low ATP/AMP ratio | Metformin acts here; ↑ FA oxidation + glucose uptake |
| **mTOR** | "Growth sensor" - protein synthesis | Activated by amino acids (leucine) + insulin + resistance exercise → hypertrophy |
| **PGC-1α** | Mitochondrial biogenesis master regulator | Induced by endurance training |

## Why this matters

<HighYield variant="boards">
- **CK elevation differential**: rhabdomyolysis (often >10,000), MI (CK-MB), polymyositis/dermatomyositis (often 2,000-20,000), muscular dystrophy (Duchenne CK can be 50,000), strenuous exercise (usually &lt;2,000).
- **Statin myopathy**: dose-dependent. Risk factors: high dose, age, female, hypothyroid, renal disease, drug interactions (CYP3A4 inhibitors, gemfibrozil). Check CK if symptomatic.
- **DKA + exercise**: lipolysis already maximal → exercising worsens ketosis. Insulin-deficient patients shouldn't exercise hard until corrected.
- **Glucose oxidation requires PDH** (thiamine, lipoate, CoA, FAD, NAD). Thiamine deficiency → lactate buildup; classic in alcoholics, refeeding syndrome, beri-beri.
- **Cyanide poisoning** = inhibits Complex IV (cytochrome c oxidase) → mitochondria can't accept electrons → forced anaerobic metabolism → lactic acidosis with bright red venous blood. Antidotes: hydroxocobalamin, nitrites + thiosulfate.
- **MELAS (mitochondrial encephalomyopathy)**: maternally inherited; lactic acidosis + stroke-like episodes + myopathy. Lactate ↑ at rest.
- **Creatine supplementation**: ↑ PCr stores, demonstrable performance boost in repeated short-burst activity; safe in healthy adults.
- **Anaerobic threshold** (lactate threshold) is the basis for endurance training zones.
</HighYield>
