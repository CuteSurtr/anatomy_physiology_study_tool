The neuromuscular junction (NMJ) is the synapse between a motor neuron and a skeletal muscle fiber. It is the most well-studied synapse in the body and the target of toxins, snake venoms, dozens of drugs, and several classic neurologic diseases. The NMJ is built to **never fail** in a healthy adult - every motor neuron action potential reliably produces a muscle fiber action potential.

## Anatomy of the NMJ

| Component | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Presynaptic terminal** | Specialized end of myelinated α-motor neuron; loses myelin near the muscle. Contains synaptic vesicles, mitochondria, voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels (P/Q type) clustered at active zones |
| **Synaptic cleft** | ~50 nm gap, filled with basal lamina containing acetylcholinesterase |
| **Postsynaptic motor end plate** | Region of sarcolemma directly under the terminal. **Junctional folds** dramatically increase surface area. The crests of the folds carry **nicotinic ACh receptors (nAChRs)**; the troughs carry voltage-gated Na⁺ channels |
| **Schwann cell cap** | Non-myelinating (terminal) Schwann cells envelop the terminal; trophic role, also guide nerve regeneration after injury |
| **Motor unit** | One α-motor neuron + all the muscle fibers it innervates. Small motor units (extraocular muscles, 5-10 fibers) allow fine control; large motor units (gastrocnemius, ~2000 fibers) generate force |

## The molecular sequence

1. **Action potential arrives** at the motor terminal. Voltage-gated **Na⁺ channels** depolarize the membrane.
2. **Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels (P/Q type)** open. Ca²⁺ enters the terminal.
3. Ca²⁺ binds **synaptotagmin** on synaptic vesicles → triggers SNARE complex fusion (synaptobrevin, syntaxin, SNAP-25).
4. **~200 vesicles per AP** release their contents (each vesicle = a quantum of ~5,000-10,000 ACh molecules).
5. **ACh diffuses across the cleft** (microseconds).
6. Each **nicotinic AChR is a pentameric ligand-gated cation channel** (2α, β, δ, ε subunits in adult; 2α, β, γ, δ in fetal). Two ACh bind the α subunits → channel opens.
7. **Na⁺ flows in, K⁺ flows out** → local depolarization of the end plate = **end-plate potential (EPP)**.
8. The EPP spreads electrotonically to the adjacent voltage-gated Na⁺ channels in the trough of each junctional fold → muscle fiber AP.
9. The fiber AP sweeps along the sarcolemma and dives into the T-tubules → triggers Ca²⁺ release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum (excitation-contraction coupling).
10. **Acetylcholinesterase (AChE)** in the basal lamina hydrolyzes ACh → choline + acetate within ~1 ms. About half the choline is taken back up by the presynaptic CHT1 transporter and reused to resynthesize ACh.

<Note title="Safety factor of the NMJ">
A healthy NMJ releases ~3-5× more ACh than is needed to reach threshold. This **safety factor** means small drops in ACh release or receptor sensitivity are clinically silent. The factor is reduced in myasthenia gravis, LEMS, and during pharmacologic blockade, so fatigue and weakness appear when reserve is exhausted.
</Note>

## End-plate potential vs miniature end-plate potential

Even at rest, single vesicles fuse spontaneously - producing **miniature end-plate potentials (MEPPs)** of ~0.5 mV. Each MEPP corresponds to one quantum. The EPP that follows a motor neuron AP is the summed response to ~200 quanta, but the summation is **not** arithmetic: as the end plate depolarizes, the driving force on Na⁺ falls, so the ~100 mV predicted by simple addition actually comes out as an EPP of ~30-40 mV. That still carries the fiber from its resting ~-90 mV past threshold (~-50 mV) with room to spare - the safety factor above.

Bernard Katz won the 1970 Nobel for showing that synaptic transmission is **quantal** by recording MEPPs and EPPs at the frog NMJ.

## Pharmacology of the NMJ - a drug at every step

<Figure name="muscular/nicotinic-ach-receptor" caption="Solved structure of the nicotinic ACh receptor, viewed from above (left) and from the side (right). The top view shows the five subunits ringing a central pore; the side view shows the large extracellular ligand-binding domain sitting above the membrane-spanning helices. Color here separates subunits, it does not name them - the gating steps (two ACh onto the alpha subunits, pore opens, Na+ in and K+ out) are described in the text, not drawn." />

| Step | Agent | Action | Clinical use / effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACh synthesis | Hemicholinium | Blocks choline reuptake | Research only |
| Vesicle loading | Vesamicol | Blocks VAChT | Research only |
| Vesicle fusion | **Botulinum toxin** | Cleaves SNARE proteins | Strabismus, dystonia, cosmetic Botox, food-borne botulism = paralysis |
| Vesicle fusion | **Black widow venom (latrotoxin)** | Massive Ca²⁺-independent vesicle release | Initial fasciculation, then depletion → weakness |
| ACh release | Aminoglycosides | Block presynaptic Ca²⁺ channels | Can unmask MG; potentiate non-depolarizing blockers |
| ACh release | LEMS antibodies | Block presynaptic VGCC | See LEMS below |
| Receptor agonist | **Nicotine, succinylcholine** | Activate nAChR | Sux: brief paralysis after fasciculation (depolarizing block) |
| Receptor antagonist | **Curare derivatives** (tubocurarine, rocuronium, vecuronium, cisatracurium) | Competitive nAChR block | Non-depolarizing neuromuscular blockade for intubation/surgery |
| Receptor antagonist | **α-bungarotoxin** | Essentially irreversible nAChR block | Lab tool for labeling and counting receptors; from the venom of the many-banded krait (*Bungarus multicinctus*) |
| AChE inhibition | **Neostigmine, pyridostigmine, edrophonium** | Reversible AChE inhibitors | Reverse non-depolarizing block; symptomatic MG treatment |
| AChE inhibition | **Organophosphates (sarin, parathion), carbamates** | Irreversible / pseudo-irreversible AChE inhibition | Pesticide / nerve agent poisoning - cholinergic crisis |
| AChE inhibition | **Pralidoxime (2-PAM)** | Regenerates AChE before "aging" | Antidote within hours of organophosphate exposure |
| Selective antagonist | **Sugammadex** | Encapsulates rocuronium / vecuronium | Rapid reversal of NM blockade |

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Depolarizing vs non-depolarizing blockers:**
- **Succinylcholine** - depolarizing. Binds nAChR as an agonist, but is not broken down by AChE (only by plasma pseudocholinesterase) → sustained depolarization → end-plate inactivation. Sequence: fasciculations → paralysis. **Cannot be reversed with neostigmine.** Risks: hyperkalemia (especially in burns, crush injury, denervation), malignant hyperthermia, masseter spasm. Pseudocholinesterase deficiency → prolonged paralysis.
- **Non-depolarizing** (rocuronium, vecuronium, cisatracurium) - competitive antagonists. No fasciculations. **Reverse with neostigmine + glycopyrrolate** (block muscarinic side effects) or **sugammadex** (for steroid-based agents).
</HighYield>

## Diseases of the NMJ

### Myasthenia gravis (MG)

**Autoantibodies against postsynaptic nAChR** (or MuSK or LRP4) → receptor internalization + complement-mediated end-plate damage → loss of junctional folds → reduced safety factor.

**Clinical:**
- Fluctuating weakness, **worse with use, better with rest**
- **Ocular** onset is the most common presentation (ptosis, diplopia) - roughly half of patients start this way, and most develop ocular involvement at some point
- Bulbar - dysphagia, dysarthria, fatigable chewing
- Limb weakness - usually proximal
- **Myasthenic crisis** - respiratory muscle failure, often triggered by infection, surgery, or medications

**Diagnosis:**
- Serum anti-AChR antibodies (positive in 85% of generalized MG)
- Anti-MuSK in seronegative cases
- **Edrophonium (Tensilon) test** - brief AChE inhibitor; weakness improves transiently (historical)
- **Ice pack test** - cold on ptotic eyelid → improvement (low-tech, ED-friendly)
- **EMG with repetitive nerve stimulation** - decremental response (>10% drop after 3-5 Hz stim)
- CT/MRI chest - **thymoma in 10-15%**, thymic hyperplasia in 65%

**Treatment:**
- **Pyridostigmine** (symptomatic)
- Immunosuppression - prednisone, azathioprine, mycophenolate, rituximab, eculizumab
- Plasma exchange or IVIG for crisis
- **Thymectomy** - benefit even without thymoma in early-onset disease

### Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome (LEMS)

**Autoantibodies against presynaptic P/Q-type voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels** → reduced ACh release.

**Paraneoplastic in ~60%** - most often **small cell lung cancer**. Always work up for occult malignancy.

**Clinical:**
- Proximal weakness (legs more than arms)
- **Strength improves with sustained or repeated effort** (post-tetanic facilitation - Ca²⁺ accumulates)
- Autonomic dysfunction - dry mouth, impotence, constipation
- Hyporeflexia that briefly returns after exercise

**EMG:** *incremental* response at high-frequency stim (opposite of MG).

**Treatment:** 3,4-diaminopyridine (3,4-DAP, **amifampridine**) - blocks K⁺ channels → prolongs presynaptic AP → more Ca²⁺ entry → more ACh. Treat underlying cancer.

### Botulism (covered in nervous physiology Clinical Case)

Botulinum toxin cleaves SNAREs → no ACh release → **descending flaccid paralysis** with autonomic features (dilated pupils, dry mouth, ileus, urinary retention).

### Organophosphate poisoning / cholinergic crisis

Irreversible AChE inhibition → ACh accumulates → continuous depolarization → muscle weakness, fasciculations, then paralysis. **Plus** massive muscarinic effects (DUMBELS or SLUDGE).

<Mnemonic title="Cholinergic crisis - DUMBELS">
- **D**iarrhea
- **U**rination
- **M**iosis
- **B**ronchospasm + bradycardia
- **E**xcitation (CNS, muscle fasciculations)
- **L**acrimation
- **S**alivation + sweating

Treat with **atropine** (muscarinic antagonist) + **pralidoxime** (regenerates AChE before "aging"). Atropine reverses muscarinic effects but does **not** treat NMJ weakness - that requires pralidoxime and time.
</Mnemonic>

## Excitation-contraction coupling (downstream of the NMJ)

<Figure name="muscular/sarcoplasmic-reticulum" caption="Cutaway of the inside of a skeletal muscle fiber: the sarcoplasmic reticulum (green) sleeves each myofibril, with transverse (T) tubules (red) running across them, and mitochondria alongside. The artwork is unlabeled - the molecular machinery at those contact points (DHPR, RyR1) is described in the text below." />

<Figure name="muscular/actin-myosin" caption="The two filaments the Ca²⁺ signal acts on. Above: the thin filament - a double helix of actin (green) with tropomyosin (dark red) winding along it and troponin (pink) at intervals. Below: the thick filament, a bundle of myosin tails with motor heads projecting from it. The lower pair shows the same two filaments brought into contact so the heads can reach actin." />

The end-plate potential triggers a muscle fiber AP. The fiber AP propagates along the sarcolemma at ~3-5 m/s and dives into the T-tubules. At triadic junctions, the **dihydropyridine receptor (DHPR, an L-type Ca²⁺ channel acting as a voltage sensor)** mechanically couples to the **ryanodine receptor (RyR1)** on the sarcoplasmic reticulum.

DHPR conformational change → RyR1 opens → SR releases Ca²⁺ into the sarcoplasm → Ca²⁺ binds **troponin C** → tropomyosin moves off the actin myosin-binding sites → cross-bridge cycle. (See **sliding-filament**.)

<Clinical>
**Malignant hyperthermia** - autosomal dominant mutation in the RyR1 gene. Triggered by volatile anesthetics (halothane, sevoflurane) and succinylcholine → uncontrolled SR Ca²⁺ release → sustained contraction, hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis, hyperkalemia, acidosis. **Treat with dantrolene** (blocks RyR1) + supportive care. Discontinue the trigger. Mortality has dropped from 70% (1960s) to &lt;5% with prompt recognition.
</Clinical>
