Skeletal muscles are organized by region. Knowing **origin, insertion, action, and innervation** for the major groups is the standard upper-division target.

<Figure name="muscular/muscles-overview" caption="Anterior and posterior views of the major skeletal muscle groups." />

## Upper limb essentials

| Muscle | Origin → Insertion | Action | Innervation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deltoid | Clavicle/scapula → humerus | Shoulder abduction | Axillary n. |
| Biceps brachii | Scapula → radius | Elbow flexion, forearm supination | Musculocutaneous n. |
| Triceps brachii | Scapula + humerus → ulna | Elbow extension | Radial n. |
| Pronator teres | Medial epicondyle → radius | Forearm pronation | Median n. |

## Lower limb essentials

| Muscle | Action | Innervation |
|---|---|---|
| Iliopsoas | Hip flexion | L1–L3 (femoral + branches) |
| Gluteus maximus | Hip extension | Inferior gluteal n. |
| Quadriceps femoris | Knee extension (+ hip flexion via rectus femoris) | Femoral n. |
| Hamstrings | Knee flexion + hip extension | Sciatic (tibial division) |
| Gastrocnemius/soleus | Ankle plantarflexion | Tibial n. |
| Tibialis anterior | Ankle dorsiflexion | Deep fibular n. |

<Clinical>
**Foot drop** = inability to dorsiflex the ankle, typically from **deep fibular nerve injury** (or L5 radiculopathy). Patients compensate with a "steppage gait" - exaggerated hip flexion to clear the floor.
</Clinical>

<Mnemonic>
**Rotator cuff = SITS**: Supraspinatus, Infraspinatus, Teres minor, Subscapularis. Three lateral rotators + one medial rotator (subscapularis).
</Mnemonic>

## Major muscles and their precise function

### Upper limb

| Muscle | Origin → Insertion | Action | Innervation | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Deltoid** | Clavicle + acromion + spine of scapula → deltoid tuberosity | Shoulder abduction (15–90°); flexion (anterior fibers); extension (posterior) | Axillary n. | Loss → can't lift arm; site of IM injections |
| **Supraspinatus** | Supraspinous fossa → greater tubercle | Initiates abduction (0–15°) | Suprascapular n. | Most-torn rotator cuff muscle |
| **Infraspinatus** | Infraspinous fossa → greater tubercle | Lateral rotation of shoulder | Suprascapular n. | Stabilizes glenohumeral joint |
| **Teres minor** | Lateral border of scapula → greater tubercle | Lateral rotation | Axillary n. | Part of rotator cuff (SITS) |
| **Subscapularis** | Subscapular fossa → lesser tubercle | Medial rotation of shoulder | Upper + lower subscapular n. | Only medial rotator of rotator cuff |
| **Biceps brachii** | Two heads from scapula → radial tuberosity + bicipital aponeurosis | Elbow flexion, forearm supination | Musculocutaneous n. | "Power supinator"; rupture of long head = "Popeye sign" |
| **Brachialis** | Distal anterior humerus → ulnar tuberosity | Pure elbow flexion (strongest flexor) | Musculocutaneous (+ small radial) | Workhorse elbow flexor regardless of forearm position |
| **Brachioradialis** | Lateral supracondylar ridge → distal radius | Elbow flexion in semipronated position | Radial n. | "Beer-drinker's muscle" |
| **Triceps brachii** | Three heads (scapula + humerus) → olecranon | Elbow extension | Radial n. | Only elbow extensor of significance |
| **Pronator teres** | Medial epicondyle + ulna → mid-radius | Forearm pronation | Median n. | Can compress median nerve (pronator teres syndrome) |
| **Flexor carpi radialis** | Medial epicondyle → 2nd/3rd metacarpal | Wrist flexion, radial deviation | Median n. | Surface marker for radial artery |
| **Flexor digitorum superficialis** | Medial epicondyle, ulna, radius → middle phalanges 2–5 | Flex PIP joints | Median n. | Tested by isolated PIP flexion |
| **Flexor digitorum profundus** | Ulna + interosseous membrane → distal phalanges 2–5 | Flex DIP joints | Median (digits 2,3) + ulnar (4,5) | DIP flexion of digit 5 tests ulnar nerve |
| **Extensor digitorum** | Lateral epicondyle → extensor expansions 2–5 | Extends MCP joints | Radial (posterior interosseous) | Wrist/finger drop in radial palsy |
| **Hand intrinsics - thenar** (APB, FPB, OP) | Carpal bones → thumb | Thumb opposition + flexion | Median n. (recurrent branch) | "Ape hand" in median nerve injury |
| **Hand intrinsics - hypothenar** | Carpal bones → little finger | Abduct + oppose 5th digit | Ulnar n. | Lost in ulnar palsy |
| **Interossei (dorsal + palmar)** | Between metacarpals → extensor expansions | Abduct (DAB) and adduct (PAD) fingers; help extend IP joints | Ulnar n. | Lost → claw hand; "froment's sign" tests adductor pollicis |
| **Lumbricals** | FDP tendons → extensor expansions | Flex MCP + extend IP | Median (1,2) + ulnar (3,4) | Coordinate fine finger movements |

### Lower limb

| Muscle | Action | Innervation | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Iliopsoas** (psoas major + iliacus) | Strongest hip flexor | L1–L3 + femoral n. | "Walking muscle"; involved in psoas abscess |
| **Gluteus maximus** | Hip extension; lateral rotation | Inferior gluteal n. | Stair-climbing, rising from chair |
| **Gluteus medius + minimus** | Hip abduction; stabilize pelvis when walking | Superior gluteal n. | **Weakness → Trendelenburg gait** (pelvis drops contralaterally) |
| **Tensor fasciae latae** | Hip flexion + abduction; tightens IT band | Superior gluteal n. | Knee stabilizer via IT band |
| **Sartorius** | Hip flexion + abduction + lateral rotation; knee flexion | Femoral n. | Longest muscle in body |
| **Quadriceps (RF, VM, VL, VI)** | Knee extension; rectus femoris also flexes hip | Femoral n. | Patellar reflex (L2–L4); essential for standing |
| **Hamstrings (BF, ST, SM)** | Knee flexion + hip extension | Sciatic (tibial division); short head BF by common fibular | Strain in sprinters; semimembranosus + semitendinosus = "guy ropes" |
| **Adductor magnus, longus, brevis, gracilis** | Hip adduction | Obturator (+ tibial for ischiocondylar part of adductor magnus) | Strain → "groin pull"; obturator block for hip surgery |
| **Gastrocnemius + soleus (triceps surae)** | Plantarflexion of ankle | Tibial n. (S1) | Calf pump for venous return; Achilles tendon |
| **Tibialis anterior** | Dorsiflexion + inversion of foot | Deep fibular n. (L4–L5) | **Lost → foot drop**; tested by dorsiflexion against resistance |
| **Tibialis posterior** | Plantarflexion + inversion | Tibial n. | Supports medial arch - dysfunction → flat foot |
| **Fibularis (peroneus) longus + brevis** | Eversion + plantarflexion | Superficial fibular n. | Lateral compartment; sprained ankles often involve them |
| **Extensor digitorum longus + extensor hallucis longus** | Dorsiflexion + toe extension | Deep fibular n. | Lost in foot drop |
| **Flexor digitorum longus + flexor hallucis longus** | Plantarflexion + toe flexion | Tibial n. | "Toe pushoff" in gait |

### Trunk and core

| Muscle | Action | Innervation | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diaphragm** | Primary inspiratory muscle | **Phrenic n. (C3, C4, C5 - "keeps the diaphragm alive")** | Loss → respiratory failure; referred pain to shoulder |
| **External intercostals** | Elevate ribs (inspiration) | Intercostal nn. | Quiet inspiration |
| **Internal intercostals** | Depress ribs (forced expiration) | Intercostal nn. | Active expiration |
| **Rectus abdominis** | Trunk flexion; increases intra-abdominal pressure | Lower 6 thoracic + subcostal nn. | "Six-pack"; tested for diastasis recti |
| **External + internal obliques + transversus abdominis** | Trunk rotation, lateral flexion; abdominal compression | Thoracoabdominal nn. | Forced expiration, Valsalva, coughing |
| **Erector spinae** (iliocostalis, longissimus, spinalis) | Vertebral extension; postural | Posterior rami | Maintain upright posture |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX assessment priorities:**
- **Foot drop**: dorsiflexion test; provide foot splint to prevent contracture in bedridden patients.
- **Range of motion** every shift in immobilized patients; passive ROM if patient can't actively move.
- **Trendelenburg gait** = weak gluteus medius - fall risk.
- **Atrophy + weakness in calf** post-cast: anticipate gradual reconditioning, no high-impact load early.
- **Crutch use**: 2-point gait for partial weight-bearing; non-weight-bearing = 3-point.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Nerve-injury patterns:**
- **Erb-Duchenne (C5–C6)** → waiter's tip arm (adducted, internally rotated, extended). Birth injury.
- **Klumpke (C8–T1)** → claw hand + Horner if T1 sympathetic involved.
- **Axillary n. (humerus surgical neck fracture)** → deltoid weakness, sensory loss over "regimental badge" area.
- **Radial n. (mid-shaft humerus / "Saturday night palsy")** → wrist drop, weak triceps if proximal.
- **Median n. (carpal tunnel / supracondylar fracture)** → thenar atrophy, "ape hand"; can't make OK sign.
- **Ulnar n. (medial epicondyle / Guyon's canal)** → claw hand of 4th–5th, weak grip, Froment sign +.
- **Sciatic n.** → posterior thigh weakness; PIRIFORMIS syndrome compresses it.
- **Common fibular n. (fibular neck)** → foot drop, can't dorsiflex/evert.
</HighYield>
