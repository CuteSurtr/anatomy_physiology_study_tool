The brain has four major divisions: **cerebrum, diencephalon, brainstem, cerebellum**. The adult brain weighs **~1.4 kg** (~2% of body weight) yet receives **~15% of cardiac output** and consumes **~20% of total body oxygen and glucose**. It is suspended in **cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)** within three meningeal layers - **dura, arachnoid, pia** - and houses approximately **86 billion neurons**, of which only ~16 billion live in the cerebral cortex.

## Meninges, in order outside → in

<Figure name="nervous/meninges" caption="The three cranial meninges (skull → dura mater → arachnoid mater → pia mater → brain), with the CSF-filled subarachnoid space between arachnoid and pia, and arachnoid granulations projecting into the superior sagittal sinus." />

- **Dura mater** - thick, two-layered (periosteal + meningeal) outermost layer. The two layers separate at certain points to enclose the **dural venous sinuses** (superior sagittal, transverse, sigmoid, cavernous, straight). Inward folds of the meningeal dura form: **falx cerebri** (between hemispheres), **tentorium cerebelli** (between cerebrum and cerebellum), **falx cerebelli** (between cerebellar hemispheres), and the **diaphragma sellae** (covering the pituitary).
- **Arachnoid mater** - thin avascular intermediate layer; sends trabeculae through the **subarachnoid space** (which contains CSF) to the pia. **Arachnoid granulations** project into the dural venous sinuses to reabsorb CSF.
- **Pia mater** - delicate vascular innermost layer adherent to the brain surface, dipping into every sulcus.

Spaces: **epidural** (between bone and dura - abnormal; site of arterial bleeds, often middle meningeal a.), **subdural** (between dura and arachnoid - abnormal; site of venous bleeds, bridging veins), **subarachnoid** (CSF-containing; site of arterial bleeds from circle of Willis aneurysms).

## Cerebrum - surface anatomy

The cerebral cortex is highly folded; about **two-thirds is hidden in sulci**. Key landmarks:

| Sulcus / fissure | Separates |
|---|---|
| **Longitudinal cerebral fissure** | Right and left hemispheres |
| **Central sulcus (of Rolando)** | Frontal lobe ↔ parietal lobe |
| **Lateral sulcus (Sylvian fissure)** | Temporal lobe ↔ frontal/parietal lobes |
| **Parieto-occipital sulcus** | Parietal lobe ↔ occipital lobe |
| **Cingulate sulcus** | Cingulate gyrus ↔ rest of medial hemisphere |
| **Calcarine sulcus** | Cuneus ↔ lingual gyrus (primary visual cortex sits here) |

The cortex is approximately **2–4 mm thick** and organized into **six histologic layers** (Brodmann) plus regional functional cytoarchitecture. Brodmann numbered 52 cytoarchitectonic areas; well-known ones include area **4** (primary motor), **3, 1, 2** (primary somatosensory), **17** (primary visual), **41, 42** (primary auditory), **44, 45** (Broca's), **22** (Wernicke's).

## Cerebrum

Two hemispheres connected by the **corpus callosum**. Four lobes per hemisphere:

| Lobe | Primary functions | Key cortical areas |
|---|---|---|
| Frontal | Voluntary motor, executive function, personality | Primary motor cortex (precentral gyrus), Broca's area (dominant hemisphere) |
| Parietal | Somatosensation, spatial awareness | Primary somatosensory (postcentral gyrus) |
| Temporal | Hearing, memory, language comprehension | Primary auditory, Wernicke's area |
| Occipital | Vision | Primary visual (calcarine sulcus) |

## White matter and deep gray nuclei

Beneath the cortex lie **three types of white-matter tracts** plus deep nuclei.

- **Commissural fibers** connect the hemispheres: **corpus callosum** (the big one), **anterior commissure** (small, connects temporal poles), **posterior commissure** (midbrain level).
- **Association fibers** stay within a hemisphere: **arcuate fasciculus** (Broca's ↔ Wernicke's - lesion = conduction aphasia), **superior longitudinal fasciculus**, **uncinate fasciculus**, **cingulum**.
- **Projection fibers** travel between cortex and brainstem/spinal cord - the **internal capsule** is the bottleneck (anterior limb, genu, posterior limb). A small lacunar infarct here can devastate motor or sensory function.

The **basal ganglia** are subcortical motor nuclei involved in voluntary movement initiation and reward:

| Nucleus | Notes |
|---|---|
| Caudate nucleus | C-shaped, follows the lateral ventricle. Atrophies in Huntington disease. |
| Putamen | Lateral; together with globus pallidus = **lentiform nucleus** |
| Globus pallidus (external + internal segments) | Major output to thalamus |
| Subthalamic nucleus | Lesion → contralateral hemiballismus |
| Substantia nigra (midbrain) | Dopaminergic projections to striatum; lost in Parkinson disease |

Direct pathway (D1, excitatory): cortex → striatum → GPi/SNr → thalamus → cortex (facilitates movement).
Indirect pathway (D2, inhibitory): cortex → striatum → GPe → STN → GPi/SNr → thalamus (inhibits movement).
Dopamine from SNc favors the direct path → loss = bradykinesia, rigidity (Parkinson).

## Diencephalon - detailed

<Figure name="nervous/limbic-lobe" caption="Limbic system structures - cingulate gyrus, hippocampus, parahippocampal gyrus, amygdala, fornix, mammillary bodies, anterior thalamic nucleus. Together they form the Papez circuit and regulate emotion, memory, and autonomic responses." />

- **Thalamus** - paired ovoid gray-matter masses; relay for nearly all sensory and motor pathways to/from the cortex (smell bypasses it). Functionally divided into nuclei:

| Thalamic nucleus | Function |
|---|---|
| Ventral posterolateral (VPL) | Body somatosensation → S1 |
| Ventral posteromedial (VPM) | Face + taste somatosensation → S1 |
| Lateral geniculate (LGN) | Vision → V1 |
| Medial geniculate (MGN) | Hearing → A1 |
| Ventral lateral (VL) | Motor relay from cerebellum + basal ganglia → motor cortex |
| Anterior | Limbic (Papez circuit) |
| Pulvinar | Higher-order visual association |

- **Hypothalamus** - autonomic, endocrine, and homeostatic control. Tiny but essential. Functional zones:
  - **Lateral**: hunger (lesion = anorexia)
  - **Ventromedial**: satiety (lesion = hyperphagia)
  - **Anterior (preoptic)**: cooling, parasympathetic
  - **Posterior**: heating, sympathetic
  - **Suprachiasmatic**: circadian (input from retinohypothalamic tract)
  - **Supraoptic + paraventricular**: ADH + oxytocin synthesis (released from posterior pituitary)
  - **Arcuate**: regulates anterior pituitary (releasing/inhibiting hormones, dopamine)

- **Epithalamus** - pineal gland (melatonin → circadian), habenula (limbic).
- **Subthalamus** - sits below thalamus, part of basal-ganglia circuitry.

## Brainstem - detailed

Three segments, rostral to caudal: **midbrain → pons → medulla oblongata**. The brainstem houses:
- Cranial nerve nuclei (CN III–XII; CN I and II are not true cranial nerves anatomically)
- Long ascending sensory and descending motor tracts
- The **reticular formation** - arousal, autonomic regulation, pattern generators

### Midbrain (mesencephalon)

- **Tectum** (dorsal): **superior colliculi** (visual reflexes - saccades) and **inferior colliculi** (auditory relay to MGN).
- **Tegmentum** (middle): red nucleus, periaqueductal gray (pain modulation), substantia nigra (anterior).
- **Cerebral peduncles** (ventral): contain descending corticospinal + corticobulbar fibers.
- **Cerebral aqueduct of Sylvius** runs through midbrain - narrow → common site of obstructive hydrocephalus.
- **Cranial nerves emerging:** CN III (oculomotor, ventral), CN IV (trochlear - only CN exiting dorsally and only one that decussates entirely).

### Pons

- Houses **pontine nuclei** that relay cortex → cerebellum (via middle cerebellar peduncles - the largest cerebellar peduncle).
- **Pontine respiratory groups** (apneustic, pneumotaxic) tune the rhythm set by medullary respiratory centers.
- **CN V** (trigeminal) emerges from mid-pons; **CN VI, VII, VIII** from the pontomedullary junction.

### Medulla oblongata

- **Pyramids** (anterior): corticospinal tracts; **pyramidal decussation** at the cervicomedullary junction is where motor fibers cross.
- **Olives** (lateral): inferior olivary nucleus → climbing fibers to cerebellum.
- **Cardiovascular centers, respiratory rhythm generators, vomiting center, swallowing center**.
- **Cranial nerves emerging:** CN IX, X, XI (from the postolivary sulcus), CN XII (from the preolivary sulcus).

<Clinical>
**Medial medullary syndrome** (anterior spinal artery occlusion): contralateral hemiparesis (pyramid), contralateral loss of proprioception/vibration (medial lemniscus), ipsilateral tongue weakness (CN XII).

**Lateral medullary (Wallenberg) syndrome** (PICA occlusion): ipsilateral facial pain/temperature loss (spinal trigeminal), contralateral body pain/temperature loss (spinothalamic), ipsilateral Horner syndrome (descending sympathetic), dysphagia/hoarseness (nucleus ambiguus → CN IX, X), vertigo, ataxia.

**Locked-in syndrome** (basilar artery occlusion at the pons): bilateral corticospinal + corticobulbar loss; sensation and vertical gaze + blinking spared. Patients are conscious but cannot move below the eyes.
</Clinical>

## Cerebellum - detailed

<Figure name="nervous/cerebellum" caption="Cerebellum - both hemispheres with the central vermis, characteristic folia, and the arbor vitae of white matter visible in cross-section." />

<Figure name="nervous/cerebellum-detail" caption="Cerebellum gross anatomy. Hemispheres (lateral) for limb coordination, vermis (medial) for posture/gait, flocculonodular lobe for balance/eye movements. Three peduncles connect to brainstem: superior (→ midbrain), middle (→ pons), inferior (→ medulla)." />

<Figure name="nervous/cerebellar-peduncles" caption="Three pairs of cerebellar peduncles connecting cerebellum to brainstem. Superior peduncle (→ midbrain, mostly efferent). Middle peduncle (← pons, largest, conveys cortico-ponto-cerebellar input). Inferior peduncle (↔ medulla, vestibular + spinocerebellar input)." />

"Little brain" sitting in the posterior cranial fossa behind the brainstem, attached to it by three pairs of **cerebellar peduncles** (superior → midbrain, middle → pons, inferior → medulla). Two hemispheres + the central **vermis**, organized into three functional zones:

| Zone | Function | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Vermis (spinocerebellum medial)** | Truncal posture and gait | Fastigial nucleus |
| **Paravermis (spinocerebellum lateral)** | Distal limb coordination | Interposed nuclei (globose + emboliform) |
| **Cerebrocerebellum (lateral hemispheres)** | Planning and timing of voluntary movement | Dentate nucleus |
| **Vestibulocerebellum (flocculonodular lobe)** | Balance, eye movements | Direct to vestibular nuclei |

Histologically, the cerebellar cortex has **three layers** (vs. six in cerebrum): molecular (with stellate + basket cells), Purkinje (single layer of huge Purkinje cells - the sole output), granular (with granule cells and Golgi cells).

The cerebellum **does not initiate** movement - it modulates and corrects. Damage produces:
- **Ataxia** - wide-based, unsteady gait
- **Dysmetria** - overshoot/undershoot on finger-to-nose test
- **Intention tremor** - worsens as the hand approaches the target (vs. resting tremor of Parkinson)
- **Dysdiadochokinesia** - clumsy alternating movements
- **Scanning speech** - irregular cadence
- **Hypotonia** - pendular reflexes
- Lesions cause **ipsilateral** signs (cerebellar output decussates to opposite cortex, then corticospinal decussates again at the medullary pyramids - two crosses cancel).

<Clinical>
**Broca's aphasia** (lesion in dominant inferior frontal gyrus): non-fluent, halting speech, but comprehension is preserved - patients are frustrated because they know what they want to say. **Wernicke's aphasia** (dominant superior temporal gyrus): fluent but nonsensical speech, with impaired comprehension - patients don't realize they're not making sense.
</Clinical>

## Vascular supply - circle of Willis

The brain has a **dual arterial supply** that anastomoses at the **circle of Willis** on the base of the brain. Two anterior sources (internal carotids) + one posterior source (vertebrobasilar) form a pentagon around the optic chiasm:

```
ACA - Anterior Communicating - ACA
 |                                |
ICA                              ICA
 |                                |
PComm - - - PCA   PCA - - - PComm
              \ /
            Basilar a.
              |
        2 vertebral aa.
```

| Artery | Major branches | Territory |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior cerebral (ACA)** | Pericallosal, callosomarginal | Medial frontal + parietal - contralateral lower-limb weakness/sensory loss |
| **Middle cerebral (MCA)** | Lenticulostriate, superior + inferior divisions | Lateral cortex - contralateral face + upper-limb weakness/sensory loss; if dominant: aphasia |
| **Posterior cerebral (PCA)** | P1, P2, calcarine | Occipital lobe, inferior/medial temporal - contralateral homonymous hemianopia (macular sparing if cortical) |
| **Basilar** | Pontine perforators, AICA, superior cerebellar | Pons, midbrain, cerebellum - locked-in if proximal occlusion |
| **PICA** (off vertebral) | - | Lateral medulla + posterior inferior cerebellum (Wallenberg) |

Watershed zones between ACA-MCA (parasagittal cortex) and MCA-PCA (occipitoparietal) are vulnerable in global hypoperfusion (cardiac arrest, hypovolemic shock) - produces "man-in-a-barrel" weakness (bilateral proximal arms more than legs).

### Berry aneurysms

The arterial circle is the prototype site of **saccular (berry) aneurysms** - typically at branch points, ~85% in the anterior circulation. Most common locations: **anterior communicating > posterior communicating > MCA bifurcation**. Rupture causes **subarachnoid hemorrhage** ("worst headache of my life," xanthochromia on LP if delayed). Associated with ADPKD, Ehlers-Danlos, Marfan, coarctation of aorta.

## Ventricles & CSF

<Figure name="nervous/cerebrospinal-fluid" caption="CSF circulation - produced by the choroid plexus of the ventricles, flows through the ventricular system (lateral → 3rd → cerebral aqueduct → 4th), exits to the subarachnoid space, and is reabsorbed at arachnoid villi into the venous sinuses." />

Four ventricles produce and circulate **~500 mL of CSF per day** (~150 mL total in the system at any moment).

**Lateral ventricles** (×2, in cerebral hemispheres) → **interventricular foramen of Monro** → **third ventricle** (diencephalon) → **cerebral aqueduct of Sylvius** → **fourth ventricle** (between pons/medulla and cerebellum) → exits via **foramina of Luschka (×2 lateral)** and **Magendie (midline)** into the subarachnoid space → reabsorbed by **arachnoid granulations** into the dural venous sinuses.

CSF is produced by **choroid plexus** (mostly in the lateral ventricles).

<Clinical>
**Obstructive (non-communicating) hydrocephalus**: blockage in the ventricular system (aqueductal stenosis is classic) → upstream dilation, downstream is normal.

**Communicating hydrocephalus**: impaired reabsorption at the arachnoid granulations (post-SAH, post-meningitis) → all ventricles dilate.

**Normal pressure hydrocephalus**: classic triad **"wet, wobbly, wacky"** - urinary incontinence, ataxic gait, cognitive decline. Ventricles enlarge with normal opening pressures on LP.
</Clinical>

## Every brain structure - what it does and why it matters

### Meninges and CSF system

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Dura mater (periosteal + meningeal)** | Tough outer protective layer | Anchors brain; encloses dural venous sinuses; epidural bleeds peel periosteal off bone |
| **Falx cerebri** | Sickle-shaped dural fold between hemispheres | Limits hemisphere movement; subfalcine herniation slips brain under it |
| **Tentorium cerebelli** | Dural shelf between cerebrum and cerebellum | Defines supratentorial vs infratentorial; uncal herniation slides over its edge |
| **Diaphragma sellae** | Covers pituitary fossa | Pituitary tumors growing upward push against optic chiasm |
| **Arachnoid mater** | Avascular middle layer | Bridging veins from cortex to dural sinuses pass through subdural space - torn → SDH |
| **Subarachnoid space** | CSF-containing space between arachnoid and pia | Site of berry-aneurysm rupture (SAH); cisterns enlarged here (e.g., cisterna magna) |
| **Pia mater** | Delicate inner layer hugging brain surface | Carries surface vessels; dips into every sulcus |
| **Arachnoid granulations** | Project into superior sagittal sinus | **Reabsorb CSF** into venous circulation - block them and you get communicating hydrocephalus |
| **Choroid plexus** | Modified ependyma + capillaries in ventricles | **Produce CSF** (~500 mL/day); located mainly in lateral ventricles |
| **Lateral ventricles (×2)** | Largest CSF spaces; one per hemisphere | Frontal horn, body, occipital horn, temporal horn; first to dilate in obstructive hydrocephalus |
| **Foramen of Monro (×2)** | Lateral ventricle → 3rd ventricle | Obstruction → unilateral hydrocephalus |
| **Third ventricle** | Slit-like cavity in diencephalon | Surrounded by thalamus and hypothalamus |
| **Cerebral aqueduct (of Sylvius)** | 3rd → 4th ventricle | Narrowest CSF pathway → most common congenital obstructive hydrocephalus site |
| **Fourth ventricle** | Diamond-shaped cavity between pons/medulla and cerebellum | Receives output of cerebral aqueduct |
| **Foramina of Luschka (×2 lateral)** | 4th ventricle → subarachnoid space (lateral) | CSF exit to subarachnoid space |
| **Foramen of Magendie (midline)** | 4th ventricle → cisterna magna | Main exit to subarachnoid space |
| **Dural venous sinuses (sagittal, transverse, sigmoid, cavernous, straight)** | Channel cerebral venous blood to internal jugular | Avalvular; thrombosis (CVST) causes raised ICP and stroke |

### Cerebral cortex landmarks and lobes

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Frontal lobe** | Voluntary motor (M1), executive function, personality, Broca's area, Brodmann 6/8 (gaze, premotor) | Damage → disinhibition (e.g., Phineas Gage), expressive aphasia, contralateral hemiparesis |
| **Parietal lobe** | Primary somatosensation (S1), spatial awareness, body schema | Right parietal lesion → neglect; left → Gerstmann (agraphia, acalculia, finger agnosia, L-R confusion) |
| **Temporal lobe** | Hearing (A1), memory (hippocampus), language comprehension (Wernicke's), object recognition | Bilateral lesions → Klüver-Bucy (hyperorality, hypersexuality, placidity); medial → amnesia |
| **Occipital lobe** | Vision (V1, around calcarine sulcus) | Bilateral injury → cortical blindness (often with Anton syndrome - denial of blindness) |
| **Insular cortex** | Visceral sensation, taste, autonomic regulation, interoception | Lesions in stroke can present atypically |
| **Limbic lobe (cingulate, parahippocampal)** | Emotion, motivation, memory, Papez circuit | Cingulate damage → akinetic mutism |
| **Precentral gyrus** | Primary motor cortex (M1, Brodmann 4) | Contralateral motor control - homunculus is mapped here |
| **Postcentral gyrus** | Primary somatosensory cortex (S1, Brodmann 3,1,2) | Contralateral fine touch, proprioception |
| **Central sulcus** | Separates frontal from parietal | Boundary between motor and sensory cortex |
| **Lateral sulcus (Sylvian fissure)** | Separates temporal from frontal/parietal | MCA territory landmark |
| **Calcarine sulcus** | Houses primary visual cortex | V1 lies on its banks |
| **Broca's area** (Brodmann 44, 45, inferior frontal gyrus, dominant hemisphere) | Speech production | Lesion → non-fluent (expressive) aphasia with preserved comprehension |
| **Wernicke's area** (Brodmann 22, superior temporal gyrus, dominant) | Language comprehension | Lesion → fluent but nonsensical speech with poor comprehension |

### White matter and deep gray nuclei

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Corpus callosum** | Largest commissure; ~200 million axons connecting hemispheres | Section (callosotomy for refractory epilepsy) → split-brain syndromes |
| **Anterior commissure** | Connects temporal poles | Smaller commissural pathway |
| **Internal capsule** | Bottleneck of all motor + sensory fibers between cortex and brainstem/spinal cord | Lacunar infarcts here cause pure motor stroke (posterior limb) or pure sensory stroke |
| **Arcuate fasciculus** | Connects Broca's and Wernicke's areas | Lesion → conduction aphasia (poor repetition, preserved comprehension + fluency) |
| **Caudate nucleus** | Part of striatum; cognition + motor | Atrophies in **Huntington disease** (also globus pallidus); on imaging the lateral ventricles look "boxed" |
| **Putamen** | Part of striatum (with caudate); main input to basal ganglia from cortex | Hypertensive hemorrhages commonly here |
| **Globus pallidus (external + internal)** | Major output of basal ganglia to thalamus | Direct pathway disinhibits movement; indirect inhibits |
| **Subthalamic nucleus** | Drives indirect pathway (excites GPi) | **Lesion → contralateral hemiballismus** (wild flinging movements); DBS target in Parkinson |
| **Substantia nigra (pars compacta)** | Dopaminergic projection to striatum | **Lost in Parkinson disease** → bradykinesia, rigidity, resting tremor |
| **Red nucleus (midbrain)** | Origin of rubrospinal tract | Modulates flexor tone in upper limbs |
| **Amygdala** | Emotional processing, fear conditioning, social recognition | Bilateral damage → loss of fear response; central to PTSD |
| **Hippocampus** | Declarative memory consolidation | Bilateral damage → anterograde amnesia (H.M. case); shrinks in Alzheimer disease |

### Thalamic nuclei

| Nucleus | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **VPL** | Body somatosensation → S1 | Thalamic pain syndrome if injured |
| **VPM** | Face + taste → S1 | Trigeminal pathway terminus |
| **LGN** | Vision → V1 | Six layers (3 from each eye), parvocellular + magnocellular |
| **MGN** | Hearing → A1 | Tonotopically organized |
| **VL** | Cerebellum + basal ganglia → motor cortex | Common DBS target for tremor (VIM nucleus subdivision) |
| **VA** | Basal ganglia → premotor cortex | Movement planning |
| **Anterior nuclei** | Mammillary bodies → cingulate (Papez circuit) | Memory; affected in Korsakoff syndrome |
| **Pulvinar** | Visual association, attention | Largest thalamic nucleus |
| **Reticular nucleus** | Inhibitory shell around thalamus | Gates sensory flow; involved in absence seizures |

### Hypothalamic nuclei

| Nucleus | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Supraoptic** | Synthesizes ADH | Released from posterior pituitary; lesion → central DI |
| **Paraventricular** | Synthesizes oxytocin (also some ADH, CRH) | Parturition, lactation, stress response |
| **Suprachiasmatic** | Master circadian clock; receives retinohypothalamic input | Sets sleep-wake; jet lag and shift-work effects |
| **Arcuate** | Releases hypothalamic releasing/inhibiting hormones; secretes dopamine (PIF) | Controls anterior pituitary |
| **Preoptic / anterior** | Cooling and parasympathetic | Damage → hyperthermia |
| **Posterior** | Heating and sympathetic | Damage → poikilothermia |
| **Ventromedial** | Satiety center | Lesion → hyperphagia, obesity |
| **Lateral** | Hunger center | Lesion → anorexia, wasting |
| **Mammillary bodies** | Receive fornix; output to anterior thalamus | Damage in Wernicke-Korsakoff (thiamine deficiency) |

### Brainstem structures

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Midbrain - superior colliculi** | Visual orienting reflexes (saccades) | Parinaud syndrome (upgaze palsy) when compressed (pinealoma) |
| **Midbrain - inferior colliculi** | Auditory relay | Tonotopic; lemniscal pathway pit-stop |
| **Periaqueductal gray (PAG)** | Endogenous opioid pain modulation | Site of descending analgesia; stimulated by opioids |
| **Cerebral peduncles** | Carry corticospinal + corticobulbar tracts | Weber syndrome (midbrain stroke) → ipsilateral CN III + contralateral hemiparesis |
| **Pons - pontine nuclei** | Relay cortex → cerebellum via middle cerebellar peduncle | Allows cortex to coordinate movement via cerebellum |
| **Pons - apneustic + pneumotaxic centers** | Tune respiratory rhythm | Modulate rate and depth of breathing |
| **Medulla - pyramids** | Corticospinal tract; decussation at cervicomedullary junction | 90% of motor fibers cross here → contralateral motor control |
| **Medulla - olives** | Climbing fibers to cerebellum (motor learning) | Hypertrophic olivary degeneration on MRI is a recognizable lesion pattern |
| **Medulla - cardiovascular + respiratory centers** | Generate breathing rhythm; tonic sympathetic outflow | Damage → respiratory arrest |
| **Reticular formation** | Diffuse arousal network; sleep-wake; pattern generators | Lesions → coma |

### Cerebellar zones and nuclei

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Vermis (spinocerebellum medial)** | Truncal posture and gait | Damage (alcoholic degeneration) → wide-based gait, truncal ataxia |
| **Paravermis (spinocerebellum lateral)** | Distal limb coordination | Damage → limb ataxia |
| **Cerebrocerebellum (lateral hemispheres)** | Planning and timing of voluntary movement | Damage → dysmetria, intention tremor |
| **Vestibulocerebellum (flocculonodular)** | Balance and eye movements | Damage → vertigo, nystagmus |
| **Purkinje cells** | Sole output of cerebellar cortex; GABAergic | Lost in spinocerebellar ataxias; selectively vulnerable to ischemia and alcohol |
| **Granule cells** | Most numerous neuron in the brain | Provide parallel fibers to Purkinje cells |
| **Climbing fibers (from olives)** | Each Purkinje cell gets one - powerful, all-or-none | Drive motor learning |
| **Mossy fibers** | Excitatory inputs from cortex via pontine nuclei | Convey context |
| **Fastigial nucleus** | Output of vermis | To vestibular nuclei + reticular formation |
| **Interposed nuclei (globose + emboliform)** | Output of paravermis | To red nucleus + thalamus |
| **Dentate nucleus** | Largest cerebellar nucleus; output of cerebrocerebellum | To thalamus → motor cortex |

### Vascular supply (already summarized above)

| Vessel | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior cerebral artery (ACA)** | Medial frontal + parietal cortex | Lower-limb dominant in motor/sensory loss |
| **Middle cerebral artery (MCA)** | Lateral cortex, basal ganglia (lenticulostriate) | Face + upper limb dominant; if dominant hemisphere → aphasia |
| **Posterior cerebral artery (PCA)** | Occipital lobe, inferior temporal, thalamus | Contralateral homonymous hemianopia (macular sparing if cortical) |
| **Anterior communicating** | Connects two ACAs | #1 berry aneurysm site; rupture → SAH |
| **Posterior communicating** | Connects ICA to PCA | #2 berry aneurysm site; compression of CN III → "down-and-out" eye with blown pupil |
| **Basilar artery** | Pons, midbrain, posterior circulation | Apex strokes → top-of-the-basilar syndrome |
| **PICA** | Lateral medulla + posterior inferior cerebellum | Occlusion → Wallenberg syndrome |
| **AICA** | Lateral pons + anterior inferior cerebellum | Occlusion → ipsilateral facial palsy + hearing loss + ataxia |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX neuro priorities:**
- **Stroke (FAST)**: Face droop, Arm weakness, Speech slurred, Time critical. **Within 4.5 h** = tPA candidate (if no contraindications).
- **Increased ICP**: Cushing's triad (hypertension + bradycardia + irregular respirations); position **HOB 30°**, avoid Valsalva, maintain PaCO₂ ~35.
- **Seizure precautions**: padded rails, no oral airway insertion during seizure, side-lying after seizure, suction available.
- **Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS)**: Eye (1–4) + Verbal (1–5) + Motor (1–6) = 3–15. **&lt;8 → intubate**.
- **Decorticate** (cortex damage) = arms flexed; **decerebrate** (brainstem) = arms extended - worse prognosis.
- **CSF leak after head trauma**: halo sign on gauze; do NOT pack nares; ↑ infection risk.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Stroke localization & syndromes:**
- **MCA stroke**: contralateral face + UE weakness/sensory loss; if dominant → aphasia (Broca proximal, Wernicke distal).
- **ACA stroke**: contralateral LE weakness/sensory loss.
- **PCA stroke**: contralateral homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing.
- **Lacunar (lenticulostriate)**: pure motor (internal capsule) or pure sensory (thalamus).
- **Lateral medullary (Wallenberg)**: ipsilateral facial pain/temp loss + Horner + ataxia; contralateral body pain/temp loss; dysphagia.
- **Locked-in (basilar)**: quadriplegia, mute, only vertical gaze + blink preserved.
- **Watershed (ACA-MCA)**: bilateral proximal arm weakness ("man in a barrel").
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Neurology pharm:**
- **tPA (alteplase)** - within 4.5 h of stroke onset; contraindicated: recent hemorrhage, surgery, severe HTN, INR &gt;1.7.
- **Mannitol** - osmotic diuretic for ↑ ICP; monitor osmolality.
- **Levodopa-carbidopa** - Parkinson; carbidopa blocks peripheral conversion → less peripheral side effects.
- **Donepezil / rivastigmine / galantamine** - AChE inhibitors for Alzheimer.
- **Memantine** - NMDA antagonist for moderate-severe Alzheimer.
- **Triptans** - 5-HT1B/1D agonists for migraine; contraindicated with CAD.
- **Antiepileptics**: phenytoin (gingival hyperplasia), valproate (hepatotoxic, teratogenic), levetiracetam (mood changes).
</HighYield>

### Major spinal cord tracts - function reference

| Tract | Direction | Function | Decussation site | Lesion findings |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dorsal column–medial lemniscus** (gracile + cuneate fasciculi) | Ascending sensory | Fine touch, vibration, proprioception | Medulla (internal arcuate fibers) | Loss of fine touch, vibration, proprioception **below** lesion, **ipsilateral** if cord; **contralateral** if above medulla |
| **Lateral spinothalamic tract** | Ascending sensory | Pain, temperature | Anterior white commissure of cord (1–2 segments above entry) | **Contralateral** pain/temp loss below lesion |
| **Anterior spinothalamic tract** | Ascending sensory | Crude touch, pressure | Anterior white commissure | Often spared (redundancy) |
| **Spinocerebellar tracts (anterior + posterior)** | Ascending | Unconscious proprioception → cerebellum | Anterior crosses twice (net ipsilateral); posterior doesn't | Ataxia |
| **Lateral corticospinal tract** | Descending motor | Voluntary movement | Pyramidal decussation at cervicomedullary junction | **Ipsilateral** weakness below lesion (spastic, hyperreflexive); **contralateral** if above pyramids |
| **Anterior corticospinal tract** | Descending motor | Axial/trunk muscles | At spinal level of synapse | Bilateral truncal effects |
| **Rubrospinal tract** | Descending motor | Flexor tone in upper limbs | At midbrain (red nucleus) | Loss of flexor tone |
| **Vestibulospinal tract** | Descending motor | Antigravity (extensor) tone, posture | Doesn't cross | Postural instability |
| **Reticulospinal tract** | Descending motor | Voluntary + reflex motor; sympathetic outflow | Variable | Multiple deficits |

<Clinical>
**Brown-Séquard syndrome** (hemicord injury): ipsilateral loss of fine touch/vibration/proprioception + motor below; contralateral loss of pain/temp ~2 segments below.

**Anterior cord syndrome** (anterior spinal artery): bilateral motor + pain/temp loss; **fine touch + vibration spared**.

**Central cord syndrome** (cervical, hyperextension, syringomyelia): **upper limbs > lower limbs** motor loss; "cape-like" pain/temp loss as crossing fibers in anterior commissure are hit.

**Posterior cord syndrome**: dorsal column loss only (rare; B12 deficiency = subacute combined degeneration also gets corticospinal).
</Clinical>

## Special senses - eye and ear

<Figure name="nervous/eye-layers" caption="Eye anatomy showing the three concentric layers: fibrous (cornea + sclera), vascular (uveal: choroid + ciliary body + iris), and neural (retina). Light passes cornea → aqueous → pupil → lens → vitreous → retina → optic nerve → LGN → V1." />

### Eye anatomy (functional)

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Cornea** | Avascular transparent anterior fibrous layer | Most refractive power (~2/3 of total); damage → scarring (transplant most successful) |
| **Sclera** | White fibrous outer coat | Protective; attachment for extraocular muscles |
| **Choroid** | Vascular pigmented middle layer | Nourishes outer retina |
| **Ciliary body** | Smooth muscle + processes secreting aqueous humor | Accommodation; glaucoma target |
| **Iris** | Pigmented diaphragm; controls pupil size | Sphincter (parasymp, M3) constricts; dilator (symp, α1) dilates |
| **Lens** | Biconvex transparent body; suspended by zonules | Fine-tunes focus; opacity → cataract |
| **Retina** | Neural layer with rods + cones + neurons | Phototransduction → visual signaling |
| **Optic nerve (CN II)** | Carries visual information to LGN | Optic neuritis = MS pathognomonic |
| **Aqueous humor** | Anterior chamber fluid; produced by ciliary body | Drains via canal of Schlemm; backup → glaucoma |
| **Vitreous humor** | Gel filling posterior chamber | Detachment → flashers/floaters |

<Figure name="nervous/ear-anatomy" caption="Human ear cross-section. Outer ear (green): auricle + external auditory canal + tympanic membrane. Middle ear (red): malleus → incus → stapes ossicles + Eustachian tube. Inner ear (purple): cochlea (hearing) + semicircular canals + vestibule (balance)." />

### Ear anatomy (functional)

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Auricle (pinna)** | Collects sound | Cosmetic + sound localization |
| **External auditory canal** | Conducts sound; produces cerumen | Otitis externa = "swimmer's ear" |
| **Tympanic membrane (eardrum)** | Vibrates with sound waves | Perforation → conductive hearing loss |
| **Malleus → Incus → Stapes** | Amplify sound mechanically (~20×) | Otosclerosis = stapes immobile; ossiculoplasty |
| **Eustachian tube** | Equalizes middle-ear pressure with pharynx | Otitis media in children (shorter, more horizontal tube) |
| **Oval window** | Stapes → inner ear interface | Where mechanical → fluid wave |
| **Round window** | Pressure release for cochlea | Allows fluid wave to propagate |
| **Cochlea** | Spiraled tube; contains organ of Corti | Hearing transduction; tonotopic |
| **Organ of Corti** | Inner + outer hair cells on basilar membrane | Mechanotransduction → CN VIII |
| **Semicircular canals (3)** | Angular acceleration | BPPV (benign positional vertigo) |
| **Utricle + saccule** | Linear acceleration + gravity | Otoliths |
