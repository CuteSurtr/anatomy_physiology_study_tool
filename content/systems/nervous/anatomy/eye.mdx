The eye is a ~24 mm sphere built from **three concentric layers** plus the lens that bends incoming light onto the retina. Two clinically critical fluid compartments (aqueous + vitreous humor) maintain shape and supply nutrients to the avascular cornea and lens. Six cranial nerves are involved in normal eye function (CN II, III, IV, V1, VI, VII), making the orbit a high-yield exam region.

## The orbit - bony anatomy

The bony orbit is a four-sided pyramid pointing posteriorly. Seven bones contribute:

| Wall | Bones |
|---|---|
| **Roof** | Frontal bone + lesser wing of sphenoid |
| **Medial wall** | Ethmoid (paper-thin lamina papyracea - fractures easily) + lacrimal + maxilla + sphenoid |
| **Floor** | Maxilla + zygomatic + palatine |
| **Lateral wall** | Greater wing of sphenoid + zygomatic |

### Orbital apertures

| Opening | Transmits |
|---|---|
| **Optic canal** | CN II + ophthalmic artery |
| **Superior orbital fissure** | CN III, IV, **V1 (ophthalmic)**, VI; superior ophthalmic vein |
| **Inferior orbital fissure** | V2 maxillary branch; infraorbital nerve + vessels |
| **Nasolacrimal canal** | Nasolacrimal duct (drains tears to inferior meatus of nose) |

<Clinical>
**Blowout fracture**: blunt impact to the orbital rim (a baseball, fist) transmits pressure that fractures the **thinnest walls**: floor (maxilla) and medial wall (ethmoid). Orbital contents herniate into the maxillary sinus → **enophthalmos** + **inferior rectus entrapment** (can't look up) + infraorbital n. (V2) numbness over the cheek.
</Clinical>

## Three layers of the eye

### 1. Fibrous layer (outer)

| Part | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sclera** | White, opaque, vascular | Continuous with dura around CN II; muscle attachment |
| **Cornea** | Transparent, **avascular**, anterior 1/6 | Most refractive power (~2/3 of eye's total); receives O₂ from atmosphere + tear film; can be transplanted without immunosuppression |

### 2. Vascular (uveal) layer (middle)

| Part | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Choroid** | Posterior, pigmented + highly vascular | Nourishes outer retina |
| **Ciliary body** | Smooth muscle ring at the lens | Secretes aqueous humor; changes lens shape |
| **Iris** | Pigmented diaphragm with central pupil | Pupil size regulated by autonomics |

### 3. Neural layer (inner)

| Part | Description |
|---|---|
| **Retina - pigmented layer** | Absorbs scatter light |
| **Retina - neural layer** | Rods + cones + bipolar cells + ganglion cells |
| **Macula + fovea** | Central vision area; fovea = pit of densely packed cones |
| **Optic disc** | Where ganglion cell axons converge to form CN II - **physiologic blind spot** (no photoreceptors) |

## Fluid compartments

```
Anterior segment (anterior + posterior chambers - divided by iris):
  - Aqueous humor - produced by ciliary body
  - Function: nourishes lens + cornea; maintains intraocular pressure

Posterior segment (behind lens):
  - Vitreous humor - gel-like, ~80% of eye volume
  - Function: maintains shape, transmits light
```

### Flow of aqueous humor

1. **Ciliary body** secretes aqueous into the **posterior chamber** (between iris and lens)
2. Flows through the **pupil** into the **anterior chamber** (between iris and cornea)
3. Drains through the **trabecular meshwork** at the **iridocorneal angle**
4. Enters **canal of Schlemm** → episcleral veins → systemic circulation

<Clinical>
**Glaucoma** is increased intraocular pressure damaging the optic nerve. Two anatomic mechanisms:
- **Open-angle (90%)**: trabecular meshwork stenosis impedes outflow. Insidious, painless, peripheral → central vision loss. Treat with prostaglandin analogs (latanoprost) or β-blockers (timolol).
- **Closed-angle (acute angle-closure)**: lens + iris obstruct the angle. **Sudden severe eye pain + halos around lights + nausea + fixed mid-dilated pupil**. **Ophthalmic emergency**. Precipitated by **pupillary dilation** (anticholinergics, sympathomimetics, dim light). Treat with pilocarpine (miosis), acetazolamide, IV mannitol; definitive treatment is laser peripheral iridotomy.

**Anticholinergic toxicity → mydriasis → can trigger acute angle closure** in patients with anatomically narrow angles.
</Clinical>

## The lens and accommodation

The lens is a **biconvex transparent disc** suspended by **zonular fibers** from the ciliary body. Lens shape changes for focusing:

| Vision target | Ciliary muscle | Zonular tension | Lens shape | Innervation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Far** | Relaxed | Taut | Flat (less refraction) | Sympathetic (minor) + parasympathetic withdrawal |
| **Near** | Contracted (sphincter-like) | Loose | Rounded (more refraction) | Parasympathetic via CN III → ciliary ganglion (M3) |

<Note title="Cataract">
Lens opacification from protein cross-linking. Causes: aging (most common), diabetes (sorbitol pathway), corticosteroids, congenital (TORCH infections), trauma, radiation. Treatment: phacoemulsification + intraocular lens implant.
</Note>

## Autonomic innervation of the eye

<Figure name="nervous/eye-nerves" caption="Nerves of the orbit. Parasympathetic fibers from CN III synapse in the ciliary ganglion before reaching the pupillary sphincter + ciliary muscle. Sympathetic fibers reach the dilator pupillae and Müller's muscle via the long ciliary nerves." />

| Target | Sympathetic | Parasympathetic |
|---|---|---|
| **Pupillary sphincter (iris constrictor)** | None | **CN III → ciliary ganglion → M3 → miosis** |
| **Pupillary dilator (radial)** | **α1 → mydriasis** | None (passive flattening when constrictor relaxes) |
| **Ciliary muscle (lens accommodation)** | None | **CN III → M3 → near vision** |
| **Lacrimal gland** | None significant | **CN VII → pterygopalatine ganglion → M3** (tears) |
| **Müller's muscle (upper eyelid)** | **α1 → keeps eyelid open** | None |

<Clinical>
**Horner syndrome**: sympathetic lesion → loss of α1 input. Triad:
- **Miosis** (loss of dilator pupillae)
- **Ptosis** (partial - Müller's muscle weakness; not total ptosis like in CN III palsy)
- **Anhidrosis** (ipsilateral face - only if first-order or second-order neuron lesion)

Causes: Pancoast tumor (lung apex compresses sympathetic chain), carotid artery dissection, brainstem stroke (lateral medullary/Wallenberg), syringomyelia.
</Clinical>

## Extraocular muscles - CN III, IV, VI

| Muscle | Action | Nerve |
|---|---|---|
| **Superior rectus** | Elevation (eye up); also intortion, adduction | CN III |
| **Inferior rectus** | Depression (eye down); extortion, adduction | CN III |
| **Medial rectus** | Adduction (eye in) | CN III |
| **Lateral rectus** | Abduction (eye out) | **CN VI** |
| **Superior oblique** | Depression of adducted eye + intortion + abduction | **CN IV** |
| **Inferior oblique** | Elevation of adducted eye + extortion + abduction | CN III |
| **Levator palpebrae superioris** | Raises upper eyelid | CN III |

<Mnemonic>
**LR₆ SO₄ - rest is 3**: **L**ateral **R**ectus = CN **6**; **S**uperior **O**blique = CN **4**; all the rest = CN **3**.
</Mnemonic>

## Tears and the nasolacrimal apparatus

Tears are produced by the **lacrimal gland** (upper outer orbit, CN VII parasympathetic) and flow:

1. Across the cornea (lubrication, oxygen delivery, antibacterial lysozyme)
2. Into the **lacrimal puncta** at the medial canthus (upper + lower lids)
3. Through the **canaliculi** → **lacrimal sac** → **nasolacrimal duct**
4. Empties into the **inferior meatus of the nasal cavity**

This is why crying makes your nose run - and why **nasolacrimal occlusion** during eye-drop instillation:
- ↑ **Drug retention on the eye** (efficacy)
- ↓ **Systemic absorption + adverse effects** (timolol can cause bradycardia + bronchospasm via nasal mucosal uptake)

## Visual pathway

<Figure name="nervous/optical-pathways" caption="Visual pathway, inferior view of the brain. Nasal-retina fibers from each eye decussate at the optic chiasm; temporal-retina fibers do not. After the LGN, optic radiations sweep through parietal + temporal lobes to reach primary visual cortex (V1). Lesion location maps cleanly to the visual-field defect (see table below)." />

```
Retinal ganglion cells
      ↓ (axons converge)
   Optic nerve (CN II)
      ↓
Optic chiasm (nasal fibers cross)
      ↓
   Optic tract
      ↓
Lateral geniculate nucleus (LGN) of thalamus
      ↓
Optic radiations
  - Upper radiations (parietal) → upper bank of calcarine sulcus
  - Lower radiations (Meyer's loop, temporal) → lower bank
      ↓
Primary visual cortex (V1, occipital)
```

Visual field defects map directly to lesion location:

| Lesion | Defect |
|---|---|
| Optic nerve | Monocular blindness or scotoma |
| Optic chiasm (central) | **Bitemporal hemianopia** (pituitary tumor) |
| Optic chiasm (lateral) | Ipsilateral nasal hemianopia (internal carotid aneurysm) |
| Optic tract | Contralateral homonymous hemianopia |
| Meyer's loop (temporal) | Contralateral "**pie in the sky**" - superior quadrantanopia |
| Parietal optic radiation | Contralateral "**pie on the floor**" - inferior quadrantanopia |
| Occipital cortex | Contralateral homonymous hemianopia **with macular sparing** |

## Photoreceptors - rods vs cones

<Figure name="nervous/retina" caption="Retinal layers - light passes through the inner layers first and reaches photoreceptors (rods + cones) at the back; signal then flows forward through bipolar cells to ganglion cells, whose axons converge at the optic disc to form CN II." />

| Feature | Rods | Cones |
|---|---|---|
| **Numerosity** | 100 million; peripheral | 6 million; central (fovea) |
| **Function** | Dim light, motion, peripheral, low resolution | Bright light, color, high resolution |
| **Pigment** | Rhodopsin (1 type) | 3 types (red, green, blue cones) |
| **Convergence** | Many rods → 1 ganglion cell → high sensitivity, low acuity | Cone → 1 ganglion (in fovea) → low sensitivity, high acuity |
| **Adaptation** | Fast (~25 min dark adapt) | Slow but immediate to bright |

<Clinical>
**Macular degeneration** (age-related): central vision loss because cone-rich fovea is damaged. Two types: dry (drusen accumulation) and wet (abnormal choroidal neovascularization → bevacizumab/ranibizumab intravitreal injection).

**Retinitis pigmentosa**: rod degeneration → night blindness + peripheral vision loss first.

**Retinal detachment**: separation of neural retina from RPE. Sudden flashes, floaters, "curtain" over vision. Surgical emergency.
</Clinical>

## Every eye structure - function reference

| Structure | What it does | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Sclera** | Tough outer fibrous coat | Maintains shape; site of EOM attachment |
| **Cornea** | Transparent anterior fibrous; main refractive surface | Avascular → graft without immunosuppression; refractive surgery target (LASIK) |
| **Conjunctiva** | Mucous membrane lining eyelids + anterior sclera | Conjunctivitis = "pink eye" |
| **Choroid** | Vascular middle layer | Nourishes outer retina |
| **Ciliary body** | Smooth muscle + secretory epithelium | Aqueous humor + accommodation |
| **Iris** | Pigmented diaphragm | Pupillary size; eye color from melanocytes |
| **Pupil** | Hole in iris | Mydriasis/miosis controlled by autonomics |
| **Lens** | Biconvex transparent | Fine focus via accommodation; opacifies in cataract |
| **Zonular fibers** | Suspend lens from ciliary body | Loosen when ciliary muscle contracts |
| **Retina** | Photoreceptor + initial processing layer | Site of vision; only place CNS is visible (fundoscopy) |
| **Macula** | Central retina | High-acuity vision |
| **Fovea** | Cone-rich pit in center of macula | Sharpest vision |
| **Optic disc** | CN II exit point | Physiologic blind spot |
| **Aqueous humor** | Anterior segment fluid | Maintains pressure; nourishes lens + cornea |
| **Vitreous humor** | Posterior segment gel | Maintains shape; transmits light |
| **Trabecular meshwork** | Aqueous outflow | Blocked in open-angle glaucoma |
| **Canal of Schlemm** | Final aqueous drainage | Empties to episcleral veins |
| **Lacrimal gland** | Tear production | CN VII parasympathetic; loss → dry eye |
| **Nasolacrimal duct** | Tear drainage to nose | Obstruction → epiphora (overflow tearing) |
| **Optic nerve (CN II)** | Carries vision to brain | Actually a CNS tract - vulnerable to MS demyelination |
| **Optic chiasm** | Nasal retinal fibers decussate | Compressed by pituitary tumor → bitemporal hemianopia |
| **LGN (thalamus)** | Visual relay | 6 layers; magnocellular (motion) + parvocellular (color) |
| **V1 (calcarine cortex)** | Primary visual processing | Lesion → contralateral homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Visual pathway lesions** (high-yield Step 1):
- **Pituitary adenoma** → bitemporal hemianopia from chiasm compression (think: prolactinoma in young woman with galactorrhea + amenorrhea).
- **MCA stroke** affecting Meyer's loop → contralateral superior quadrantanopia.
- **PCA stroke** → contralateral homonymous hemianopia with **macular sparing** (collateral supply from MCA to occipital pole).
- **Optic neuritis** in young woman + Lhermitte sign + foot numbness → **multiple sclerosis**.

**Cranial nerve eye signs**:
- **CN III palsy** (compressive - PCA aneurysm): "down + out" eye, ptosis, **blown pupil** (parasympathetic fibers on outside of nerve are hit first).
- **CN III palsy** (ischemic - diabetic): same as above but **pupil-sparing** (internal motor fibers hit first; outer parasympathetic spared).
- **CN IV palsy**: difficulty going down stairs (loss of intorsion + depression of adducted eye).
- **CN VI palsy**: eye can't abduct; classic false-localizing sign in raised ICP.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Eye pharmacology essentials:**
- **Mydriatics**: tropicamide (anticholinergic - short-acting), atropine (anticholinergic - long); phenylephrine (α1 agonist). Used for fundoscopy.
- **Miotics**: pilocarpine (M3 agonist) - closed-angle glaucoma emergency.
- **Open-angle glaucoma**:
  - Prostaglandin analogs (latanoprost, bimatoprost): ↑ uveoscleral outflow; iris darkening + eyelash growth side effects.
  - β-blockers (timolol): ↓ aqueous production; **caution in asthma, COPD, heart block**.
  - α2 agonists (brimonidine): ↓ aqueous production.
  - Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (dorzolamide topical, acetazolamide oral).
- **Wet AMD**: intravitreal **anti-VEGF** (bevacizumab, ranibizumab, aflibercept) - monthly injections.
- **Drugs that can precipitate acute angle closure** in narrow-angle patients: anticholinergics (atropine, TCAs, diphenhydramine), sympathomimetics, topiramate.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX eye care priorities:**
- **Eye drop instillation**: pull lower lid down to form pocket; drop into pocket without touching tip; press inner canthus for 1 min (**nasolacrimal occlusion**) to reduce systemic effects of timolol etc.
- **Angle-closure glaucoma emergency**: sudden eye pain + halos + nausea + vomiting → urgent ophthalmology consult; do not give anticholinergics (e.g., scopolamine for nausea - would worsen it!).
- **Post-cataract surgery teaching**: eye shield at night, no bending/lifting, antibiotic + steroid drops on schedule; report sudden flashes/floaters (retinal detachment).
- **Visual field check after stroke**: confrontation testing.
</HighYield>
