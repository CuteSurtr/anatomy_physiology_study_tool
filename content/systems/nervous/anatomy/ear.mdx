The ear converts mechanical vibrations into electrical signals (**hearing**) and detects head position + movement (**equilibrium**), both via **CN VIII (vestibulocochlear)**. It has three compartments - outer, middle, inner - each with distinct anatomy, pathology, and drug-administration considerations.

## Outer ear

Air-filled, opens to the outside world.

| Structure | Function | Clinical |
|---|---|---|
| **Auricle (pinna)** | Collects + funnels sound | Sound localization; cosmetic |
| **External auditory canal** | Conducts sound to tympanic membrane (~2.5 cm) | Lined by skin with ceruminous + sebaceous glands |
| **Cerumen (earwax)** | Antimicrobial, lubricant, traps debris | Impaction → conductive hearing loss; don't use Q-tips |
| **Tympanic membrane (eardrum)** | Vibrates with sound waves | CN V (anterior) + CN X (posterior) + CN VII (chorda tympani crosses behind it) innervate |

<Clinical>
**Otitis externa ("swimmer's ear")**: infection of external ear canal, often Pseudomonas. Hallmark: **pain on traction of the auricle**. Treat with **topical otic** antibiotics (e.g., ciprofloxacin + dexamethasone drops) - oral antibiotics not needed.
</Clinical>

## Middle ear

<Figure name="nervous/auditory-ossicles" caption="The auditory ossicles - malleus, incus, stapes. The three smallest bones in the body amplify pressure ~20× from tympanic membrane to oval window." />

Air-filled cavity in the temporal bone. Contains three ossicles in series:

| Ossicle | Latin/meaning | Connection |
|---|---|---|
| **Malleus** | "Hammer" | Attached to tympanic membrane |
| **Incus** | "Anvil" | Between malleus and stapes |
| **Stapes** | "Stirrup" - **smallest bone in body** | Footplate in **oval window** |

Two tiny middle-ear muscles dampen loud sounds:
- **Tensor tympani** (innervated by CN V3) → attached to malleus
- **Stapedius** (innervated by **CN VII**) → attached to stapes

The **Eustachian tube** (auditory tube, pharyngotympanic tube) connects the middle ear to the **nasopharynx**, equalizing pressure with atmosphere. It runs **down + forward + medially** - shorter and more horizontal in children → why kids get otitis media so often.

<Clinical>
**Otitis media**: middle ear infection (often after URI; bacterial: S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, M. catarrhalis). Symptoms: ear pain + **fever** + bulging tympanic membrane + decreased hearing. Treat with **oral** antibiotics (amoxicillin first-line) - otic drops can't reach behind an intact eardrum. Recurrent OM → tympanostomy tubes.

**Mastoiditis**: complication of OM extending into mastoid air cells; postauricular swelling + erythema.

**Otosclerosis**: abnormal bone growth fixing the stapes → conductive hearing loss; treatment is stapedectomy.

**Cholesteatoma**: keratinizing squamous epithelium in middle ear → erodes ossicles + temporal bone.

**Hyperacusis** in CN VII (facial) palsy: loss of stapedius reflex → sounds painfully loud on affected side.
</Clinical>

## Inner ear

<Figure name="nervous/cochlea-cross-section-gray" caption="Cochlea cross-section - three fluid-filled scalae (vestibuli, media, tympani); basilar membrane carrying the organ of Corti with hair cells; tectorial membrane (Gray's Anatomy, 1918)." />

<Figure name="nervous/ear-anatomy-detailed" caption="Detailed anatomy of the human ear - outer (auricle, canal, tympanic membrane), middle (ossicles, Eustachian tube), inner (cochlea, semicircular canals, vestibule, CN VIII)." />

Fluid-filled labyrinthine structures within the temporal bone. Two main divisions:

### Cochlea (hearing)

Spiral, snail-shell shaped (Greek *kokhlias*). Contains three fluid-filled compartments:

| Scala | Contents | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Scala vestibuli** | Perilymph | Receives vibrations from stapes (oval window) |
| **Scala media** (cochlear duct) | **Endolymph** + organ of Corti | Houses hair cells - the actual transducer |
| **Scala tympani** | Perilymph | Terminates at round window (pressure release) |

**Organ of Corti** sits on the **basilar membrane** in the scala media. Contains hair cells (inner + outer) whose stereocilia contact the **tectorial membrane**. Fluid waves bend stereocilia → opens K⁺ channels → depolarization → glutamate release → cochlear branch of CN VIII fires.

<Figure name="nervous/organ-of-corti" caption="Organ of Corti - one row of inner hair cells (the actual transducers, ~95% of CN VIII afferents) and three rows of outer hair cells (cochlear amplifier) sit on the basilar membrane; stereocilia contact the overlying tectorial membrane." />

**Tonotopy**: the basilar membrane is **stiff + narrow at the base** (high frequencies) and **floppy + wide at the apex** (low frequencies). Different frequencies maximally stimulate different locations along the length. **Presbycusis** (age-related hearing loss) preferentially loses **high frequencies** because base hair cells are exposed to all frequencies and wear out first.

### Vestibular apparatus (balance + equilibrium)

| Structure | Detects | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Three semicircular canals** (anterior, posterior, lateral, at right angles) | **Angular acceleration** (rotation) | Fluid movement bends cupula in each ampulla |
| **Utricle** | Linear acceleration (**horizontal**) + gravity | Otoliths (calcium carbonate crystals) on otolithic membrane |
| **Saccule** | Linear acceleration (**vertical**) + gravity | Same as utricle |

<Clinical>
**Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV)**: otoconia dislodge from utricle/saccule and float into a semicircular canal (usually posterior) → with head movement, they shift fluid and trigger vertigo. Diagnose: **Dix-Hallpike maneuver**. Treat: **Epley maneuver** (reposition canalith).

**Ménière disease**: endolymphatic hydrops (↑ pressure in endolymph). Triad: **episodic vertigo + tinnitus + sensorineural hearing loss** + aural fullness. Treat with low-salt diet + diuretics; severe cases get gentamicin chemical labyrinthectomy.

**Vestibular schwannoma (acoustic neuroma)**: tumor of CN VIII Schwann cells at cerebellopontine angle. Unilateral SNHL + tinnitus + balance issues. Bilateral = **NF2** (chromosome 22).

**Labyrinthitis**: inflammation of the labyrinth, usually viral → vertigo + N/V + sometimes hearing loss. Self-limited.
</Clinical>

## Sound transmission pathway

<Figure name="nervous/auditory-brainstem" caption="Auditory pathway from cochlea to cortex - CN VIII → cochlear nuclei (medulla) → superior olive (bilateral input, sound localization) → lateral lemniscus → inferior colliculus → MGN (thalamus) → primary auditory cortex (Heschl gyrus, superior temporal gyrus)." />

```
Sound waves in air
       ↓
Auricle + ear canal (concentrate + amplify)
       ↓
Tympanic membrane (vibrates)
       ↓
Ossicles (malleus → incus → stapes) - mechanical amplification ~22×
       ↓
Stapes footplate → oval window → perilymph in scala vestibuli
       ↓
Fluid waves → basilar membrane displacement
       ↓
Hair cells of organ of Corti bend → K⁺ influx → depolarize → glutamate release
       ↓
Cochlear branch of CN VIII
       ↓
Cochlear nuclei (medulla)
       ↓
Superior olivary complex (sound localization via interaural time + intensity differences) - bilateral
       ↓
Lateral lemniscus
       ↓
Inferior colliculus (midbrain)
       ↓
Medial geniculate nucleus (thalamus)
       ↓
Primary auditory cortex (transverse temporal gyrus of Heschl, A1)
```

## Hearing loss - types

| Type | Lesion location | Rinne | Weber lateralization |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | - | AC > BC (positive Rinne) | Midline |
| **Conductive** | Outer or middle ear (cerumen, OM, otosclerosis, perforated TM) | **BC > AC** (negative Rinne) on affected side | Lateralizes to **affected** ear |
| **Sensorineural** | Inner ear, CN VIII, or central (presbycusis, noise-induced, ototoxicity, Ménière, schwannoma) | AC > BC (positive Rinne) on affected side | Lateralizes to **unaffected** ear |

<Note title="Why the Weber test goes opposite directions">
In conductive loss, the affected ear isn't hearing the background room noise normally, so it picks up the tuning fork better (lateralizes to the affected side). In sensorineural loss, the affected ear can't transduce sound well, so the good ear wins.
</Note>

## Ototoxic drugs

Several drug classes damage hair cells of the cochlea (and sometimes vestibular apparatus):

| Class | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Aminoglycosides** (gentamicin, streptomycin, neomycin, amikacin) | Concentrate in hair cells; generate ROS | Can be **vestibulotoxic** > cochleotoxic (streptomycin, gentamicin); irreversible |
| **Loop diuretics** (furosemide, bumetanide, ethacrynic acid) | Alter cochlear endolymph ion balance | Usually reversible if recognized; high IV doses + renal failure ↑ risk |
| **Salicylates** (aspirin) | Cochlear hair cell dysfunction | **Tinnitus** is early sign of toxicity; usually reversible |
| **Cisplatin** | Cochlear hair cell DNA damage | Irreversible, dose-dependent |
| **Vancomycin** | Likely synergistic with aminoglycosides | Less ototoxic alone than once thought |
| **Macrolides** (high-dose erythromycin, azithromycin) | Mechanism unclear | Usually reversible |

<Clinical>
**Classic case for boards**: heart failure patient with worsening renal function. Their loop diuretic dose was increased + they're on an aminoglycoside for hospital infection → develops tinnitus + hearing loss. The two classes are synergistically ototoxic.
</Clinical>

## Every ear structure - function reference

| Structure | What it does | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Auricle** | Collects + localizes sound | Skin malignancies (SCC) common |
| **External auditory canal** | Conducts sound | Otitis externa site |
| **Cerumen** | Antibacterial + lubricant | Impaction common |
| **Tympanic membrane** | Vibrates with sound | Rupture → conductive HL; mixed innervation |
| **Malleus, incus, stapes** | Amplify ~22× through lever action + area ratio | Otosclerosis fixes stapes (conductive HL) |
| **Stapedius muscle** | Dampens loud sound | Innervated by CN VII; lost → hyperacusis |
| **Tensor tympani muscle** | Dampens loud sound | Innervated by CN V3 |
| **Eustachian tube** | Pressure equalization | OM in kids - shorter, horizontal tube |
| **Oval window** | Stapes footplate enters here | Mechanical → fluid signal transition |
| **Round window** | Pressure release | Bulges out as oval window pushes in |
| **Cochlea** | Hearing - tonotopic transduction | Base = high freq; apex = low freq |
| **Organ of Corti** | Hair cells on basilar membrane | Site of actual transduction |
| **Inner hair cells** | Afferent signal (95% of CN VIII output) | The "real" sensors |
| **Outer hair cells** | Active amplification (motile) | Otoacoustic emissions test their function |
| **Tectorial membrane** | Bends stereocilia | Reference for hair cell deflection |
| **Endolymph** | High K⁺ - fluid inside scala media | Different from typical extracellular fluid |
| **Perilymph** | Normal ECF composition | Fills scala vestibuli + scala tympani |
| **Semicircular canals (3)** | Angular acceleration | Three planes; rotation detection |
| **Cupula** | Gelatinous mass in each ampulla | Bends with fluid → hair cell stimulation |
| **Utricle** | Horizontal linear acceleration | Otoconia on otolithic membrane |
| **Saccule** | Vertical linear acceleration | Same mechanism as utricle |
| **Endolymphatic sac** | Reabsorbs endolymph | Dilated in Ménière |
| **CN VIII (vestibulocochlear)** | Sensory to brainstem | Cochlear + vestibular divisions |
| **Internal acoustic meatus** | Bony canal through temporal bone | Carries CN VII, CN VIII, labyrinthine artery |
| **Cerebellopontine angle** | Where CN VII, VIII, V exit | Site of vestibular schwannoma |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX ear-care priorities:**
- **Otic drop administration**: adults - pull auricle **up and back**; children &lt; 3 - pull **down and back**. Have patient remain lying ~5 min.
- **OE vs OM**: otitis externa = topical drops OK; **otitis media needs systemic antibiotics** (drops can't get past intact eardrum).
- **Post-op cochlear implant teaching**: no MRI without prior clearance, no heavy lifting initially, watch for infection.
- **Ménière's teaching**: low-salt diet, avoid caffeine/nicotine/alcohol triggers, safety with vertigo episodes.
- **Tinnitus + new hearing loss after starting a new medication** = consider ototoxicity (aspirin, loop diuretics, aminoglycosides) - discuss with prescriber.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Vertigo localization:**
- **Peripheral (BPPV, Ménière, labyrinthitis, vestibular neuritis)**: severe vertigo + N/V, fatigable nystagmus, intact other neuro exam.
- **Central (brainstem stroke, cerebellar lesion)**: less severe vertigo but **other neurologic findings** (diplopia, dysarthria, ataxia, weakness); non-fatigable nystagmus that changes direction.
- **HINTS exam** distinguishes: **H**ead **I**mpulse, **N**ystagmus pattern, **T**est of **S**kew. Central HINTS → CT angiography to rule out posterior circulation stroke.

**CN VIII tumor (vestibular schwannoma)** - bilateral = NF2; unilateral most common. Imaging: gadolinium-enhanced MRI of IAC.

**Conductive vs sensorineural** - Weber/Rinne mastery is consistently tested.
</HighYield>
