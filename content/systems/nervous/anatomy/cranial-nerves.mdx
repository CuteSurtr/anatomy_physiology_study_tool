The cranial nerves are **12 pairs** (or **13** if you count the terminal nerve, CN 0) that emerge directly from the brain - bypassing the spinal cord. They carry sensory, motor, and autonomic (parasympathetic) fibers to and from the head, neck, and (in the case of CN X) the thoracic and abdominal viscera.

<Figure name="nervous/cranial-nerves-labeled" caption="Inferior view of the brain showing all 12 cranial nerves emerging at their origins. CN I (olfactory) is anterior; CN XII (hypoglossal) is most caudal." />

## CN 0 - Terminal nerve (nervus terminalis)

The **forgotten thirteenth cranial nerve**. Discovered in sharks by **Gustav Fritsch in 1878** and confirmed in humans (Brookover, 1914). It runs anterior to the olfactory tract along the medial surface of the frontal lobe and pierces the cribriform plate to terminate in the nasal mucosa.

Key features:
- Unmyelinated, with associated ganglion cells along its course
- Contains **GnRH-secreting neurons** that migrate along its path during embryonic development - failure of this migration causes **Kallmann syndrome** (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism + anosmia)
- May carry **autonomic vasomotor fibers** to nasal vessels
- A possible role in **pheromone-like chemoreception** has been proposed but is unproven in humans (the vomeronasal organ regresses in adults)

<Clinical>
**Kallmann syndrome**: failure of GnRH-secreting neurons (and olfactory neurons) to migrate from the olfactory placode along the terminal nerve path → no pulsatile GnRH → no LH/FSH → delayed/absent puberty + anosmia. KAL1 (X-linked) and other genes implicated. Anosmia testing is therefore part of the hypogonadism workup.
</Clinical>

## CN I - Olfactory nerve

<Figure name="nervous/olfactory-nerve-gray775" caption="Olfactory nerve (CN I) - bipolar olfactory receptor neurons in nasal epithelium → axons through cribriform plate → olfactory bulb → olfactory tract → primary olfactory cortex (the only sense that bypasses the thalamus on the way to cortex)." />

**Pure sensory.** ~10–20 bundles of unmyelinated axons from olfactory neurons in the **superior nasal mucosa** → pass through the **cribriform plate** of the ethmoid → synapse in the **olfactory bulb** → olfactory tract → primary olfactory cortex (piriform cortex, amygdala) **without first going through the thalamus** (unique among sensory pathways).

**Test**: present familiar odors to each nostril separately.

<Clinical>
**Anosmia causes**: head trauma (shearing through cribriform plate is classic), URI/sinusitis (commonest), neurodegenerative disease (early sign of Parkinson + Alzheimer), tumor (olfactory groove meningioma → **Foster Kennedy syndrome**: ipsilateral anosmia + optic atrophy + contralateral papilledema), COVID-19, congenital (Kallmann).
</Clinical>

## CN II - Optic nerve

**Pure sensory.** Not technically a peripheral nerve - it's a **CNS tract** wrapped in meninges (the optic nerve sheath continues with dura → SAH can extend along it). Carries axons from retinal ganglion cells → optic chiasm → optic tracts → LGN of thalamus → optic radiations → V1.

<Figure name="nervous/eye-nerves" caption="Nerves of the eye and orbit. CN II carries vision; CN III, IV, VI control extraocular muscles; V1 carries sensation; parasympathetic fibers of CN III synapse in the ciliary ganglion." />

**Visual-field defects map**:

| Lesion site | Defect |
|---|---|
| Optic nerve | Monocular vision loss / scotoma in same eye |
| Optic chiasm | **Bitemporal hemianopsia** (nasal retinal fibers crossed - hit first) |
| Optic tract | Contralateral homonymous hemianopsia |
| Meyer's loop (temporal) | Contralateral "**pie in the sky**" (superior quadrantanopia) |
| Parietal optic radiation | Contralateral "**pie on the floor**" (inferior quadrantanopia) |
| Occipital cortex | Contralateral homonymous hemianopsia with **macular sparing** |

<Clinical>
**Optic neuritis**: painful monocular vision loss + color desaturation, **classic for MS**. RAPD (relative afferent pupillary defect) present.

**Papilledema**: bilateral optic disc swelling from ↑ ICP. Always bilateral (vs unilateral disc edema in optic neuritis).
</Clinical>

## CN III - Oculomotor nerve

<Figure name="nervous/oculomotor-trochlear-abducens-gray" caption="CN III (oculomotor), IV (trochlear), and VI (abducens) — the three extraocular nerves. CN III: MR, IR, SR, IO, levator palpebrae + parasympathetic to pupil/ciliary. CN IV: SO. CN VI: LR. Mnemonic: LR6SO4 — lateral rectus = CN VI, superior oblique = CN IV, rest = CN III." />

**Motor + parasympathetic.** Exits from **interpeduncular fossa of the midbrain** → passes through cavernous sinus → superior orbital fissure → orbit.

**Innervates** all extraocular muscles **except** lateral rectus (CN VI) and superior oblique (CN IV) - so: **MR, SR, IR, IO, levator palpebrae superioris**. Parasympathetic fibers via **Edinger-Westphal nucleus → ciliary ganglion → pupillary sphincter + ciliary muscle** (accommodation).

**Lesion**: classic "**down-and-out**" eye (unopposed LR + SO), **ptosis** (loss of levator palpebrae), and - if parasympathetic fibers involved - **dilated pupil** ("blown pupil").

<Clinical>
**Two classic CN III lesion patterns**:

- **Compressive lesion** (PCA aneurysm, uncal herniation) - affects **outer parasympathetic fibers first** → **pupil blown early**. Surgical emergency.
- **Ischemic lesion** (diabetic mononeuropathy, vasculopathy) - affects **inner motor fibers first**, **pupil spared** until late. Usually resolves in 3 months.

**Uncal herniation** through the tentorial notch first compresses CN III → ipsilateral dilated pupil + down-and-out eye (then false-localizing CN VI palsy as ICP rises further).
</Clinical>

## CN IV - Trochlear nerve

<Figure name="nervous/oculomotor-trochlear-abducens-gray" caption="Trochlear nerve (CN IV) - innervates only the superior oblique muscle (depresses + intorts + abducts an adducted eye). Smallest CN; only one from dorsal brainstem; longest intracranial course." />

**Motor only.** Smallest cranial nerve. **Only CN that exits the brainstem dorsally** and **only CN that decussates entirely** (its nucleus is on the contralateral side from the muscle it innervates).

Supplies the **superior oblique** muscle - **SO₄ (depresses + intorts an adducted eye)**.

**Test**: ask the patient to look down and inward (toward the nose) - selective SO action. Tilting head **away** from affected side compensates (Bielschowsky head tilt test).

<Clinical>
**CN IV palsy**: most common cause of vertical diplopia. Patients tilt head away from the lesion. Classic presentation: difficulty going down stairs (where SO normally depresses the adducted eye). Often congenital but can present after minor trauma.
</Clinical>

## CN V - Trigeminal nerve

<Figure name="nervous/cranial-nerves-gray780" caption="Trigeminal nerve (CN V) and its three divisions - ophthalmic (V1), maxillary (V2), mandibular (V3). Sensory to face; motor to muscles of mastication via V3 (Gray's Anatomy, 1918, PD)." />

**Both (sensory + motor).** Three divisions, each exiting through a different foramen:

| Division | Foramen | Sensation | Motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **V1 - Ophthalmic** | Superior orbital fissure | Forehead, scalp anterior to vertex, upper eyelid, cornea, dorsum of nose | - |
| **V2 - Maxillary** | Foramen rotundum | Cheek, upper lip, upper teeth, lateral nose, palate | - |
| **V3 - Mandibular** | Foramen ovale | Lower lip, lower teeth, anterior 2/3 of tongue (general sensation only), chin | **Muscles of mastication**: masseter, temporalis, medial + lateral pterygoid; also mylohyoid, anterior digastric, tensor tympani, tensor veli palatini |

<Figure name="nervous/trigeminal-gray778" caption="Distribution of maxillary (V2) and mandibular (V3) divisions of the trigeminal nerve and associated ganglia (Gray's plate 778, 1918, public domain). Note the dense innervation of the upper and lower teeth and the wide cutaneous coverage of the face." />

<Mnemonic>
**"Standing Room Only"** - V1 exits **S**uperior orbital fissure, V2 exits foramen **R**otundum, V3 exits foramen **O**vale.
</Mnemonic>

<Clinical>
**Trigeminal neuralgia (tic douloureux)**: paroxysmal lightning-stab pain in V2 or V3 distribution, triggered by touch/chewing/wind. Often from vascular compression of the nerve root (superior cerebellar artery). **Carbamazepine** is first-line; microvascular decompression for refractory cases.

**Corneal (blink) reflex**: V1 afferent → CN VII motor efferent (orbicularis oculi). Loss of blink with intact afferent suggests CN VII lesion; absent afferent suggests V1 lesion.
</Clinical>

## CN VI - Abducens nerve

<Figure name="nervous/oculomotor-trochlear-abducens-gray" caption="Abducens nerve (CN VI) - innervates only the lateral rectus (abducts eye). Long intracranial course → susceptible to ↑ICP. Palsy: medial deviation, inability to abduct." />

**Motor only.** Innervates the **lateral rectus** - **LR₆ (abducts the eye)**. Has the **longest intracranial course** of any CN - makes it especially vulnerable to elevated ICP (**false-localizing sign**).

**Lesion**: eye adducted (unopposed MR), can't abduct past midline → horizontal diplopia.

<Clinical>
**False-localizing CN VI palsy**: in any cause of raised ICP (pseudotumor, mass, hydrocephalus), CN VI can be stretched over the petrous ridge - bilateral involvement is possible. Means "the palsy is real but doesn't tell you where the lesion is."

**Foville and Millard-Gubler syndromes** (pontine strokes): ipsilateral CN VI (and VII in Millard-Gubler) palsy + contralateral hemiparesis (corticospinal involvement before pyramidal decussation).
</Clinical>

## CN VII - Facial nerve

<Figure name="nervous/cranial-nerves-gray792" caption="Facial nerve (CN VII) plan and its 5 motor branches (temporal, zygomatic, buccal, marginal mandibular, cervical) plus chorda tympani (taste, anterior 2/3 tongue) and nerve to stapedius (Gray's 1918, PD)." />

**Both + parasympathetic + taste.** Exits the pons at the cerebellopontine angle (with CN VIII) → internal acoustic meatus → through the **facial canal** in the petrous temporal bone (giving off branches) → exits at the **stylomastoid foramen** → enters the parotid gland (which it does NOT innervate - CN IX does) and splits into 5 terminal branches.

**Functions** (rich and varied - high-yield):
- **Motor**: muscles of facial expression (orbicularis oculi/oris, etc.); stapedius (dampens loud sounds - lesion causes **hyperacusis**); stylohyoid; posterior digastric.
- **Parasympathetic**: lacrimal gland (via **greater petrosal nerve** → pterygopalatine ganglion); submandibular + sublingual glands (via **chorda tympani** → submandibular ganglion).
- **Taste**: anterior 2/3 of tongue (via chorda tympani).

<Figure name="nervous/facial-gray790" caption="Facial nerve (CN VII) and its five terminal branches: temporal, zygomatic, buccal, mandibular, cervical (Gray's plate 790). The branches fan out within the parotid gland." />

<Mnemonic>
**"To Zanzibar By Motor Car"** - **T**emporal, **Z**ygomatic, **B**uccal, **M**andibular, **C**ervical (the 5 terminal branches of CN VII, superior to inferior).
</Mnemonic>

<Clinical>
**Bell's palsy** - LMN facial weakness affecting the **entire ipsilateral half of the face** (including forehead). Often post-viral (HSV reactivation). 

vs.

**Central (UMN) facial weakness** - from a contralateral stroke. The **forehead is spared** because the upper face has bilateral cortical innervation. 

This anatomic fact is the highest-yield CN VII clinical pearl in medicine.

Other CN VII findings: dry eye (loss of lacrimation), loss of taste anterior 2/3 tongue, hyperacusis (loss of stapedius dampening), drooping mouth, inability to close eye.

**Ramsay Hunt syndrome**: VZV reactivation in geniculate ganglion → Bell's palsy + vesicles in external auditory canal + ipsilateral hearing loss.
</Clinical>

## CN VIII - Vestibulocochlear nerve

<Figure name="nervous/vestibulocochlear-gray789" caption="Vestibulocochlear nerve (CN VIII) - vestibular branch from semicircular canals + utricle/saccule → vestibular nuclei; cochlear branch from organ of Corti → cochlear nuclei." />

**Pure sensory.** Two divisions:
- **Cochlear** - hearing; from spiral ganglion of the cochlea
- **Vestibular** - balance, head position; from vestibular ganglion of inner ear

Exits at cerebellopontine angle alongside CN VII; both enter the internal acoustic meatus.

**Tests**: Rinne (air vs bone conduction at mastoid) + Weber (tuning fork on forehead) to distinguish conductive from sensorineural hearing loss.

<Clinical>
**Vestibular schwannoma (acoustic neuroma)** at the cerebellopontine angle - slow-growing tumor of CN VIII Schwann cells. Presents with **unilateral sensorineural hearing loss + tinnitus + balance issues**; can compress CN VII (facial weakness) and CN V (facial sensation) as it grows. **Bilateral acoustic neuromas = NF2** (chromosome 22).

**Ménière's disease**: episodic vertigo + tinnitus + sensorineural hearing loss + aural fullness from endolymphatic hydrops. Treat with low-salt diet + diuretics.
</Clinical>

## CN IX - Glossopharyngeal nerve

<Figure name="nervous/glossopharyngeal-gray793" caption="Glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX) - posterior 1/3 tongue taste + general sensation, stylopharyngeus muscle, parotid (parasympathetic via otic ganglion), carotid body + sinus afferents." />

**Both + parasympathetic + taste.** Exits the medulla → jugular foramen.

**Functions**:
- **Motor**: **stylopharyngeus** (elevates pharynx during swallow) - that's it
- **Sensory**: pharynx (afferent limb of **gag reflex**), middle ear, eustachian tube, **carotid sinus** (baroreceptor) and **carotid body** (chemoreceptor)
- **Parasympathetic**: **parotid gland** (via **lesser petrosal nerve** → otic ganglion)
- **Taste**: posterior 1/3 of tongue

<Clinical>
**Glossopharyngeal neuralgia**: stabbing pain in throat/ear triggered by swallowing. Rare. Treat like trigeminal neuralgia.

**Loss of gag reflex**: lesion of CN IX (afferent) or CN X (efferent - palatal/pharyngeal muscles). Test by touching the posterior pharyngeal wall.
</Clinical>

## CN X - Vagus nerve

<Figure name="nervous/vagus-nerve-gray794" caption="Vagus nerve (CN X) - longest cranial nerve. Parasympathetic to heart, lungs, GI to splenic flexure; motor to pharynx + larynx (recurrent laryngeal); taste from epiglottis; aortic baro/chemoreceptors." />

**Both + parasympathetic + taste.** The longest CN, wandering ("vagus") into the thorax and abdomen. Exits jugular foramen, descends in the carotid sheath, gives off **recurrent laryngeal nerves**, enters thorax to supply heart and lungs, passes through the diaphragm to reach all foregut + midgut viscera (up to the **splenic flexure** of the colon - distal to that, **pelvic splanchnics** take over).

<Figure name="nervous/vagus-gray793" caption="Course and distribution of the vagus nerve in the neck, thorax, and abdomen (Gray's plate 793). Note the recurrent laryngeal nerves, cardiac plexus, pulmonary plexus, and abdominal branches." />

**Functions**:
- **Motor**: all pharyngeal muscles except stylopharyngeus (IX) and tensor veli palatini (V3); all laryngeal muscles (via recurrent + superior laryngeal branches)
- **Sensory**: laryngeal sensation, ear (auricular branch - only CN giving cutaneous innervation to the external ear via Arnold's nerve), aortic arch baroreceptors
- **Parasympathetic**: heart, lungs, GI tract to splenic flexure
- **Taste**: epiglottis (small contribution)

<Clinical>
**Recurrent laryngeal nerve injury** → hoarseness (unilateral) or aphonia + stridor (bilateral).
- **Left RLN loops under aortic arch** - vulnerable in lung cancer (Pancoast tumor, AP window LN), aortic surgery, mediastinal tumor.
- **Right RLN loops under right subclavian** - vulnerable in thyroid surgery (both sides are vulnerable in thyroidectomy).

**Uvula deviation** in CN X lesion: deviates **AWAY from the lesion** (intact side pulls it).

**Vagal stimulation** therapeutically: VNS for refractory epilepsy + depression.
</Clinical>

## CN XI - Accessory nerve

<Figure name="nervous/cranial-nerves-gray697" caption="Spinal accessory nerve (CN XI) - emerges from spinal cord (C1-C5), ascends through foramen magnum, exits via jugular foramen with CN IX + X. Innervates SCM + trapezius." />

**Motor only.** Unique origin: arises from the **upper cervical spinal cord (C1–C5/6)**, ascends through the foramen magnum, joins CN X briefly, then exits via the **jugular foramen**.

Innervates **sternocleidomastoid (SCM)** and **trapezius (upper part)**.

**Tests**:
- SCM: turn head **AWAY** from the muscle being tested against resistance (contraction of left SCM turns head to the right)
- Trapezius: shrug shoulders against resistance

<Clinical>
**CN XI injury** is most common after **posterior triangle of neck surgery** (lymph node biopsy) - the spinal accessory runs through the posterior triangle and is vulnerable. Results in inability to shrug + shoulder drop + winged scapula (lateral winging, vs medial winging from long thoracic n. injury).
</Clinical>

## CN XII - Hypoglossal nerve

<Figure name="nervous/spinal-cord-sectional" caption="Spinal cord sectional anatomy. Dorsal (sensory) + ventral (motor) horns of gray matter; ascending + descending tracts in white matter columns; dorsal + ventral roots with the dorsal root ganglion." />

**Motor only.** Exits medulla via hypoglossal canal. Innervates all intrinsic tongue muscles + most extrinsic (genioglossus, hyoglossus, styloglossus) - **EXCEPT** palatoglossus (CN X).

**Test**: stick out tongue. **Tongue deviates TOWARD the lesion** (the intact genioglossus pushes the tongue toward the weak side).

<Clinical>
**LMN lesion** (e.g., medullary infarct, ALS) → ipsilateral tongue weakness, fasciculations, atrophy → **deviation toward** lesion.

**UMN lesion** (e.g., cortical stroke) → contralateral tongue weakness without atrophy or fasciculations → **deviation away** from the cortical lesion (but TOWARD the weakness, which is contralateral to the lesion).

**Medial medullary syndrome**: anterior spinal artery → ipsilateral CN XII palsy + contralateral hemiparesis + contralateral loss of proprioception/vibration (medial lemniscus).
</Clinical>

## Foramina cheat sheet

<Figure name="skeletal/rib-cage" caption="Related figure for context — Foramina cheat sheet." />

| Foramen | Through it passes |
|---|---|
| **Cribriform plate** | CN I (olfactory filaments); CN 0 (terminal) |
| **Optic canal** | CN II; ophthalmic artery |
| **Superior orbital fissure** | CN III, IV, **V1**, VI; superior ophthalmic vein; sympathetic fibers |
| **Foramen rotundum** | **V2** |

| **Foramen spinosum** | Middle meningeal artery (not a CN, but tested) |

| **Hypoglossal canal** | CN XII |
| **Foramen magnum** | Brainstem → spinal cord transition; spinal portion of CN XI (ascending); vertebral arteries |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX neuro priorities for cranial nerves:**
- **Stroke FAST exam** uses CN VII (face), CN XII (speech via tongue), and motor/sensory.
- **Aspiration risk** post-stroke: assess gag reflex (CN IX/X) and swallow before oral intake.
- **Bell's palsy patient teaching**: protect cornea (artificial tears, eye patch at night - can't close eye), prednisone with food, expect improvement over weeks-months.
- **Trigeminal neuralgia**: minimize triggers (cold air, light touch); carbamazepine teaching - monitor for SJS, hyponatremia, blood dyscrasias.
- **Tongue assessment** after stroke: deviation indicates side of CN XII or contralateral cortex lesion.
</HighYield>

**USMLE Step 1 patterns:**
- **CN nuclei locations**: midbrain (III, IV), pons (V, VI, VII, VIII), medulla (IX, X, XI [classically], XII).
- **Modality (S/M/B)**: **S**ome **S**ay **M**arry **M**oney **B**ut **M**y **B**rother **S**ays **B**ig **B**rains **M**atter **M**ore.
- **Cavernous sinus syndrome**: lateral wall contains CN III, IV, V1, V2 + ICA + sympathetic - multiple CN palsies + Horner from a single lesion.
- **CPA tumor**: CN VIII (hearing/balance) + VII + V → vestibular schwannoma in NF2 is the prototype.
- **Foramina**: **O**l = cribriform, **O**p = optic canal, **III/IV/V1/VI** = SOF, **V2** = rotundum, **V3** = ovale, **VII/VIII** = IAM, **IX/X/XI** = jugular, **XII** = hypoglossal canal.

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Cranial-nerve relevant pharmacology:**
- **Carbamazepine** - first-line for trigeminal neuralgia; rare SJS (HLA-B*1502 in Asians: screen!); aplastic anemia/SIADH/agranulocytosis monitoring.
- **Oxcarbazepine** - fewer enzyme-inducing interactions vs carbamazepine; also for trigeminal neuralgia.
- **Botulinum toxin** - therapeutic uses: blepharospasm, hemifacial spasm, strabismus, cervical dystonia, migraine prophylaxis; mechanism = SNARE cleavage → no ACh release at NMJ.
- **Pilocarpine** - muscarinic agonist; treats dry mouth in Sjögren / post-radiation; closes angle in glaucoma (CN III parasympathetic mimic).
- **Tropicamide / atropine eye drops** - mydriasis for fundoscopy; muscarinic antagonist blocks CN III parasympathetic to pupillary sphincter.
- **Edrophonium** historically used to diagnose myasthenia (short-acting AChE inhibitor); now replaced by anti-AChR antibody testing + ice-pack test.
</HighYield>
