The **autonomic nervous system (ANS)** is the involuntary branch of the PNS that controls smooth muscle, cardiac muscle, and glands. Every organ in your chest, abdomen, and pelvis is dual-innervated by **sympathetic** ("fight or flight") and **parasympathetic** ("rest and digest") fibers that usually have opposing effects - and almost every drug class you'll learn in pharmacology either mimics or blocks one of these two systems.

## Somatic vs autonomic - the big split

| Feature | Somatic | Autonomic |
|---|---|---|
| **Target** | Skeletal muscle | Smooth muscle, cardiac muscle, glands |
| **Control** | Voluntary | Involuntary |
| **Number of neurons in pathway** | 1 (motor neuron → muscle) | 2 (preganglionic + postganglionic, with synapse in a ganglion) |
| **Neurotransmitter at target** | Acetylcholine (nicotinic) | Sympathetic: NE; Parasympathetic: ACh (muscarinic) |
| **Effect on target** | Always **excitatory** | Can excite OR inhibit, depending on receptor |

## Sympathetic vs parasympathetic - at a glance

<Figure name="nervous/autonomic-overview" caption="Autonomic nervous system overview - sympathetic (thoracolumbar T1-L2/L3) and parasympathetic (craniosacral CN III, VII, IX, X + S2-S4). Two-neuron chain: pre-ganglionic (cholinergic) → post-ganglionic (sympathetic mostly noradrenergic; parasympathetic cholinergic on muscarinic)." />

| Feature | Sympathetic | Parasympathetic |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin (CNS)** | **Thoracolumbar** (T1 – L2 lateral horn) | **Craniosacral** (CN III, VII, IX, X + S2–S4) |
| **Preganglionic neuron length** | Short | Long |
| **Postganglionic length** | Long | Short |
| **Ganglion location** | Close to CNS (paravertebral chain or prevertebral) - large, grossly visible | Close to target organ (terminal or intramural) - small, microscopic |
| **Postganglionic NT** | **Norepinephrine** (mostly) - acts on α/β receptors | **Acetylcholine** - acts on muscarinic receptors |
| **Body domain** | All tissues including skin + skeletal muscle vasculature + adrenal medulla + sweat glands | Visceral organs only (no skin, no skeletal muscle vasculature) |
| **Mood** | Mobilizes for emergency | Conserves and restores |

## Sympathetic pathway - step by step

The classic "ramus communicans" cascade:

1. **Cell body** of preganglionic neuron in **lateral horn of T1–L2 spinal cord**.
2. Axon leaves via the **ventral root** of the spinal nerve.
3. Joins the **spinal nerve** briefly.
4. Exits via the **white ramus communicans** (myelinated → white) into the **sympathetic chain (paravertebral) ganglion**.
5. Inside the ganglion, the neuron has **three choices**:
   - **a. Synapse here** → postganglionic neuron returns to the spinal nerve via the **gray ramus communicans** (unmyelinated) and travels to skin/blood vessels/sweat glands.
   - **b. Continue up or down the chain** to a different chain ganglion, then synapse there.
   - **c. Pass through without synapsing** and continue medially as a **splanchnic nerve** to a **prevertebral ganglion** (celiac, superior mesenteric, inferior mesenteric, aorticorenal).

The splanchnic-nerve path supplies the **abdominal viscera**. The chain-ganglion path supplies **everything else** (head, neck, thorax, body wall, skin, blood vessels, sweat glands).

<Mnemonic>
**Gray-Whitten rule for rami**: **Wh**ite = goes **In** to the chain (myelinated preganglionic); **G**ray = **G**oing **out** of the chain (unmyelinated postganglionic). Whites only exist at T1–L2; gray rami exist at every spinal level (because all spinal nerves need sympathetic supply for vessels + sweat glands).
</Mnemonic>

## Parasympathetic pathway

<Figure name="nervous/parasympathetic-connections" caption="Parasympathetic nervous system connections (OpenStax). Craniosacral outflow: CN III, VII, IX, X (vagus innervates most thoracic + abdominal viscera) + S2-S4 to pelvic viscera. Long preganglionic, short postganglionic, both cholinergic." />

Two origins:

### Cranial parasympathetic (CN III, VII, IX, X)

| CN | Preganglionic from | Ganglion | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CN III (oculomotor)** | Edinger-Westphal nucleus | **Ciliary** | Pupillary sphincter + ciliary muscle (lens accommodation) |
| **CN VII (facial)** | Superior salivatory nucleus | **Pterygopalatine** (via greater petrosal n.) | Lacrimal gland, nasal + palatine mucosa |
| **CN VII (facial)** | Superior salivatory nucleus | **Submandibular** (via chorda tympani) | Submandibular + sublingual salivary glands |
| **CN IX (glossopharyngeal)** | Inferior salivatory nucleus | **Otic** (via lesser petrosal n.) | Parotid gland |
| **CN X (vagus)** | Dorsal motor nucleus of vagus + nucleus ambiguus | **Intramural** (in organ walls) | All thoracic + abdominal viscera **down to the splenic flexure of the colon** |

### Sacral parasympathetic (S2–S4)

Through **pelvic splanchnic nerves** to intramural ganglia in **hindgut** (distal to splenic flexure), urinary tract, and reproductive organs.

<Note title="Splanchnic nerves - a confusing word">
"Splanchnic" just means "visceral." But the term is used for both directions:
- **Thoracic, lumbar, and sacral splanchnic nerves** = **sympathetic** preganglionic fibers heading to prevertebral ganglia (except sacral splanchnics are sympathetic post-ganglionic, weird exception).
- **Pelvic splanchnic nerves** = **parasympathetic** preganglionic fibers from S2–S4.

A pelvic splanchnic is the **only parasympathetic splanchnic**. Everyone else is sympathetic.
</Note>

## Vagus nerve - the parasympathetic workhorse

The vagus (CN X) provides parasympathetic innervation to **almost everything from the neck to the splenic flexure**:
- Pharynx, larynx (motor + sensation)
- Heart (↓ rate, ↓ AV conduction)
- Bronchial smooth muscle (constriction)
- GI tract from esophagus through transverse colon (motility, secretion)
- Liver, pancreas, gallbladder

The vagus continues with the esophagus through the diaphragm into the abdomen as the **anterior + posterior esophageal trunks**, then forms the **anterior + posterior esophageal plexuses**. Mnemonic for which goes where: **"Left vagus is not left behind - it's anterior."** (Left vagus rotates anteriorly during gut rotation; right rotates posteriorly.)

## ANS effects on every organ - function table

| Organ | Sympathetic (NE on receptor) | Parasympathetic (ACh on M) |
|---|---|---|
| **Eye - iris** | Dilates pupil (α1, **mydriasis**) | Constricts pupil (M3, **miosis**) |
| **Eye - lens** | Flattens (far vision; minor indirect) | Rounds (near vision, M3) |
| **Salivary glands** | Thick, scanty secretion (α1); decreased blood flow → dry mouth | Watery, copious secretion (M3) |
| **Lungs - bronchi** | Dilates (β2) | Constricts (M3); ↑ mucus |
| **Heart - SA node** | ↑ rate (β1) | ↓ rate (M2) |
| **Heart - myocardium** | ↑ contractility (β1) | Minimal direct effect on ventricles |
| **Heart - AV node** | ↑ conduction (β1) | ↓ conduction (M2) - basis of vagal maneuvers for SVT |
| **GI - motility/secretion** | ↓ (α2, β2) | ↑ (M3) |
| **GI sphincters** | Contract | Relax |
| **Liver** | Glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis (β2, α1) | Glycogen synthesis (M3) |
| **Pancreas - exocrine** | ↓ secretion (α) | ↑ secretion (M3) |
| **Pancreas - endocrine** | ↓ insulin (α2); ↑ glucagon (β2) | ↑ insulin (M3) |
| **Bladder - detrusor** | Relaxes (β3) | Contracts (M3) → micturition |
| **Bladder - internal sphincter** | Contracts (α1) → continence | Relaxes |
| **Sex organs - male** | Ejaculation (α1) | Erection (M3, NO release) |
| **Sex organs - female** | Orgasm | Engorgement, lubrication |
| **Skin - sweat glands** | **Sympathetic cholinergic** (M3) - atypical! | No innervation |
| **Skin - arrector pili** | Contracts (α1) → piloerection | No innervation |
| **Skin/skeletal muscle vasculature** | Vasoconstriction (α1) in skin; vasodilation in skeletal muscle (β2) | No innervation (most beds) |
| **Adrenal medulla** | **Preganglionic sympathetic directly innervates** chromaffin cells (= postganglionic-equivalent), causing EPI + NE release | No innervation |
| **Kidney - afferent arteriole** | Vasoconstriction (α1); renin release (β1) | No significant innervation |

<Mnemonic>
**"Point and shoot"** - Parasympathetic = erection (point); Sympathetic = ejaculation (shoot).
</Mnemonic>

<Mnemonic>
**Anticholinergic toxidrome** (e.g., diphenhydramine, atropine overdose): "**Blind as a bat** (mydriasis + cycloplegia), **mad as a hatter** (delirium), **dry as a bone** (anhidrosis + dry mouth), **hot as a hare** (hyperthermia - can't sweat), **red as a beet** (cutaneous vasodilation), **full as a flask** (urinary retention)."
</Mnemonic>

## ANS receptors - the pharmacology backbone

| Receptor | Where | Action | Drug examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **α1** | Vascular smooth muscle, iris dilator, bladder neck | Vasoconstriction, mydriasis, sphincter contraction | Phenylephrine (agonist); prazosin, tamsulosin, doxazosin (antagonists for HTN, BPH) |
| **α2** | Presynaptic in CNS; pancreatic β cells | Feedback inhibition of NT release | Clonidine, methyldopa (agonists for HTN, opioid withdrawal) |
| **β1** | Heart, kidney JG cells | ↑ HR, ↑ contractility, ↑ renin | Dobutamine (agonist for cardiogenic shock); metoprolol, atenolol, bisoprolol (antagonists) |
| **β2** | Bronchial + skeletal-muscle-vascular smooth muscle, uterus, liver | Bronchodilation, vasodilation, tocolysis, glycogenolysis | Albuterol, salmeterol (asthma); terbutaline (tocolytic); propranolol (non-selective β-blocker) |
| **β3** | Adipose, detrusor | Lipolysis, detrusor relaxation | Mirabegron (overactive bladder) |
| **M1** | CNS, gastric parietal (minor) | Memory, motor control | Atropine, scopolamine (antagonists) |
| **M2** | Heart | ↓ HR, ↓ AV conduction | Atropine reverses bradycardia |
| **M3** | Smooth muscle, glands, eye | Smooth muscle contraction, glandular secretion, miosis | Pilocarpine (agonist for glaucoma, Sjögren); ipratropium, tiotropium (antagonists for COPD); atropine, oxybutynin |
| **Nicotinic (NN)** | Autonomic ganglia | Postganglionic depolarization | Nicotine; hexamethonium (historical) |
| **Nicotinic (NM)** | NMJ | Skeletal muscle contraction | Succinylcholine (depolarizing); rocuronium, vecuronium (non-depolarizing) |

## Every ANS structure - function reference

| Structure | What it does | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Lateral horn of spinal cord (T1–L2)** | Origin of all sympathetic preganglionic neurons | Damage above this level (cervical SCI) → loss of sympathetic tone, hypotension, neurogenic shock |
| **Edinger-Westphal nucleus** | Parasympathetic to pupil + lens (via CN III) | Targeted by ↑ ICP → pupillary dilation (uncal herniation) |
| **Sympathetic chain ganglia** | Paravertebral ganglia for synapse + chain transit | Damage → Horner syndrome (miosis, ptosis, anhidrosis) if cervical |
| **White ramus communicans** | Myelinated preganglionic into chain | T1–L2 only |
| **Gray ramus communicans** | Unmyelinated postganglionic out of chain | At every spinal level |
| **Splanchnic nerves (thoracic/lumbar)** | Preganglionic sympathetic to prevertebral ganglia | Carry visceral pain afferents back too |
| **Celiac ganglion** | Prevertebral; supplies foregut | Foregut visceral pain referred to epigastrium |
| **Superior mesenteric ganglion** | Prevertebral; supplies midgut | Midgut pain referred to periumbilical |
| **Inferior mesenteric ganglion** | Prevertebral; supplies hindgut | Hindgut pain referred to hypogastrium |
| **Pelvic splanchnic nerves (S2–S4)** | Parasympathetic to hindgut + pelvic viscera | Erection, defecation, micturition reflexes |
| **Vagus nerve (CN X)** | Major parasympathetic conduit | Pharynx, larynx, thoracic + abdominal viscera to splenic flexure |
| **Intramural ganglia** | Parasympathetic ganglia in organ walls | Short postganglionic neurons innervate the organ they sit in |
| **Adrenal medulla** | Modified sympathetic ganglion - chromaffin cells | Releases EPI (80%) + NE (20%) directly into blood when stimulated by preganglionic ACh |
| **Carotid body** | Chemoreceptor at carotid bifurcation | Senses PO₂, PCO₂, pH; afferent via CN IX |
| **Carotid sinus** | Baroreceptor at internal carotid origin | Senses arterial stretch; afferent via CN IX |
| **Aortic body + arch** | Chemo + baroreceptor | Afferent via CN X |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="step1">
**ANS clinical correlates:**
- **Horner syndrome** (miosis, ptosis, anhidrosis) = lesion of sympathetic chain. Look for Pancoast tumor at lung apex, carotid dissection, brainstem stroke.
- **Adie tonic pupil** = damaged ciliary ganglion → mid-dilated pupil with poor light reaction.
- **Argyll Robertson pupil** ("prostitute's pupil") = accommodates but doesn't react to light; classic for neurosyphilis.
- **Cervical SCI** above T1 → loss of sympathetic outflow → **neurogenic shock** (hypotension WITHOUT tachycardia, because vagal tone is unopposed). Distinguishes from hypovolemic shock.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Receptor-targeting drugs by category:**
- **α1 antagonists** for BPH: tamsulosin (selective for α1A in prostate; less BP drop), doxazosin/terazosin (also lower BP - useful in HTN + BPH overlap).
- **β-blockers**: cardioselective (β1) - metoprolol, atenolol, bisoprolol - preferred in HF + post-MI + asthma comorbid. Non-selective - propranolol, nadolol - useful for performance anxiety, tremor, portal HTN.
- **Anticholinergics**: ipratropium/tiotropium (COPD); oxybutynin/tolterodine (overactive bladder); scopolamine (motion sickness); atropine (bradycardia, organophosphate poisoning).
- **Cholinesterase inhibitors**: physostigmine (crosses BBB - antidote for anticholinergic toxicity); neostigmine, pyridostigmine (don't cross BBB - for myasthenia, post-op ileus).
</HighYield>
