Every behavior - thought, movement, autonomic regulation, perception - is built from synapses using one of about a dozen **neurotransmitters** and a handful of stereotyped **spinal reflexes**. Understanding what each NT does + where each reflex sits is the basis for every neuro/psych drug class and every clinical localization.

## The ten major neurotransmitters

| NT | Synthesized from | Receptor type | Main location | Disease association |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Glutamate** | Glutamine (glia recycle) | NMDA, AMPA, kainate (ionotropic); mGluR (metabotropic) | Most excitatory in CNS | Stroke (excitotoxicity), epilepsy |
| **GABA** | Glutamate (via GAD) | GABA-A (Cl⁻ in - ionotropic); GABA-B (Gi - metabotropic) | Major inhibitory in CNS | Anxiety (↓ GABA); benzo + barbiturate site |
| **Glycine** | Serine | Glycine receptor (Cl⁻) | Inhibitory in spinal cord + brainstem | Tetanus (toxin blocks release) → muscle spasm |
| **ACh** | Choline + acetyl-CoA (ChAT) | Nicotinic (ionotropic); Muscarinic M1-5 (Gq, Gi, Gq, Gi, Gq) | NMJ, autonomic, basal forebrain → cortex | Alzheimer (↓ basal forebrain ACh); MG; cholinergic crisis |
| **Norepinephrine** | Tyrosine → DOPA → dopamine → NE | α1, α2 (Gq, Gi); β1, β2, β3 (Gs) | Locus coeruleus → cortex; sympathetic postganglionic | Depression, anxiety, PTSD |
| **Dopamine** | Tyrosine → DOPA → dopamine | D1, D5 (Gs); D2, D3, D4 (Gi) | Substantia nigra → striatum (motor); VTA → cortex/limbic (reward) | Parkinson (↓ nigrostriatal); schizophrenia (↑ mesolimbic) |
| **Serotonin (5-HT)** | Tryptophan | 7 families (5HT1-7); mostly metabotropic except 5HT3 (ionotropic) | Raphe nuclei → diffuse projections; gut (90% of body's 5-HT) | Depression, anxiety, migraine, carcinoid |
| **Histamine** | Histidine | H1 (Gq), H2 (Gs), H3 (Gi), H4 (Gi) | Tuberomammillary nucleus → cortex (wakefulness); mast cells (peripheral) | Allergy; H1 sedation; H2 acid |
| **Substance P** | Peptide | NK1 (Gq) | Pain afferents (C fibers); enteric | Chronic pain; aprepitant for chemo nausea (NK1 block) |
| **Endogenous opioids** (endorphin, enkephalin, dynorphin) | Peptides | μ, δ, κ (Gi) | Pain modulation; reward | Addiction; analgesia |

### Mechanism + clock speed
| Type | Speed | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Ionotropic (ligand-gated channel)** | Milliseconds | NT binds → channel opens → ion flux → direct PSP |
| **Metabotropic (G-protein coupled)** | 100s ms - seconds | NT binds → G-protein → cascade → indirect channel modulation, second messengers, gene expression |

Almost all psychiatric drugs target metabotropic systems (slow); anesthetics + benzos hit ionotropic.

## Excitatory vs inhibitory at a glance

| Excitatory | Inhibitory |
|---|---|
| Glutamate (most CNS) | GABA (most CNS) |
| ACh (NMJ, autonomic ganglia, some CNS) | Glycine (spinal cord + brainstem) |
| NE, dopamine, 5-HT, histamine, substance P (varies by receptor) | Dopamine D2; some 5-HT subtypes; α2 adrenergic (presynaptic) |

The same NT can be excitatory at one receptor and inhibitory at another - the receptor determines the sign.

<Mnemonic>
**"Big NT = small synapse"** - small-molecule NTs (glutamate, GABA, ACh, monoamines) act at classical synapses with fast effects. Peptide NTs (substance P, opioids) are co-released, act on slower metabotropic receptors, often modulate other transmission.
</Mnemonic>

## Synaptic mechanics - life of a vesicle

<Figure name="nervous/synapse" caption="Chemical synapse - vesicles loaded with neurotransmitter dock at the active zone; depolarization opens VGCCs; Ca²⁺ entry triggers vesicle fusion via SNAREs (synaptobrevin, syntaxin, SNAP-25)." />

1. AP arrives at presynaptic terminal → depolarizes membrane.
2. **Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels** open → Ca²⁺ enters.
3. Ca²⁺ binds **synaptotagmin** → triggers **SNARE complex** (syntaxin + SNAP-25 + synaptobrevin) → vesicle fuses with presynaptic membrane.
4. NT released into cleft (~20 nm wide).
5. NT binds receptors on postsynaptic membrane → channel opens / G-protein activated.
6. NT cleared from cleft by: **reuptake** (transporters), **enzymatic degradation** (AChE, MAO, COMT), or **diffusion**.

### Disrupting each step - toxins + drugs

| Step | Disrupted by | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Ca²⁺ entry | **Lambert-Eaton** antibodies to VGCC | ↓ NT release → muscle weakness improves with use |
| SNARE complex | **Botulinum toxin** (cleaves SNAREs) | Flaccid paralysis (used cosmetically) |
| SNARE in inhibitory neurons | **Tetanus toxin** | Spastic paralysis (blocks inhibitory release in cord) |
| NT receptor (post) | **Myasthenia gravis** (anti-AChR) | Fluctuating muscle weakness, worse with use |
| AChE | **Organophosphates, carbamates** | Cholinergic crisis (SLUDGE-M) |
| Dopamine D2 receptor | **Antipsychotics** | Extrapyramidal effects, hyperprolactinemia |
| Serotonin reuptake | **SSRIs** | ↑ 5-HT in cleft → mood elevation |
| NE + 5HT reuptake | **SNRIs, TCAs** | Same but broader |
| MAO | **MAOIs** | Boost monoamines; hypertensive crisis with tyramine |

## Spinal cord reflexes - six common patterns

<Figure name="nervous/patellar-reflex-arc" caption="Patellar tendon reflex arc - tap stretches the quadriceps tendon → muscle spindle (Ia afferent) → monosynaptic excitation of α-motor neuron → quadriceps contracts. Reciprocal inhibition relaxes hamstrings (L2-L4)." />

<Figure name="nervous/reflex-arc" caption="Reflex arc - sensory afferent → spinal interneuron (in polysynaptic reflexes) → motor efferent. Monosynaptic reflexes like the patellar tap skip the interneuron." />

| Reflex | Neuron count | Test/example | Lesion localizes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stretch (monosynaptic)** | 1 synapse (Ia → α-motor) | Patellar (L3-L4), Achilles (S1-S2), biceps (C5-C6), triceps (C7-C8) | Tests a specific root |
| **Inverse stretch (Golgi)** | 2 synapses (Ib → inhibitory → α-motor) | Tendon protection - relaxes muscle under high load | Loss → tendon ruptures |
| **Withdrawal (flexor)** | Polysynaptic | Step on tack → flex hip/knee on that side | Spinothalamic + dorsal horn integrity |
| **Crossed extensor** | Polysynaptic | Step on tack → extend opposite leg (so you don't fall) | Bilateral spinal coordination |
| **Babinski** | Polysynaptic | Stroking sole → great toe **dorsiflexes** in pathology (normally plantarflexes after age 2) | **UMN lesion** above the level tested |
| **Clonus** | Pathologic | Sustained DF of ankle → rhythmic beating | UMN lesion |

### Upper vs lower motor neuron lesion signs

| Sign | UMN lesion | LMN lesion |
|---|---|---|
| **Reflexes** | Hyperreflexia | Hyporeflexia / areflexia |
| **Tone** | Spasticity, clasp-knife | Flaccid |
| **Babinski** | Present (positive) | Absent |
| **Fasciculations** | No | Yes |
| **Atrophy** | Late (disuse) | Early + marked (denervation) |
| **Weakness pattern** | Pyramidal (extensors of upper limb, flexors of lower) | Specific muscles supplied by injured neuron |

**ALS** is the classic disease with **BOTH** UMN + LMN signs (mixed). MG, GBS = pure LMN. Stroke, MS, cervical myelopathy = UMN.

## Spinal cord tracts - key columns

| Tract | Function | Decussation | Lesion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lateral corticospinal** | Voluntary motor (UMN to LMN) | Medullary pyramids | Contralateral UMN signs below lesion |
| **Anterior corticospinal** | Axial/proximal motor | At spinal level | Minor |
| **Dorsal column–medial lemniscus** | Vibration, proprioception, fine touch | Medulla (after gracilis/cuneatus) | Ipsilateral loss below; loss of vibration, position |
| **Spinothalamic** | Pain + temperature + crude touch | At spinal level (2-3 segments above entry) | Contralateral pain/temp loss 2+ levels below |
| **Spinocerebellar** | Proprioception to cerebellum | Doesn't decussate (or doubles) | Ipsilateral ataxia |
| **Reticulospinal** | Posture + locomotion automatism | - | Variable |
| **Rubrospinal** | Distal flexor tone (more prominent in animals) | - | Subtle |
| **Vestibulospinal** | Balance via lateral vestibular nucleus | - | Extensor tone |

<Clinical>
**Brown-Séquard syndrome** (hemisection):
- Ipsilateral UMN signs below the lesion (corticospinal)
- Ipsilateral DCML loss below (vibration + proprio + fine touch)
- Contralateral spinothalamic loss starting 1-2 levels below
- Ipsilateral LMN signs at the level (segmental gray matter)

**ASA infarct** (anterior 2/3 cord): bilateral motor + spinothalamic loss; **DCML preserved** (PCAs supply the posterior cord).

**Tabes dorsalis** (syphilis, late): demyelination of dorsal columns → loss of vibration + proprioception → wide-based gait, Romberg positive, Argyll Robertson pupils.

**Friedreich ataxia**: triple-repeat (GAA in frataxin). Hits spinocerebellar + DCML + corticospinal → ataxia + areflexia + dysarthria + cardiomyopathy + diabetes; ~age 10-15.

**Syringomyelia**: central cord cavity → first hits the crossing spinothalamic fibers (ventral white commissure) → "cape distribution" loss of pain/temp + preserved touch.
</Clinical>

## Reflex arcs in clinical use

| Reflex | Tests | Root |
|---|---|---|
| **Biceps** | Musculocutaneous + C5/C6 | C5 |
| **Brachioradialis** | Radial + C5/C6 | C6 |
| **Triceps** | Radial + C7 | C7 |
| **Patellar** | Femoral + L3/L4 | L4 |
| **Achilles** | Tibial + S1/S2 | S1 |
| **Plantar (Babinski)** | Tibial + S1 (and UMN above) | UMN integrity |
| **Cremasteric** | Inner thigh stroke → testis lifts | L1/L2 |
| **Anal wink** | Perianal stroke → external sphincter contracts | S2-S4 |
| **Bulbocavernosus** | Squeeze glans → anal sphincter contracts | S2-S4 |

The last three are spinal-cord function tests after trauma - loss = spinal shock or cauda equina.

## Function reference - every key player

| Component | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **NMDA receptor** | Coincidence detector (needs glutamate + depolarization + glycine); Ca²⁺ permeable | Substrate of LTP/memory; target of ketamine, memantine, alcohol |
| **AMPA receptor** | Fast Na⁺ excitatory glutamate | Stroke excitotoxicity |
| **GABA-A** | Cl⁻ channel; pentameric | Site of benzos (↑ frequency of opening) + barbiturates (↑ duration) + alcohol + propofol + volatile anesthetics |
| **GABA-B** | Gi-coupled; baclofen target | Spasticity (baclofen) |
| **VGCC (P/Q-type)** | NT release at NMJ + most CNS synapses | Lambert-Eaton antibodies; gabapentin/pregabalin (α2δ subunit) |
| **SNARE complex** | Vesicle fusion machinery | Botulinum/tetanus toxin substrate |
| **VMAT** | Loads monoamines into vesicles | Reserpine, tetrabenazine block (chorea, HTN) |
| **MAO-A** | NE + 5-HT + tyramine degradation | MAO-A inhibitors → tyramine crisis with cheese, wine |
| **MAO-B** | Dopamine degradation predominantly | Selegiline (Parkinson) |
| **COMT** | Catecholamine methylation | Entacapone for Parkinson |
| **AChE** | ACh hydrolysis | Pyridostigmine (MG), donepezil (Alzheimer), organophosphate poisoning |
| **DAT/SERT/NET** | Reuptake transporters | Cocaine blocks all three; SSRIs/SNRIs/bupropion (DA + NE) targets |
| **Substantia nigra pars compacta** | Dopaminergic projection to striatum | Lost in Parkinson |
| **Locus coeruleus** | Major NE source for forebrain | ↑ in arousal/anxiety/PTSD; α2 agonists (clonidine, guanfacine) reduce |
| **Raphe nuclei** | Major 5-HT source | Depression, sleep |
| **Basal nucleus of Meynert** | Major cortical ACh source | Atrophies in Alzheimer |
| **Tuberomammillary nucleus** | Cortical histamine (wakefulness) | H1 blockers cause sedation |

## Why this matters

<HighYield variant="boards">
- **Parkinson disease** = ↓ nigrostriatal dopamine. Triad: resting tremor, bradykinesia, rigidity. Treat: levodopa + carbidopa (peripheral DOPA decarboxylase inhibitor), DA agonists, MAO-B inhibitors, COMT inhibitors. Late: surgical DBS.
- **Schizophrenia** = ↑ mesolimbic D2 (positive symptoms), ↓ mesocortical D2 (negative symptoms). Atypical antipsychotics (D2 + 5HT2A) preferred for fewer EPS.
- **Alzheimer** = ↓ basal nucleus ACh → AChE inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine) + NMDA antagonist (memantine).
- **Depression** = ↓ monoamines (mainly 5-HT + NE). SSRIs first-line (sertraline, escitalopram). SNRI (venlafaxine, duloxetine) for comorbid pain. Bupropion (NE + DA reuptake) for low energy / no sexual side effects.
- **Anxiety**: short term - benzos; long term - SSRIs/SNRIs. Buspirone (5HT1A partial agonist) - non-addictive but slow.
- **Migraine**: triptans = 5HT1B/1D agonists → vasoconstrict + inhibit substance P. CGRP antagonists (-gepants, monoclonals) for prevention.
- **Epilepsy**: most AEDs act on Na⁺ channels (phenytoin, carbamazepine), Ca²⁺ channels (ethosuximide T-type), or GABA (benzos, phenobarbital, valproate).
- **GABA cocktail**: benzos and barbiturates both work at GABA-A but mechanistically distinct - benzos ↑ frequency of channel openings (no overdose without alcohol), barbiturates ↑ duration of openings (lethal alone).
- **Excitotoxicity**: stroke, brain trauma → glutamate spillage → NMDA overactivation → Ca²⁺ flood → cell death. Memantine in Alzheimer's modulates this.
- **Restless legs**: low iron + dopamine dysfunction. Treat: iron, DA agonists (pramipexole, ropinirole), α2δ ligands (gabapentin enacarbil).
</HighYield>
