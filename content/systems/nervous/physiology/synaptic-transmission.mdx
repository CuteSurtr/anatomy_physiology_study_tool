A neuron talks to another cell (neuron, muscle, gland) at a **synapse**. Two kinds exist: **chemical** (most synapses, slow, modifiable, the basis of learning) and **electrical** (gap junctions, fast, used for synchrony in the retina, heart, smooth muscle, and inferior olive). This page covers the chemical synapse, which underlies essentially all signaling in the CNS and most of the PNS.

## The chemical synapse, step by step

1. **Action potential arrives** at the presynaptic terminal.
2. **Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open**, Ca²⁺ enters - 100× higher gradient than the Na⁺ gradient.
3. **Vesicle fusion** - Ca²⁺ binds synaptotagmin, which triggers the SNARE complex (synaptobrevin, syntaxin, SNAP-25) to fuse vesicle with active zone.
4. **Neurotransmitter (NT) is released** into the cleft - typically a quantum per vesicle (~5,000 molecules).
5. **NT diffuses 20 nm**, binds postsynaptic receptors.
6. **Receptor activation**:
   - **Ionotropic** (ligand-gated ion channel) → fast (ms), brief, opens specific ion channel
   - **Metabotropic** (GPCR) → slow (10s of ms to seconds), uses second messengers, modulates many downstream channels
7. **NT is cleared** from the cleft by:
   - **Reuptake** by transporters (SERT for serotonin, NET for NE, DAT for dopamine, GAT for GABA, EAAT for glutamate)
   - **Enzymatic degradation** (AChE for ACh, MAO and COMT for monoamines)
   - **Diffusion** away from the cleft

## Postsynaptic potentials

The receptor's effect determines whether the postsynaptic cell becomes more or less likely to fire:

| Type | Effect | Ion movement | Example neurotransmitter |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EPSP** - excitatory postsynaptic potential | Depolarization | Na⁺ in (and K⁺ out) via AMPA/NMDA/nAChR | Glutamate, ACh (nicotinic) |
| **IPSP** - inhibitory postsynaptic potential | Hyperpolarization | Cl⁻ in (GABA-A) or K⁺ out (GABA-B) | GABA, glycine |

Postsynaptic potentials are **graded** (size depends on amount of NT) and **decremental** (decay with distance from the synapse). They sum at the axon hillock - the trigger zone where, if threshold is reached, an action potential is generated.

## Summation - the integration rule

A single EPSP rarely reaches threshold. Neurons integrate hundreds to thousands of synaptic inputs across space and time:

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Temporal summation** | Repeated EPSPs from the *same* presynaptic neuron arrive faster than the membrane can repolarize → potentials stack |
| **Spatial summation** | EPSPs from *different* synapses on the same postsynaptic neuron coincide → potentials add |

EPSPs and IPSPs can also cancel each other - a single inhibitory synapse near the axon hillock can veto thousands of distant EPSPs. This is how **inhibitory interneurons** sharpen signals.

|---|---|---|---|
| **Glutamate** | Amino acid | AMPA, NMDA, kainate (ionotropic); mGluR (metabotropic) | Principal CNS excitatory NT; learning, memory, sensation |
| **GABA** | Amino acid | GABA-A (Cl⁻ channel, ionotropic), GABA-B (K⁺ channel via G protein) | Principal CNS inhibitory NT; sedatives target GABA-A |
| **Glycine** | Amino acid | Glycine receptor (Cl⁻ channel) | Inhibitory NT in spinal cord and brainstem; strychnine blocks |
| **ACh** | Cholinergic | Nicotinic (ionotropic, Na⁺ + K⁺); Muscarinic (M1-M5, GPCR) | Neuromuscular junction; autonomic ganglia; parasympathetic effectors; CNS arousal + memory |
| **Norepinephrine** | Monoamine | α₁, α₂, β₁, β₂, β₃ (all GPCR) | Sympathetic effectors; CNS arousal, attention, mood |
| **Dopamine** | Monoamine | D1, D2 families (GPCR) | Reward, motor control (nigrostriatal), prolactin inhibition, executive function |
| **Serotonin (5-HT)** | Monoamine | 5-HT1-7 families (mostly GPCR; 5-HT3 is ionotropic) | Mood, sleep, appetite, GI motility, vasoconstriction |
| **Histamine** | Monoamine | H1, H2, H3 (GPCR) | Wakefulness, allergic response, gastric acid |
| **Endorphins / enkephalins** | Peptide | μ, δ, κ opioid receptors (GPCR) | Pain modulation, reward |
| **Substance P** | Peptide | NK1 receptor (GPCR) | Pain, vomiting |
| **Nitric oxide (NO)** | Gas | Diffuses into cell, activates guanylyl cyclase | Retrograde signaling, vasodilation |
| **Endocannabinoids** | Lipid | CB1, CB2 (GPCR) | Retrograde modulator, appetite, pain, mood |

<Mnemonic title="Mood disorders and neurotransmitters">
- **Depression** → low NE / 5-HT / dopamine (monoamine hypothesis). Treated with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, TCAs - all of which raise synaptic monoamines.
- **Anxiety** → low GABA. Treated with benzodiazepines and other GABA-A potentiators.
- **Schizophrenia** → ↑ mesolimbic dopamine (positive symptoms) + ↓ mesocortical dopamine (negative symptoms). Treated with D2 antagonists.
- **Parkinson disease** → loss of nigrostriatal dopamine. Treated with L-DOPA + decarboxylase inhibitor.
- **Alzheimer disease** → ↓ ACh (basal nucleus of Meynert). Treated with AChE inhibitors.
- **Huntington disease** → ↓ GABA + ACh, ↑ dopamine in striatum.
</Mnemonic>

## Drug action at the synapse - canonical targets

| Step | Drug class | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Presynaptic Ca²⁺ influx | Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ blockers (presynaptic) | Gabapentin, pregabalin (α₂δ subunit) |
| NT synthesis | Substrate / cofactor | L-DOPA → dopamine |
| Vesicle loading | VMAT2 inhibitor | Reserpine, tetrabenazine |
| Release | Botulinum toxin (cleaves SNAREs) | Botox - paralysis |
| Release | Black widow toxin | Massive vesicle release |
| Receptor agonist | Direct activation | Morphine (μ opioid), benzodiazepines (GABA-A allosteric) |
| Receptor antagonist | Block receptor | Naloxone (opioid), flumazenil (BZD), antipsychotics (D2) |
| Reuptake | Block transporter | SSRIs (SERT), SNRIs, TCAs, cocaine (DAT + SERT + NET), amphetamine (reverse DAT) |
| Enzymatic breakdown | Block enzyme | AChE inhibitors (donepezil, neostigmine, organophosphates); MAOIs |

## Long-term potentiation (LTP) - synaptic learning

The synapse is plastic. The same input can produce a larger postsynaptic response after a brief high-frequency stimulus - this is **LTP**, the cellular basis of learning and memory.

**Mechanism at glutamatergic synapses (hippocampus, cortex):**
1. High-frequency stimulation depolarizes the postsynaptic membrane.
2. Depolarization expels Mg²⁺ from the NMDA receptor pore.
3. With glutamate bound + depolarization, NMDA opens → Ca²⁺ influx.
4. Ca²⁺ activates CaMKII → phosphorylates AMPA receptors and inserts more into the membrane.
5. Same glutamate input now produces a larger EPSP - the synapse has "remembered."

The opposite process - **long-term depression (LTD)** - weakens synapses with low-frequency, weakly correlated activity. Together LTP and LTD are the cellular substrate of Hebbian learning ("neurons that fire together, wire together").

<Clinical>
**Why NMDA matters clinically:**
- **Ketamine** is a noncompetitive NMDA antagonist - rapid-onset antidepressant + dissociative anesthetic.
- **Memantine** is a low-affinity NMDA antagonist - used in moderate-severe Alzheimer to dampen glutamate excitotoxicity.
- **PCP / dextromethorphan** are also NMDA antagonists at high doses → dissociation, psychosis.
- **Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis** (Dalmau syndrome) → antibodies against NMDA receptors → psychiatric symptoms, seizures, autonomic instability. Often paraneoplastic (ovarian teratoma in young women).
</Clinical>

## Electrical synapses (gap junctions)

A small minority of synapses use **connexin hemichannels** that align to form **gap junctions** - direct cytoplasmic continuity between cells. Properties:
- **Fast** - no synaptic delay (no NT release/diffusion)
- **Bidirectional** - usually
- **Synchronizing** - propagates depolarization simultaneously to many cells
- **Found in** - cardiac and smooth muscle, retina (horizontal + amacrine cells), inferior olive, parts of the cortex during development
