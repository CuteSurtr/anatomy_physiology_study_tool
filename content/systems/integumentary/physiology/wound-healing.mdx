Wound healing follows four overlapping stages - **hemostasis → inflammation → proliferation → remodeling** - over hours to years. The mechanism is the same for skin, gut, vessels, or any tissue. Knowing the timeline + the cells involved explains every chronic-wound condition, every scar variant, and every surgical scar prediction.

## Four phases - timeline + key events

<Figure name="integumentary/wound-healing-phases" caption="The four phases of wound healing - hemostasis (minutes), inflammation (days 1-3), proliferation/granulation (days 3-21), remodeling (3 wk - 2 yr). Different cell populations + cytokines dominate each phase." />

| Phase | Time | Key events |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Hemostasis** | Minutes | Vasoconstriction → platelet plug → coagulation cascade → fibrin mesh + clot |
| **2. Inflammatory** | Hours to 3 days | Neutrophils (peak ~24-48 h); macrophages arrive day 2-3; phagocytose debris + release growth factors |
| **3. Proliferative** | Day 3 to ~3 weeks | Granulation tissue (fibroblasts + capillaries + macrophages); re-epithelialization; collagen III deposition; wound contraction (myofibroblasts) |
| **4. Remodeling (maturation)** | 3 weeks to >1 year | Collagen III → I conversion; ↑ tensile strength; scar fades; max ~80% of original strength |

## Hemostasis (minutes)

<Figure name="integumentary/healing" caption="Sequential view of cutaneous wound healing - hemostasis (platelet plug + fibrin mesh), then inflammatory and proliferative phases (granulation tissue, re-epithelialization), then remodeling to restore the epidermis." />

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Vasoconstriction** | Brief; mediated by neural reflex + endothelin-1 |
| **Platelet adhesion** | vWF bridges exposed collagen + GP1b on platelet |
| **Platelet activation** | TXA2, ADP, serotonin released; shape change; activation of GP2b/3a |
| **Platelet aggregation** | GP2b/3a + fibrinogen → platelet plug |
| **Coagulation cascade** | Tissue factor exposed → extrinsic pathway → thrombin → fibrin → cross-linked mesh |

The platelet plug + fibrin = **primary clot**; growth factors (PDGF, TGF-β, EGF, VEGF) released from platelet α-granules direct the next phases.

## Inflammatory phase (hours-days)

| Cell | Arrival | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Neutrophils** | 6-12 h, peak 1-2 d | First responders; phagocytose bacteria + debris; oxidative burst |
| **Macrophages** | Day 2-3, dominant day 3-7 | Phagocytose dead cells + debris; **central orchestrators** - release PDGF, TGF-β, IL-1, TNF, VEGF, FGF; switch M1 (inflammatory) → M2 (healing) |
| **Lymphocytes** | Day 5-7 | Modulate, not essential for simple healing |
| **Mast cells** | Resident | Histamine + heparin + TNF release |

**Without macrophages**, wound healing fails. Steroids suppress macrophages → impaired healing.

## Proliferative phase (days-weeks)

Three parallel processes:

### 1. Re-epithelialization
- Basal keratinocytes at wound edges + hair follicle bulge stem cells migrate across the wound.
- Need a moist environment (modern wound dressings keep wounds occluded for this reason - beats "let it dry out").
- Loose connective tissue from granulation provides scaffolding.
- Inhibited by infection, low O₂, dry surface, large gap.

### 2. Granulation tissue formation
- Pink, vascular, granular ("granulation") tissue fills the defect from the bottom up.
- **Fibroblasts** lay down type III collagen (provisional matrix).
- **Endothelial cells** sprout new capillaries (angiogenesis; VEGF-driven).
- **Macrophages** persist + direct.

### 3. Wound contraction
- **Myofibroblasts** (differentiated from fibroblasts) - contain α-SMA, contract using actin-myosin like smooth muscle.
- Pull wound edges inward → ~70% reduction in open-wound area possible.
- Over-contraction → **contracture** (joint deformity), especially after burns.

## Remodeling (weeks-years)

| Process | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Collagen turnover** | Initial type III (loose, scaffold) → type I (mature, organized) |
| **Increased cross-linking** | ↑ tensile strength (~50% of normal at 6 weeks; ~80% maximum at ~1 year - never reaches 100%) |
| **Capillary regression** | Hypervascular granulation tissue prunes back → pale mature scar |
| **Myofibroblast apoptosis** | Stop contracting |

The result is a scar - never normal skin (no rete ridges, fewer adnexa, different architecture).

## Two healing modes

| Mode | When | Mechanism | Scar |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary intention** | Clean, opposed edges (surgical incision sutured) | Minimal granulation; epithelium meets in days | Thin, linear |
| **Secondary intention** | Wide gap, lost tissue (ulcer, infected wound) | Extensive granulation, contraction, slow epithelialization | Larger, broader, with possible contracture |
| **Tertiary (delayed primary)** | Contaminated wound; debride, leave open 4-5 days, then close | Decompress infection → suture later | Variable |

## Factors that impair healing

| Local factors | Systemic factors |
|---|---|
| Infection | Diabetes (vascular + neuropathy + impaired neutrophils) |
| Foreign body | Glucocorticoids (suppress macrophages, ↓ collagen) |
| Ischemia (PAD, smoking) | Vitamin C deficiency (no proline hydroxylation) |
| Mechanical stress | Vitamin K deficiency (clotting impaired) |
| Radiation damage | Zinc deficiency (cofactor for collagen synthesis enzymes) |
| Necrosis | Renal failure (uremia) |
| Cold temperatures | Malnutrition (low protein → no substrate) |
| Edema | Old age (slower) |
| Hematoma | Chemotherapy |

<Clinical>
**Diabetic foot ulcer** - perfect storm: peripheral neuropathy (don't feel injury), vascular disease (poor delivery of immune cells + nutrients), hyperglycemia (impaired neutrophils, ↑ infection risk, AGEs damaging collagen). Treatment: glycemic control, debridement, offloading (special footwear), antibiotics, sometimes hyperbaric oxygen.

**Vitamin C deficiency (scurvy)** - proline + lysine hydroxylation in collagen requires vit C as cofactor. Deficient collagen → poor wound healing + bleeding gums + perifollicular hemorrhage + corkscrew hairs + impaired bone formation in children.

**Ehlers-Danlos syndrome** - collagen synthesis defects (many types). Hyperextensible joints + skin + easy bruising + poor wound healing + arterial fragility (type IV especially).

**Pressure ulcers** - sustained pressure (>2 hr) → ischemia → necrosis. Stage I (intact red skin) → IV (bone exposed). Prevention: turning q2h, pressure-redistribution mattress, nutrition.
</Clinical>

## Scar pathology - what can go wrong

### Hypertrophic scar
- Raised, red, but **stays within wound boundaries**.
- More collagen than normal, but type III dominant.
- Slowly remodels over months-years; can regress.

### Keloid
- Raised, red, **extends beyond** wound boundaries.
- Disorganized type I + III collagen; no remodeling.
- Common in genetic predisposition (Black, Asian skin > White), ear, chest, shoulder.
- Treatment: intralesional steroids, cryotherapy, 5-FU, surgical excision + radiation (high recurrence).

### Wound dehiscence
- Surgical wound reopens, usually 5-10 days post-op when collagen is at its weakest transition.
- Risk: infection, mechanical strain, poor surgical technique, malnutrition, obesity, advanced age.

### Contracture
- Excessive wound contraction → joint deformity.
- Burns over flexor surfaces are highest risk. Prevention: positioning, splinting, early mobilization, skin grafting.

### Non-healing chronic wounds
- Stuck in inflammatory phase >4 weeks.
- Factors: ischemia, infection, persistent mechanical stress, foreign body, malignancy (Marjolin ulcer: SCC arising in chronic wound).

## Tissue regeneration vs repair

| Type | Healing | Tissues |
|---|---|---|
| **Labile** | Continuous turnover; complete regeneration possible | Skin epidermis, gut mucosa, hematopoietic, mucosal epithelium |
| **Stable (quiescent)** | Low turnover, but can re-enter cell cycle on demand | Liver hepatocytes, renal tubules, smooth muscle, fibroblasts |
| **Permanent** | Cannot regenerate; only repair (scar) | Cardiac muscle, neurons, skeletal muscle (limited via satellite cells) |

This is why MI heals with scar (not new myocytes), while a paper cut heals with new epidermis.

## Function reference - every key player

| Component | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Platelets** | Initial plug + growth factor release (PDGF, TGF-β, VEGF) | Aspirin-treated patients have delayed initial hemostasis but normal healing |
| **PDGF** | Recruits macrophages + fibroblasts; angiogenic | Major factor in healing |
| **TGF-β** | Drives fibroblast proliferation + collagen synthesis | Excess = fibrosis; targeted therapeutically in some scars |
| **VEGF** | Drives angiogenesis | Hypoxia ↑ HIF-1α ↑ VEGF - explains tumor + ulcer angiogenesis |
| **FGF** | Re-epithelialization + angiogenesis | - |
| **EGF** | Keratinocyte proliferation | - |
| **Macrophages** | Central orchestrator | M1 (inflammatory) early; M2 (healing) later; steroid block both → impaired healing |
| **Myofibroblasts** | Wound contraction (α-SMA + actin/myosin) | Excess → contracture |
| **Collagen** | Type III in granulation; type I in mature scar | Vitamin C needed for hydroxylation; copper for lysyl oxidase cross-linking |
| **Lysyl oxidase** | Cross-links collagen + elastin | Requires copper; **Menkes disease** (copper transport) → connective tissue weakness |
| **MMPs (matrix metalloproteinases)** | Collagen remodeling | Inhibited by TIMPs; excess MMPs in chronic ulcers |
| **Keratinocyte stem cells (bulge of hair follicle)** | Source of re-epithelialization | Lost in full-thickness burns → no spontaneous re-epithelialization; need graft |
| **VEGF/HIF-1α axis** | Hypoxia-driven angiogenesis | Hyperbaric O₂ raises tissue PO₂, paradoxically boosts angiogenesis afterward |
| **Bradykinin + histamine** | Permeability + pain | First-line inflammatory mediators |

## Why this matters

<HighYield variant="boards">
- **Vitamin C deficiency**: poor wound healing + perifollicular hemorrhages + corkscrew hairs + bleeding gums. Pathophysiology: lack of cofactor for prolyl + lysyl hydroxylase → unstable triple helix → defective collagen.
- **Glucocorticoids and healing**: inhibit phospholipase A2, ↓ inflammatory mediators, suppress macrophages, ↓ fibroblast proliferation, ↓ collagen synthesis. Wounds heal poorly; bruising; thin skin.
- **Hyperbaric O₂** indications: chronic diabetic ulcers, radiation tissue injury, gas gangrene, CO poisoning, severe necrotizing infection.
- **NPWT (vacuum-assisted closure)**: negative pressure ↑ blood flow, removes exudate + bacteria, mechanical strain promotes granulation. Game-changer for chronic wounds.
- **Burns**:
  - 1st-degree (epidermis only): erythema, painful, heals in days
  - 2nd-degree superficial (papillary dermis): blisters, very painful, heals in 2 weeks
  - 2nd-degree deep (reticular dermis): waxy, less painful (damaged nerves), heals slowly with scar
  - 3rd-degree (full thickness): white/leathery, painless (nerves destroyed), needs grafting
  - 4th-degree (involves underlying structures): bone/muscle damage
- **Rule of 9s** for burn % TBSA: head 9, each arm 9, anterior trunk 18, posterior trunk 18, each leg 18, perineum 1.
- **Parkland formula**: 4 mL × kg × %TBSA Ringer's lactate over 24 h (half in first 8 h).
- **Marjolin ulcer**: SCC arising in chronic wound, scar, or burn. Suspect when a long-standing wound changes appearance.
- **Keloid vs hypertrophic scar**: keloid extends beyond original wound boundaries; hypertrophic stays within.
</HighYield>
