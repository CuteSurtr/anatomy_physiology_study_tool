The skin is more than a barrier - it is the body's largest **sensory organ**, packed with mechanoreceptors, thermoreceptors, and nociceptors, and the **only site of cholecalciferol (vitamin D₃) synthesis**. Two functions, two physiology stories.

## Sensory receptors in the skin

Five major classes of cutaneous receptors transduce mechanical, thermal, and noxious stimuli into action potentials.

<Figure name="integumentary/tactile-receptors-overview" caption="Skin tactile receptors - Merkel discs (slow-adapting, fine touch/pressure, superficial), Meissner corpuscles (rapid-adapting, light touch/low-frequency vibration, dermal papillae), Pacinian (lamellated) corpuscles (rapid-adapting, deep pressure/high-frequency vibration, deep dermis), Ruffini endings (slow-adapting, sustained stretch). Free nerve endings carry pain and temperature." />

| Receptor | Location | Stimulus | Adaptation | Receptive field |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Meissner corpuscle** | Dermal papillae (just below epidermis, glabrous skin: fingertips, lips, palms) | Light touch, low-frequency vibration (~30 Hz) | Rapid | Small |
| **Merkel disc** | Stratum basale of epidermis (fingertips, glabrous skin) | Fine touch, sustained pressure, texture | **Slow** | Small |
| **Pacinian (lamellated) corpuscle** | Deep dermis, hypodermis, joints, viscera | Deep pressure, **high-frequency vibration (~250 Hz)** | Very rapid | Large |
| **Ruffini ending** | Dermis (especially fingers, soles) | Sustained skin stretch, joint movement | **Slow** | Large |
| **Free nerve endings** | Throughout dermis and epidermis | Pain, temperature, crude touch | Slow or none | Variable |
| **Hair follicle receptors** | Around base of hair | Hair movement | Rapid | Hair-sized |

## Adaptation - rapid vs slow

| Adapting type | What it signals | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Rapid (phasic)** - fires at onset and offset, silent during sustained stim | *Change* | Meissner, Pacinian - you don't feel your clothes once they're on |
| **Slow (tonic)** - fires continuously during stim | *Magnitude* and *duration* | Merkel, Ruffini, free nerve endings - you do continue to feel a sustained pressure |

This is why a vibrating phone is felt instantly but a static touch becomes background noise.

## Pain - nociception

Free nerve endings express **TRPV1** (heat + capsaicin), **TRPM8** (cold + menthol), **ASICs** (acid), and chemical receptors. Two fiber types:

| Fiber | Diameter | Myelination | Speed | Sensation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Aδ** | 1-5 μm | Thin myelin | 5-30 m/s | **Fast, sharp**, well-localized pain ("first pain") |
| **C** | 0.2-1.5 μm | Unmyelinated | 0.5-2 m/s | **Slow, dull**, burning, poorly localized ("second pain") |

Compare to:
- **Aα** (12-20 μm, fastest, ~120 m/s) - muscle spindle afferents, motor efferents
- **Aβ** (5-12 μm, fast) - touch and pressure (Meissner, Merkel, Pacinian, Ruffini)
- **Aγ** - intrafusal motor fibers
- **B** (1-3 μm, thinly myelinated) - autonomic preganglionic
- **C** - both pain (above) and autonomic postganglionic

<Note title="Pain modulation - the gate control theory">
Aβ fibers (touch) and C fibers (pain) both synapse in the dorsal horn of the spinal cord. Activity in Aβ inhibits transmission of C fiber signals via spinal interneurons - this is why **rubbing a stubbed toe makes it feel better**. Descending pathways from the periaqueductal gray (PAG) and rostral ventromedial medulla (RVM) release **endogenous opioids** and serotonin, further dampening dorsal horn nociceptive transmission. Morphine works at the same μ receptors used by these endogenous pathways.
</Note>

## Two-point discrimination - a measure of receptor density

The smallest distance at which two simultaneous touches feel separate. Fingertip: 2-3 mm. Lips: 4-5 mm. Forearm: 30 mm. Back: 40-50 mm. This reflects the **receptive field size and density of Merkel/Meissner receptors**.

Lost early in **diabetic peripheral neuropathy** (length-dependent) and **leprosy**. Foot exam in diabetes uses the 10 g monofilament to test protective sensation.

## Dermatomes - segmental innervation

A **dermatome** is the strip of skin innervated by a single spinal nerve root (one DRG). Useful for localizing radiculopathy and spinal cord lesions:

| Landmark | Dermatome |
|---|---|
| Top of shoulder | C4 |
| Thumb | C6 |
| Middle finger | C7 |
| Little finger | C8 |
| Nipple | T4 |
| Xiphoid | T7 |
| Umbilicus | T10 |
| Inguinal ligament | L1 |
| Knee | L4 |
| Big toe | L5 |
| Lateral foot, S1 | S1 |
| Perianal | S4-S5 |

**Shingles (herpes zoster)** reactivates from a single DRG → vesicular rash in a single dermatome that **does not cross the midline**. Treat with valacyclovir/acyclovir within 72 h. Postherpetic neuralgia is a feared complication.

## Skin as endocrine organ - vitamin D synthesis

Vitamin D₃ (cholecalciferol) is produced in skin when UVB photons (290-315 nm) hit **7-dehydrocholesterol** in the stratum basale and spinosum. The pathway:

1. **Skin (UVB)** - 7-dehydrocholesterol → previtamin D₃ → cholecalciferol (D₃). Dietary D₂ (ergocalciferol, from plants/fungi) enters the same downstream pathway.
2. **Liver** - 25-hydroxylase → **25-hydroxyvitamin D (calcidiol)** - the main storage and lab-measured form.
3. **Kidney** - **1α-hydroxylase** → **1,25-(OH)₂ vitamin D (calcitriol)** - the active form. PTH stimulates this step; FGF23 inhibits it.

**Calcitriol actions:**
- ↑ Intestinal absorption of **Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻** (major action)
- ↑ Renal Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻ reabsorption
- ↑ Bone resorption (synergizes with PTH)
- Negative feedback on PTH synthesis
- Immune modulation - immune cells express 1α-hydroxylase locally; calcitriol enhances macrophage antimicrobial function (cathelicidin)

<Clinical>
**Vitamin D deficiency** (25-OH-D < 20 ng/mL) - more common than ever due to indoor lifestyles, sunscreen, dark skin, and high-latitude winters. Causes:
- **Rickets** in children (defective bone mineralization → bowed legs, rachitic rosary, frontal bossing, delayed fontanelle closure)
- **Osteomalacia** in adults (bone pain, proximal weakness, pseudofractures)
- **Secondary hyperparathyroidism** with hypocalcemia compensated by ↑ PTH

**Risk factors:**
- Dark skin (melanin absorbs UVB) - 6× longer sun exposure needed
- Latitude > 35° in winter (insufficient UVB)
- Sunscreen SPF 30 blocks ~95% of UVB
- Obesity (vitamin D sequestered in adipose)
- Malabsorption (celiac, Crohn, gastric bypass)
- Chronic kidney disease (impaired 1α-hydroxylation - need active calcitriol, not D₂/D₃)
- Anticonvulsants (induce CYP450, accelerate D metabolism)

**Replacement:** vitamin D₃ 1000-2000 IU daily for maintenance; 50,000 IU weekly for 8-12 weeks for deficiency repletion.
</Clinical>

## Other skin physiologic roles (brief recap)

- **Barrier** - stratum corneum + tight junctions; prevents water loss and pathogen entry
- **Thermoregulation** - vasodilation/constriction; sweating (see **thermoregulation** page)
- **Wound healing** - hemostasis → inflammation → proliferation → remodeling (see **wound-healing**)
- **Immune surveillance** - Langerhans cells (epidermal dendritic), dermal dendritic cells, T cells
- **Excretion** - small contribution (urea, electrolytes via sweat)
