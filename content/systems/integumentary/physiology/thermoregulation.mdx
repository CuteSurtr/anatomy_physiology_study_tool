Core body temperature is held near **37 °C (98.6 °F)** with a normal diurnal variation of ~0.5 °C (lowest in early morning, highest late afternoon). The **preoptic area of the hypothalamus** is the set-point controller, comparing core + skin temperature inputs against the set point and triggering effector responses.

## Heat-loss mechanisms

Physics: heat leaves the body via four routes.

| Mechanism | How | When dominant |
|---|---|---|
| **Radiation** | Infrared emission to cooler surroundings | ~60% of resting loss at room temperature |
| **Conduction** | Direct contact with cooler object | Minor unless in water |
| **Convection** | Moving air/water carries heat away | Significant in wind/water |
| **Evaporation** | Sweat or insensible loss of water vapor | Dominant when ambient T > skin T (only mechanism that works in hot weather) |

## Physiologic responses to heat

- **Cutaneous vasodilation** - blood diverted to skin surface → more heat radiated. Driven by withdrawal of sympathetic tone + active vasodilation.
- **Sweating** - eccrine glands; up to ~1 L/h. Cools by **evaporation**, not the sweat itself - wiping it off defeats the purpose.
- **Behavioral** - seek shade, remove clothing.

## Physiologic responses to cold

- **Cutaneous vasoconstriction** - skin pales, heat conserved in core.
- **Piloerection** - useful in furred animals; vestigial in humans (goosebumps).
- **Shivering** - involuntary skeletal muscle contraction; ↑ heat production 4–5×.
- **Non-shivering thermogenesis** - brown adipose tissue (esp. infants, less in adults), uncoupling protein-1 (UCP-1) dissipates the mitochondrial proton gradient as heat.
- **Hormonal**: chronic cold → ↑ thyroid hormone → ↑ basal metabolic rate.

<Clinical>
**Heat stroke**: core > 40 °C with CNS dysfunction (confusion, seizures). **Classic** (elderly in heat waves, no sweating) vs. **exertional** (young athletes, often still sweating). Cool aggressively (ice baths) - minutes matter; mortality rises sharply per minute above 40 °C.

**Hypothermia**: core < 35 °C. Mild → shivering, confusion. Moderate → loss of shivering (paradoxical undressing). Severe → cardiac arrhythmias (Osborn J waves on ECG), VFib risk. **"Not dead until warm and dead"** - full neurologic recovery is possible after prolonged arrest in hypothermia because cold protects the brain.

**Fever** vs. hyperthermia: fever raises the *set point* (prostaglandins from pyrogens act on the hypothalamus); the body actively defends a new higher temperature. Hyperthermia is failure of cooling against an unchanged set point. Antipyretics (acetaminophen, NSAIDs) work for fever, not hyperthermia.
</Clinical>

## Every thermoregulatory mechanism - function reference

### Heat exchange routes

| Mechanism | Direction | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Radiation** | Outward (or inward from hot environment) | ~60% of resting heat loss at room temp | Largest loss route at normal ambient |
| **Conduction** | Direct contact | Minor unless in water or on cold surface | Water cooling - drowning hypothermia |
| **Convection** | Moving fluid carries heat | Wind chill effect | Major loss in wind/water |
| **Evaporation** | Outward only | **Only mechanism that works when ambient > skin temp** | Sweating saves you in heat |

### Effector responses to heat

| Response | Mechanism | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Cutaneous vasodilation** | ↓ sympathetic tone + active vasodilation (likely via VIP cotransmission with ACh in sweat glands) | Brings hot blood to surface for heat loss |
| **Sweating (eccrine)** | Sympathetic cholinergic (ACh) on M3 receptors | Up to 1 L/h; cools by evaporation |
| **Behavioral** | Shade-seeking, clothing removal | Most efficient response |
| **↑ Respiratory rate** | Some panting in humans, larger role in furred animals | Evaporation from airways |

### Effector responses to cold

| Response | Mechanism | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Cutaneous vasoconstriction** | α1-adrenergic | Shunts blood from periphery to core |
| **Shivering** | Hypothalamic drive to motor cortex via shivering-pattern generator | ↑ heat production 4–5× via muscle work |
| **Non-shivering thermogenesis** | UCP-1 in brown adipose mitochondria - uncouples proton gradient from ATP, dissipates as heat | Major in infants; some in adults around neck/clavicles |
| **Piloerection** | Sympathetic on arrector pili | Vestigial in humans (goosebumps); traps air in furred animals |
| **↑ Thyroid hormone (chronic)** | TRH → TSH → T3/T4; ↑ basal metabolic rate | Adaptive over weeks |
| **Behavioral** | Seeking warmth, clothing | Most effective |

### Sensors and integrators

| Component | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Cutaneous cold + warm receptors** | TRPM8 (cold), TRPV1 (heat) channels | Detect skin temperature |
| **Hypothalamic preoptic area neurons** | Direct temperature sensing of blood | Set the "set point" |
| **Hypothalamic comparator** | Compares afferent input vs set point | Drives effector responses |
| **PGE₂ (prostaglandin E₂)** | Endogenous pyrogens (IL-1, IL-6, TNF) → ↑ COX-2 → PGE₂ in hypothalamus | **Raises set point → fever**; aspirin/NSAIDs/acetaminophen ↓ PGE₂ |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX temperature management:**
- **Fever in neutropenic patient** = emergency; cultures + broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 h.
- **Heat stroke management**: rapid cooling (ice water immersion, evaporative cooling); IV fluids; do NOT give antipyretics (set point isn't elevated).
- **Hypothermia rewarming**: passive external (blankets) → active external (warming blankets) → active core (warmed IV fluids, lavage); cardiac arrest patients are warmed before declared dead ("not dead till warm and dead").
- **Postoperative hypothermia**: shivering ↑ O₂ consumption; warming devices, monitor temp.
- **Pediatric fever teaching**: hydrate, lightweight clothing; antipyretics (no aspirin in kids - **Reye syndrome**).
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Thermoregulation pathology:**
- **Malignant hyperthermia**: AD, RyR1 mutation; triggered by volatile anesthetics + succinylcholine; treat with **dantrolene**.
- **Neuroleptic malignant syndrome** (antipsychotics): hyperthermia + rigidity + autonomic instability + altered MS; also treated with dantrolene + bromocriptine.
- **Serotonin syndrome** (SSRI overdose, MAOI + SSRI): hyperthermia + clonus + agitation; treat with **cyproheptadine** + supportive care.
- **Hypothalamic injury**: poikilothermia (body temp varies with environment).
- **Sepsis** can present with **hypothermia** in elderly/immunocompromised - temp instability is a SIRS criterion.
- **Heat stroke vs heat exhaustion**: stroke has **CNS dysfunction + temp &gt;40°C**; exhaustion preserves mental status.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Antipyretics + thermal pharm:**
- **Acetaminophen**: 1st-line; hepatotoxic in overdose; **antidote = N-acetylcysteine** within 8–10 h ideal.
- **Ibuprofen, naproxen**: NSAIDs; renal + GI risks; avoid in CKD.
- **Aspirin**: irreversible COX inhibition; **Reye syndrome** in children with viral illness (varicella, influenza); avoid before age 19.
- **Dantrolene**: RyR1 blocker; **antidote for MH + NMS**.
- **Cyproheptadine**: 5-HT2A antagonist; serotonin syndrome.
- **Bromocriptine**: D2 agonist; NMS, Parkinson.
</HighYield>
