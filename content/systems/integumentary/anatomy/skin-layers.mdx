## Epidermis

**Stratified squamous keratinized epithelium**, avascular. Five strata in thick skin (palms, soles); four in thin skin (no stratum lucidum). From deep to superficial:

1. **Stratum basale** - single layer of keratinocytes on the basement membrane. Includes **melanocytes**, **Merkel cells** (touch), tactile epithelial cells.
2. **Stratum spinosum** - "spiny" because of desmosomal connections. Includes **Langerhans cells** (dendritic APCs).
3. **Stratum granulosum** - keratohyalin and lamellar granules; cells start dying as they're cut off from nutrients.
4. **Stratum lucidum** - translucent, thick-skin only.
5. **Stratum corneum** - 15–30 layers of dead, keratinized "squames." Continuously shed; complete turnover in ~30–40 days.

<Mnemonic>
"**B**asale → **S**pinosum → **G**ranulosum → **L**ucidum → **C**orneum" - *Beautiful Skin Generally Looks Clean*.
</Mnemonic>

## Dermis

Dense irregular connective tissue. Two layers:

- **Papillary** (superficial, loose CT) - dermal papillae project into the epidermis, increasing surface area for exchange and grip; contain capillaries and **Meissner corpuscles** (light touch).
- **Reticular** (deep, dense irregular CT) - collagen + elastin; houses **Pacinian corpuscles** (vibration, deep pressure), **Ruffini endings** (stretch), hair follicles, sebaceous + sweat glands, blood vessels, nerves.

## Hypodermis (subcutaneous)

Not technically skin, but tightly bound to it. Mostly **adipose** - insulation, energy store, mechanical cushion. Site of subcutaneous injection.

## Accessory structures

- **Hair follicles** - invaginations of epidermis; **arrector pili** muscle attached. Each follicle has a cycle: anagen (growth) → catagen (regression) → telogen (rest).
- **Sebaceous glands** - holocrine; secrete sebum into hair follicles. Lubricates, antimicrobial.
- **Eccrine sweat glands** - distributed everywhere; merocrine; thermoregulation. Innervation: sympathetic, but uses **acetylcholine** (unusual).
- **Apocrine sweat glands** - axillae, groin; activate at puberty; bacterial action on apocrine sweat → body odor.
- **Nails** - keratinized epidermal derivatives; grow from the nail matrix.

<Clinical>
**Burn depth**:
- Superficial (1st-degree): epidermis only, painful, erythematous (sunburn)
- Partial-thickness (2nd): into dermis, blisters, very painful
- Full-thickness (3rd): through dermis into hypodermis, painless (nerves destroyed), white/charred - requires grafting

**Rule of 9s** for total body surface area: head 9%, each arm 9%, anterior trunk 18%, posterior trunk 18%, each leg 18%, perineum 1%.
</Clinical>

## Every skin structure - what it does and why it matters

### Epidermis layers (basale → corneum)

| Layer | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Stratum basale** | Single layer of mitotically active keratinocytes; contains melanocytes, Merkel cells | Regenerative reservoir; ~28-day turnover; tumors include basal cell carcinoma |
| **Melanocyte** | Synthesizes melanin from tyrosine; transfers via melanosomes | Pigmentation; UV protection; tumor → melanoma |
| **Merkel cell** | Touch receptor (Type I slow-adapting) | Fine 2-point discrimination on fingertips; tumor → Merkel cell carcinoma |
| **Stratum spinosum** | "Spiny" appearance from desmosomes; many cell layers | Mechanical strength; houses Langerhans cells; tumor → squamous cell carcinoma |
| **Langerhans (dendritic) cell** | Antigen-presenting cell of skin | Immune surveillance; first line for contact dermatitis (Type IV hypersensitivity) |
| **Stratum granulosum** | Keratohyalin granules + lamellar bodies; cells start dying | Site of profilaggrin → filaggrin processing; lamellar bodies extrude lipid (waterproofing) |
| **Stratum lucidum** | Translucent layer; only in thick skin (palms, soles) | Extra abrasion resistance |
| **Stratum corneum** | 15–30 layers of dead, keratinized "squames" | **Outermost barrier**; sheds + replaces ~30–40 day cycle; thickens with chronic friction (callus) |

### Dermis

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Papillary dermis** | Loose CT with capillaries, Meissner corpuscles | Dermal papillae interlock with epidermis (basal undulations) - visible as fingerprints |
| **Reticular dermis** | Dense irregular CT (collagen + elastin) | Tensile strength; aging + UV → wrinkles via collagen/elastin breakdown |
| **Meissner corpuscle (papillary dermis)** | Light touch, low-frequency vibration | Fast-adapting; densest in lips, fingertips |
| **Pacinian (lamellar) corpuscle (reticular dermis + hypodermis)** | Deep pressure, high-frequency vibration | Onion-like layers; fast-adapting |
| **Ruffini ending** | Sustained pressure, skin stretch | Slow-adapting |
| **Free nerve endings** | Pain, temperature, crude touch | Most numerous receptors; both A-delta + C fibers |
| **Cutaneous plexus** | Vascular network at dermo-hypodermal junction | Thermoregulation via vasodilation/constriction |

### Hypodermis (subcutaneous tissue)

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Adipose tissue** | Insulation, energy storage, mechanical cushion | Site of subcutaneous injection (insulin); brown fat in infants for thermogenesis |
| **Connective tissue septa** | Anchor skin to deep fascia | Cellulitis here is rapidly progressive |

### Accessory structures

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Hair follicle** | Invagination of epidermis surrounding the hair shaft | Site of androgen action (DHT - male pattern baldness); folliculitis if infected |
| **Hair shaft** | Keratinized hair extending above skin | Cuticle + cortex + medulla |
| **Hair bulb (base of follicle)** | Matrix cells + dermal papilla | Site of hair growth and melanocyte input |
| **Arrector pili muscle** | Smooth muscle from follicle to dermis | Goosebumps; sympathetic-driven |
| **Sebaceous gland** | Holocrine secretion of sebum into hair follicle | Lubrication, antimicrobial; ↑↑ at puberty → acne when blocked |
| **Eccrine sweat gland** | Merocrine; ubiquitous; thermoregulatory sweat | Sympathetic cholinergic innervation (atypical); controls body temp by evaporation |
| **Apocrine sweat gland** | Holocrine-like; axilla, groin; active at puberty | Bacterial action on apocrine sweat → body odor; hidradenitis suppurativa here |
| **Apoeccrine glands** | Hybrid; axillary | Source of profuse axillary sweating |
| **Ceruminous glands (ear canal)** | Modified apocrine; produce earwax | Trap debris; impaction → hearing loss |
| **Mammary glands** | Modified apocrine | Lactation; common cancer site |
| **Nail matrix** | Proliferative zone at proximal nail | Generates nail plate; injury here → permanent ridges |
| **Nail plate** | Keratinized hard surface | Protection; mirror of systemic disease (clubbing, koilonychia, Beau's lines) |
| **Lunula** | Visible portion of matrix at base of nail | White crescent - distal matrix |
| **Eponychium (cuticle)** | Seals nail to skin | Trauma here → paronychia |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX integumentary priorities:**
- **Pressure injury (decubitus) staging**:
  - Stage 1: intact skin, non-blanchable erythema
  - Stage 2: partial-thickness loss (dermis exposed)
  - Stage 3: full-thickness, fat visible
  - Stage 4: bone/tendon/muscle exposed
  - Unstageable: covered by slough/eschar
  - Deep tissue: persistent non-blanchable maroon/purple
- **Prevention**: reposition q2h, pressure-redistribution surface, keep skin dry, nutrition (protein, vit C), Braden Scale assessment.
- **Burn % using Rule of 9's**: head 9, arm 9 each, anterior trunk 18, posterior trunk 18, leg 18 each, perineum 1.
- **Burn fluid (Parkland formula)**: 4 mL × kg × %TBSA over 24 h; half in first 8 h from injury time.
- **Skin cancer ABCDE**: Asymmetry, Border irregular, Color varied, Diameter &gt;6 mm, Evolving.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Dermatology pattern recognition:**
- **Psoriasis**: silvery scales on extensor surfaces; nail pitting; ↑ keratinocyte turnover.
- **Atopic dermatitis**: flexural surfaces; "atopic triad" (asthma + allergic rhinitis + AD).
- **Pemphigus vulgaris**: anti-desmoglein → intraepidermal blisters; positive Nikolsky.
- **Bullous pemphigoid**: anti-hemidesmosome → subepidermal blisters; tense bullae; older adults.
- **SJS / TEN**: drug-induced (sulfa, anticonvulsants, allopurinol); epidermal detachment; TEN &gt;30% TBSA; high mortality.
- **Erythema multiforme**: target lesions; HSV, mycoplasma.
- **Acanthosis nigricans**: dark velvety neck/axilla; insulin resistance or paraneoplastic (gastric Ca).
- **Melanoma sites**: men back, women legs; depth (Breslow) is strongest prognostic factor.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Dermatology pharm:**
- **Topical steroids**: low → high potency; face = low; thicker skin tolerates higher. Long-term: atrophy, telangiectasia, striae.
- **Tretinoin (retinoid)**: acne, photoaging; photosensitivity; teratogen.
- **Isotretinoin**: severe cystic acne; iPLEDGE program; **teratogen**, ↑ lipids, depression risk.
- **Imiquimod**: TLR-7 agonist; warts, actinic keratosis.
- **5-fluorouracil topical**: actinic keratosis.
- **Biologics for psoriasis**: TNF inhibitors, IL-17 (secukinumab), IL-23 (guselkumab).
- **Sunscreen teaching**: SPF 30+, broad-spectrum (UVA + UVB), reapply q2h or after water.
</HighYield>

## Hair follicle - detailed anatomy

<Figure name="integumentary/hair-follicle" caption="Hair follicle structure showing shaft, root, bulb with matrix and dermal papilla, sebaceous gland, arrector pili muscle, and the pilosebaceous unit." />

The **pilosebaceous unit** = hair follicle + sebaceous gland + arrector pili. Each part:

| Component | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Hair shaft** | Keratinized hair above skin surface | Three concentric layers (medulla, cortex, cuticle); pigmented by melanocytes in bulb |
| **Hair root** | Portion below skin surface | Surrounded by inner + outer root sheaths |
| **Hair bulb** | Expanded base | Site of cell proliferation (matrix) + melanocyte input |
| **Hair matrix** | Mitotically active cells at bulb base | Generate hair shaft + inner root sheath |
| **Dermal papilla** | Vascular CT projection into bulb | Provides nutrients; signals matrix cells; alopecia treatments (minoxidil) target it |
| **Inner root sheath** | Surrounds growing hair | Ends below sebaceous duct |
| **Outer root sheath** | Continuous with epidermis | Source of stem cells for re-epithelialization after wound |
| **Bulge** | Stem-cell niche on outer root sheath | Critical reservoir; activated in wound healing |
| **Sebaceous gland** | Holocrine secretion of sebum into follicle | Antimicrobial, lubrication; ↑↑ at puberty → acne when blocked |
| **Arrector pili muscle** | Smooth muscle attaching follicle to dermis | Goosebumps; sympathetic-driven |
| **Apocrine duct** (in some regions) | Opens into follicle above sebaceous | Active at puberty; bacterial action → odor |

### Hair growth cycle

| Phase | Duration | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Anagen (growth)** | 2–7 years | Active matrix proliferation; 85–90% of scalp hairs at any time |
| **Catagen (regression)** | ~2–3 weeks | Matrix involutes; follicle shortens |
| **Telogen (rest)** | ~3 months | Hair held loosely; sheds when next anagen pushes new hair up |
| **Exogen** | - | Active shedding phase |

<Clinical>
**Androgenic alopecia (male/female pattern)**: DHT shrinks follicles in genetically susceptible scalp areas; treat with **minoxidil (topical, vasodilator)** or **finasteride (5α-reductase inhibitor → ↓ DHT)**.
**Alopecia areata**: autoimmune attack on hair follicles; smooth patchy hair loss; exclamation-point hairs at edge.
**Telogen effluvium**: diffuse shedding 3 months after stressor (illness, pregnancy, surgery); resolves spontaneously.
**Tinea capitis**: dermatophyte infection of scalp follicles; treat with systemic antifungal (griseofulvin, terbinafine).
</Clinical>
