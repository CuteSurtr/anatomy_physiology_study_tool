Major endocrine glands: **hypothalamus, pituitary, pineal, thyroid, parathyroids, thymus, adrenals (cortex + medulla), pancreas (islets), gonads (ovaries / testes)**. Hormones fall into three chemical classes: **peptide/protein** (water-soluble, surface receptors, fast - e.g., insulin), **steroid** (lipid-soluble from cholesterol, intracellular receptors, slow but durable - e.g., cortisol), and **amine** (mixed properties - catecholamines, thyroid hormones).

## What the lecture adds beyond the simple gland chart

The slide deck leans on three practical ideas that the overview chart alone does not show well:

- The **hypothalamus** is functionally endocrine because it releases hormones into portal vessels that target the pituitary.
- The **pineal gland** is small but still high yield because **melatonin** ties anatomy to circadian physiology.
- The **pancreas** is mostly exocrine tissue, but its scattered **islets of Langerhans** are the endocrine control center for glucose homeostasis.
