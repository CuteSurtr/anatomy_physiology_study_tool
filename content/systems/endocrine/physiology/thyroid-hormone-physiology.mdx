Thyroid hormones (T₃ and T₄) set the basal metabolic rate of every cell in the body. They drive heat production, glucose use, lipid turnover, protein synthesis, fetal CNS development, and growth. Too much causes a hyperdynamic, heat-intolerant patient; too little produces a slowed, cold-intolerant patient. The axis is one of the most reliable test items in endocrinology because the **TSH ↔ free T₄ relationship is so clean.**

## Synthesis - the seven steps

<Figure name="endocrine/thyroid-hormone-synthesis" caption="Thyroid hormone synthesis 7 steps - NIS traps iodide, pendrin moves it to colloid, TPO oxidizes + organifies (iodinates Tg) + couples (MIT+DIT→T3, DIT+DIT→T4), endocytosed, lysosomal proteolysis frees T3/T4, secreted." />

1. **Iodide trapping** - **Na⁺/I⁻ symporter (NIS)** on the basolateral side of the follicular cell pumps I⁻ from blood into the cell (against gradient). Concentrates I⁻ 30-fold.
2. **Transport into colloid** - **pendrin** transporter moves I⁻ across the apical membrane into the colloid.
3. **Oxidation** - **thyroid peroxidase (TPO)** oxidizes I⁻ to I⁰ in the presence of H₂O₂.
4. **Iodination of thyroglobulin** - TPO attaches I to tyrosine residues on **thyroglobulin (Tg)** → MIT (monoiodotyrosine) and DIT (diiodotyrosine).
5. **Coupling** - TPO couples iodinated tyrosines on Tg: MIT + DIT = T₃; DIT + DIT = T₄. ~90% of secreted hormone is T₄.
6. **Endocytosis and proteolysis** - When TSH stimulates, colloid is endocytosed; lysosomes hydrolyze Tg → T₃ and T₄ free in cytoplasm.
7. **Secretion** - T₃ and T₄ diffuse into the blood.

<Note title="Why iodine deficiency matters">
Without iodine, the thyroid can't make hormone. The pituitary keeps pushing TSH (no negative feedback) → follicular hyperplasia → **goiter**. Severe iodine deficiency in pregnancy → fetal hypothyroidism → **cretinism** (intellectual disability, deafness, short stature). Iodization of salt (US, 1924) eradicated endemic goiter in most developed countries. Iodine deficiency remains the #1 cause of preventable intellectual disability worldwide.
</Note>

## Transport in blood

Thyroid hormones are hydrophobic and ~99.97% protein-bound in plasma:
- **Thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG)** - carries 70% of T₄
- **Albumin** and **transthyretin (TTR)** - carry the rest

Only the **free fraction (~0.03%)** is biologically active. TBG goes up in pregnancy and with estrogens → total T₄ rises but free T₄ stays normal. **Always measure free T₄, not total T₄**, when TBG state is in question.

## Conversion - T₄ is a prohormone

**T₄ is the main secreted form, but T₃ is the active form.** Peripheral tissues convert T₄ → T₃ via **deiodinases**:

| Enzyme | Location | Reaction | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1** | Liver, kidney, thyroid | T₄ → T₃ (and T₄ → rT₃) | Source of most plasma T₃ |
| **D2** | CNS, pituitary, brown fat | T₄ → T₃ | Local production for tissue use; provides T₃ for negative feedback in pituitary |
| **D3** | Placenta, fetus, brain | T₄ → **reverse T₃ (rT₃)**, T₃ → T₂ | Inactivates; protects fetus from maternal hormone |

**Reverse T₃ (rT₃)** is inactive. In sick euthyroid syndrome, D1 is suppressed and D3 is induced → low T₃, high rT₃, normal-low TSH.

## Mechanism at the cell

T₃ enters the cell via MCT8 transporter → binds **nuclear thyroid receptors (TRα, TRβ)** that act as transcription factors with retinoid-X-receptor (RXR) heterodimers on thyroid response elements (TREs). T₃ has ~10× the affinity of T₄ - explains why T₃ is the active form.

## Physiologic effects - everywhere

| System | Effect | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Basal metabolic rate** | ↑ | Mitochondrial biogenesis, Na/K-ATPase upregulation, uncoupling |
| **Heat production** | ↑ | "Calorigenic effect" - obligatory heat from increased metabolism |
| **Cardiovascular** | ↑ HR, ↑ contractility, ↑ CO, ↓ SVR | Upregulates β1 receptors; vasodilation from increased metabolic demand |
| **Respiratory** | ↑ RR, ↑ minute ventilation | Brainstem drive |
| **CNS** | Critical for fetal brain development; affects mood, cognition in adults | Synaptic / myelination effects |
| **Skeletal** | Bone turnover ↑; required for normal linear growth | Synergizes with GH and IGF-1 |
| **GI** | Motility ↑ | Smooth muscle effect |
| **Lipid** | LDL receptor expression ↑ → cholesterol ↓ | Hepatic effect |
| **Carbohydrate** | Glucose absorption ↑, glycogenolysis ↑ | Counterregulatory + permissive |
| **Reproductive** | Permissive for normal menstrual cycles; T₃ enters fetus | Hyperthyroidism → oligomenorrhea; hypo → menorrhagia |

## The HPT axis - regulation

<Figure name="endocrine/pituitary-hormones-summary" caption="Anterior pituitary hormones - TSH drives the thyroid axis. T₃ feeds back negatively at hypothalamus (TRH) and pituitary (TSH)." />

<Figure name="endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary" caption="Hypothalamic-pituitary anatomy - TRH from hypothalamus → portal blood → anterior pituitary thyrotrophs → TSH → thyroid follicular cells." />

```
Hypothalamus → TRH → Anterior pituitary → TSH → Thyroid → T₄ + T₃
                            ↑                              │
                            └──── (−) negative feedback ───┘
```

- **TRH** (thyrotropin-releasing hormone) from hypothalamus → drives TSH
- **TSH** (thyroid-stimulating hormone) from anterior pituitary thyrotrophs → drives synthesis + release of T₃/T₄
- **Negative feedback** - T₃ (from local D2 conversion in pituitary) suppresses TSH; also suppresses hypothalamic TRH

**The TSH-free T₄ relationship is log-linear.** A small drop in free T₄ produces a large rise in TSH - making TSH the most sensitive marker of thyroid dysfunction in *primary* thyroid disease.

| State | TSH | Free T₄ | Free T₃ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary hypothyroidism** | ↑↑ | ↓ | Low or normal |
| **Subclinical hypothyroidism** | ↑ | Normal | Normal |
| **Central hypothyroidism** (pituitary/hypothalamic) | Low or normal | ↓ | Low |
| **Primary hyperthyroidism** | ↓↓ | ↑ | ↑ |
| **Subclinical hyperthyroidism** | ↓ | Normal | Normal |
| **TSH-secreting pituitary adenoma** | ↑ (or normal) | ↑ | ↑ |
| **Sick euthyroid** | Low or normal | Low or normal | ↓, rT₃ ↑ |
| **Pregnancy (T1)** | ↓ (hCG cross-reacts with TSHR) | Normal-high | Normal-high |

## Hyperthyroidism

**Most common causes:**
- **Graves disease** - thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulins (TSI / anti-TSHR antibodies) activate TSH receptor. Diffuse goiter, ophthalmopathy, pretibial myxedema, ↑ radioiodine uptake (diffuse).
- **Toxic multinodular goiter** - autonomous nodules; patchy uptake.
- **Toxic adenoma** - one hot nodule; rest of gland suppressed.
- **Thyroiditis** (subacute, postpartum, painless) - inflammatory release of preformed hormone, *low* radioiodine uptake.
- **Exogenous T₄** - factitious or over-replacement; low Tg.

**Symptoms:** weight loss with increased appetite, heat intolerance, tachycardia, palpitations, AF, tremor, anxiety, insomnia, lid lag, hyperreflexia, oligomenorrhea, diarrhea.

**Thyroid storm** - life-threatening hyperthyroidism: T > 40°C, severe tachycardia (often AF), CNS dysfunction (agitation → coma), GI failure, high-output heart failure. Treat with **β-blockers** (propranolol), **PTU or methimazole**, **iodine (Lugol's) - given an hour after the thionamide** (Wolff-Chaikoff effect), **glucocorticoids** (blocks T₄ → T₃ conversion), and cooling.

## Hypothyroidism

**Most common causes:**
- **Hashimoto thyroiditis** - autoimmune (TPO + anti-Tg antibodies), chronic lymphocytic infiltrate. Most common cause in iodine-replete populations.
- **Iodine deficiency** - most common cause worldwide.
- **Post-ablation / surgical** - radioactive iodine, thyroidectomy.
- **Drug-induced** - amiodarone, lithium, interferon, checkpoint inhibitors.
- **Central** - hypothalamic or pituitary failure.

**Symptoms:** weight gain with decreased appetite, cold intolerance, bradycardia, fatigue, constipation, dry skin, brittle hair, slow reflexes (with delayed relaxation), menorrhagia, depression, myxedema.

**Myxedema coma** - decompensated hypothyroidism with hypothermia, hyponatremia, hypoventilation, hypotension, AMS. Mortality high. Treat with **IV levothyroxine + IV hydrocortisone** (cover potential coexisting adrenal insufficiency) + supportive care.

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Antibody patterns:**
- **Graves** → anti-TSHR (TSI), can also have anti-TPO
- **Hashimoto** → anti-TPO (highly sensitive), anti-Tg
- **Postpartum thyroiditis** → anti-TPO

**Radioactive iodine uptake (RAIU):**
- ↑ diffuse → Graves
- ↑ patchy → toxic MNG
- ↑ single nodule → toxic adenoma
- ↓ → thyroiditis (preformed hormone leaks out) or exogenous T₄

**Hallmark physical findings:**
- Graves → exophthalmos, lid lag, pretibial myxedema
- Hashimoto → diffusely firm, sometimes lobulated goiter
- Subacute (de Quervain) thyroiditis → tender goiter, post-viral
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Antithyroid drugs:**
- **Methimazole** - inhibits TPO (organification + coupling). First-line. Avoid in T1 pregnancy (teratogenic - aplasia cutis, choanal atresia).
- **PTU** (propylthiouracil) - inhibits TPO + inhibits peripheral T₄→T₃ conversion. Use in T1 pregnancy and thyroid storm. **Risks: hepatotoxicity, ANCA-associated vasculitis.**
- **β-blockers** (propranolol) - symptomatic; propranolol also inhibits T₄→T₃ conversion at high doses.
- **Iodine (Lugol's, SSKI)** - **Wolff-Chaikoff effect**: high iodine transiently suppresses hormone synthesis. Used pre-thyroidectomy, in storm, and in radiation exposure (saturates the gland with cold iodine). Don't give first in storm - give *after* thionamide to prevent **Jod-Basedow** (iodine-induced hyperthyroidism in nodular goiter).
- **Radioactive iodine (¹³¹I)** - ablative therapy for Graves, MNG, adenoma. Contraindicated in pregnancy and breastfeeding.
- **Levothyroxine (synthetic T₄)** - hypothyroidism treatment. Take on empty stomach; absorption ↓ by Ca²⁺, iron, fiber, PPIs. Goal: normalize TSH (in primary disease).
</HighYield>

## Pregnancy and the thyroid

<Figure name="endocrine/glucose-homeostasis-loop" caption="A classic endocrine negative feedback loop (blood glucose) - same logic applies to the HPT axis: rising T₃/T₄ suppresses TRH + TSH; falling T₃/T₄ disinhibits both (Wikimedia)." />

- TBG ↑ → total T₄ ↑; free T₄ stays normal
- hCG cross-reacts weakly with the TSH receptor → mild ↑ free T₄, ↓ TSH in T1 (physiologic; resembles mild hyper)
- Fetal thyroid begins functioning at week 12 - before that, fetus depends on maternal T₄
- Maternal hypothyroidism → fetal CNS deficits, miscarriage, preterm birth → **screen TSH in early pregnancy and increase levothyroxine dose by ~30% as soon as pregnancy is confirmed**
