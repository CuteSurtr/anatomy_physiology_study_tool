The **hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis** is the body's master stress response, on top of a normal diurnal cycle.

<Figure name="endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary-portal-slide" caption="Lecture-slide wiring diagram of the hypothalamus, pituitary stalk, and anterior pituitary - the upstream half of the HPA axis." />

<Figure name="endocrine/steroidogenesis" caption="Cortisol synthesis pathway - driven by ACTH from the HPA axis." />

<Figure name="endocrine/adrenal-cortex-medulla-slide" caption="Lecture slide clarifying that ACTH targets the adrenal cortex, while the medulla belongs functionally to the autonomic stress response." />

## The axis, in order

<Figure name="endocrine/hpa-axis-combined" caption="HPA axis - hypothalamus CRH → anterior pituitary ACTH → adrenal cortex cortisol → negative feedback at hypothalamus + pituitary." />

1. **Hypothalamus** (paraventricular nucleus) releases **CRH** in response to stress, low cortisol, or the morning circadian peak.
2. CRH → **anterior pituitary corticotrophs** → release **ACTH** (cleaved from POMC).
3. ACTH → **adrenal cortex (zona fasciculata)** → release **cortisol**.
4. Cortisol exerts effects + **negatively feeds back** on hypothalamus and pituitary.

Cortisol peaks ~6–8 AM and troughs around midnight in normal physiology.

Think of the page in two layers: the **pituitary figure is the wiring diagram**, while the **steroidogenesis figure is the chemistry diagram** that explains what ACTH is ultimately trying to increase.

## Cortisol actions (permissive, catabolic, anti-inflammatory)

<Figure name="endocrine/hpa-axis-combined" caption="" />


- **Metabolic**: ↑ gluconeogenesis, ↑ lipolysis, ↑ proteolysis (in muscle). Spares glucose for the brain.
- **Cardiovascular**: ↑ vascular response to catecholamines (permissive). Cortisol deficiency → hypotension.
- **Immune**: anti-inflammatory at physiologic doses, profoundly immunosuppressive at pharmacologic doses.
- **Bone**: ↑ resorption, ↓ formation → osteoporosis with chronic excess.

## Clinical states to recognize

| Condition | Mechanism | Cortisol | ACTH | Key features |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cushing disease** | ACTH-secreting pituitary adenoma | ↑ | ↑ | Central obesity, moon facies, striae, hyperglycemia |
| **Cushing syndrome (adrenal)** | Adrenal cortisol-secreting tumor | ↑ | ↓ | Same features, suppressed ACTH |
| **Ectopic ACTH** | Small-cell lung cancer most commonly | ↑↑ | ↑↑ | Often less classic Cushing, more hyperpigmentation + hypokalemia |
| **Addison disease** | Primary adrenal failure (autoimmune) | ↓ | ↑ | Hyperpigmentation (ACTH = MSH effect), hypotension, hyperkalemia |
| **Secondary adrenal insufficiency** | Pituitary failure or exogenous steroid withdrawal | ↓ | ↓ | No hyperpigmentation, no hyperkalemia |

<Clinical>
**Never stop chronic glucocorticoids abruptly.** Exogenous steroids suppress the HPA axis; sudden cessation can precipitate **adrenal crisis** (hypotension, hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypoglycemia, shock). Taper.
</Clinical>

<Note title="Dexamethasone suppression test">
Give dex (a potent synthetic glucocorticoid) and measure cortisol the next morning. Normal: cortisol suppressed. Cushing: not suppressed. Pattern of suppression at low vs. high dose helps localize source (pituitary usually suppresses at high dose; adrenal and ectopic typically don't).
</Note>

## Every HPA-axis component and adrenal hormone - function reference

### Adrenal cortex zones

| Zone | Hormone | Function | Regulator |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zona glomerulosa** (outer) | **Aldosterone** (mineralocorticoid) | ↑ Na⁺ + H₂O reabsorption; ↑ K⁺ + H⁺ excretion | RAAS (mainly), ↑ K⁺, ACTH (minor) |
| **Zona fasciculata** (middle) | **Cortisol** (glucocorticoid) | Catabolic, anti-inflammatory, permissive | ACTH |
| **Zona reticularis** (inner) | **DHEA, androstenedione** (androgens) | Mild androgenic effects; converted to estrogen in periphery | ACTH |

### Adrenal medulla

| Cell | Hormone | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Chromaffin cells** | **Epinephrine** (~80%), **norepinephrine** (~20%) | Fight-or-flight: tachycardia, vasoconstriction, bronchodilation, mobilization of glucose |
| Note: medulla = modified sympathetic ganglion (pre-ganglionic ACh → chromaffin cells) |  |  |

### Cortisol effects in one table

| Action | Effect | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| ↑ Gluconeogenesis (liver) | ↑ Blood glucose | Stress hyperglycemia; **steroid diabetes** with chronic use |
| ↑ Lipolysis | Frees FFAs as energy | Truncal fat redistribution in Cushing |
| ↑ Proteolysis (muscle) | Muscle wasting; "thin extremities" of Cushing | Skin thinning, easy bruising |
| ↓ Inflammation | Anti-inflammatory (mediator suppression) | Why steroids treat asthma, autoimmune; **mask infection** signs |
| ↓ Immune (high-dose) | Lymphocyte apoptosis | Pharmacologic immunosuppression; opportunistic infection risk |
| ↑ Cardiovascular response to catecholamines (permissive) | Maintains BP | Addison → hypotension |
| ↑ Bone resorption | Osteoporosis with chronic use | Steroid-induced osteoporosis |
| ↓ Bone formation | Osteoblast inhibition | Compounds resorption |
| ↑ GFR | Diuretic effect (with intact ADH suppression) | Steroid edema is mild |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX adrenal priorities:**
- **Addisonian crisis**: hypotension, hypoglycemia, hyperkalemia, hyponatremia → **IV hydrocortisone immediately** + saline.
- **Steroid taper teaching**: never abruptly stop; risk of adrenal crisis; medical-alert bracelet; stress-dose during illness/surgery.
- **Cushing patient teaching**: weight, glucose, BP monitoring; skin protection (thinning); infection signs may be masked.
- **Steroid administration**: take with food; monitor blood sugar; risk of GI bleed (add PPI if chronic).
- **Pheochromocytoma**: avoid abdominal palpation (can trigger crisis); α-blocker BEFORE β-blocker (avoid unopposed α).
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Diagnostic patterns:**
- **Cushing workup**: 24-h urinary free cortisol, late-night salivary cortisol, low-dose dex suppression → confirms hypercortisolism; high-dose dex differentiates (pituitary suppresses, adrenal/ectopic don't).
- **Adrenal insufficiency**: cortrosyn (ACTH) stim test; primary fails to respond, secondary may.
- **Conn (primary hyperaldo)**: HTN + hypokalemia + alkalosis; ↑ aldo/renin ratio.
- **Pheo (10s)**: 10% bilateral, 10% extra-adrenal, 10% malignant, 10% familial (MEN 2A/2B, VHL, NF1).
- **CAH (21-hydroxylase deficiency)**: classic salt-wasting form in newborns; ambiguous genitalia in females; ↑ 17-OH-progesterone.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Steroid pharm:**
- **Hydrocortisone** (= cortisol): replacement; physiologic. Has mineralocorticoid activity.
- **Prednisone, prednisolone, methylprednisolone**: intermediate-acting; common for inflammation.
- **Dexamethasone**: long-acting (~36 h); ~25× cortisol potency; used in dex suppression test, cerebral edema, fetal lung maturity.
- **Fludrocortisone**: pure mineralocorticoid; for Addison + orthostatic hypotension.
- **Mitotane**: adrenolytic; for adrenal cortical carcinoma.
- **Ketoconazole, metyrapone**: inhibit steroid synthesis; used preoperatively for Cushing.
- **Mifepristone**: glucocorticoid receptor antagonist; Cushing in DM; also abortifacient.
</HighYield>

## Adrenal gland anatomy + zones

<Figure name="endocrine/adrenal-gland" caption="Adrenal gland anatomy showing the cortex (zona glomerulosa, fasciculata, reticularis) producing aldosterone, cortisol, and androgens; and the medulla producing catecholamines." />

The adrenal has two embryologically distinct parts on top of each kidney:

- **Cortex** (mesodermal): three zones, each producing a different hormone class. Mnemonic: **GFR → Salt, Sugar, Sex** (Glomerulosa-aldo-salt; Fasciculata-cortisol-sugar; Reticularis-androgens-sex). Deeper = more lipid (steroid synthesis).
- **Medulla** (neural crest, modified sympathetic ganglion): chromaffin cells secrete catecholamines under preganglionic sympathetic control (pre-ganglionic ACh directly innervates them - no postganglionic).

The adrenal is supplied by **3 arteries** (superior, middle, inferior suprarenal) but drained by a single **adrenal vein** (right → IVC, left → left renal vein). This asymmetry matters for adrenal vein sampling in workup of primary hyperaldosteronism.
