Plasma glucose is maintained in a narrow range (70–110 mg/dL fasting) by a tightly tuned hormonal balance between **insulin** (storage / "fed") and **glucagon, epinephrine, cortisol, GH** (mobilization / "fasting"). The endocrine pancreas is the central regulator; failure of this regulation = **diabetes mellitus**, the most common endocrine disease in the world.

## The endocrine pancreas - islets of Langerhans

<Figure name="endocrine/pancreatic-islet-cells" caption="Pancreatic islet cell types - α (glucagon, peripheral), β (insulin + amylin, central, ~70%), δ (somatostatin), PP/F (pancreatic polypeptide), ε (ghrelin)." />

Scattered throughout the exocrine acinar tissue of the pancreas are ~1 million **islets of Langerhans** (~1–2% of pancreas mass). Each contains 4 cell types.

| Cell | % of islet | Hormone | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **β cells** (central) | 65–80% | **Insulin** + amylin + C-peptide | Lowers glucose; signals "fed" state |
| **α cells** (peripheral) | 15–20% | **Glucagon** | Raises glucose; signals "fasting" |
| **δ cells** | 5–10% | **Somatostatin** | Pan-inhibitor - suppresses insulin + glucagon + GI hormones |
| **PP (F) cells** | 1–2% | **Pancreatic polypeptide** | Inhibits pancreatic exocrine secretion + gallbladder contraction |

Within each islet, β cells are central and α/δ are peripheral - local **paracrine cross-talk**: insulin inhibits glucagon; somatostatin inhibits both.

### Blood supply quirk
Islet blood enters at the center (β cells) and percolates outward, so β-cell hormones bathe the α and δ cells. This local insulin signal helps suppress glucagon in the fed state - and is lost in type 1 diabetes (β-cell destruction → inappropriately high glucagon, contributing to hyperglycemia).

## Insulin - the storage hormone

<Figure name="endocrine/incretin-mechanism" caption="Insulin secretion + the incretin effect. Oral glucose → GLP-1 + GIP from gut → potentiate β-cell insulin release. Semaglutide and other GLP-1 agonists mimic this." />

### Structure
- **51 amino acids** in two chains (A + B) linked by 2 disulfide bonds.
- Synthesized as **preproinsulin** → proinsulin → cleaved into insulin + **C-peptide** (equimolar). **C-peptide** has no biologic activity but is a useful marker of endogenous insulin production (because exogenous insulin lacks it - distinguishes endogenous hyperinsulinemia / insulinoma from factitious / surreptitious insulin use).

### Stimuli for release
| Strongest | Weaker |
|---|---|
| **Glucose** (most potent) | Amino acids (especially arginine, leucine) |
| **GLP-1, GIP** (incretins - gut hormones potentiate glucose response) | Vagal stimulation (M3) |
| Sulfonylureas (close K_ATP) | β-adrenergic |

Inhibited by: α2 stimulation, somatostatin, fasting, **stress hormones**.

### β-cell stimulus-secretion coupling (board-favorite step-by-step)

1. **Glucose enters β cell via GLUT2** (insulin-independent, low affinity → senses high glucose).
2. **Glucokinase** phosphorylates → glucose-6-P → glycolysis → ↑ ATP.
3. **K_ATP channel closes** (blocked by ATP) → membrane depolarizes.
4. **L-type Ca²⁺ channels open** → Ca²⁺ influx.
5. **Insulin vesicles fuse** with membrane → insulin + C-peptide release.

**Sulfonylureas** (glyburide, glipizide) and **meglitinides** close K_ATP directly → insulin release regardless of glucose. Risk: hypoglycemia.

### Insulin signaling pathway

Insulin binds **insulin receptor** (tetrameric tyrosine kinase) → autophosphorylation → IRS-1 phosphorylation → two arms:
- **PI3K → Akt** → GLUT4 translocation (muscle, adipose) + glycogen synthase + protein synthesis
- **MAPK** → growth + gene expression

### Insulin actions per tissue
| Tissue | Action |
|---|---|
| **Liver** | ↑ glycogenesis, ↑ lipogenesis, ↓ gluconeogenesis, ↓ glycogenolysis, ↓ ketogenesis |
| **Skeletal muscle** | ↑ GLUT4 → ↑ glucose uptake; ↑ glycogen synthesis; ↑ protein synthesis |
| **Adipose** | ↑ GLUT4 → ↑ glucose uptake; ↑ lipogenesis; inhibits hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL) → ↓ lipolysis |
| **Brain** | None directly - brain uses GLUT1/GLUT3 (insulin-independent). But hypoglycemia is sensed and triggers counterregulation |

### GLUT transporter family
| GLUT | Where | Insulin-dependent? | Km / function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GLUT1** | RBC, brain, BBB | No | Constitutive; basal uptake |
| **GLUT2** | β cells, liver, kidney, intestine | No | **Low affinity (high Km)** → senses high [glucose] |
| **GLUT3** | Neurons, placenta | No | High affinity; constitutive |
| **GLUT4** | Skeletal muscle, adipose | **Yes** - insulin-regulated | Inserted upon insulin signaling |
| **GLUT5** | Small intestine, sperm | No | Transports fructose |

## Glucagon - the mobilization hormone

<Figure name="digestive/gi-hormones-summary" caption="Related figure for context — Glucagon - the mobilization hormone." />

- Single chain, 29 aa, from α cells.
- **Trigger**: hypoglycemia (most potent), amino acids (especially arginine), sympathetic input, cortisol/epi (permissive).
- **Inhibited by**: insulin (paracrine in the islet), somatostatin, glucose, free fatty acids.

### Actions (all in the liver - opposite of insulin)
| Pathway | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Glycogenolysis** | ↑ |
| **Gluconeogenesis** | ↑ |
| **Ketogenesis** | ↑ (via ↑ CPT-1) |
| **Lipolysis** (adipose) | ↑ |

Signaling: glucagon receptor → Gs → ↑ cAMP → PKA → phosphorylates glycogen phosphorylase + glycogen synthase + HSL.

## Counter-regulation - four hormones oppose insulin

<Figure name="endocrine/steroidogenesis" caption="Cortisol is the slower counter-regulatory hormone in the set. Its synthesis pathway matters because stress, ACTH, and adrenal disease all change glucose handling through this axis." />

| Hormone | Source | Speed | Main action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Glucagon** | α cells | Minutes | First-line for hypoglycemia |
| **Epinephrine** | Adrenal medulla | Seconds | ↑ glycogenolysis (β2 in muscle), ↑ lipolysis (β3), suppresses insulin (α2) |
| **Cortisol** | Adrenal cortex | Hours | ↑ gluconeogenesis, ↑ proteolysis (provides AA substrate), permissive for other counterreg hormones |
| **Growth hormone** | Ant. pituitary | Hours | ↑ lipolysis, ↑ gluconeogenesis, ↓ peripheral glucose uptake (insulin resistance) |

Diabetics with **autonomic neuropathy** lose epinephrine and glucagon responses to hypoglycemia → **hypoglycemic unawareness** + delayed counterregulation → severe hypoglycemic events.

## Three metabolic states

<Figure name="endocrine/glucose-homeostasis-loop" caption="Blood glucose negative feedback - high glucose → insulin (β cells) → glucose uptake + storage; low glucose → glucagon (α cells) → glycogenolysis + gluconeogenesis. The cleanest endocrine feedback loop in the body (Wikimedia)." />

### Fed state (~2–4 h after meal)
- Insulin ↑, glucagon ↓.
- Glucose → liver (glycogen + lipogenesis), muscle (glycogen + glucose oxidation), adipose (TG synthesis).
- Net: storage.

### Post-absorptive / early fasting (~4–24 h)
- Insulin ↓, glucagon ↑.
- **Liver glycogenolysis** primary source of plasma glucose (liver glycogen ~75 g, sustains ~12 h fast).
- Some gluconeogenesis ramping up.

### Prolonged fasting (>24 h)
- Liver glycogen depleted → **gluconeogenesis dominant** (substrates: amino acids from muscle proteolysis, glycerol from adipose lipolysis, lactate from RBC + muscle).
- After ~3 days: **ketogenesis** ramps up (acetoacetate + β-hydroxybutyrate from fatty acid oxidation) → brain shifts from glucose to ketones (~75% of brain energy by 1 week starvation) → protein-sparing.

<Mnemonic>
**"Gly → Glu → Keto"** - first burn glycogen (hours), then make glucose (days), then make ketones (weeks). Order of metabolic substrates during fasting.
</Mnemonic>

## Diabetes mellitus - failure of the system

<Figure name="endocrine/diabetes-t2dm" caption="Type 2 diabetes - insulin resistance + relative β-cell dysfunction → chronic hyperglycemia → microvascular (retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy) + macrovascular (CAD, stroke, PAD) complications." />

| Feature | Type 1 | Type 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Autoimmune β-cell destruction | Insulin resistance + relative β-cell failure |
| **Genetics** | HLA-DR3/DR4 | Polygenic (TCF7L2, etc.) - strong family history |
| **Body habitus** | Lean, young (often &lt;30) | Obese, older (usually >40) |
| **Insulin levels** | Absent | High early (resistance) → low late (β-cell exhaustion) |
| **C-peptide** | Absent / low | Present |
| **Autoantibodies** | Anti-GAD, anti-islet, anti-insulin | Absent |
| **Acute decompensation** | **DKA** | **HHS** (hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state) - less ketosis |
| **Treatment** | Insulin (always) | Lifestyle + metformin + others; insulin if needed |

### Diagnostic criteria (any one):
- Fasting glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL
- 2-h OGTT ≥ 200 mg/dL
- **HbA1c ≥ 6.5%** (reflects ~3 month average)
- Random glucose ≥ 200 + symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss)

### DKA - type 1 emergency
Triad: **hyperglycemia + ketosis + anion-gap metabolic acidosis**. Mechanism: absolute insulin deficiency → unopposed lipolysis → fatty acid flux → β-oxidation → ketone bodies. Treatment: IV fluids, **K⁺ repletion** (insulin will shift K⁺ intracellularly), insulin drip, fix precipitant.

## Function reference - every key player

| Component | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **β cells** | Insulin + C-peptide | Destroyed in T1DM; exhausted in late T2DM |
| **α cells** | Glucagon | Inappropriately ↑ in T1DM (loss of paracrine insulin) - contributes to hyperglycemia + ketosis |
| **δ cells** | Somatostatin | Source of somatostatinoma (rare); octreotide is an analog |
| **GLUT2** | β-cell glucose sensing + liver/kidney | Failure → defective insulin secretion |
| **K_ATP channel** | Couples ATP to insulin release | Target of sulfonylureas; activating mutations → neonatal diabetes (treatable with sulfonylureas) |
| **GLUT4** | Insulin-regulated glucose uptake in muscle + adipose | Translocation defect = core lesion in T2DM |
| **Insulin receptor + IRS-1** | Tyrosine kinase signaling | Phosphoserine on IRS-1 (from TNF-α in obesity) = mechanism of insulin resistance |
| **GLP-1** | Incretin from L cells of ileum/colon | Potentiates glucose-stimulated insulin secretion. GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide) + DPP-4 inhibitors (sitagliptin) are major T2DM drugs |
| **GIP** | Incretin from K cells of duodenum | Second incretin; tirzepatide hits both GLP-1 + GIP |
| **SGLT2** | Renal proximal tubule | Reabsorbs filtered glucose. SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin) cause glucosuria → ↓ glucose + weight + CV/renal benefit |
| **Hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL)** | Adipose lipolysis | Inhibited by insulin; activated by epi/glucagon/cortisol |
| **Amylin** | Co-secreted with insulin from β cells | Slows gastric emptying + ↓ glucagon; pramlintide is synthetic |

## Why this matters

<HighYield variant="boards">
- **Insulin** - single best test to **distinguish endogenous (insulinoma) from exogenous (factitious) hyperinsulinemia** is **C-peptide**. ↑ insulin + ↑ C-peptide = endogenous; ↑ insulin + ↓ C-peptide = exogenous.
- **Metformin** mechanism: ↓ hepatic gluconeogenesis (inhibits complex I → ↑ AMPK). First-line T2DM. Doesn't cause hypoglycemia. Lactic acidosis risk in renal failure.
- **GLP-1 agonists** (semaglutide, liraglutide): weight loss, CV benefit, GI side effects, rare pancreatitis. **Tirzepatide**: dual GLP-1/GIP - most effective oral/SQ weight + glucose drug currently.
- **SGLT2 inhibitors**: reduce CV mortality + slow CKD + treat heart failure. Side effects: glucosuria → UTI/genital infections; euglycemic DKA in stress.
- **Insulin's K⁺ effect**: drives K⁺ into cells (Na/K-ATPase activation). Used to treat hyperkalemia (insulin + glucose).
- **Honeymoon period** in new T1DM: residual β-cell function gives transient relief; can lead to false reassurance.
- **Whipple's triad** for hypoglycemia: (1) symptoms of low glucose + (2) measured low glucose + (3) symptoms resolve with glucose. Required to call something hypoglycemia.
- **Maturity-onset diabetes of the young (MODY)**: autosomal dominant; commonly glucokinase mutation (MODY2) - mild fasting hyperglycemia, no complications.
</HighYield>
