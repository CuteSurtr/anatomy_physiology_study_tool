The adrenal gland is two endocrine organs stuck together. The **cortex** (mesoderm origin) makes steroid hormones in three concentric layers; the **medulla** (neural crest origin, modified sympathetic ganglion) makes catecholamines. Both feed into the body's response to stress, but with different speeds and different effects.

## Three cortical zones - "GFR makes Salt, Sugar, Sex"

| Zone | Hormone | Major regulator | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Glomerulosa** | Aldosterone (mineralocorticoid) | **Angiotensin II** + hyperkalemia | Na⁺ retention, K⁺/H⁺ excretion |
| **Fasciculata** | **Cortisol** (glucocorticoid) | **ACTH** from anterior pituitary | Metabolism, immune suppression, stress response |
| **Reticularis** | DHEA / DHEA-S (androgen) | ACTH | Precursor for sex steroids; small direct effect |

The cortex synthesizes all its steroids from cholesterol via cytochrome P450 enzymes. **Pregnenolone** is the first intermediate, generated from cholesterol by **side-chain cleavage enzyme (P450scc / CYP11A1)** - the rate-limiting step, activated by ACTH.

<Figure name="endocrine/steroidogenesis" caption="Adrenal steroidogenesis - cholesterol → pregnenolone (CYP11A1, rate-limiting, ACTH-stimulated) → branches into mineralocorticoid (aldosterone), glucocorticoid (cortisol), and androgen (DHEA) pathways. 21-hydroxylase (CYP21A2) and 17α-hydroxylase (CYP17A1) deficiencies cause congenital adrenal hyperplasia." />

## Cortisol - the stress steroid

<Figure name="endocrine/steroidogenesis-detail" caption="Adrenal + gonadal steroidogenesis. Cholesterol → pregnenolone via P450scc (rate-limiting, ACTH-stimulated). Diverges into mineralocorticoid (aldosterone), glucocorticoid (cortisol), and androgen (DHEA → testosterone/estradiol) pathways." />

### Synthesis and secretion

ACTH (from anterior pituitary corticotrophs, processed from POMC) → adrenal fasciculata → cortisol secretion. Diurnal pattern: peak ~6-8 a.m., trough ~midnight. Roughly **20 mg cortisol/day** at baseline; rises 10-fold in major stress.

### Transport

90% bound to **corticosteroid-binding globulin (CBG)** and albumin. The free fraction (~10%) is biologically active. CBG rises with estrogens (pregnancy, OCPs) - total cortisol rises but free is normal.

### Mechanism

Lipid-soluble → enters cell → binds **glucocorticoid receptor** (GR) in cytoplasm → translocates to nucleus → modifies transcription. Effects manifest over hours.

### Physiologic effects - "Catabolic, anti-everything, salt-retaining at high doses"

| Action | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Glucose** | ↑ Gluconeogenesis (hepatic), ↓ peripheral glucose uptake → ↑ blood glucose |
| **Protein** | Net catabolism (muscle wasting, thinning skin, easy bruising) |
| **Lipid** | Net lipolysis, but redistributes fat centrally (truncal obesity, moon face, buffalo hump) |
| **Bone** | Inhibits osteoblasts, ↓ Ca²⁺ absorption → osteoporosis with chronic excess |
| **Cardiovascular** | Maintains vascular sensitivity to catecholamines ("permissive"); ↑ BP via mineralocorticoid effect at high doses |
| **Immune / inflammation** | Inhibits phospholipase A₂ → ↓ prostaglandins, leukotrienes; inhibits NF-κB → ↓ cytokines; lymphocyte apoptosis; neutrophilia (demargination) |
| **Connective tissue** | Inhibits fibroblast collagen → poor wound healing, striae |
| **CNS** | Mood elevation acutely, depression/psychosis with chronic excess; insomnia |
| **GI** | ↑ Gastric acid → ulcer risk |
| **Renal** | Increases free water clearance (suppresses ADH); at high doses, MR cross-activation → Na⁺ retention |

## The HPA axis and feedback

CRH from paraventricular hypothalamus → ACTH from corticotrophs → cortisol from adrenal fasciculata. **Cortisol feeds back negatively** at both hypothalamus and pituitary.

| Disorder | ACTH | Cortisol | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cushing disease** (pituitary) | ↑ | ↑↑ | Pituitary adenoma; high-dose dexamethasone suppresses |
| **Adrenal Cushing** | ↓ | ↑↑ | Adrenal adenoma or carcinoma; no suppression with dexamethasone |
| **Ectopic ACTH** | ↑↑↑ | ↑↑ | Small cell lung cancer most common; no suppression with high-dose dexamethasone |
| **Exogenous steroids** (iatrogenic, most common) | ↓ | ↓ | Suppressed adrenal cortex |
| **Primary adrenal insufficiency (Addison)** | ↑ | ↓ | Autoimmune adrenalitis, TB, hemorrhage. Aldosterone also ↓ → ↑K⁺, ↓Na⁺, **hyperpigmentation** (POMC-derived MSH) |
| **Secondary adrenal insufficiency** | ↓ | ↓ | Pituitary failure or chronic exogenous steroid; aldosterone intact (RAAS independent); no hyperpigmentation |

<Clinical>
**Adrenal crisis** - severe acute adrenal insufficiency. Hypotension refractory to fluids and pressors, hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, vomiting, abdominal pain. Triggered by infection, surgery, or abrupt steroid withdrawal in a chronic-steroid patient. Treat with **stress-dose hydrocortisone** (100 mg IV bolus, then 50 mg q6h) + IV fluids. Mortality high if delayed - **treat empirically while confirming**.
</Clinical>

## Aldosterone (recap from urinary)

Made in zona glomerulosa. Triggered by **angiotensin II** (the main signal) and **hyperkalemia**. Acts on collecting duct principal cells → ENaC + Na/K-ATPase upregulation → Na⁺/H₂O retention + K⁺/H⁺ excretion.

| Disorder | Renin | Aldosterone | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary hyperaldosteronism** (Conn syndrome) | ↓ | ↑ | HTN, hypokalemia, metabolic alkalosis. Aldosterone:renin > 30 |
| **Secondary hyperaldosteronism** | ↑ | ↑ | Renal artery stenosis, CHF, cirrhosis, nephrotic - all reduce effective volume |
| **Hypoaldosteronism / Addison** | ↑ | ↓ | Hypotension, hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis |
| **Liddle syndrome** | ↓ | ↓ | Constitutively active ENaC mimics hyperaldosteronism - "pseudoaldosteronism." Treat with amiloride |
| **17α-hydroxylase deficiency CAH** | ↓ | ↑ (corticosterone, DOC) | HTN, hypokalemia, no sex steroids → female phenotype regardless of karyotype |
| **21-hydroxylase deficiency CAH** (most common, 95%) | ↑ | ↓ | Salt-wasting, ambiguous genitalia in females (excess androgens) |

## Adrenal androgens

DHEA and DHEA-S come mostly from the zona reticularis. **Adrenarche** (~age 8-10) precedes gonadarche → axillary + pubic hair, body odor. In adult women, adrenal androgens are the primary source - relevant in hirsutism workup. In men, they contribute little compared to testicular testosterone.

## Adrenal medulla - the catecholamine factory

**Chromaffin cells** are modified postganglionic sympathetic neurons. They release into blood rather than synapses. Innervated by preganglionic sympathetic fibers (T5-T11 via the splanchnic nerves) - ACh stimulation triggers catecholamine release.

### Catecholamine synthesis

Tyrosine → DOPA (tyrosine hydroxylase, rate-limiting) → Dopamine → Norepinephrine → **Epinephrine** (PNMT, induced by cortisol).

The medulla secretes **~80% epinephrine** and ~20% NE. NE is the dominant sympathetic neurotransmitter elsewhere (postganglionic at effectors), but in blood, epinephrine dominates.

### Effects of catecholamines

| Receptor | Distribution | Major effects |
|---|---|---|
| **α₁** | Vascular smooth muscle | Vasoconstriction (skin, splanchnic), ↑ BP; mydriasis |
| **α₂** | Presynaptic, pancreas, platelets | ↓ NE release, ↓ insulin, ↑ platelet aggregation |
| **β₁** | Heart, JG cells | ↑ HR, ↑ contractility, ↑ renin |
| **β₂** | Bronchi, smooth muscle, liver | Bronchodilation, vasodilation in skeletal muscle, glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis, lipolysis |
| **β₃** | Adipose tissue, bladder detrusor | Lipolysis (limited clinical use); bladder relaxation (mirabegron for overactive bladder) |

### Metabolism

NE and Epi are inactivated by **MAO** (intracellular) and **COMT** (extracellular). Final products: **metanephrine, normetanephrine, vanillylmandelic acid (VMA)** - measured in urine for diagnosis of pheochromocytoma.

## Pheochromocytoma

Catecholamine-secreting tumor of chromaffin cells. Classic triad: **paroxysmal headache, palpitations, diaphoresis** + sustained or paroxysmal HTN. **"10% tumor"**: 10% bilateral, 10% malignant, 10% extra-adrenal (organ of Zuckerkandl), 10% familial (MEN2A, MEN2B, NF1, VHL), 10% pediatric.

**Diagnosis:** plasma free metanephrines or 24-h urinary fractionated metanephrines (most sensitive). CT or MRI to localize. MIBG scintigraphy for ambiguous cases.

**Treatment:** **α-blockade FIRST** (phenoxybenzamine) for 10-14 days → then β-blocker added → then surgical resection. **Never β-block first** - unopposed α stimulation can cause severe hypertensive crisis.
