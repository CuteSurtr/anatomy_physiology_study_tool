A pea-sized gland sitting in the **sella turcica** of the sphenoid bone, hung from the hypothalamus by the **infundibulum**. Two functionally distinct lobes with different embryologic origins.

<Figure name="endocrine/pituitary-gland" caption="Pituitary anatomy - anterior (adenohypophysis) + posterior (neurohypophysis) lobes, connected to hypothalamus via the infundibulum + portal system." />

<Figure name="endocrine/pituitary-portal-histology-slide" caption="Lecture-slide histology view of the pituitary stalk and portal vessels, emphasizing that the anterior and posterior lobes are different tissues." />

## Anterior pituitary (adenohypophysis)

Glandular tissue. Hormones travel from hypothalamus → anterior pituitary via the **hypothalamic-hypophyseal portal system** (NOT down the infundibulum). Hypothalamic releasing/inhibiting hormones reach the anterior lobe directly, avoiding systemic dilution.

| Cell | Hormone | Target | Hypothalamic control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Somatotroph | **GH** | Liver, body | GHRH (+), Somatostatin (−) |
| Lactotroph | **Prolactin** | Mammary | Dopamine (−) - net inhibition |
| Thyrotroph | **TSH** | Thyroid | TRH (+) |
| Corticotroph | **ACTH** | Adrenal cortex | CRH (+) |
| Gonadotroph | **FSH, LH** | Gonads | GnRH (+, pulsatile) |

## Posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis)

Neural tissue. Hormones are made in **hypothalamic neurons** (supraoptic + paraventricular nuclei) and travel down their axons to be released here.

- **Oxytocin** - uterine contraction (parturition), milk let-down
- **ADH (vasopressin)** - water reabsorption at the collecting duct; vasoconstriction at high doses

## Hypothalamus–pituitary axes

Six classic axes (CRH→ACTH→cortisol, TRH→TSH→T3/T4, GnRH→LH/FSH→sex steroids, GHRH→GH→IGF-1, dopamine⊣prolactin, oxytocin/ADH posterior). Each has **negative feedback** at multiple levels.

<Mnemonic>
**FLAT PiG** for anterior pituitary hormones: **F**SH, **L**H, **A**CTH, **T**SH, **P**rolactin, **G**H. (i = ignore; some include MSH.)
</Mnemonic>

<Clinical>
**Pituitary adenoma**: most are benign and most secrete prolactin (**prolactinoma**) → galactorrhea + amenorrhea (women) or hypogonadism (men). Treat with dopamine agonists (cabergoline, bromocriptine) - they shrink the tumor and restore normal feedback.

**Sheehan syndrome**: postpartum pituitary infarction (the gland enlarges in pregnancy and is sensitive to hemorrhagic hypotension). Presents with failure to lactate (prolactin) and later panhypopituitarism.
</Clinical>

## Every pituitary structure and target - what it does and why it matters

### Pituitary anatomy

| Structure | Function | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|
| **Sella turcica** | Bony fossa of sphenoid housing pituitary | Pituitary tumors expand here; visible on lateral skull X-ray as bony erosion |
| **Diaphragma sellae** | Dural roof covering pituitary | Pituitary stalk pierces it |
| **Infundibulum (pituitary stalk)** | Connects hypothalamus to pituitary | Severance → diabetes insipidus + loss of anterior hormones (stalk section syndrome) |
| **Anterior pituitary (adenohypophysis)** | Glandular tissue from Rathke's pouch (oral ectoderm) | Synthesizes 6 trophic hormones; tumors usually arise here |
| **Posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis)** | Neural tissue from neuroectoderm | Stores ADH + oxytocin made in hypothalamus |
| **Pars intermedia** | Vestigial between anterior + posterior | Produces MSH; can be cyst site |
| **Hypothalamic–hypophyseal portal system** | Capillary bed bringing hypothalamic releasing hormones directly to anterior pituitary | High local hormone concentration without dilution into systemic circulation |
| **Pituitary capillary fenestrations** | Allow hormones into bloodstream | Required for endocrine output |

### Anterior pituitary cells and their hormones

| Cell | Hormone | Target | Action | Hypothalamic control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Somatotroph (~50%)** | **GH** | Liver, body | ↑ growth (via IGF-1), ↑ lipolysis, ↑ blood glucose | GHRH (+), Somatostatin (−) |
| **Lactotroph (~20%)** | **Prolactin** | Mammary | Lactation; also inhibits GnRH | **Dopamine (−)** dominant - net inhibition |
| **Thyrotroph (~5%)** | **TSH** | Thyroid | ↑ T3/T4 synthesis + release | TRH (+) |
| **Corticotroph (~15%)** | **ACTH** (from POMC) | Adrenal cortex | ↑ Cortisol (and androgens, mildly aldosterone) | CRH (+) |
| **Gonadotroph (~10%)** | **FSH, LH** | Gonads | Gametogenesis + sex steroid synthesis | GnRH (+, pulsatile) |

### Posterior pituitary hormones

| Hormone | Source neuron | Target | Action | Why crucial |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ADH (vasopressin)** | Supraoptic + paraventricular nuclei | Kidney collecting duct (V2 → aquaporin-2 insertion); vasculature (V1 → vasoconstriction at high doses) | Water reabsorption; ↑ BP | Loss → diabetes insipidus; excess → SIADH (hyponatremia) |
| **Oxytocin** | Paraventricular > supraoptic | Uterus, mammary myoepithelium | Uterine contraction; milk let-down | Pitocin used to induce labor; positive feedback loop in parturition |

### Hypothalamic releasing/inhibiting hormones

| Hormone | From | To anterior pituitary cell | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GHRH** | Arcuate nucleus | Somatotroph | ↑ GH |
| **Somatostatin (GHIH)** | Periventricular nucleus | Somatotroph | ↓ GH (also ↓ TSH) |
| **TRH** | Paraventricular | Thyrotroph (also lactotroph) | ↑ TSH; ↑ Prolactin (mild) |
| **CRH** | Paraventricular | Corticotroph | ↑ ACTH |
| **GnRH** | Arcuate (pulsatile) | Gonadotroph | ↑ LH/FSH; continuous dosing → suppression (leuprolide) |
| **Dopamine (PIF)** | Arcuate | Lactotroph | **↓ Prolactin** |

## High-yield exam patterns

<HighYield exam="nclex">
**NCLEX endocrine priorities:**
- **Post-hypophysectomy**: monitor for DI (polyuria, ↑ Na⁺), bleeding, CSF leak (clear nasal drainage, halo sign); **no straining, no sneezing with mouth closed, no toothbrush ×2 wk**.
- **DI teaching**: monitor weight + I/O daily; vasopressin nasal spray timing; medical-alert bracelet.
- **SIADH precautions**: fluid restriction; seizure precautions if Na⁺ &lt;120; rise &lt;0.5 mEq/L/h to prevent CPM.
- **Acromegaly post-op**: hourly vitals, neuro checks for CN deficits; visual fields.
- **Hypopituitarism**: cortisol + thyroid replacement priority before surgery.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="step1">
**Pituitary disease patterns:**
- **Prolactinoma**: galactorrhea + amenorrhea (women), hypogonadism + erectile dysfunction (men); **dopamine agonists** (cabergoline, bromocriptine) shrink them.
- **Acromegaly**: tall + organ enlargement in adult (GH after epiphyseal closure); ↑ IGF-1; OGTT fails to suppress GH; cardiomyopathy + colon polyps; somatostatin analogs.
- **Cushing disease** (pituitary ACTH): suppressed by high-dose dex; vs ectopic ACTH (not suppressed).
- **Sheehan syndrome**: postpartum pituitary infarction; failure to lactate first sign.
- **Empty sella syndrome**: arachnoid herniation; often asymptomatic, MRI finding.
- **Bitemporal hemianopsia**: pituitary tumor compressing optic chiasm.
</HighYield>

<HighYield exam="pharm">
**Pituitary pharm:**
- **Desmopressin (DDAVP)**: synthetic ADH, V2-selective; treats central DI + von Willebrand + bedwetting.
- **Cabergoline / bromocriptine**: D2 agonists; first-line for prolactinoma.
- **Octreotide / lanreotide**: somatostatin analogs; acromegaly, NETs, variceal bleeding, secretory diarrhea.
- **Pegvisomant**: GH receptor antagonist; acromegaly when others fail.
- **Conivaptan / tolvaptan**: V2 antagonists; SIADH, hyponatremia.
- **GH (somatropin)**: replacement in deficiency; not for short stature without deficiency.
</HighYield>

## Thyroid + parathyroid anatomy

<Figure name="endocrine/thyroid-parathyroid" caption="Thyroid + parathyroid glands. Thyroid is the butterfly-shaped gland anterior to the trachea; 4 parathyroid glands (typically) sit on its posterior surface." />

### Thyroid

- Bilobed gland anterior to trachea at C5–T1, connected by an isthmus over the 2nd–4th tracheal rings.
- Weight ~25 g; vascular (3rd most blood flow per gram of any tissue).
- **Follicular cells** secrete T3/T4 - colloid (thyroglobulin + iodide) is stored in follicles.
- **Parafollicular (C) cells** secrete calcitonin.

### Parathyroid (4 glands)

- Pea-sized, on posterior thyroid surface; **superior pair from 4th pharyngeal pouch, inferior pair from 3rd** (along with thymus).
- **Chief cells** secrete PTH in response to low Ca²⁺.
- **Oxyphil cells** - function unclear, increase with age.

<Clinical>
**Hyperthyroidism (Graves)**: TSI antibodies stimulate TSH receptor → diffuse goiter, exophthalmos, pretibial myxedema. Tx: methimazole/PTU, radioactive iodine, surgery.

**Hypothyroidism (Hashimoto)**: anti-TPO antibodies → atrophic thyroid; fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain. Tx: levothyroxine.

**Primary hyperparathyroidism**: usually adenoma; ↑ Ca²⁺, ↓ PO₄³⁻, ↑ PTH; "stones, bones, groans, psychic moans."

**Thyroid surgery risk**: **recurrent laryngeal nerve** (hoarseness if unilateral; airway emergency if bilateral); **parathyroid glands** - hypocalcemia if all removed.
</Clinical>
