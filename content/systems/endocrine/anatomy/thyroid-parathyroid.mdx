The thyroid and parathyroid glands sit on the front of the trachea, just below the larynx. Despite occupying the same neighborhood, they regulate two unrelated systems: the **thyroid** sets metabolic rate via T3/T4, while the **parathyroids** govern calcium homeostasis via PTH. Both are surgical landmarks - and parathyroids are the cardinal complication of any total thyroidectomy.

## Thyroid - gross anatomy

- **Shape**: butterfly - two **lobes** connected by a thin midline **isthmus**.
- **Location**: anterior neck, **C5–T1**; isthmus crosses **tracheal rings 2–4**.
- **Capsule**: false (visceral pretracheal fascia) + true (gland's own fibrous capsule). Connective tissue septa divide it into lobules.
- **Weight**: 20–30 g normally.

### Blood supply
| Artery | Source | Supplies |
|---|---|---|
| **Superior thyroid artery** | External carotid (first anterior branch) | Upper poles + most of gland |
| **Inferior thyroid artery** | Thyrocervical trunk → subclavian | Lower poles |
| **Thyroid ima** (10% of people) | Brachiocephalic or aorta directly | Variable - surgical hazard |

### Venous drainage
| Vein | Drains to |
|---|---|
| **Superior thyroid** | Internal jugular |
| **Middle thyroid** | Internal jugular |
| **Inferior thyroid** | Brachiocephalic vein |

### Nerves at risk during thyroid surgery
| Nerve | Course | Injury → |
|---|---|---|
| **Recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN)** | Runs in **tracheo-esophageal groove**, behind inferior thyroid artery | Hoarseness (unilateral); airway obstruction (bilateral) - emergency |
| **External branch of superior laryngeal nerve** | Travels with superior thyroid artery to cricothyroid muscle | Loss of high-pitched phonation (opera singer's nerve) |

<Clinical>
**Hoarseness post-thyroidectomy** = recurrent laryngeal nerve injury until proven otherwise. The **left RLN** loops under the aortic arch, the **right RLN** under the right subclavian - both ascend in the tracheo-esophageal groove and are vulnerable when ligating the inferior thyroid artery.

**A non-recurrent right "RLN"** occurs in ~1% (associated with aberrant right subclavian) - a notorious surgical trap because it enters laterally from the vagus, not from below.
</Clinical>

## Thyroid - microanatomy

The thyroid is built of **follicles** - spheres of follicular cells around a central **colloid** (protein-rich storage).

| Cell | Location | Hormone | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Follicular cells** | Wall of follicle (cuboidal at rest, columnar when active) | **T3** + **T4** | Set basal metabolic rate, growth, neural development |
| **Parafollicular (C) cells** | Between follicles | **Calcitonin** | Lowers serum Ca²⁺ (minor role in humans) |

### Thyroid hormone synthesis - 6 steps (board favorite)

1. **Iodide uptake** - Na/I symporter (NIS) on basolateral membrane (concentrates iodide 30×). Blocked by **perchlorate** and **thiocyanate**.
2. **Oxidation** - thyroid peroxidase (TPO) oxidizes I⁻ to I₀ on the apical membrane.
3. **Organification** - TPO iodinates tyrosine residues on **thyroglobulin** in the colloid → MIT (mono-) and DIT (di-iodotyrosine). Blocked by **PTU + methimazole**.
4. **Coupling** - TPO couples MIT + DIT → T3; DIT + DIT → T4. Also blocked by PTU + methimazole.
5. **Endocytosis + proteolysis** - TSH-stimulated follicular cells endocytose colloid, lysosomes cleave thyroglobulin → release T3 + T4.
6. **Peripheral conversion** - T4 → T3 by **5'-deiodinase** in liver, kidney, pituitary. Blocked by **PTU**, **propranolol**, **glucocorticoids**, and **amiodarone** (acute).

**T4 is 20× more abundant** in plasma, but **T3 is 4× more potent** and is the active form at the receptor (nuclear, TR-α + TR-β).

<Mnemonic>
**"PTU blocks Peripheral conversion Too"** - propylthiouracil blocks both TPO (steps 2–4) and 5'-deiodinase (step 6). Methimazole only blocks TPO. Use PTU in (1) thyroid storm and (2) first trimester pregnancy (methimazole is teratogenic).
</Mnemonic>

## Hypothalamus-Pituitary-Thyroid (HPT) axis

```
TRH (hypothalamus) → TSH (anterior pituitary) → T3/T4 (thyroid) → negative feedback on pituitary + hypothalamus
```

- **TRH** = thyrotropin-releasing hormone, tripeptide from paraventricular nucleus.
- **TSH** = thyroid-stimulating hormone, glycoprotein; binds TSH-R (Gs) on follicular cells → ↑ cAMP → ↑ every step of synthesis + growth.
- **Negative feedback**: T3/T4 inhibit TRH + TSH. **TSH** is the **most sensitive screening test** for thyroid function.

## Parathyroid glands

- **Number**: usually **4** (superior + inferior pair on each side); 5–10% have ≥5 glands.
- **Location**: posterior surface of thyroid, embedded in thyroid capsule but with their own.
- **Size**: 5 mm, ~30 mg each. Color: yellow-tan ("mustard seed").

### Embryology
| Pair | Origin |
|---|---|
| **Superior parathyroids** | **4th** pharyngeal pouch (so does the thyroid C cells) |
| **Inferior parathyroids** | **3rd** pharyngeal pouch (so does the thymus - migrate inferiorly together) |

Because the inferior parathyroids and the thymus share an origin, **ectopic inferior parathyroids** can be found in the mediastinum (in the thymus). The superior parathyroids are more constant in location.

### Two cell types
| Cell | Function |
|---|---|
| **Chief cells** | Secrete **PTH** in response to ↓ Ca²⁺ (sensed by CaSR) |
| **Oxyphil cells** | Increase with age; function uncertain |

## PTH actions - calcium guardian

| Target | Action | Net effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Bone** | Stimulates osteoblasts → RANK-L → osteoclast activation → release Ca²⁺ + PO₄³⁻ | ↑ serum Ca²⁺, ↑ PO₄³⁻ |
| **Kidney (DCT)** | ↑ Ca²⁺ reabsorption (TRPV5) | ↑ serum Ca²⁺ |
| **Kidney (PCT)** | ↓ PO₄³⁻ reabsorption (via Na/Pi cotransporter internalization) | ↓ serum PO₄³⁻ |
| **Kidney** | ↑ 1α-hydroxylase → activates vitamin D | ↑ intestinal Ca²⁺ + PO₄³⁻ absorption |

Net: **↑ serum Ca²⁺, ↓ serum PO₄³⁻** (kidney phosphate loss outpaces gut absorption).

### Calcium homeostasis - three hormones
| Hormone | Source | ↑ Ca²⁺ | ↑ PO₄³⁻ | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PTH** | Chief cells | Yes | **No** - drops PO₄³⁻ | ↓ Ca²⁺ |
| **Calcitriol** (active vitamin D) | Kidney 1α-hydroxylase | Yes | Yes | PTH + ↓ PO₄³⁻ |
| **Calcitonin** | C cells of thyroid | **Lowers** | Lowers | ↑ Ca²⁺ |

Calcitonin's physiologic role is minor in humans - losing both thyroids (post-thyroidectomy) doesn't cause hypercalcemia.

<Clinical>
**Primary hyperparathyroidism** (parathyroid adenoma): ↑ Ca²⁺, ↓ PO₄³⁻, ↑ PTH. "Stones, bones, abdominal moans, psychiatric overtones."

**Secondary hyperparathyroidism** (CKD): ↓ Ca²⁺ + ↑ PO₄³⁻ → chronic PTH drive → bone resorption. Treat with calcitriol + phosphate binders.

**DiGeorge syndrome** (22q11.2 deletion): failure of 3rd + 4th pharyngeal pouches → no parathyroids (hypocalcemia + tetany) + no thymus (T-cell deficiency) + cardiac defects + cleft palate. **"CATCH-22"**.
</Clinical>

## Function reference - every component

| Structure | Function | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Thyroid follicles** | Store and release T3/T4 | Goiter = follicular hyperplasia; nodules can be benign or malignant |
| **Follicular cells** | Synthesize thyroid hormone | Target of Graves antibodies (TSI), Hashimoto antibodies (anti-TPO, anti-Tg) |
| **Colloid** | Extracellular store of thyroglobulin-bound hormone | Thyroglobulin used as **tumor marker** post-thyroidectomy for papillary/follicular cancer |
| **Parafollicular C cells** | Make calcitonin | Source of **medullary thyroid carcinoma** (sporadic + MEN2A/B); calcitonin = tumor marker |
| **Recurrent laryngeal n.** | Motor to intrinsic laryngeal muscles (except cricothyroid) | Surgical injury → vocal cord paralysis |
| **External laryngeal n.** | Motor to cricothyroid | Injury → can't pitch voice high |
| **Chief cells (parathyroid)** | Secrete PTH | Target of CaSR - cinacalcet activates this receptor to suppress PTH |
| **CaSR (calcium-sensing receptor)** | On chief cells + thick ascending limb of kidney | Mutations: familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia (loss-of-function), autosomal dominant hypocalcemia (gain-of-function) |
| **Pyramidal lobe** (50%) | Embryonic remnant of thyroglossal duct extending up from isthmus | Site of ectopic thyroid; thyroglossal duct cyst occurs along this midline tract |

## Why this matters

<HighYield variant="boards">
- **TSH** is the single best screen for thyroid disease. High TSH = hypothyroid; low TSH = hyperthyroid (assuming intact pituitary).
- **Hashimoto** (lymphocytic thyroiditis): hypothyroidism + **anti-TPO + anti-thyroglobulin antibodies** + ↑ TSH + ↓ free T4. Histology: lymphoid follicles with germinal centers; Hürthle cells.
- **Graves** disease: hyperthyroidism + ophthalmopathy + pretibial myxedema. Cause: **TSI (TSH receptor-stimulating Ig)**. Histology: scalloped colloid, tall columnar follicular cells.
- **Thyroid storm**: extreme thyrotoxicosis (HR > 140, T > 39°C, AMS). Treat with: **propranolol** (β-block + ↓ T4→T3) + **PTU** (↓ synthesis + ↓ peripheral conversion) + **iodide** (Wolff-Chaikoff) - but iodide AFTER PTU (otherwise it loads the thyroid). Plus glucocorticoids.
- **Subacute (de Quervain's) thyroiditis**: post-viral, painful, tender thyroid, ↑ ESR. Triphasic course: hyper → hypo → euthyroid. NSAIDs ± steroids.
- **Papillary thyroid cancer**: most common (80%); psammoma bodies, Orphan Annie nuclei, lymphatic spread; excellent prognosis.
- **Hypocalcemia signs**: **Trousseau** (carpal spasm with BP cuff) + **Chvostek** (facial twitch with tap on facial n.) + perioral tingling. Cause = post-thyroidectomy parathyroid injury until proven otherwise.
- **Primary hyperparathyroidism workup**: ↑ Ca²⁺ + ↑ PTH = adenoma (85%) > hyperplasia (15%) > carcinoma (rare). Localize with sestamibi scan; minimally-invasive parathyroidectomy.
</HighYield>
