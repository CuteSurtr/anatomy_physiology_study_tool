These structures are easy to under-cover because they are small or embedded in other organs, but the lecture puts them on the same priority tier as the bigger endocrine glands. Together they explain **pituitary control**, **circadian timing**, and **minute-to-minute glucose regulation**.

## Hypothalamus

The **hypothalamus** sits inferior and medial to the thalamus. It is the endocrine system's upstream command center because its releasing hormones enter the **hypophyseal portal vessels** and directly regulate the anterior pituitary.

<Figure name="endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary-portal-slide" caption="Lecture slide simplifying the hypothalamus-to-pituitary portal circuit that carries releasing hormones into the anterior pituitary." />

High-yield releasing hormones from the slides:

- **CRH** → ACTH
- **TRH** → TSH
- **GnRH** → FSH and LH

That is why the lecture repeatedly groups the **hypothalamus, pituitary stalk, and anterior pituitary** together instead of teaching them as isolated structures.

## Pineal gland

<Figure name="endocrine/pineal-gland-slide" caption="Lecture slide locating the pineal gland posterior to the thalamus and linking it to melatonin secretion." />

The **pineal gland** is a tiny midline structure posterior to the thalamus, but it matters because it secretes **melatonin**, which helps align sleep-wake cycles with light exposure. The lecture uses it as a reminder that not every endocrine organ is large or bilateral.

## Pancreatic islets

The pancreas is mostly an **exocrine digestive gland**, but its pale-staining **islets of Langerhans** are the endocrine portion.

<Figure name="endocrine/pancreas-islet-slide" caption="Lecture slide showing an endocrine islet embedded inside much darker exocrine pancreatic tissue." />

Key cell types from the lecture:

| Cell | Hormone | Main effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Alpha** | Glucagon | Raises blood glucose |
| **Beta** | Insulin | Lowers blood glucose |
| **Delta** | Somatostatin | Inhibits both alpha and beta cells |

The slide deck emphasizes the contrast:

- **Exocrine pancreas** secretes digestive enzymes into ducts.
- **Endocrine pancreas** secretes hormones into blood.

## Why these glands matter together

<Figure name="endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary" caption="" />


These organs fill lecture-coverage gaps that are easy to miss when a site only focuses on pituitary, thyroid, and adrenal anatomy:

- **Hypothalamus** explains portal control of the anterior pituitary.
- **Pineal gland** connects neuroanatomy to endocrine timing.
- **Pancreatic islets** connect anatomy directly to glucose homeostasis and diabetes.
