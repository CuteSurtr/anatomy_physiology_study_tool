The prescription "**Sig**" (signa = "let it be labeled") tells the patient how to take the drug. Many abbreviations come from Latin and are commonly handwritten on Rx pads even today.

## Standard Rx anatomy

A typical handwritten/electronic prescription contains:

1. **Patient info**: name, date of birth, address.
2. **Date**.
3. **Rx symbol**: "Rx" (Latin "recipe").
4. **Drug + strength + dosage form**: e.g., "Lisinopril 10 mg tablets."
5. **Sig**: directions to patient. "Take 1 tablet by mouth daily."
6. **Disp**: quantity. "Disp: #90 (ninety)."
7. **Refills**: "Refills: 3."
8. **DAW**: dispense as written (no generic substitution).
9. **Prescriber info**: name, DEA #, signature.
10. **Indication**: optional but reduces errors.

Example:

```
Lisinopril 10 mg tablets
Sig: Take 1 tablet PO daily for hypertension
Disp: #90 (ninety)
Refills: 3
```

## Controlled substance schedules (DEA)

| Schedule | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Heroin, LSD, marijuana (federal), psilocybin | No medical use (federal); research only |
| **II** | Oxycodone, hydrocodone, morphine, fentanyl, methylphenidate, amphetamines | No refills - new Rx each time |
| **III** | Codeine combos, ketamine, buprenorphine, anabolic steroids | Up to 5 refills × 6 months |
| **IV** | Benzodiazepines, tramadol, zolpidem, modafinil | Same |
| **V** | Pregabalin, low-dose codeine (Robitussin AC), lacosamide | Same |

**Controlled Rx requirements** (CII): DEA number, written + signed (electronic acceptable in most states), specific quantity (no "PRN"), legible.

## ⚠ Abbreviations to AVOID (Joint Commission / ISMP)

These cause real medication errors:

| Avoid | Problem | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| **U, IU** | Mistaken for 0, 4, cc | "unit" |
| **QD, q.d.** | Confused with QID | "daily" |
| **QOD** | Confused with QID, QD | "every other day" |
| **Trailing zero** (5.0 mg) | Looks like 50 | "5 mg" |
| **No leading zero** (.5 mg) | Looks like 5 | "0.5 mg" |
| **MS, MSO₄, MgSO₄** | Morphine vs magnesium | Spell out |
| **µg** | Confused with mg | "mcg" or "microgram" |
| **HS** | Half-strength vs bedtime | Spell out |
| **TIW** | Confused with TID, BIW | "3 times weekly" |
| **D/C** | Discharge vs discontinue | Spell out |
| **cc** | Confused with U | "mL" |
| **AD, AS, AU** | Right/left/both ears | Spell out |
| **OD, OS, OU** | Right/left/both eyes | Spell out |
| **&lt;, &gt;** | Confused with each other | "less than" / "greater than" |
| **@** | Mistaken for 2 | "at" |
| **&** | Mistaken for 2 | "and" |

## How to read a Sig

"Take 1 tab PO q6h prn pain, NTE 4 g/d" =
"Take one tablet by mouth every 6 hours as needed for pain, not to exceed 4 grams per day."

"Apply 2 gtt OU BID × 7 d" (in old-school Rx) =
"Apply 2 drops to both eyes twice daily for 7 days." (Modern: spell out OU.)

"Inject 5 units SC BID with meals and 10 units SC QHS" =
"Inject 5 units subcutaneously twice daily with meals + 10 units at bedtime."

## Patient counseling shorthand

| Abbreviation | What to tell patient |
|---|---|
| **HS** | "Take at bedtime" |
| **AC** | "Take before meals" |
| **PC** | "Take after meals" |
| **PRN** | "Take only as needed for [indication]" |
| **STAT** | "Right now" |
| **30 min before food** | Levothyroxine, alendronate |
| **With food** | NSAIDs, metformin XR (reduce GI), itraconazole capsule, posaconazole suspension |
| **Empty stomach** | Many antibiotics, levothyroxine |

- "**PO**" = by mouth (per os).
- "**QHS**" = at bedtime (use this; not "HS").
- "**STAT**" = immediately.
- "**QD**" + "**QOD**" = avoid (spell out "daily" / "every other day").
- "**U**" = avoid (write "unit").
- "**cc**" = avoid (write "mL").
- "**µg**" = avoid (write "mcg").
- "**MS**" = avoid (write "morphine sulfate" or "magnesium sulfate").
- Trailing zero (5.0) = NEVER. Leading zero (0.5) = ALWAYS.
- Schedule II → no refills, new Rx each time.
- Schedule III-V → up to 5 refills within 6 months.
- DEA number required for controlled substances.
