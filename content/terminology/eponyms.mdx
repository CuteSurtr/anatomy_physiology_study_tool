Eponyms = terms named after someone (physician, anatomist, or rarely patient). Modern style increasingly avoids possessive 's, but usage is inconsistent. Many eponyms are being replaced with descriptive terms (e.g., "Wegener granulomatosis" → "GPA"), but the eponyms remain in heavy use clinically.

## Eponym vs descriptive shift

Many fields have moved (or are moving) toward descriptive terms over eponyms (for clarity, neutrality):

| Old | New (sort of) |
|---|---|
| **Wegener granulomatosis** | GPA (granulomatosis with polyangiitis) |
| **Churg-Strauss syndrome** | EGPA |
| **Christmas disease** | Hemophilia B |
| **Stein-Leventhal** | PCOS |
| **Reiter syndrome** | Reactive arthritis |
| **Wernicke encephalopathy** | Thiamine deficiency-related encephalopathy (still used) |

Most eponyms persist because the descriptive name is too unwieldy.

## Possessive vs non-possessive

AMA Manual prefers non-possessive: **Down syndrome**, **Crohn disease**, **Hodgkin lymphoma**. Many texts and journals use possessive: **Down's**, **Crohn's**, **Hodgkin's**. Both occur; consistency matters more than which.
