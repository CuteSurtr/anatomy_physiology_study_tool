## Calcineurin inhibitors

**Cyclosporine, Tacrolimus**

**Mechanism**: bind cyclophilin (cyclosporine) or FKBP-12 (tacrolimus) → block calcineurin → ↓ NFAT-driven IL-2 transcription → ↓ T-cell activation.

**Use**: solid organ + HSCT immunosuppression; psoriasis; uveitis; severe atopic dermatitis.

**Side effects**:
- **Nephrotoxicity** (chronic afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction) - main long-term issue.
- **HTN**, **hyperlipidemia**, **new-onset diabetes** (tacro > cyclo for diabetes).
- **Neurotoxicity** - tremor, PRES, headache (tacro).
- **Gingival hyperplasia, hirsutism** (cyclosporine).
- **Alopecia** (tacrolimus).
- **CYP3A4 substrate** - watch interactions (azoles, erythromycin, grapefruit ↑ levels; rifampin ↓).
- **TDM**: trough levels essential (narrow TI).

## mTOR inhibitors

**Sirolimus (Rapamycin), Everolimus**

**Mechanism**: bind FKBP-12 → block mTOR → ↓ cell proliferation.

**Use**: transplant (often replacing calcineurin for renal sparing), rare benefits in cancer (RCC, breast - everolimus), tuberous sclerosis (subependymal giant cell astrocytoma), drug-eluting stents.

**Side effects**:
- **Hyperlipidemia** (significant).
- **Cytopenias**.
- **Stomatitis, mucositis**.
- **Poor wound healing** - hold around surgery.
- **Pneumonitis**.
- **Proteinuria**.
- Edema.

## Antimetabolite immunosuppressants

### Mycophenolate (MMF, MPA)

**Mechanism**: inhibits inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (IMPDH) → ↓ purine synthesis (selective for lymphocytes that lack salvage pathway).

**Use**: solid organ + HSCT; SLE nephritis; vasculitis; autoimmune disease.

**Side effects**:
- **GI** (diarrhea, abdominal pain - dose-limiting).
- Marrow suppression.
- Infection.
- **Teratogen** - REMS program for women of childbearing age.

### Azathioprine

**Mechanism**: prodrug → 6-MP → blocks purine synthesis.

**Use**: IBD, RA, transplant maintenance, autoimmune (SLE, vasculitis).

**Side effects**:
- Marrow suppression.
- Hepatotoxicity.
- Pancreatitis.
- **Allopurinol interaction** - blocks 6-MP metabolism → ↑ toxicity; reduce dose 50-75%.
- **TPMT polymorphism** - check before starting (poor metabolizers → severe marrow toxicity).

## Methotrexate

(See [antineoplastics](/pharmacology/antineoplastics) for high-dose details.)

Low-dose weekly in: RA, psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, JIA, IBD (Crohn's). Always with **folic acid** to reduce GI + hematologic side effects.

## Leflunomide

DHODH inhibitor → ↓ pyrimidine synthesis. Use: RA, PsA.

Side effects: GI, hepatotoxicity, marrow, alopecia, **teratogen** (long t½ → cholestyramine washout needed before conception).

## Hydroxychloroquine + Chloroquine

**Mechanism**: ↑ lysosomal pH → blocks antigen processing.

**Use**: malaria, RA, **SLE** (cornerstone), Sjögren.

**Side effects**:
- **Retinopathy** (irreversible) - dose-related; baseline + annual ophtho exam.
- QT prolongation (controversy with azithromycin).
- Hemolysis G6PD.

## Sulfasalazine

Prodrug → sulfapyridine + 5-aminosalicylate (anti-inflammatory).

**Use**: IBD (esp. UC), RA, PsA.

**Side effects**: GI, photosensitivity, reversible male infertility, hemolysis G6PD, sulfa allergy.

## TNF-α inhibitors

| Drug | Format |
|---|---|
| **Infliximab** | IV chimeric mAb |
| **Adalimumab** | SC humanized mAb (Humira) |
| **Etanercept** | SC fusion protein (soluble TNFR-Fc) |
| **Certolizumab pegol** | Pegylated Fab; no Fc → safe in pregnancy |
| **Golimumab** | SC monthly |

**Use**: RA, PsA, AS, IBD (Crohn's > UC), psoriasis. Not for HF (worsens). Not GPA (etanercept ineffective).

**Side effects**:
- **Reactivation of TB** - screen + treat LTBI before starting.
- Reactivation hepatitis B - screen.
- Infection risk in general (esp. opportunistic).
- **Demyelinating disease** (MS, optic neuritis).
- **Lupus-like syndrome**.
- **HF worsening**.
- Lymphoma signal (esp. hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma in young IBD males - rare).
- **Anti-drug antibodies** can cause loss of efficacy.

**Etanercept** - no IBD efficacy; binds only soluble TNF.

## JAK inhibitors

**Tofacitinib, Baricitinib, Upadacitinib, Filgotinib** (JAK1/2 primarily); **Ruxolitinib** (JAK1/2 for myelofibrosis, PV, GVHD).

**Use**: RA, PsA, UC, AD, alopecia areata, COVID-19 (baricitinib).

**Side effects (boxed)**:
- Serious infections (TB, fungal, herpes zoster - vaccinate before).
- **Thrombosis** (VTE, ATE).
- Malignancy.
- Major adverse CV events.

ORAL Surveillance trial → JAK inhibitors → ↑ MACE + cancer + thrombosis vs anti-TNF in RA patients >50 with CV risk factors → use with caution.

## B-cell targeted

### Rituximab

Anti-CD20 mAb. Use: NHL, CLL, RA, GPA + MPA (vasculitis), pemphigus vulgaris, MS, lupus nephritis (alt).

Side effects: infusion reactions (pretreat with steroids, antihistamine), **HBV reactivation** (screen!), **PML** (rare), late-onset neutropenia, hypogammaglobulinemia.

### Belimumab

Anti-BAFF (BLyS) mAb. Use: SLE (cuts BAFF → ↓ B cell survival).

### Ocrelizumab, Ofatumumab

Newer anti-CD20 for MS.

### Anifrolumab

Anti-type I interferon receptor. Use: SLE.

## Complement inhibitors

**Eculizumab, Ravulizumab** - anti-C5. Block formation of MAC.

Use: **PNH** (paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria), **atypical HUS**, generalized myasthenia gravis (AChR Ab+), neuromyelitis optica.

Side effects: **meningococcal disease** (vaccinate Men ACWY + Men B 2 weeks before starting, then prophylactic penicillin).

**Pegcetacoplan** - anti-C3 (broader); PNH.

## IVIG (intravenous immunoglobulin)

Pool of plasma IgG from thousands of donors.

**Use**: replacement (CVID, X-linked agammaglobulinemia, hypogammaglobulinemia post-transplant), **autoimmune** (ITP, Kawasaki, Guillain-Barré, CIDP, myasthenia crisis, dermatomyositis), passive immunization.

**Side effects**: thrombosis, AKI, hypersensitivity, aseptic meningitis, hemolysis (if patient is non-A/B/O), anaphylaxis in **IgA deficiency** (recipient has anti-IgA antibodies → rare).

## Antibody-based induction (transplant)

- **Antithymocyte globulin (ATG, Thymoglobulin)** - polyclonal anti-T cell. Induction or rejection.
- **Basiliximab** - anti-CD25 (IL-2 receptor); induction (less toxic than ATG).
- **Alemtuzumab** - anti-CD52; profound lymphocyte depletion.

**Side effects (ATG)**: cytokine release syndrome (premedicate with steroids, antihistamine, acetaminophen), profound cytopenias, infection, serum sickness.

## DMARDs at a glance

Disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs - alter disease progression in RA + spondyloarthritis.

**Conventional**: methotrexate, sulfasalazine, hydroxychloroquine, leflunomide.

**Biologic**: anti-TNF, anti-IL-6, abatacept, rituximab.

**Targeted synthetic**: JAK inhibitors.

**RA treatment ladder (2021 ACR)**:
1. Methotrexate.
2. Add biologic or JAK inhibitor + MTX (or switch).
3. Switch biologic class.

## Vaccines + immunosuppression

**Live vaccines** generally **contraindicated** during immunosuppression: MMR, varicella, zoster (live - Zostavax; Shingrix recombinant is safe), oral polio, yellow fever, BCG, oral typhoid, intranasal flu.

**Inactivated/recombinant/subunit safe**: pneumococcal, IM influenza, HPV, hepatitis A/B, meningococcal, Tdap, Shingrix.

**Timing**:
- Live vaccines: 4 weeks before immunosuppression starts OR delay until off therapy.
- Inactivated: ideally 2-4 weeks before treatment for best response.

## Inflammatory bowel disease step-up

1. **5-ASA** (mesalamine, sulfasalazine).
2. **Glucocorticoids** (acute).
3. **Immunomodulator** (azathioprine, MTX).
4. **Biologic** (anti-TNF, vedolizumab, ustekinumab, risankizumab).
5. **JAK** (tofacitinib for UC; upadacitinib).
6. **S1P modulator** (ozanimod for UC).
7. **Surgery** (UC - colectomy curative; Crohn's - segmental).

<Clinical>
**RA newly diagnosed**: methotrexate + folic acid + NSAID/glucocorticoid bridge. Add biologic at 3-6 months if not at target (CDAI &lt;10).

**SLE flare with renal involvement**: induction = mycophenolate + steroids (cyclophosphamide alternative for severe). Maintenance = mycophenolate, azathioprine, hydroxychloroquine baseline.

**ITP severe with bleeding**: IVIG 1 g/kg + IV methylprednisolone. Rituximab or thrombopoietin-receptor agonist (eltrombopag, romiplostim) for refractory.

**Transplant immunosuppression typical**: induction (basiliximab or ATG), then maintenance with **calcineurin inhibitor** + **antimetabolite** + steroid. Steroid-free protocols increasingly common.

**Post-transplant infection prophylaxis**: TMP-SMX (PCP, toxo, listeria, nocardia) + valganciclovir (CMV) + nystatin oral (candida) + acyclovir (HSV/VZV in CMV-).
</Clinical>
