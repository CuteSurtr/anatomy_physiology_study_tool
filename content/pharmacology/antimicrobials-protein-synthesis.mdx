## 30S subunit inhibitors

### Aminoglycosides

**Gentamicin, Tobramycin, Amikacin, Streptomycin, Neomycin, Plazomicin (newest, vs ESBL/CRE)**

**Mechanism**: bind 30S → ↓ initiation complex + ↑ misreading mRNA → faulty proteins. **Bactericidal** (concentration-dependent killing).

**Spectrum**: Gram-negatives (esp. Pseudomonas, Enterobacterales). NO anaerobic activity (requires O2 for uptake). Synergistic with cell-wall agents for Gram-positive endocarditis (S. viridans, Enterococcus).

**Special uses**:
- **Streptomycin**: TB (2nd line), plague, tularemia.
- **Neomycin**: PO for bowel prep + hepatic encephalopathy (not absorbed); topical.
- **Tobramycin**: inhaled for cystic fibrosis Pseudomonas.
- **Plazomicin**: MDR Enterobacterales (incl. CRE).

**Pharmacology**:
- IV (poor oral absorption).
- Once-daily dosing (5-7 mg/kg) preferred - dose-dependent killing + post-antibiotic effect; lowers nephrotoxicity vs divided dosing.
- Monitor: peak (efficacy) + trough (toxicity); often empiric for short courses (&lt;5 d).
- Renally cleared - adjust for CrCl.

**Side effects**:
- **Nephrotoxicity** - proximal tubular necrosis; reversible if caught. ↑ with high troughs, longer duration, advanced age, contrast, other nephrotoxins.
- **Ototoxicity** - **irreversible**; vestibular (gentamicin, streptomycin) vs cochlear (amikacin, neomycin). Cumulative dose matters.
- **NMJ blockade** - rare; potentiates myasthenia gravis; avoid with neuromuscular blockers.
- **Teratogenic** - cranial nerve VIII damage (pregnancy avoid).
- **Resistance** = β-lactamase-like enzymes (AAC, ANT, APH) - inactivate the drug.

### Tetracyclines

**Doxycycline, Tetracycline, Minocycline, Tigecycline (glycylcycline), Eravacycline, Omadacycline**

**Mechanism**: bind 30S → block tRNA binding to A site. **Bacteriostatic**.

**Spectrum**: broad - atypicals (Rickettsia, Chlamydia, Mycoplasma, Coxiella, Ehrlichia), spirochetes (Borrelia, Leptospira), Vibrio, Listeria (alt), some MRSA (doxycycline, minocycline).

**Uses**:
- **Doxycycline** = workhorse: CAP atypical coverage, Lyme, Rickettsia (RMSF), Chlamydia, malaria prophylaxis, anthrax prophylaxis, acne, MRSA skin (CA-MRSA).
- **Minocycline** = acne; some MRSA + atypicals; nocardiosis.
- **Tigecycline** = broad MDR; **avoid in bloodstream/HAP/VAP** (mortality signal); use for complicated intra-abdominal, skin.
- **Eravacycline, Omadacycline** = next-gen; complicated UTI, intra-abdominal.

**Pharmacology**: PO (doxycycline excellent bioavailability), IV options. Hepatic clearance for most (safe in renal failure). **Doxycycline** preferred in renal failure.

**Side effects**:
- **Tooth/bone discoloration** in **children &lt;8** + **pregnancy** (3rd trimester) - permanent yellowing of teeth.
- **Photosensitivity** - severe sunburn.
- **GI** - esophagitis (take with full glass of water, stay upright), nausea.
- **Hepatotoxicity** - rare; fatty liver in pregnancy with IV high-dose.
- **Pseudotumor cerebri** (rare) - tetracycline, minocycline.
- **Vestibular** (minocycline).
- **DRESS, drug-induced lupus** (minocycline).
- **Fanconi syndrome** with expired/degraded tetracycline (historical).

**Interactions**: chelated by polyvalent cations (Ca, Mg, Fe, Al, Zn) - take 2 h apart from dairy, antacids, iron, multivitamins.

## 50S subunit inhibitors

### Macrolides

**Azithromycin, Erythromycin, Clarithromycin, Fidaxomicin**

**Mechanism**: bind 50S (23S rRNA) → block peptide chain elongation. **Bacteriostatic** (cidal at high concentrations vs sensitive organisms).

**Spectrum**: Gram+ (less than penicillins), atypical bacteria (Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, Legionella - hence CAP coverage), H. pylori (clarithromycin), MAC (azithromycin), pertussis (azithromycin).

**Drug-specific**:
- **Azithromycin**: long t½ (3-day, 5-day courses; "Z-Pak"); 1g single dose for chlamydia (now doxycycline preferred). CV death risk (QT) in elderly.
- **Erythromycin**: GI motility (prokinetic - sometimes used for gastroparesis); least preferred (GI side effects, QT, many CYP3A4 interactions).
- **Clarithromycin**: H. pylori, MAC. BID dosing.
- **Fidaxomicin**: C. difficile (poor systemic absorption - stays in colon); fewer recurrences than vancomycin.

**Side effects**:
- **QT prolongation** - especially erythromycin, clarithromycin; risk of torsades.
- **GI** - nausea, vomiting (erythromycin worst - motilin agonist).
- **Hepatotoxicity** - cholestatic, especially estolate form.
- **Drug interactions** - **CYP3A4 inhibition** (erythromycin > clarithromycin > azithromycin minimal): ↑ statins, warfarin, theophylline, carbamazepine, CCBs.
- **Hearing loss** (rare) - prolonged use.

### Clindamycin

**Mechanism**: 50S binding (same site as macrolides → cross-resistance, **D-test** to check).

**Spectrum**: anaerobes **above the diaphragm** + Gram-positives (Strep, MSSA, some CA-MRSA). Toxin-mediated infections (toxic shock syndrome, necrotizing fasciitis - suppresses toxin production at ribosome).

**Uses**:
- **Anaerobic infections** above diaphragm (lung abscess, dental).
- **Toxic shock**, **necrotizing fasciitis** - combine with β-lactam (penicillin G, piperacillin-tazobactam) for toxin suppression.
- **Acne** (topical).
- **Aspiration pneumonia** (anaerobes).
- **PCP** (with primaquine) - alternative to TMP-SMX.
- **MRSA skin** (oral option).

**Side effects**:
- **C. difficile** - classic association (any antibiotic can, but clindamycin classic).
- Rash, GI upset.

### Chloramphenicol

**Mechanism**: 50S binding.

**Spectrum**: broad - Gram+, Gram-, anaerobes, spirochetes, Rickettsia, atypicals.

**Use**: rarely in US (rocky mountain spotted fever in pregnancy alt; eye drops; meningitis 2nd-line). More used in developing countries (typhoid, plague, meningitis).

**Side effects** (why rarely used):
- **Aplastic anemia** (idiosyncratic; 1/25,000-40,000) - **irreversible, often fatal**.
- **Dose-related marrow suppression** (reversible).
- **Gray baby syndrome** in neonates (↓ glucuronidation → drug accumulates → cardiovascular collapse).

### Linezolid + Tedizolid

**Oxazolidinones** - bind 50S; block formation of 70S initiation complex.

**Spectrum**: Gram-positives **including MRSA, VRE**. Some mycobacteria (off-label for resistant TB).

**Uses**: complicated skin infections, hospital pneumonia (incl. MRSA), VRE infections.

**Pharmacology**: oral + IV (100% bioavailability), good lung penetration.

**Side effects**:
- **Reversible MAO inhibition** → **serotonin syndrome** with SSRI, SNRI, TCA, tramadol, meperidine.
- **Thrombocytopenia** - dose-related; check CBC weekly; reversible.
- **Peripheral neuropathy, optic neuropathy** - prolonged use.
- **Lactic acidosis** (rare).
- **Tedizolid** - newer, fewer hematologic side effects.

### Streptogramins (Quinupristin-Dalfopristin)

50S binding; **VRE bacteremia** option. IV only; phlebitis common. Arthralgia/myalgia. Hyperbilirubinemia.

Largely replaced by linezolid + daptomycin.

## Antimicrobial pearls

### Bone penetration

- Excellent: ciprofloxacin, clindamycin, doxycycline, fluoroquinolones, linezolid.
- Adequate: TMP-SMX, vancomycin.
- Poor: many cephalosporins (need higher doses or longer course).

### CSF penetration

- Excellent: 3rd-gen cephalosporins (ceftriaxone, cefotaxime), 4th-gen (cefepime), meropenem (not imipenem), chloramphenicol, fluoroquinolones, TMP-SMX, fluconazole, isoniazid, pyrazinamide.
- With inflammation only: penicillins, ampicillin, vancomycin (need higher doses for meningitis).
- Poor: aminoglycosides, 1st/2nd-gen cephalosporins, clindamycin, daptomycin, macrolides.

### Pregnancy categories

- **Safe**: penicillins, cephalosporins, azithromycin, nitrofurantoin (avoid 3rd trim), metronidazole (avoid 1st trim), fosfomycin, vancomycin, clindamycin.
- **Avoid**: tetracyclines (tooth, bone), fluoroquinolones (cartilage), aminoglycosides (CN VIII), sulfa (kernicterus near term), TMP (folate antagonist).

### Antibiotics that ↓ OC efficacy

Only **rifampin** is clearly proven to ↓ contraceptive efficacy via CYP induction. Other antibiotics' historical concerns are largely refuted, but **counsel anyway**.

### Disulfiram-like reactions

- Metronidazole (1-2 days post-last dose).
- Cefotetan, cefoxitin (cephalosporins with methylthiotetrazole side chain).
- TMP-SMX (rarely).

### Tendon rupture

Fluoroquinolones (Achilles especially); risk ↑ age >60, steroids, organ transplant.

<Clinical>
**Doxycycline for tick-borne illness in a kid &lt;8**: AAP + CDC now consider this safe for short courses (≤21 days) - no significant tooth staining in modern doses. Untreated RMSF is fatal; old "don't use in kids" advice is being revised.

**Patient on linezolid + SSRI**: hold SSRI 2 weeks before starting linezolid (or fluoxetine 5 weeks). If urgent: choose tedizolid or alternative class.

**Aminoglycoside synergy for endocarditis**: low-dose gentamicin (1 mg/kg q8h) PLUS β-lactam for first 2 weeks of treatment in S. viridans or enterococcus endocarditis. Older synergistic dosing - not the high-dose nephrotoxic regimen.

**C. diff treatment 2024**: vancomycin PO 125 mg QID or fidaxomicin PO 200 mg BID × 10 days. Add IV metronidazole for severe/fulminant. Bezlotoxumab (anti-toxin B antibody) for recurrences. Fecal microbiota transplant for refractory.

**Doxycycline in elderly with hyponatremia from SIADH**: doxycycline can be used to treat SIADH (induces nephrogenic DI). Classic boards finding.
</Clinical>
