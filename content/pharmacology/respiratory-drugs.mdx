## Asthma + COPD overview

### Inhaler classes

| Class | Examples | Onset | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SABA** (short-acting β2) | Albuterol, Levalbuterol | 5 min | 4-6 h |
| **LABA** (long-acting β2) | Salmeterol, Formoterol, Vilanterol | 5-30 min | 12-24 h |
| **SAMA** (short-acting muscarinic antagonist) | Ipratropium | 15 min | 4-6 h |
| **LAMA** (long-acting muscarinic antagonist) | Tiotropium, Umeclidinium, Aclidinium, Glycopyrronium | 30-60 min | 24 h |
| **ICS** (inhaled corticosteroid) | Beclomethasone, Budesonide, Fluticasone, Mometasone, Ciclesonide | days | maintenance |

### Asthma stepwise (GINA 2024)

GINA now recommends **ICS-formoterol** as both **maintenance + reliever** (MART) for adults + adolescents. SABA alone is no longer recommended.

| Step | Therapy |
|---|---|
| **1-2** | Low-dose ICS-formoterol PRN |
| **3** | Low-dose ICS-formoterol maintenance + reliever |
| **4** | Medium-dose ICS-formoterol maintenance + reliever |
| **5** | High-dose ICS + LABA + LAMA + consider biologic, oral steroid, allergen immunotherapy |

### COPD stepwise (GOLD 2024)

Initial therapy based on **GOLD ABE** group (symptom + exacerbation):
- **A** (mild symptoms, infrequent exacerbations): bronchodilator (SABA, SAMA, LABA, or LAMA).
- **B** (more symptoms, infrequent exacerbations): LABA + LAMA combo.
- **E** (frequent exacerbations): LABA + LAMA + ICS if eosinophils ≥300 or asthma overlap; otherwise LABA + LAMA.

ICS **should NOT** be used alone for COPD (asthma exception). Risk of pneumonia with ICS in COPD.

### β2 agonists - mechanism + side effects

**Mechanism**: β2 agonism → Gs → ↑ cAMP → smooth muscle relaxation.

**Side effects**:
- **Tremor** (skeletal muscle β2).
- **Tachycardia** (some β1 activity at high doses).
- **Hypokalemia** (K shift intracellular - useful in hyperK; risk with combined with insulin).
- **Tachyphylaxis / receptor downregulation** with chronic use.
- LABA alone in asthma → ↑ mortality - **must combine with ICS**.

### Muscarinic antagonists

Block M3 → bronchodilation + ↓ secretions.

**Tiotropium** - 24-h LAMA workhorse for COPD; adjunct in severe asthma.

**Ipratropium** - acute SAMA combined with SABA (DuoNeb) in severe acute asthma + COPD exacerbations.

Side effects: dry mouth (most common), urinary retention, narrow-angle glaucoma exacerbation, paradoxical bronchospasm (rare).

### Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS)

**Mechanism**: ↓ inflammation; ↓ Th2 cytokines; ↓ leukocyte trafficking.

**Effects** require regular use (NOT rescue).

**Side effects**:
- **Oral candidiasis (thrush)** - rinse mouth + use spacer.
- **Dysphonia** (hoarseness).
- **Adrenal suppression** at high doses + cumulative use - especially fluticasone in kids.
- **Growth velocity** slightly reduced in kids (mild; benefit outweighs).
- **Cataracts, glaucoma** with chronic high dose.
- **Pneumonia** in COPD (esp. fluticasone).
- **Easy bruising, skin thinning** rarely with very high doses.

### Combination inhalers

Common single-device combinations:
- **ICS-LABA**: Symbicort (budesonide-formoterol), Advair (fluticasone-salmeterol), Wixela, Breo (fluticasone-vilanterol).
- **LABA-LAMA**: Anoro (umeclidinium-vilanterol), Stiolto (olodaterol-tiotropium), Bevespi (formoterol-glycopyrronium).
- **Triple (ICS-LABA-LAMA)**: Trelegy (fluticasone-umeclidinium-vilanterol), Breztri (budesonide-glycopyrronium-formoterol).

## Oral asthma controllers

### Leukotriene modifiers

| Drug | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Montelukast** | CysLT1 receptor antagonist |
| **Zafirlukast** | CysLT1 receptor antagonist |
| **Zileuton** | 5-lipoxygenase inhibitor (blocks LT synthesis) |

**Use**: asthma (less effective than ICS - generally add-on), **exercise-induced bronchospasm**, **allergic rhinitis**, **aspirin-sensitive asthma (AERD)**.

**Side effects**:
- **Montelukast neuropsychiatric** - depression, suicidality, behavior change (FDA boxed warning, 2020) - especially kids.
- Zileuton → hepatotoxicity (LFT monitoring).
- Churg-Strauss / EGPA - rare emergence on LTRA (probably unmasked by steroid taper).

### Theophylline

**Mechanism**: PDE inhibition + adenosine receptor antagonism → ↑ cAMP → bronchodilation; ↑ diaphragm contractility.

**Use**: largely abandoned (narrow TI); occasionally add-on for severe COPD or asthma.

**Side effects**:
- Narrow TI (10-20 mcg/mL).
- N/V, headache, tachycardia, **seizures + arrhythmias** at toxic levels.
- Many drug interactions (CYP1A2 substrate - smoking ↓ levels; cipro, fluvoxamine ↑).

**Toxicity treatment**: activated charcoal, β-blocker for arrhythmia (paradoxically; or BZD for seizure), hemodialysis.

### Mast cell stabilizers

**Cromolyn, Nedocromil** - prevent mast cell degranulation. Prophylactic only. Largely abandoned for asthma; cromolyn still used for **mastocytosis** + topical for allergic conjunctivitis/rhinitis.

### Anticholinergics

Tiotropium added in step-up severe asthma (TIOSPIR + similar trials).

## Asthma biologics

(See [immunopharmacology](/pharmacology/immunopharmacology) for full table.)

| Drug | Target | Phenotype |
|---|---|---|
| **Omalizumab** | IgE | Allergic asthma, ↑ IgE |
| **Mepolizumab, Reslizumab** | IL-5 | Eosinophilic |
| **Benralizumab** | IL-5Rα | Eosinophilic (depletes eos) |
| **Dupilumab** | IL-4Rα | Eosinophilic + Th2 high (also AD, EoE, CRSwNP) |
| **Tezepelumab** | TSLP | Broad - eosinophilic + non-eosinophilic |

## Acute asthma exacerbation

1. **SABA + ipratropium** nebulized (back-to-back × 3).
2. **Systemic glucocorticoid** PO (prednisone 40-50 mg) or IV (methylprednisolone if can't take PO).
3. **Magnesium sulfate** IV 2 g if severe (smooth muscle relaxation).
4. **Heliox** (helium-oxygen) - rare.
5. **Intubation** if refractory respiratory failure (challenging - high airway pressures, breath-stacking risk).

## COPD exacerbation

1. **SABA + ipratropium** (DuoNeb).
2. **Systemic glucocorticoid** PO/IV (REDUCE - 5 days adequate).
3. **Antibiotic** if any 2 of 3: ↑ sputum, ↑ purulence, ↑ dyspnea, or vent support → amoxicillin-clav, macrolide, or doxycycline.
4. **NIPPV** for hypercapnic respiratory failure.
5. Oxygen titrated to SpO₂ 88-92 (avoid CO₂ retention from over-oxygenation).

## Smoking cessation

- **Behavioral counseling**.
- **NRT** (patch + gum/lozenge combo).
- **Varenicline** (α4β2 partial agonist) - best efficacy.
- **Bupropion** - DA/NE reuptake; also helps depression.
- **Cytisine** (newer, not FDA-approved US).

## Antitussives + expectorants

### Antitussives (cough suppressants)

| Drug | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Dextromethorphan** | Sigma + NMDA modulation; weak opioid receptor; abuse in adolescents |
| **Codeine** | Opioid; antitussive at low dose; constipation, sedation; avoid in kids |
| **Benzonatate** | Local anesthetic on respiratory stretch receptors; pearls - DO NOT crush/chew (oropharyngeal anesthesia) |

### Expectorants

**Guaifenesin** - thins mucus. Modest evidence. OTC; safe.

### Mucolytics

- **N-acetylcysteine (NAC)** - breaks disulfide bonds in mucus; inhalation for CF, bronchiectasis. Also IV for acetaminophen overdose.
- **Dornase alfa (Pulmozyme)** - recombinant DNase; CF (breaks up extracellular DNA in mucus).

## Decongestants

| Drug | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Pseudoephedrine** | α1 + indirect → vasoconstriction |
| **Phenylephrine** | α1 direct (oral PE - poor efficacy debated) |
| **Oxymetazoline** | Topical α1 - **rhinitis medicamentosa** if used >3 days (rebound congestion) |

**Side effects**: HTN, palpitations, insomnia, anxiety, urinary retention, ↑ IOP. Avoid in HTN, CAD, hyperthyroid.

## Antihistamines

| Generation | Drugs | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **1st** | Diphenhydramine, Chlorpheniramine, Hydroxyzine, Cyproheptadine, Promethazine | Sedating; anticholinergic (avoid elderly per Beers) |
| **2nd** | Loratadine, Cetirizine, Levocetirizine, Fexofenadine, Desloratadine | Non-sedating; preferred for allergies |
| **Intranasal** | Azelastine, Olopatadine | Fast-acting allergic rhinitis |

Use: allergic rhinitis, urticaria, motion sickness (1st gen), insomnia (off-label 1st gen - tolerance + cognition concerns).

## CF specifics

- **CFTR modulators**: Ivacaftor (G551D), Tezacaftor + Ivacaftor, Elexacaftor + Tezacaftor + Ivacaftor (**Trikafta** - revolutionary for most patients with F508del).
- **Dornase alfa** - mucus.
- **Hypertonic saline** inhaled - mucus clearance.
- **Mannitol** dry powder inhaled.
- **Inhaled antibiotics** (tobramycin, aztreonam) for Pseudomonas suppression.
- **Pancreatic enzymes** (Pancreaze).
- **High-fat / high-calorie diet** + ADEK vitamins.

## Pulmonary hypertension drugs

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH, group 1) treatment by pathway:

| Pathway | Drugs |
|---|---|
| **Endothelin** receptor antagonists | **Bosentan, Macitentan, Ambrisentan** - block ETA/ETB |
| **PDE5 inhibitors** | **Sildenafil, Tadalafil** - ↑ cGMP |
| **Soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator** | **Riociguat** - ↑ cGMP (NO-independent); can't combine with PDE5i |
| **Prostacyclin pathway** | **Epoprostenol** (IV continuous), **Treprostinil** (SC/IV/oral/inhaled), **Iloprost** (inhaled), **Selexipag** (oral IP-receptor agonist) |

**Combination** therapy is standard for higher-risk PAH (AMBITION, GRIPHON).

**Side effects**:
- Bosentan, macitentan: hepatotoxicity (LFT monitoring), teratogen (REMS).
- Riociguat: hypotension, teratogen (REMS).
- Prostacyclins: jaw pain, headache, flushing, line infections (IV), pump issues.

**Avoid β-blocker, CCB** (except in vasoreactive PAH responders - rare).

## Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis

| Drug | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Pirfenidone** | Anti-fibrotic + anti-inflammatory; ↓ TGF-β |
| **Nintedanib** | Multi-TKI (VEGFR, FGFR, PDGFR) |

**Effect**: slow FVC decline (modest); no clear mortality benefit alone. Lung transplant remains definitive.

**Side effects**: GI (especially nintedanib), hepatotoxicity, photosensitivity (pirfenidone).

## RSV in adults

- **Nirsevimab** (infants/young children).
- **RSV vaccines** for ≥60 (Arexvy, Abrysvo) + pregnancy (Abrysvo).
- Supportive care (no proven antiviral for adult RSV; ribavirin rare in transplant).

## ARDS pharmacology

No specific drug cures ARDS:
- **Low tidal volume + permissive hypercapnia** (mechanical, not pharm).
- **Glucocorticoid** (dexamethasone) - for COVID-19 ARDS.
- **Neuromuscular blockade** (cisatracurium) for severe early ARDS (controversial).
- **Pulmonary vasodilators** (inhaled NO, prostacyclins) - rescue.
- **Prone positioning** - improves outcomes.

<Clinical>
**Patient with new-onset wheeze + dyspnea**: spirometry → asthma vs COPD. Asthma reversible (FEV1 ↑ ≥12% + 200 mL post-bronchodilator). Counsel on inhaler technique - 70% misuse.

**Asthma + young athlete with EIB**: pre-exercise SABA (10-15 min before); LTRA daily if frequent. Address triggers.

**Hospitalized COPD exacerbation**: oral prednisone 40 mg × 5 days (REDUCE), azithromycin × 5 days, nebulized bronchodilator, inhaler optimization. NIPPV if hypercapnic.

**Pediatric chronic asthma + montelukast**: counsel families on neuropsychiatric warning. Reserve for cases where ICS+LABA inadequate or unable.

**CF patient with new Pseudomonas isolation**: inhaled tobramycin (Tobi) cycles × 28-day on/off. Eventual chronic suppression. Triple CFTR modulators dramatically reduce exacerbations.

**Idiopathic pulmonary HTN**: vasoreactivity test → if responder (rare), high-dose CCB. Otherwise endothelin antagonist + PDE5 + prostacyclin combination.
</Clinical>
