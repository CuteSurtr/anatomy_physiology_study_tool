## Mechanism summary

| Receptor | Effect of block |
|---|---|
| **D2** (mesolimbic) | ↓ positive symptoms (hallucinations, delusions) - therapeutic |
| **D2** (nigrostriatal) | EPS (parkinsonism, dystonia, akathisia, tardive dyskinesia) |
| **D2** (tuberoinfundibular) | ↑ prolactin (galactorrhea, gynecomastia, sexual dysfunction) |
| **D2** (mesocortical) | Worse negative + cognitive symptoms |
| **5-HT2A** | ↓ EPS risk; ↑ negative symptom improvement (atypical advantage) |
| **α1** | Orthostasis, dizziness |
| **H1** | Sedation, weight gain |
| **M** | Anticholinergic burden |

## Typical (first-generation) antipsychotics

Primary D2 antagonism. **Higher EPS, less metabolic, less weight gain**.

| Drug | Potency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Haloperidol** | **High** | Workhorse; PO/IM/long-acting IM; emergency agitation; **NMS risk highest** |
| **Fluphenazine** | High | Long-acting injectable (decanoate q2-4 weeks) |
| **Perphenazine** | Medium | Tolerated; CATIE comparison |
| **Trifluoperazine** | High | |
| **Chlorpromazine (Thorazine)** | **Low** | Sedating; **corneal/lens deposits**; phototoxicity |
| **Thioridazine** | Low | **Retinal pigmentation**; **QT prolongation**; rarely used now |

**Mnemonic**: **High-potency** = high EPS, low autonomic/anticholinergic/sedation (HALOperidol = HIGH). **Low-potency** = low EPS but high sedation/orthostasis/anticholinergic (chlorpromazine, thioridazine).

### EPS spectrum (typical antipsychotic side effects)

| Type | Time | Symptoms | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute dystonia** | Hours-days | Sustained muscle spasm; **torticollis, oculogyric crisis, laryngospasm** | **Benztropine** or **diphenhydramine** IM |
| **Akathisia** | Days-weeks | Restlessness, can't sit still; suicide risk | β-blocker (propranolol), BZD, ↓ dose |
| **Parkinsonism** | Weeks-months | Tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia | Anticholinergic (benztropine), ↓ dose; **avoid L-DOPA** (psychosis) |
| **Tardive dyskinesia (TD)** | Months-years | Choreiform movements (face, mouth, tongue, fingers); often irreversible | **VMAT2 inhibitor** (valbenazine, deutetrabenazine); ↓ or switch to atypical |

### Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS)

**Trigger**: D2 antagonist (or sudden dopaminergic withdrawal in Parkinson disease).

**Onset**: days to weeks; mortality 10-20%.

**Tetrad**:
1. **Fever** (often >40°C).
2. **Rigidity** ("lead pipe" - upper > lower extremities).
3. **Autonomic instability** (BP swings, tachycardia, diaphoresis).
4. **Encephalopathy** (altered mental status).

**Labs**: ↑ CK (rhabdo), leukocytosis, transaminitis, metabolic acidosis.

**Treatment**:
1. **Stop antipsychotic**.
2. Cooling, fluids.
3. **Dantrolene** (rhabdo + rigidity).
4. **Bromocriptine** or **amantadine** (dopamine agonists).
5. BZD for agitation; avoid antipsychotics for delirium.
6. ICU monitoring.

Rechallenge with **different class** (atypical) after weeks, at lower dose.

## Atypical (second-generation) antipsychotics

D2 + 5-HT2A antagonism (or partial D2 agonism). **Less EPS, more metabolic side effects**.

| Drug | Key features |
|---|---|
| **Risperidone** | Moderate D2; **most prolactin** of atypicals; injection (Risperdal Consta q2 weeks; paliperidone monthly/3-monthly) |
| **Paliperidone** | Active metabolite of risperidone; daily oral; **long-acting injectables** (Sustenna monthly, Trinza q3 months, Hafyera q6 months) |
| **Olanzapine** | Strong sedation; **worst weight gain + metabolic syndrome** |
| **Quetiapine** | Highly sedating (H1); often misused for sleep; little EPS; **anticholinergic** + orthostasis |
| **Clozapine** | **Most effective for treatment-resistant schizophrenia** + ↓ suicide; ↓ EPS; **agranulocytosis** (1%) → weekly CBC × 6 months → monthly thereafter; **myocarditis**, seizures (dose-related), sialorrhea, **metabolic syndrome**, ileus |
| **Aripiprazole** | **D2 partial agonist** (3rd-gen "dopamine system stabilizer"); akathisia common; less weight gain; injection (Abilify Maintena monthly, Aristada monthly+) |
| **Brexpiprazole, Cariprazine** | Similar to aripiprazole; less akathisia (brexpiprazole) |
| **Ziprasidone** | **QT prolongation**; weight neutral; needs to be taken with food (500+ kcal for absorption) |
| **Lurasidone** | **Weight neutral**; bipolar depression indication; take with food |
| **Asenapine** | SL or transdermal; less weight gain; SL oral hypoesthesia |
| **Iloperidone** | Orthostasis; slow titration |
| **Pimavanserin** | **5-HT2A inverse agonist** with no D2 effect → Parkinson disease psychosis (doesn't worsen motor); also dementia psychosis |

### Side effects of atypicals - "metabolic" issue

- **Weight gain**: olanzapine > clozapine > quetiapine, risperidone > aripiprazole, ziprasidone, lurasidone (weight-neutral).
- **Diabetes / lipids**: similar order.
- **QT**: ziprasidone, iloperidone > others.
- **Sedation**: quetiapine, olanzapine.
- **Orthostasis**: quetiapine, iloperidone.
- **Prolactin**: risperidone > paliperidone > olanzapine > others.

**Monitoring**: baseline + periodic - weight/BMI, BP, fasting glucose, lipids, AIMS (abnormal involuntary movement scale) for tardive dyskinesia.

## Clozapine - the special case

**Use**: treatment-resistant schizophrenia (failed ≥2 antipsychotics) or suicidal patient. **Only drug shown to ↓ suicide** in schizophrenia.

**Pharmacology**:
- D4 > D2; 5-HT2A; H1; M; α1.
- CYP1A2 substrate (smoking ↓ levels by 50%; cessation → ↑ levels dangerously).

**Side effects**:
- **Agranulocytosis** (1%) - REMS program - weekly CBC × 6 months, biweekly × 6 months, monthly thereafter. Hold if ANC &lt;1500.
- **Seizures** (dose-related; >600 mg/day risk).
- **Myocarditis** (early - first 8 weeks; check troponin if symptoms).
- **Sialorrhea** (drooling).
- **Constipation → ileus** - leading cause of death (severe).
- **Metabolic** (diabetes, weight gain, lipids).
- **Orthostasis** + sedation (slow titration mandatory).

## Long-acting injectable antipsychotics (LAIs)

**Use**: poor adherence, severe disease, frequent relapse.

| Drug | Interval |
|---|---|
| **Haloperidol decanoate** | q4 weeks |
| **Fluphenazine decanoate** | q2-4 weeks |
| **Risperidone (Risperdal Consta)** | q2 weeks (needs oral overlap × 3 weeks) |
| **Paliperidone** | Sustenna monthly, Trinza q3 mo, Hafyera q6 mo |
| **Olanzapine pamoate (Zyprexa Relprevv)** | q2-4 weeks; **post-injection delirium/sedation** (3-h observation) |
| **Aripiprazole** | Maintena monthly, Aristada monthly/6-week/2-month |

## Antipsychotic in special populations

### Elderly + dementia
**Black box**: ↑ all-cause mortality + stroke in dementia-related psychosis. Use lowest dose, shortest duration, or non-pharmacologic approaches first. **Pimavanserin** option for Parkinson psychosis without worsening motor.

### Pregnancy
Most antipsychotics relatively safe; haloperidol, olanzapine, risperidone, quetiapine - some data. Atypicals → ↑ gestational diabetes. Withdrawal symptoms in newborn (3rd trimester exposure).

### Children
Few approved indications: aripiprazole, risperidone for Tourette + autism-associated irritability. Monitor metabolics aggressively.

## Treatment of TD

**VMAT2 inhibitors** - novel + reversible (vs prior treatments):
- **Valbenazine** (Ingrezza) - daily.
- **Deutetrabenazine** (Austedo) - BID.
- **Tetrabenazine** (Xenazine) - older, used for Huntington chorea; depression black box.

Other approaches: switch to clozapine (least TD risk); switch to lower-EPS atypical; clonazepam, vitamin E adjuncts.

## "Choosing an antipsychotic" matrix

| Scenario | Preferred |
|---|---|
| First-episode psychosis | Risperidone, aripiprazole, olanzapine |
| Treatment-resistant schizophrenia | **Clozapine** |
| Severe agitation acute | **Haloperidol IM** (+ lorazepam + diphenhydramine - "B-52") |
| Bipolar mania | Olanzapine, risperidone, quetiapine, aripiprazole, asenapine, ziprasidone, cariprazine |
| Bipolar depression | **Lurasidone, quetiapine, cariprazine, olanzapine + fluoxetine (Symbyax)** |
| Schizoaffective | Paliperidone (FDA indication), others |
| Parkinson psychosis | **Pimavanserin, quetiapine, clozapine** (low D2 affinity to spare motor) |
| Dementia behavioral symptoms | Non-pharm first; quetiapine or risperidone short term + counseling on mortality |
| Adolescent | Aripiprazole, risperidone |
| Diabetic / weight concerns | Aripiprazole, ziprasidone, lurasidone (weight neutral) |
| QT concerns | Avoid ziprasidone, iloperidone; use aripiprazole |
| Adherence problem | LAI (paliperidone palmitate, aripiprazole) |

<Clinical>
**Acute agitation in ED**: "B-52" = Benadryl 50 mg + haloperidol 5 mg + lorazepam 2 mg IM. Or droperidol 5 mg IM (was popular; QT concern but reduced).

**First-episode psychosis**: low-dose atypical (aripiprazole 10-15 mg or risperidone 2-4 mg). Avoid haloperidol if possible due to EPS. Comprehensive care includes psychosocial.

**Patient on clozapine + smoker, hospitalized + no smoking**: levels ↑ ~50%. Risk of toxicity. Reduce dose ~30-50% during admission, retitrate at discharge if resumes smoking.

**EPS dystonia in ED**: IV diphenhydramine 25-50 mg or benztropine 2 mg works within minutes. Lifesaving for laryngospasm. Continue oral × few days.

**NMS in patient on antipsychotic**: stop drug, ICU, cooling, IV fluids, dantrolene + bromocriptine. Wait 2 weeks before rechallenging with different class.

**Tardive dyskinesia**: switch to clozapine or add VMAT2 inhibitor. Vitamin E modest.
</Clinical>
