## Parkinson disease pharmacology

Goal: restore dopamine in nigrostriatal pathway. Strategies:
1. Increase dopamine (L-DOPA).
2. Mimic dopamine (D2 agonists).
3. Slow dopamine breakdown (MAO-B inhibitors, COMT inhibitors).
4. Block ACh (anticholinergic - now niche).

### L-DOPA + Carbidopa (Sinemet, Rytary, Duopa)

**Why combination**:
- L-DOPA = dopamine precursor that crosses BBB (dopamine itself doesn't).
- **Carbidopa** = peripheral DOPA decarboxylase inhibitor; doesn't cross BBB → blocks peripheral conversion of L-DOPA → dopamine → less peripheral side effects (nausea, hypotension) and more L-DOPA reaches brain.

**Most effective Parkinson drug** for motor symptoms.

**Long-term complications** ("L-DOPA paradox"):
- **Motor fluctuations**: wearing-off (end-of-dose), on-off (random), delayed-on.
- **Dyskinesias** - involuntary movements at peak L-DOPA effect.
- After 5+ years, dose-response window narrows.

**Strategies to delay these**: start at lowest dose, use sustained-release, add ancillary drugs (MAO-B, COMT, dopamine agonist) for "L-DOPA sparing."

**Side effects**:
- GI (nausea, vomiting - take with food after stable).
- Orthostasis.
- Psychiatric (hallucinations, vivid dreams, impulse control disorders).
- Dyskinesias.

**Diet**: high-protein meals reduce L-DOPA absorption (competing amino acids). Time meds with low-protein meals or take 30 min before eating.

### Dopamine agonists (DA)

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Pramipexole** (Mirapex) | Non-ergot; PO; restless legs |
| **Ropinirole** (Requip) | Non-ergot; PO; restless legs |
| **Rotigotine** (Neupro) | Non-ergot; transdermal patch (24-h delivery) |
| **Apomorphine** | Subcut rescue for off-state; daytime injection or pump |
| **Bromocriptine, Pergolide, Cabergoline** | Ergot - largely abandoned (cardiac valve fibrosis, pleural fibrosis) |

**Pros**: less dyskinesia than L-DOPA; useful as monotherapy in younger patients to delay L-DOPA initiation.

**Cons**:
- More **psychiatric** side effects (hallucinations, **impulse control disorders**: gambling, hypersexuality, binge eating - mention this).
- **Sleep attacks** (sudden onset).
- Orthostasis, nausea.
- Edema (ankle).

### MAO-B inhibitors

**Selegiline, Rasagiline, Safinamide** - block dopamine breakdown in CNS.

**Use**: early monotherapy (mild benefit), or adjunct to L-DOPA to prolong effect.

**Side effects**: insomnia (selegiline → amphetamine metabolite), serotonergic interactions (avoid with SSRI/SNRI/meperidine/tramadol).

### COMT inhibitors

**Entacapone, Tolcapone, Opicapone** - block peripheral COMT → ↑ L-DOPA bioavailability + duration.

**Use**: with L-DOPA for end-of-dose wearing-off.

**Side effects**: orange-brown urine, diarrhea, dyskinesia (enhances L-DOPA). **Tolcapone** → hepatotoxicity (black box - LFT monitoring).

### Amantadine

**Mechanism**: NMDA antagonist + dopamine release.

**Use**: mild Parkinson, **L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia** (best evidence). Also: influenza A (historical), MS fatigue.

**Side effects**: livedo reticularis, anticholinergic, hallucinations, ankle edema.

### Anticholinergics

**Trihexyphenidyl, Benztropine** - block muscarinic. Useful for **tremor-predominant young PD**. Avoid in elderly (cognitive worsening).

### Psychosis in Parkinson

Up to 50% of PD patients develop psychosis (hallucinations, delusions). Worsens with dopaminergic drugs.

Treatment: simplify regimen, then add **pimavanserin** (5-HT2A inverse agonist, no D2) or **quetiapine** or **clozapine** (low D2 affinity, no motor worsening). **Avoid typical antipsychotics** (haloperidol, risperidone, etc. - worsen motor).

### Deep brain stimulation (DBS)

Subthalamic nucleus or globus pallidus internus stimulation - effective for refractory motor symptoms, dyskinesia. Doesn't treat cognitive/balance/autonomic issues.

## Alzheimer disease pharmacology

### Cholinesterase inhibitors

| Drug | Form |
|---|---|
| **Donepezil** | Daily oral (5, 10, 23 mg) |
| **Rivastigmine** | Oral BID; transdermal patch (best tolerance) |
| **Galantamine** | Oral BID; +allosteric nicotinic receptor modulation |
| **Tacrine** | Hepatotoxic - withdrawn |

**Use**: mild-moderate Alzheimer's, dementia with Lewy bodies, Parkinson dementia.

**Effect**: modest (~6-12 month delay in functional decline). Don't reverse or stop disease.

**Side effects**: GI (nausea, diarrhea, anorexia, weight loss), bradycardia, syncope, vivid dreams. Start low + titrate.

### Memantine

**Mechanism**: uncompetitive NMDA antagonist - blocks excessive glutamate (excitotoxicity) without preventing normal signaling.

**Use**: moderate-severe Alzheimer's. Often combined with donepezil.

**Side effects**: dizziness, confusion (paradoxical), constipation.

### Anti-amyloid antibodies (the new wave)

| Drug | Status |
|---|---|
| **Aducanumab** (Aduhelm) | FDA accelerated approval 2021; controversial - withdrawn 2024 |
| **Lecanemab** (Leqembi) | Approved 2023; clearly modest cognitive benefit + ARIA risk |
| **Donanemab** (Kisunla) | Approved 2024; similar profile |

**Mechanism**: bind aggregated amyloid → clearance.

**Use**: early Alzheimer's with amyloid PET or CSF biomarker confirmation.

**Side effects**: **ARIA** (amyloid-related imaging abnormalities) - cerebral microhemorrhages (ARIA-H) or edema (ARIA-E). Risk higher in APOE4 homozygotes. Infusion reactions. Brain MRI before each infusion.

**Cost + access** debates ongoing.

### Things that DON'T work (despite popularity)

- Ginkgo biloba.
- Vitamin E mega-doses.
- Cholinergic supplements (lecithin, etc.).

Lifestyle: exercise, Mediterranean diet, social engagement, hearing aids - all have modest evidence.

## ALS (motor neuron disease)

### Riluzole

**Mechanism**: glutamate release inhibitor + Na channel block.

**Effect**: prolongs life ~3 months in ALS. Modest.

**Side effects**: nausea, hepatotoxicity (LFT monitoring).

### Edaravone

**Mechanism**: free-radical scavenger.

**Effect**: modest slowing of functional decline.

**Administration**: IV infusion (originally) or oral suspension - 14-day cycles with off periods.

### Sodium phenylbutyrate + taurursodiol (Relyvrio)

Mitochondrial + ER stress modulator. Withdrawn after failed phase 3 (2024).

### Tofersen

Antisense oligonucleotide for SOD1-mutation ALS (5% of familial). Intrathecal monthly.

## Huntington disease

| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| **Tetrabenazine, Deutetrabenazine, Valbenazine** | VMAT2 inhibitor - reduces chorea |
| **Antipsychotics** | Olanzapine, risperidone - chorea + behavioral symptoms |
| **SSRIs** | Depression + irritability |

No disease-modifying drug yet.

## Restless legs syndrome (RLS)

1. **Iron** if ferritin &lt;75 - oral or IV iron sucrose.
2. **α2δ ligands** (gabapentin enacarbil, pregabalin) - first-line per recent guidelines (avoid augmentation issue).
3. **Dopamine agonists** (pramipexole, ropinirole, rotigotine patch) - effective but **augmentation** (worsening over time) limits long-term use.
4. **Low-dose opioid** (oxycodone, methadone) for refractory.

## Tourette syndrome

| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| **α2 agonists** (clonidine, guanfacine) | First-line, esp. mild |
| **VMAT2 inhibitors** (tetrabenazine, deutetrabenazine) | Reduce tics, no D2 receptor |
| **Antipsychotics** (haloperidol, aripiprazole, risperidone) | Effective but EPS risk |

<Clinical>
**New diagnosis of Parkinson's, young patient**: start with MAO-B inhibitor (rasagiline) or dopamine agonist (pramipexole) to delay L-DOPA + later motor complications.

**Older patient or significant disability**: start L-DOPA + carbidopa at lowest effective dose.

**On-off fluctuations**: add COMT inhibitor (entacapone), MAO-B, dopamine agonist, or extended-release L-DOPA. Apomorphine SC rescue for severe "off."

**Severe end-stage PD with dysphagia**: enteral L-DOPA gel (Duopa) via PEG-J, apomorphine pump, or DBS.

**Mild cognitive impairment**: cholinesterase inhibitor only modest evidence; lifestyle + cognitive training has equal evidence.

**Moderate Alzheimer's with behavioral symptoms**: try non-pharmacologic first. If antipsychotic essential, low-dose risperidone or quetiapine after counseling on mortality risk (black box).

**Newly diagnosed MS, relapsing-remitting**: oral DMT (dimethyl fumarate, fingolimod, teriflunomide) or injection (interferon, glatiramer) early. Highly active disease → ocrelizumab or natalizumab.
</Clinical>

<HighYield variant="boards">
- Carbidopa = peripheral DOPA decarboxylase inhibitor → spares peripheral conversion → more L-DOPA to brain + less nausea/hypotension.
- Dopamine agonists (pramipexole, ropinirole) → impulse control disorders (gambling, hypersexuality, binge eating).
- Ergot DAs (bromocriptine, pergolide) → cardiac valve fibrosis - largely abandoned.
- Selegiline → amphetamine metabolite → insomnia.
- Tolcapone → hepatotoxicity (black box; LFTs).
- Anticholinergics for tremor-predominant young PD; AVOID elderly.
- PD psychosis → pimavanserin, quetiapine, clozapine. NOT haloperidol or risperidone.
- Donepezil → bradycardia/syncope; nausea/diarrhea/anorexia common.
- Memantine = NMDA antagonist; moderate-severe AD.
- Lecanemab/donanemab → ARIA (microhemorrhage/edema); APOE4 homozygotes highest risk.
- Riluzole = ALS, modest benefit (~3 months survival).
- Natalizumab → PML (JCV+ risk); REMS program.
- Augmentation = dopamine-agonist-induced worsening of RLS with prolonged use.
- MS relapse = high-dose IV methylprednisolone.
</HighYield>
