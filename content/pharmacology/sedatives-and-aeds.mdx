## Benzodiazepines

**Mechanism**: bind allosteric site on GABA-A receptor → ↑ **frequency** of Cl⁻ channel opening (potentiation - not direct activation). Need endogenous GABA present.

| Duration | Drug | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Short** (2-8 h) | Midazolam | Procedural sedation, GA induction, status epilepticus |
| **Short** | Triazolam | Insomnia (rarely used - amnesia) |
| **Intermediate** (10-30 h) | Lorazepam, Oxazepam, Temazepam | Anxiety, ETOH withdrawal, status; **LOT** = NOT hepatic metabolism (glucuronidation only - safe in liver disease + elderly) |
| **Intermediate** | Alprazolam | Anxiety, panic (high abuse risk) |
| **Long** (24-48+ h) | Diazepam, Clonazepam, Chlordiazepoxide, Flurazepam | Anxiety, seizure, alcohol withdrawal |

### Indications
- Anxiety (short-term).
- Insomnia (short-term).
- Alcohol withdrawal (DT prevention) - **chlordiazepoxide or diazepam** scheduled or symptom-triggered (CIWA).
- Status epilepticus - **lorazepam** IV.
- Procedural sedation - **midazolam**.
- Muscle spasm - diazepam.
- Restless legs - clonazepam.

### Side effects
- Sedation, ataxia, confusion (esp. elderly).
- Anterograde amnesia.
- Respiratory depression - **massive when combined with opioids or alcohol**.
- Tolerance + physical dependence + withdrawal.
- **Paradoxical disinhibition** in elderly + kids - agitation, confusion.

### Withdrawal

After 4+ weeks regular use, especially abrupt stop of short-acting:
- Anxiety, tremor, insomnia, autonomic excitation.
- **Seizures** (severe withdrawal - life-threatening).
- Hallucinations, delirium.

Taper SLOWLY (10% per week) or substitute long-acting.

### Reversal

**Flumazenil** - competitive antagonist. Acute reversal in BZD overdose.

**Caution**: precipitates withdrawal in chronic BZD users; can trigger seizures (esp. if BZD was masking another seizure tendency). Avoid routine "wake-up" reversal.

## Z-drugs (non-BZD hypnotics)

**Zolpidem (Ambien), Eszopiclone (Lunesta), Zaleplon (Sonata)**

**Mechanism**: bind same BZD site but selectively for **α1 subunit** → mostly sedative effects without much anxiolysis.

**Pros**: less hangover, less abuse potential than BZD, less effect on sleep architecture.

**Cons**:
- Complex sleep behaviors (sleep driving, sleep eating) - **FDA black box**.
- Anterograde amnesia.
- Dependence with chronic use.
- **Falls in elderly** (low-dose; women: 5 mg zolpidem, not 10).

**Antagonist**: also flumazenil.

## Other hypnotics

| Drug | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ramelteon** | Melatonin MT1/MT2 agonist | Insomnia (sleep onset); no abuse potential; OK long-term |
| **Suvorexant, Lemborexant, Daridorexant** | Orexin (OX1+OX2) antagonist | Sleep maintenance; non-addictive; vivid dreams, daytime sleepiness |
| **Diphenhydramine, Doxylamine** | H1 antagonist (1st gen) | OTC; anticholinergic burden (avoid elderly); tolerance |
| **Doxepin** (low dose) | H1 antagonist | Sleep maintenance; FDA-approved low dose |
| **Trazodone** | 5-HT antagonist + α1 block | Common off-label; priapism, orthostatic |

## Barbiturates

**Phenobarbital, Pentobarbital, Thiopental, Methohexital**

**Mechanism**: bind GABA-A → ↑ **duration** of Cl⁻ channel opening; at high dose, direct GABA-mimetic (no GABA needed) → no ceiling on toxicity.

**Uses**: pediatric seizures (phenobarbital), induction (thiopental - historic), ECT (methohexital), refractory ICU sedation (pentobarbital coma).

**Side effects**: respiratory depression (lethal in overdose - no ceiling), induce hepatic CYPs (major drug interactions), tolerance, dependence, sedation.

**Largely replaced** by BZDs except specific indications.

## Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs)

### Mechanisms

| Mechanism | Drugs |
|---|---|
| **Na channel block (fast)** | Phenytoin, Carbamazepine, Oxcarbazepine, Lamotrigine, Lacosamide |
| **Ca channel block (T-type)** | Ethosuximide |
| **Ca channel block (α2δ subunit, presynaptic)** | Gabapentin, Pregabalin |
| **GABA enhancer (BZD site)** | Diazepam, Lorazepam, Clobazam |
| **GABA enhancer (other)** | Phenobarbital, Vigabatrin (irrev GABA-T), Tiagabine (GAT-1 block) |
| **Synaptic vesicle SV2A binding** | Levetiracetam, Brivaracetam |
| **AMPA antagonist** | Perampanel |
| **Multiple mechanisms** | Valproate (Na + Ca + GABA + ↓ NMDA), Topiramate (Na + GABA + CA inhib + AMPA), Felbamate, Cenobamate |

### AEDs by seizure type

| Type | First-line |
|---|---|
| **Focal seizures (partial)** | Carbamazepine, Oxcarbazepine, Lamotrigine, Levetiracetam, Lacosamide |
| **Generalized tonic-clonic** | Valproate, Lamotrigine, Topiramate, Levetiracetam |
| **Absence (petit mal)** | **Ethosuximide** (1st line), Valproate (also if other generalized seizures present), Lamotrigine |
| **Myoclonic / JME** | Valproate, Levetiracetam, Lamotrigine (caution - can worsen myoclonus), Topiramate |
| **Atonic / Lennox-Gastaut** | Valproate, Lamotrigine, Topiramate, Felbamate, Cannabidiol, Clobazam |
| **Status epilepticus** | Lorazepam IV → fosphenytoin/phenytoin IV → levetiracetam IV → propofol/midazolam coma |
| **Trigeminal neuralgia** | Carbamazepine 1st line, oxcarbazepine |
| **Bipolar maintenance** | Valproate, Lamotrigine, Carbamazepine |

### Drug-specific points

#### Phenytoin

- Na-channel block; zero-order kinetics (saturable) - small dose ↑ = big level ↑.
- Many side effects:
  - **Gingival hyperplasia**, hirsutism, coarse facial features.
  - Megaloblastic anemia (folate).
  - Peripheral neuropathy.
  - **Stevens-Johnson** (HLA-B*1502 in Asians).
  - Cerebellar atrophy chronic use.
  - **Fetal hydantoin syndrome** (cleft lip/palate, microcephaly, digital hypoplasia).
- IV phenytoin = dangerous (purple glove syndrome, hypotension, arrhythmia from propylene glycol). Use **fosphenytoin** IV instead.
- CYP inducer + extensive interactions.

#### Carbamazepine

- Na-channel block; structural cousin to TCA.
- Side effects:
  - **SIADH** → hyponatremia.
  - **Aplastic anemia, agranulocytosis** (rare but serious).
  - **Stevens-Johnson** (HLA-B*1502 in Asians - test before starting).
  - Diplopia, ataxia.
  - **Strong CYP inducer** (drops OC, warfarin levels) - induces its own metabolism.
- **Oxcarbazepine** - prodrug; fewer drug interactions; same SIADH risk + worse hyponatremia.

#### Valproate

- Many mechanisms; broad-spectrum.
- Side effects:
  - **Hepatotoxicity** (worst &lt;2 yrs - check LFT).
  - **Pancreatitis**.
  - **Teratogenic** - neural tube defects (folate supplementation, avoid if possible in pregnancy).
  - **Thrombocytopenia**.
  - **Hyperammonemic encephalopathy** (in OTC deficiency carriers).
  - Weight gain, alopecia, tremor, GI upset.
- CYP inhibitor.

#### Lamotrigine

- Na-channel block.
- **Slow titration** required - **Stevens-Johnson/TEN** risk if titrated quickly (esp. with valproate which doubles lamotrigine half-life).
- Generally well-tolerated; mood-stabilizing properties.
- Pregnancy: relatively safer than valproate.

#### Levetiracetam (Keppra)

- SV2A binding (unique mechanism); no drug interactions.
- Side effects: **mood/behavior** (irritability, depression, "keppra rage"), sedation.
- Renal dose adjust.
- Available IV - good for status.

#### Topiramate

- Multiple mechanisms.
- Side effects: cognitive ("dopa-max"), word-finding difficulty, weight loss (anorexia, used off-label for obesity), kidney stones (carbonic anhydrase inhibitor), oligohidrosis + hyperthermia in kids, **glaucoma** (acute angle-closure), metabolic acidosis.
- Also used for migraine prophylaxis.

#### Gabapentin + Pregabalin

- α2δ Ca-channel block.
- Used more for neuropathic pain than seizures.
- Pregabalin → faster onset + ↑ bioavailability (linear kinetics) vs gabapentin (saturable).
- Side effects: sedation, dizziness, weight gain, edema. Misuse potential (esp. combined with opioids).

#### Lacosamide

- Enhances slow inactivation of Na channels (different mechanism than other Na blockers).
- Well-tolerated; PR prolongation; works as monotherapy.

#### Ethosuximide

- T-type Ca channel block in thalamus.
- 1st line for **absence seizures**.
- Side effects: GI, headache, fatigue, rarely SJS, aplastic anemia.

#### Vigabatrin

- Irreversible GABA transaminase inhibitor → ↑ GABA.
- Restricted (REMS): **visual field defects** (irreversible).
- Use: infantile spasms (West syndrome), refractory focal seizures.

#### Felbamate

- Black box: aplastic anemia + hepatic failure. Restricted use.

#### Perampanel

- AMPA antagonist; partial + generalized; **psychiatric/aggression** signal.

#### Cannabidiol (Epidiolex)

- FDA-approved for **Dravet, Lennox-Gastaut, tuberous sclerosis**.
- Side effects: somnolence, ALT ↑, drug interactions (CYP).

#### Cenobamate

- Newer; very effective for focal seizures; **DRESS** risk → slow titration.

### Status epilepticus algorithm

1. **0-5 min**: Time = brain. ABCs, glucose, IV access, labs.
2. **5-20 min**: **IV lorazepam** 0.1 mg/kg (max 4 mg per dose, repeat once).
3. **20-40 min**: **Fosphenytoin** 20 mg PE/kg IV or **levetiracetam** 60 mg/kg IV or **valproate** 40 mg/kg IV.
4. **Refractory (>40 min)**: continuous IV anesthesia - **midazolam, propofol, pentobarbital** drip. Continuous EEG.

## Pregnancy + AEDs

- Most teratogens to some degree; **valproate worst** (NTDs, autism, low IQ).
- Lamotrigine + levetiracetam **safest** in pregnancy.
- All women of childbearing age on AEDs need **folate** (4 mg/day if prior NTD; 0.4-1 mg otherwise).
- Carbamazepine + phenytoin → vitamin K deficiency in newborn (give vitamin K at delivery).

## AEDs in clinical scenarios

- **Hepatic disease**: avoid valproate, phenytoin (hepatic clearance). Levetiracetam, gabapentin, pregabalin OK (renal).
- **Renal disease**: dose-adjust levetiracetam, gabapentin, pregabalin, lacosamide. Phenytoin OK (hepatic).
- **Bipolar**: lamotrigine (depression-predominant), valproate (mania), carbamazepine.
- **Pregnancy**: lamotrigine + levetiracetam preferred.
- **Migraine prophylaxis**: topiramate, valproate.

<Clinical>
**ETOH withdrawal**: CIWA-Aa scoring. Mild → outpatient with PO chlordiazepoxide or diazepam taper. Moderate-severe → inpatient IV BZD (lorazepam or diazepam) symptom-triggered. Thiamine + folate + multivitamin always. Phenobarbital second-line. Refractory DT → consider phenobarbital or propofol drip.

**Insomnia in elderly**: avoid BZD + Z-drugs (falls, cognition). Try CBT-I first. If pharmacologic needed: low-dose doxepin, ramelteon, suvorexant.

**Lamotrigine titration**: 25 mg/day × 2 weeks → 50 mg/day × 2 weeks → 100 mg/day, etc. If on valproate, halve all doses (valproate doubles lamotrigine t½). Missed doses → restart at low dose if >5 days missed.

**Switching AEDs**: cross-taper over 4-6 weeks. Never stop suddenly (rebound seizures). Levetiracetam → most patients can cross-taper to phenytoin/lamotrigine/carbamazepine.

**Newly diagnosed seizure**: levetiracetam often first choice now (no drug interactions, IV form, fast onset, broad-spectrum). Carbamazepine/phenytoin if specifically focal seizure with focal lesion.
</Clinical>
