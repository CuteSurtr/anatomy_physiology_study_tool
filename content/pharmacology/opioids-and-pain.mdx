## Opioid receptors

| Receptor | Effects of activation |
|---|---|
| **μ (MOR)** | Analgesia, euphoria, sedation, respiratory depression, miosis, constipation, physical dependence |
| **κ (KOR)** | Spinal analgesia, dysphoria, miosis, sedation, diuresis |
| **δ (DOR)** | Modulatory; less clinical relevance |
| **NOP (ORL1)** | Anxiolytic, antinociceptive (atypical) |

All are Gi-coupled GPCRs → ↓ cAMP, ↓ Ca channel, ↑ K channel → neuronal hyperpolarization.

## Full μ agonists

| Drug | Onset / Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Morphine** | Slow IV onset; 3–4 h duration | Histamine release (urticaria, bronchospasm); active metabolite (M6G) accumulates in renal failure |
| **Hydromorphone (Dilaudid)** | Faster onset, more potent than morphine | Less histamine; safer in renal failure than morphine |
| **Codeine** | PO; weak | Prodrug → morphine via CYP2D6. Poor metabolizers get no relief; ultra-rapid get morphine surge (fatal in kids) |
| **Hydrocodone** | PO | Common in fixed combos (Vicodin = hydrocodone + APAP) |
| **Oxycodone** | PO; 1.5× morphine potency | Combos: Percocet (with APAP), OxyContin (XR) |
| **Fentanyl** | Rapid IV; transdermal patch (72 h); SL/buccal | 100× morphine; serotonergic - **serotonin syndrome with MAOI**; chest wall rigidity at high doses |
| **Sufentanil, Alfentanil, Remifentanil** | OR anesthetics | Remifentanil cleared by plasma esterases → ultrashort |
| **Methadone** | Long t½ 24+ h; PO | OUD maintenance + chronic pain; **QT prolongation**; NMDA antagonist (efficacy in neuropathic pain) |
| **Meperidine (Demerol)** | Short | Discouraged - **normeperidine** seizures + serotonergic - **avoid with MAOI** |

## Partial agonists + mixed

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Buprenorphine** | Partial μ agonist, κ antagonist; "ceiling effect" on respiratory depression. SL film (Suboxone with naloxone) for OUD; patches for chronic pain |
| **Pentazocine, Butorphanol, Nalbuphine** | κ agonist, μ partial agonist; precipitate withdrawal in chronic μ users |
| **Tramadol** | Weak μ agonist + serotonin/NE reuptake inhibition; **seizure threshold ↓**, **serotonin syndrome** risk |
| **Tapentadol** | μ + NE reuptake; fewer GI side effects |

## Antagonists

| Drug | Route / use |
|---|---|
| **Naloxone** | IV/IM/IN; rapid opioid reversal (overdose). Short t½ (30 min) - **repeated doses needed for long-acting opioids** (methadone, sustained-release) |
| **Naltrexone** | Oral or monthly IM (Vivitrol); alcohol + opioid use disorder maintenance |
| **Methylnaltrexone, Naloxegol, Alvimopan** | Peripheral antagonists - reverse opioid-induced constipation without losing central analgesia |
| **Nalmefene** | Like naloxone but longer t½ |

## Adverse effects of opioids

- **Respiratory depression** - the killer. ↓ rate before ↓ depth. Combined with benzodiazepines or alcohol = exponential risk.
- **Sedation / cognitive** - tolerance develops to most but not all CNS effects.
- **Miosis** - "pinpoint pupils" classic toxidrome (except meperidine - mydriasis).
- **Constipation** - **no tolerance**. Bowel regimen mandatory.
- **Nausea + vomiting** - tolerance develops.
- **Pruritus** - histamine + central μ; opioid-induced, not allergy. Antihistamine palliative.
- **Urinary retention** - parasympathetic + detrusor.
- **Tolerance + dependence** with chronic use - withdrawal on cessation.
- **Hyperalgesia** - paradoxical pain ↑ on high-dose chronic opioid.
- **Endocrine** - ↓ testosterone, ↓ libido, amenorrhea.
- **QT prolongation** - methadone especially.

## Withdrawal vs intoxication

| | Intoxication (overdose) | Withdrawal |
|---|---|---|
| Pupils | **Pinpoint** | **Mydriasis** |
| Mental status | Sedation, coma | Agitation, anxiety |
| Vital signs | Hypotension, bradycardia, ↓ RR | HTN, tachycardia, ↑ RR |
| GI | ↓ motility | ↑ motility (diarrhea, vomiting, abdominal cramps) |
| Skin | Pale, cyanotic | Piloerection ("cold turkey"), sweating |
| Other | Hypothermia, ↓ bowel sounds | Yawning, rhinorrhea, lacrimation, restlessness |
| Treatment | **Naloxone** + supportive | Clonidine + supportive; buprenorphine or methadone for OUD |

**Opioid withdrawal is uncomfortable but not life-threatening** in adults (unlike alcohol or benzo withdrawal). In neonates (NAS) it can be life-threatening - treat with methadone or morphine taper.

## Opioid use disorder (OUD) treatment

| Drug | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Methadone** | Long-acting full μ agonist | Methadone clinic (regulated); ↑ overdose risk during induction; QT |
| **Buprenorphine ± naloxone** | Partial μ agonist; "ceiling" on resp depression | Office-based; precipitates withdrawal if given too early; naloxone added to prevent IV abuse |
| **Naltrexone** | μ antagonist (oral or monthly IM) | Best for highly motivated; can't be on opioids when starting |
| **Naloxone (Narcan)** | Reversal for overdose | Nasal spray OTC; widely distributed |

**MOUD** (medications for opioid use disorder) reduces mortality, infections (HIV/HCV), and crime; abstinence-only programs have high relapse rates.

## Acetaminophen

**Mechanism**: poorly defined; central COX inhibition + cannabinoid metabolite contribute.

**Properties**: analgesic + antipyretic; **NOT** anti-inflammatory (so doesn't help inflammatory pain like aspirin).

**Pharmacology**: 90% glucuronidation + sulfation; **5–10% via CYP2E1 → NAPQI**, which is detoxified by glutathione.

**Toxicity**: in overdose, glutathione depleted → NAPQI accumulates → centrilobular hepatic necrosis (zone 3). Risk ↑ with chronic ethanol, fasting, malnutrition (depleted glutathione), CYP2E1 inducers (chronic alcohol, isoniazid).

**Antidote**: **N-acetylcysteine** - replenishes glutathione. Most effective within 8 h; benefit even up to 24+ h. Rumack-Matthew nomogram for acute single ingestions.

**Therapeutic max**: 3-4 g/day adult (lower with liver disease, ethanol). 75 mg/kg/day in kids.

## NSAIDs - the analgesic adjunct

Covered in [NSAIDs](/pharmacology/nsaids) page. Combined with acetaminophen ± opioid for multimodal analgesia.

## WHO analgesic ladder + multimodal pain management

1. **Mild pain**: acetaminophen ± NSAID.
2. **Moderate**: + weak opioid (codeine, hydrocodone, tramadol).
3. **Severe**: + strong opioid (morphine, oxycodone, hydromorphone).
4. **Adjuvants** at any step: gabapentin/pregabalin (neuropathic), TCAs (neuropathic), SNRIs (duloxetine - diabetic neuropathy, fibromyalgia), topical lidocaine, capsaicin, regional blocks.

**Multimodal analgesia** (e.g., post-op) = scheduled APAP + NSAID + opioid PRN + nerve block + epidural - reduces opioid use + opioid-related side effects.

## Drug-specific pearls

- **Fentanyl patch** - reservoir; full effect at 12-24 h; remove and **clean skin** at change (residual drug on skin = overdose risk for caregivers).
- **Methadone half-life 24+ h**: analgesia lasts 6-8 h → easy to over-dose if titrated by pain response → respiratory depression days later.
- **Naloxone for fentanyl overdose**: may need higher doses + multiple administrations - fentanyl is so potent and ↓ resp depression so rapid.
- **Morphine in CKD**: avoid - M6G accumulates → CNS depression + myoclonus. Use **hydromorphone or fentanyl** instead.
- **Codeine to morphine**: CYP2D6 polymorphism critical. Avoid in kids with sleep apnea post-tonsillectomy (fatal ultra-rapid metabolizer events).
- **Tramadol** - lowest seizure threshold of opioids. Avoid in seizure history.
- **Buprenorphine** "precipitated withdrawal": never start within 12-24 h of last short-acting opioid use. Wait for moderate withdrawal symptoms (COWS score ≥8-12) first.

<Clinical>
**Opioid overdose** in the field: naloxone 0.4 mg IN/IM repeat q2–3 min. In the ED: IV titrated dose to RR ~12 + adequate sat - avoid full reversal in chronic users (precipitates miserable withdrawal). Monitor 4–6 h post-reversal (recurrent sedation as naloxone wears off, esp. with long-acting opioids).

**Cancer pain** - opioids are first-line; titrate without ceiling (full agonists). Consider methadone for neuropathic component (NMDA antagonism). Adjuncts: dexamethasone for bone pain or nerve compression; bisphosphonates for bone mets pain.

**Chronic non-cancer pain** - opioids are last-line; risks outweigh benefits for most chronic conditions (per CDC 2022 + many trials). Maximize non-opioid: PT, CBT, NSAID, gabapentinoids, topical, regional, lifestyle. If opioid used: lowest effective dose, &lt;90 MME/day, avoid combining with benzo, naloxone prescription.

**Buprenorphine induction**: home induction with COWS-guided start has become standard. Patient takes 4-8 mg buccal/SL after waiting until in moderate withdrawal. Stabilize at 8-24 mg/day.

**Acetaminophen overdose**: get level at 4 h post-ingestion (or as soon as feasible). Plot on nomogram - if above "treat" line → start NAC 72-h PO or 21-h IV protocol. Don't delay NAC for nomogram if presentation is late or staggered.
</Clinical>
