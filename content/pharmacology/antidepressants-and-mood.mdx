## Antidepressant overview

| Class | Workhorse | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **SSRI** | Sertraline, Fluoxetine, Escitalopram | 5-HT reuptake block (SERT) |
| **SNRI** | Venlafaxine, Duloxetine | 5-HT + NE reuptake block |
| **TCA** | Amitriptyline, Nortriptyline | 5-HT + NE reuptake + H1 + M + α blocks |
| **MAOI** | Phenelzine, Tranylcypromine, Selegiline | Block monoamine oxidase → ↑ all monoamines |
| **Atypical** | Bupropion, Mirtazapine, Trazodone | Various |

All antidepressants take **2–6 weeks** for clinical effect. Black box: **suicidality** in age &lt;25.

## SSRIs

**Fluoxetine, Sertraline, Paroxetine, Citalopram, Escitalopram, Fluvoxamine**

**First-line** for depression, anxiety, panic, OCD, PTSD, bulimia, premenstrual dysphoric disorder.

| Drug | Half-life | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluoxetine** | **5 days** (active metabolite **norfluoxetine** 7-14 d) | Self-taper effect; OK in pregnancy; pediatric indication |
| **Sertraline** | 26 h | Workhorse; safe in cardiac disease + pregnancy |
| **Paroxetine** | 21 h | Most anticholinergic; **avoid in pregnancy** (cardiac defects); withdrawal worst |
| **Citalopram** | 35 h | **QT prolongation** at high dose (max 40 mg, 20 mg if elderly) |
| **Escitalopram** | 30 h | Cleaner profile vs citalopram; QT but less |
| **Fluvoxamine** | 16 h | OCD; high CYP1A2/3A4 inhibition - many interactions |

**Side effects**:
- **Sexual dysfunction** (↓ libido, anorgasmia, ED) - 30–60%, often dose-limiting. Switch to bupropion or add bupropion to mitigate.
- **GI** (nausea, diarrhea) - tolerance.
- **Weight gain** - small, paroxetine worst.
- **SIADH / hyponatremia** - especially elderly + diuretics.
- **Bleeding** - inhibit platelet 5-HT (combine with NSAID = additive GI bleed).
- **QT prolongation** - especially citalopram.
- **Insomnia or sedation** - paradoxical; varies by drug.
- **Activation** - particularly young patients; can unmask mania.
- **Serotonin syndrome** when combined with other serotonergics.
- **Discontinuation syndrome** (esp. paroxetine, sertraline; rare with fluoxetine due to long t½) - flu-like, dizzy, "brain zaps."

## SNRIs

**Venlafaxine, Desvenlafaxine, Duloxetine, Levomilnacipran**

**Use**: depression, anxiety, **neuropathic pain** (duloxetine for diabetic neuropathy, fibromyalgia), stress incontinence (duloxetine in Europe).

**Side effects**: same as SSRIs + **HTN** (venlafaxine - dose-dependent), sweating, urinary hesitancy.

**Venlafaxine** withdrawal is notoriously bad (short t½, no active metabolite). Tapering essential.

**Duloxetine** - hepatic; avoid in significant alcohol use or liver disease.

## TCAs

**Amitriptyline, Nortriptyline, Imipramine, Clomipramine, Desipramine, Doxepin**

Older - largely replaced by SSRIs for depression but useful for specific conditions:
- **Neuropathic pain** (esp. amitriptyline, nortriptyline).
- **Migraine prophylaxis**.
- **Enuresis** (imipramine).
- **OCD** (clomipramine).
- **Insomnia** (low-dose doxepin).

**Mechanism**: 5-HT + NE reuptake block + H1 antihistamine + muscarinic block + α1 block + Na-channel block.

**Side effects** (the "three C's of TCA overdose"):
1. **Convulsions** (seizures).
2. **Coma** (CNS depression).
3. **Cardiac** - Na-channel block → wide QRS, AV block, arrhythmia, hypotension.

Plus chronic: anticholinergic burden (dry mouth, urinary retention, constipation, blurred vision), sedation, weight gain, orthostatic, sexual dysfunction.

**Overdose** = leading cause of antidepressant death historically. Treatment: **sodium bicarbonate** for QRS >100 ms (overcomes Na-channel block) + supportive + activated charcoal.

**Tertiary amines** (amitriptyline, imipramine, clomipramine, doxepin) = more side effects.
**Secondary amines** (nortriptyline, desipramine) = better tolerated.

## MAOIs

**Phenelzine, Tranylcypromine, Isocarboxazid** - non-selective irreversible.
**Selegiline** - selective MAO-B (low dose Parkinson; transdermal for depression bypasses gut MAO).

**Mechanism**: block monoamine oxidase → ↑ 5-HT, NE, DA, tyramine.

**Used**: treatment-resistant depression (the "last-line" antidepressant historically).

**Side effects + interactions** are why they're rarely used:
- **Hypertensive crisis** with **tyramine-rich foods** (aged cheese, wine, cured meats, fermented foods) - tyramine normally degraded by gut MAO → enters circulation → releases NE.
- **Serotonin syndrome** with any other serotonergic (SSRI, SNRI, TCA, tramadol, meperidine, linezolid, methylene blue, ondansetron, triptans).
- **2-week washout** between MAOI and any of the above (5 weeks for fluoxetine).

## Atypical antidepressants

### Bupropion

**Mechanism**: NE + DA reuptake block.

**Use**: depression, seasonal affective, smoking cessation (Zyban), ADHD adjunct, weight-neutral antidepressant.

**Pros**: no sexual side effects, no weight gain, no serotonin syndrome.

**Cons**:
- **Lowers seizure threshold** - contraindicated in seizure disorder, eating disorders (bulimia/anorexia - electrolyte derangements), recent BZD/ETOH withdrawal.
- Insomnia, anxiety (stimulating).
- HTN.

### Mirtazapine (Remeron)

**Mechanism**: α2 antagonist (↑ NE + 5-HT release) + 5-HT2/3 antagonism + H1 antagonist.

**Use**: depression with insomnia + weight loss (good for elderly, cachexia).

**Side effects**: **sedation** + **weight gain** (good for some patients), rare agranulocytosis.

### Trazodone

5-HT2 + α1 blockade + weak 5-HT reuptake block. Sedating - used **off-label for sleep** at low dose (50-100 mg).

Side effects: orthostasis, priapism (rare but serious), sedation.

### Vilazodone, Vortioxetine

Mixed SSRI + 5-HT1A partial agonist. May have fewer sexual side effects. Vortioxetine claims pro-cognitive effect (modest evidence).

### Esketamine

Intranasal NMDA antagonist (S-isomer of ketamine). FDA-approved for treatment-resistant depression as adjunct to oral antidepressant. **Black box**: dissociation, sedation - REMS program (administer in clinic with monitoring).

IV ketamine off-label for severe depression - rapid onset (hours-days vs weeks).

### Brexanolone, Zuranolone

GABA-A modulators for **postpartum depression**. IV brexanolone (60-hour infusion), oral zuranolone (14-day course).

## Serotonin syndrome

**Cause**: combining serotonergics (SSRI + MAOI most classic, but any combination). Also overdose alone.

**Triad**:
1. **Mental status changes** (agitation, confusion, hallucinations).
2. **Autonomic instability** (hyperthermia, tachycardia, ↑ BP, mydriasis, sweating, **diarrhea**).
3. **Neuromuscular hyperactivity** (myoclonus, **hyperreflexia**, **clonus** - especially lower extremities, tremor, rigidity).

**Distinguish from NMS**:
| Feature | Serotonin syndrome | NMS |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Hours | Days-weeks |
| Reflexes | **Hyperreflexia, clonus** | Hyporeflexia |
| Rigidity | Lower>upper, less dense | **Lead-pipe** (upper > lower) |
| GI | **Diarrhea, hyperactive bowel** | Normal/reduced |
| Pupils | **Mydriasis** | Normal |
| Trigger | Serotonergic drug | Dopamine antagonist or DA withdrawal |

**Treatment**:
1. Stop offending drug.
2. Supportive: cooling, IV fluids, BZD for agitation.
3. **Cyproheptadine** (5-HT2 antagonist) for severe.

## Lithium

**Mechanism**: poorly defined - ↓ second messenger turnover (IP3, DAG), ↓ glycogen synthase kinase-3.

**Use**: bipolar (acute mania + maintenance), augmentation in resistant depression.

**Pharmacology**:
- Renal clearance; **narrow TI** (0.6-1.2 mEq/L therapeutic; >1.5 toxic; >2.5 severe).
- Predictable level after **5 days at steady state**.
- Affected by: NSAIDs, thiazide diuretics, ACE-i, dehydration, low salt diet - all ↑ lithium level.

**Side effects** (acute):
- Tremor (fine).
- GI (nausea, diarrhea).
- Polyuria, polydipsia (**nephrogenic DI**).
- Weight gain.
- Acne, psoriasis worsening.
- Hypothyroidism (~30% chronic use).
- T-wave flattening (benign).

**Toxicity** (chronic + acute):
- **Tremor coarse** (vs fine baseline tremor).
- **Ataxia, dysarthria, confusion**.
- Seizures, coma.
- Arrhythmia.
- Renal: chronic interstitial nephritis.
- **Teratogen** - **Ebstein anomaly** (1st trimester).

**Treatment**: hydration, hemodialysis if >2.5 mEq/L or severe symptoms.

**Lithium monitoring**: level (target 0.6-1.0 for maintenance, 0.8-1.2 acute mania), TSH, creatinine, urine osmolality - at least every 6-12 months.

## Other mood stabilizers

| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| **Valproate** | Acute mania (best for mixed/rapid cycling); maintenance |
| **Lamotrigine** | Bipolar depression + maintenance (NOT acute mania) |
| **Carbamazepine** | Acute mania (esp. complicated) |
| **Atypical antipsychotics** | Quetiapine, olanzapine, risperidone, aripiprazole, lurasidone, cariprazine - for mania, mixed episodes, and bipolar depression (lurasidone, quetiapine) |

## Choosing an antidepressant

| Comorbidity | Preferred |
|---|---|
| Anxiety | SSRI (sertraline, escitalopram) |
| Depression + neuropathic pain | Duloxetine, TCA |
| Depression + obesity | Bupropion (weight-neutral or loss) |
| Depression + insomnia | Mirtazapine, trazodone adjunct |
| Depression + sexual side effects from prior | Bupropion |
| Pregnancy | Sertraline, fluoxetine (avoid paroxetine) |
| Cardiac disease | Sertraline (most cardiac data) |
| Severe / treatment-resistant | ECT, IV ketamine, esketamine, MAOI |
| Postpartum | Sertraline + therapy; brexanolone/zuranolone if severe |
| Atypical depression | MAOI traditionally; SSRI more practical |
| Premenstrual dysphoric disorder | SSRI - can dose only in luteal phase |
| OCD | Higher SSRI doses (sertraline up to 200, fluoxetine 60); clomipramine |
| Smoking cessation comorbid | Bupropion |
| Migraine + depression | Amitriptyline |

<Clinical>
**Depression first treatment**: SSRI - sertraline or escitalopram typically. 4-6 weeks at target dose before declaring failure. If partial response, optimize dose. If no response, switch antidepressant (within class or across class) or augment (bupropion, mirtazapine, atypical antipsychotic, lithium).

**Treatment-resistant depression** (>2 trials at adequate doses): ECT, IV ketamine, esketamine, MAOI, TMS.

**Bipolar - patient on antidepressant alone**: STOP. Antidepressant alone in bipolar can induce **manic switch** + rapid cycling. Add mood stabilizer + reassess.

**Patient on SSRI starts tramadol after surgery**: serotonin syndrome risk. Use morphine instead.

**Lithium + new NSAID for back pain**: NSAID inhibits renal PG → ↓ renal blood flow → ↑ lithium level → toxicity. Counsel + check level.

**Postpartum depression**: SSRI is safe in breastfeeding (sertraline preferred). Brexanolone (60 hr IV in hospital) or zuranolone (14-day oral) for severe.
</Clinical>
