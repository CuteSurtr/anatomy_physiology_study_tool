## The coagulation cascade refresher

- **Intrinsic** (contact - aPTT): XII → XI → IX → VIII → X.
- **Extrinsic** (tissue factor - PT/INR): VII + TF → X.
- **Common**: X + V → prothrombin (II) → thrombin (IIa) → fibrinogen → fibrin → cross-linked by XIII.
- **Anticoagulant proteins**: antithrombin (inhibits IIa, Xa); protein C/S (inactivate Va, VIIIa); tissue factor pathway inhibitor.
- **Fibrinolysis**: plasminogen → plasmin (by tPA) → breaks fibrin → D-dimer.

## Heparin family

### Unfractionated heparin (UFH)

**Mechanism**: binds antithrombin → 1000× ↑ AT activity → inactivates **IIa + Xa** (1:1).

**Monitoring**: **aPTT** (target 1.5–2.5× control) or anti-Xa level.

**Onset**: IV immediate; SC 1–2 h.

**Half-life**: 1–2 h (dose-dependent).

**Uses**: VTE prophylaxis (SC), VTE treatment (IV), ACS, bridging, CRRT, ECMO, pregnancy (doesn't cross placenta).

**Side effects**:
- Bleeding (reversible with **protamine sulfate** 1 mg per 100 U).
- **HIT (heparin-induced thrombocytopenia)** - immune; **IgG vs heparin-PF4** complex. Onset day 5–10 (or &lt;24 h if previously exposed). Platelets drop >50% from baseline. **Paradoxical thrombosis** (not bleeding). Stop heparin → switch to **argatroban or bivalirudin** or fondaparinux. Avoid platelet transfusion + warfarin until platelets recover.
- Osteoporosis with prolonged use.

### Low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH)

**Enoxaparin (Lovenox), Dalteparin**.

**Mechanism**: shorter chain → inhibits **Xa more than IIa** (4:1 ratio).

**Pros over UFH**: predictable dose-response, no aPTT monitoring (anti-Xa for select cases - pregnant, obese, renal), SC dosing, less HIT (1/10).

**Cons**: renally cleared (avoid CrCl &lt;30 or use lower dose), incomplete protamine reversal (~60% effective).

**Uses**: VTE prophylaxis + treatment (outpatient), ACS, pregnancy.

### Fondaparinux

Synthetic pentasaccharide - **selective Xa** inhibition via antithrombin. SC daily. No HIT (no PF4 affinity). Renally cleared.

Use: VTE prophylaxis + treatment, HIT (after platelets recover).

### Direct thrombin inhibitors

**Bivalirudin, Argatroban, Dabigatran (oral)**

- **Bivalirudin** - IV during PCI; short t½.
- **Argatroban** - IV; hepatic clearance (use in renal failure HIT).
- **Lepirudin** - withdrawn.

### Antidotes summary

| Drug | Reversal |
|---|---|
| **UFH** | Protamine sulfate (1 mg per 100 U) - complete |
| **LMWH** | Protamine - partial (~60%) |
| **Fondaparinux** | No reversal; supportive + rFVIIa rarely |
| **Bivalirudin/Argatroban** | No specific - short t½ → stop drug |

## Warfarin

**Mechanism**: blocks vitamin K epoxide reductase (VKOR) → ↓ functional **Factors II, VII, IX, X** + **proteins C + S**.

Protein C has shortest t½ - falls first → transient hypercoagulable state at initiation → **always bridge with heparin** for first 5 days + INR therapeutic ×2.

**Monitoring**: PT/INR. Targets:
- Most VTE, A-fib: **2.0–3.0**.
- Mechanical mitral valve, recurrent VTE on warfarin: **2.5–3.5**.

**Onset**: 36–72 h (need to consume circulating factors first). Full effect 5–7 d.

**Half-life**: 36 h.

**Side effects**:
- Bleeding.
- **Warfarin-induced skin necrosis** - protein C deficiency reveals hypercoagulability → microvascular thrombosis (esp. fatty tissue). Prevented by heparin bridge.
- **Teratogen** (1st trimester) - chondrodysplasia punctata, nasal hypoplasia. Use LMWH in pregnancy.
- **Purple toe syndrome** - cholesterol microembolization.

**Drug interactions** (huge list - CYP2C9 substrate):
- ↑ INR: amiodarone, sulfa, fluoroquinolones, metronidazole, fluconazole, TMP-SMX, omeprazole, statins.
- ↓ INR: rifampin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, St. John's wort, vitamin K, OCs, greens.

**Reversal**:
- Minor bleeding: hold + IV/PO vitamin K.
- Major bleeding: **4-factor PCC** (Kcentra) + IV vitamin K. (Reverses much faster than FFP.)
- Elective surgery: hold 5 days; bridge if mechanical valve or recent VTE.

## DOACs (Direct Oral Anticoagulants)

| Drug | Target | Renal % | Reversal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dabigatran** | IIa | 80% | **Idarucizumab** (Praxbind) |
| **Apixaban** | Xa | 25% | **Andexanet alfa** (Andexxa) |
| **Rivaroxaban** | Xa | 35% | **Andexanet alfa** |
| **Edoxaban** | Xa | 50% | **Andexanet alfa** |

**Advantages over warfarin**: no monitoring, fixed dosing, fewer drug-food interactions, faster onset, less intracranial bleed.

**Disadvantages**: more GI bleed (esp. dabigatran, rivaroxaban), expensive, antidote (especially andexanet) very expensive + short supply.

**Uses**: non-valvular A-fib (CHA2DS2-VASc ≥2 men, ≥3 women), VTE, prophylaxis after orthopedic surgery.

**Avoid in**: mechanical heart valves (RE-ALIGN dabigatran failed), antiphospholipid syndrome (TRAPS rivaroxaban failed), pregnancy.

**Apixaban** has the lowest bleed risk in head-to-head comparisons + minimal renal clearance.

**Dabigatran** can be dialyzed in overdose.

## Antiplatelets

### Aspirin

**Mechanism**: irreversible COX-1 inhibition → ↓ TXA2 in platelets (platelets can't synthesize new COX → effect lasts 7-10 d, platelet lifespan).

**Low dose** (81 mg) selectively inhibits platelet COX-1; **high dose** (325 mg+) also inhibits endothelial COX-2 prostacyclin (counterproductive).

**Uses**: secondary prevention (post-MI, post-stroke, ACS, PAD); primary prevention only in selected high-risk patients (controversial age-stratified per USPSTF 2022 - mostly age 40–59 with high ASCVD risk + low bleed risk).

**Side effects**: GI bleed (combine with PPI in high-risk), bronchospasm in **AERD/Samter triad** (asthma + nasal polyps + ASA sensitivity), Reye syndrome in kids with viral illness (avoid age &lt;16).

### P2Y12 inhibitors

| Drug | Mechanism | Reversibility | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clopidogrel** | Prodrug → active via CYP2C19 | Irreversible | Genetic poor metabolizers - ↓ efficacy |
| **Prasugrel** | Prodrug; more reliable activation | Irreversible | More bleeding; avoid age >75, weight &lt;60, prior stroke/TIA |
| **Ticagrelor** | Active drug; binds noncompetitively | **Reversible** | Dyspnea (~15%), bradycardia, ↑ uric acid; BID dosing |
| **Cangrelor** | IV; rapid on/off | Reversible | Bridge to PCI when oral not feasible |

**DAPT** (Dual antiplatelet therapy): aspirin + P2Y12 for 1–12 months after PCI (longer with drug-eluting stent or high ischemic risk).

**Premature DAPT discontinuation** is the leading cause of stent thrombosis - patient education critical.

### GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors

**Abciximab, Eptifibatide, Tirofiban** - IV; block final platelet aggregation step.

Use: rescue during PCI for thrombotic complications. Largely replaced by potent P2Y12 inhibitors.

Side effects: bleeding, thrombocytopenia.

### Other antiplatelets

- **Dipyridamole** - cAMP ↑; combined with aspirin (Aggrenox) for stroke prevention.
- **Cilostazol** - PDE3 inhibitor + antiplatelet; intermittent claudication (avoid in HF).

## Thrombolytics

**Alteplase (tPA), Tenecteplase (TNKase), Reteplase, Streptokinase (historical)**

**Mechanism**: convert plasminogen → plasmin → fibrin breakdown.

**Uses**:
- **Acute ischemic stroke**: alteplase within 4.5 h of onset (3 h with stricter criteria).
- **STEMI**: alteplase or tenecteplase within 12 h if PCI not available within 120 min.
- **Massive PE**: hemodynamically unstable.
- **DVT (catheter-directed)**: limb-threatening or iliofemoral.

**Tenecteplase**: weight-based single bolus; non-inferior to alteplase + easier dosing. Increasingly used in stroke.

**Contraindications**: recent stroke (3 mo), GI bleed (3 wk), surgery (10–14 d), known intracranial pathology, BP >185/110 in stroke, INR >1.7, plt &lt;100.

**Major complication**: bleeding (esp. intracerebral hemorrhage 6% stroke patients).

## Reversal agents summary

| Drug | Antidote |
|---|---|
| **Heparin** | Protamine (1 mg/100 U) |
| **LMWH** | Protamine (partial) |
| **Warfarin (major)** | 4F-PCC + vitamin K |
| **Warfarin (minor)** | PO/IV vitamin K |
| **Dabigatran** | Idarucizumab; dialysis |
| **Apixaban, Rivaroxaban, Edoxaban** | Andexanet alfa; 4F-PCC if andexanet unavailable |
| **tPA-induced bleed** | Cryoprecipitate (fibrinogen replacement), TXA |
| **Aspirin, P2Y12** | Platelet transfusion (if life-threatening); DDAVP |

## Bridging anticoagulation

| Risk | Strategy |
|---|---|
| **High thrombotic risk** (mechanical mitral valve, recent stroke, VTE &lt;3 mo) | LMWH bridge during warfarin hold |
| **Moderate risk** (A-fib + CHA2DS2-VASc ≥5) | Case-by-case |
| **Low risk** (A-fib + CHA2DS2-VASc ≤4, VTE >12 mo) | No bridge - BRIDGE trial showed no benefit, more bleeding |

**DOACs**: no bridging needed - just hold 24–48 h before procedure.

<Clinical>
**HIT in the cardiac ICU**: platelet drop on day 7 of heparin → 4T score → stop ALL heparin (including flushes) → start argatroban or bivalirudin. PF4 ELISA confirms. Once platelets recover, transition to warfarin (overlap 5+ days).

**A-fib stroke prevention**: CHA2DS2-VASc score; ≥2 (men) or ≥3 (women) → anticoagulate. DOAC preferred unless mechanical valve, mod-severe MS (use warfarin). Apixaban for high-bleed-risk elderly.

**VTE provoked vs unprovoked**: 3 months for provoked; indefinite for unprovoked (esp. recurrent) or with persistent risk factor (active cancer).

**Cancer-associated VTE**: previously LMWH for 3-6 months (CLOT trial). Now apixaban or rivaroxaban acceptable (CARAVAGGIO, SELECT-D). Caution with GI cancer + rivaroxaban (bleed signal).
</Clinical>
