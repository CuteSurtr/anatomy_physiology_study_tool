## Vaughan-Williams classification

| Class | Mechanism | Effect on AP |
|---|---|---|
| **IA** | Na-channel block (intermediate); also K block | ↓ phase 0 slope + ↑ AP duration |
| **IB** | Na-channel block (fast on/off) | Minimal QRS effect; ↓ AP duration |
| **IC** | Na-channel block (slow on/off) | Marked ↓ phase 0; flat AP |
| **II** | β-blockers | ↓ phase 4 slope in SA, ↓ AV conduction |
| **III** | K-channel block | ↑ AP duration + QT |
| **IV** | Ca-channel block (non-DHP) | ↓ SA, ↓ AV; ↓ phase 4 in SA |

Plus "other" - adenosine, atropine, magnesium, digoxin, ivabradine.

## Class I - sodium channel blockers

### IA - Quinidine, Procainamide, Disopyramide

**Use**: A-fib, AVRT/AVNRT, V-arrhythmias (rarely used now). Procainamide for hemodynamically stable wide-complex tachycardia + Brugada provocation.

**Side effects**:
- **Quinidine**: cinchonism (headache, tinnitus, GI), thrombocytopenia, **torsades** (QT prolongation), drug-induced lupus, ↑ digoxin level. **"Quinidine syncope"** = polymorphic VT.
- **Procainamide**: drug-induced lupus (slow acetylators - ANA, anti-histone), agranulocytosis.
- **Disopyramide**: very anticholinergic (urinary retention, dry mouth, blurred vision), negative inotrope → HF risk.

### IB - Lidocaine, Mexiletine, Phenytoin

**Use**: ventricular arrhythmias (esp. post-MI, digoxin toxicity). Phenytoin also for digoxin-induced V-arrhythmia.

**Side effects**: CNS - tremor, paresthesia, seizures, slurred speech (lidocaine toxicity).

Key trait: **Fastest on/off kinetics** → preferentially blocks already-depolarized (ischemic) tissue. That's why IB works in post-MI VT.

### IC - Flecainide, Propafenone

**Use**: A-fib rhythm control in **structurally normal hearts only** ("pill-in-the-pocket" strategy).

**CAST trial** (1989): flecainide ↑ mortality in post-MI patients → avoid in **structural heart disease or CAD**.

**Side effects**: pro-arrhythmic in structural heart disease, ↑ AV node conduction (paradoxically can cause 1:1 atrial flutter conduction → catastrophic ventricular rates - pair with AV-nodal blocker).

## Class II - β-blockers

| Drug | Uses |
|---|---|
| **Metoprolol, atenolol, propranolol** | Rate control in A-fib/flutter, SVT, post-MI |
| **Esmolol** | IV drip for rapid titration |
| **Sotalol** | Mixed class III + non-selective β; A-fib rhythm control + VT |

Mechanism: ↓ SA node firing (slowed phase 4 depolarization), ↓ AV nodal conduction. ↓ catecholamine-driven arrhythmias.

## Class III - potassium channel blockers (↑ AP duration + QT)

### Amiodarone

The kitchen-sink antiarrhythmic - blocks **all four** channel classes (some Na, K, Ca, β).

**Uses**: A-fib (rhythm + rate), V-arrhythmias (VT/VF), peri-arrest.

**Pharmacokinetics**: massive Vd (tissue-bound), t½ **40–60 days** (!). Liver-metabolized; not affected by renal function.

**Side effects** (the litany - "amiodarone affects every organ"):
- **Pulmonary fibrosis** (1–10% with chronic use; check baseline + annual PFT + CXR).
- **Hepatotoxicity** (LFT monitoring).
- **Hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism** (37% iodine by weight; check TSH).
- **Corneal microdeposits** (universal; rarely affect vision).
- **Blue-gray skin discoloration** (photosensitivity).
- **Peripheral neuropathy**.
- **QT prolongation** (less torsades than other K-blockers, paradoxically).
- **Drug interactions**: ↑ warfarin, ↑ digoxin, ↑ statin (rhabdo risk).

### Dronedarone

Amiodarone analog without iodine → no thyroid effects but ↓ efficacy + **black box for HF** (PALLAS trial - permanent A-fib + HF history → ↑ mortality).

### Sotalol

K-block + non-selective β-block. A-fib + VT. **In-hospital initiation** (QT monitoring) - **risk: torsades**. Renal dose adjust.

### Dofetilide

Pure K-blocker. A-fib rhythm control. **Strict in-hospital initiation** with continuous ECG (torsades risk). Renal dose adjust. Many drug-drug interactions (cimetidine, ketoconazole).

### Ibutilide

IV K-blocker for **acute A-fib/flutter conversion** to sinus. Quick QT prolongation; torsades risk - have magnesium ready.

## Class IV - non-dihydropyridine CCBs

**Verapamil, Diltiazem**: block Ca channels in SA + AV node → ↓ HR + AV conduction.

**Uses**: A-fib rate control (esp. in asthma where β-blocker problematic), SVT acute termination (verapamil IV - **NOT** in wide complex tachycardia).

**Avoid**: HFrEF (negative inotrope), severe AV block, **combined with β-blocker** (additive heart block).

**Side effects**: constipation (verapamil > diltiazem), peripheral edema, bradycardia.

## Other antiarrhythmics

### Adenosine

**Mechanism**: A1 receptor → ↑ K efflux → transient AV block (~6 sec).

**Use**: termination of SVT (AVNRT, AVRT) - the "pause" reveals/breaks the reentry.

**Dose**: 6 mg IV push → 12 mg → 12 mg. Use large-bore IV close to central + flush briskly.

**Side effects**: transient flushing, chest pressure, dyspnea, "doom." Brief bradycardia + asystole pause.

**Avoid in**: severe asthma (bronchospasm), heart transplant (denervated → super-sensitive).

**Useful as diagnostic**: in wide-complex tachycardia, adenosine may transiently reveal P waves if it's actually SVT with aberrancy.

### Atropine

For symptomatic bradycardia. 0.5–1 mg IV q3–5 min, max 3 mg. Won't help complete heart block (use pacing).

### Magnesium sulfate

**Use**: torsades de pointes (1st line), refractory VT/VF in arrest, hypomagnesemia, severe asthma. Mechanism poorly defined; calcium-channel modulating + membrane stabilizing.

### Digoxin

Useful for **rate control in A-fib** especially in HFrEF or hypotension where β-blocker/CCB poorly tolerated. Vagal-mediated AV slowing - **doesn't work in adrenergically driven states** (sepsis, post-op). See [heart failure drugs](/pharmacology/heart-failure-drugs) for details.

### Ivabradine

Sinus tachycardia only; won't slow A-fib (no SA node firing).

## "Drug of choice" matrix

| Arrhythmia | First-line | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **SVT (AVRT, AVNRT)** | Vagal maneuvers → adenosine → CCB/β-blocker | Ablate if recurrent |
| **A-fib rate control** | β-blocker or diltiazem/verapamil | Digoxin if HF/hypotension |
| **A-fib rhythm control (structural heart)** | Amiodarone | Dronedarone alt; avoid IC |
| **A-fib rhythm control (no structural heart)** | Flecainide, propafenone, sotalol | Pill-in-pocket option |
| **A-fib + WPW** | Procainamide IV; AVOID AV-nodal blockers (digoxin, CCB, adenosine) | Wide complex → can degenerate to VF |
| **Stable VT** | Amiodarone IV or procainamide IV | Cardiovert if unstable |
| **Pulseless VT/VF** | Defibrillate → epi → amiodarone or lidocaine | ACLS |
| **Torsades** | **Mg sulfate** IV, correct K + Mg, isoproterenol, pacing | Avoid QT prolongers |
| **Bradycardia (symptomatic)** | Atropine → epi/dopamine → pacing | |

## QT-prolonging drugs - know the offenders

| Class | Drugs |
|---|---|
| Antiarrhythmics | Quinidine, sotalol, dofetilide, ibutilide, amiodarone (less so) |
| Antibiotics | Macrolides (especially azithromycin, erythromycin), fluoroquinolones, TMP-SMX |
| Antipsychotics | Haloperidol, ziprasidone, quetiapine, droperidol |
| Antiemetics | Ondansetron, droperidol |
| Antidepressants | TCAs (amitriptyline), citalopram (dose-dependent) |
| Others | Methadone, hydroxychloroquine, ranolazine |

Risk factors: hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, female sex, bradycardia, congenital long QT.

<Clinical>
**Patient with A-fib + WPW** - never give AV-nodal blockers (CCB, β-blocker, digoxin, adenosine). They block AV node → atrial impulses race down accessory pathway → ventricular fibrillation. Use **procainamide** or cardiovert.

**Torsades de pointes** - IV **magnesium sulfate** (2 g IV bolus) regardless of serum Mg. Correct K to ≥4.5. Withdraw offending drugs. Overdrive pacing or isoproterenol if recurrent (↑ HR shortens QT).

**Adenosine for "wide complex tachycardia"** - controversial. If clearly VT, don't give. If unsure SVT with aberrancy vs VT, adenosine may be diagnostic (terminates AVNRT, not VT). Have shock pads ready.

**Long-term amiodarone monitoring**: baseline TSH, LFT, PFT, CXR, ophthalmologic exam. Repeat TSH + LFT q6 months; CXR + PFT annually or with symptoms.
</Clinical>

<HighYield variant="boards">
- "**CAST**" - Flecainide ↑ post-MI mortality. Avoid Class IC in structural heart disease.
- Amiodarone affects **lungs, liver, thyroid, skin, eyes, nerves**. Long t½ means side effects linger after stop.
- Procainamide → drug-induced lupus (slow acetylators; anti-histone Ab).
- Quinidine → cinchonism + torsades + thrombocytopenia.
- Lidocaine for V-arrhythmias, especially post-MI or digoxin toxicity. Toxicity = CNS (confusion → seizure).
- Adenosine - rapid IV push, flush. Brief asystole expected.
- Verapamil + β-blocker = heart block; never combine IV.
- WPW + A-fib = procainamide or cardiovert. NO AV-nodal blockers.
- Mg sulfate for torsades (1st line) even with normal Mg level.
- Sotalol + dofetilide need in-hospital QT monitoring at initiation.
- "Bidirectional VT" = digoxin toxicity. Antidote = Fab.
</HighYield>
