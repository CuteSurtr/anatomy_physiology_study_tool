## IV induction agents

| Drug | Mechanism | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Propofol** | GABA-A potentiation; some NMDA | Rapid on/off; antiemetic; bronchodilator | Hypotension, apnea, **propofol infusion syndrome** (prolonged use: rhabdo, metabolic acidosis), pain on injection, lipid emulsion (TG load), no analgesia |
| **Etomidate** | GABA-A | Hemodynamic stability (best for unstable patient) | **Adrenal suppression** (single dose can suppress 24 h); myoclonus on injection; no analgesia |
| **Ketamine** | NMDA antagonist | Bronchodilation, analgesia, preserved respiratory drive, hemodynamic support (catecholamine release) | Dissociation, emergence delirium, ↑ ICP/IOP (caveats), tachycardia, hallucinations, hypersalivation; depression treatment in low dose IV |
| **Midazolam** | GABA-A (allosteric BZD) | Amnesia, anxiolysis | Respiratory depression (esp. + opioid); ↓ BP; reversal = **flumazenil** |
| **Thiopental** | GABA-A barbiturate | Historic; rapid | Hypotension, respiratory depression, ↓ ICP (used in ↑ICP); largely replaced |

## Volatile (inhaled) anesthetics

| Drug | MAC (%) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sevoflurane** | 2.0 | Sweet smell - inhalation induction in peds; not pungent; **renal toxicity** at high doses (Compound A) with low fresh gas flow |
| **Isoflurane** | 1.15 | Workhorse; pungent (not for induction); preserves CO better than des/sevo |
| **Desflurane** | 6.0 | Fastest on/off; pungent (NO INDUCTION); ozone-depleting; "tachycardia + HTN" with rapid increase |
| **Nitrous oxide (N₂O)** | 105 | Adjunct only - can't achieve full anesthesia alone; ↓ vitamin B12 (megaloblastic anemia with prolonged use); diffuses into air-filled cavities (avoid in bowel obstruction, pneumothorax) |
| **Halothane** | 0.75 | Hepatitis (autoimmune); arrhythmia; rarely used now (still common in low-resource settings) |

**MAC** (Minimum Alveolar Concentration) = concentration at which 50% of patients don't move to surgical incision. **Higher MAC = less potent** (need more drug). MAC = oil:gas partition coefficient inversely related (more lipid soluble = lower MAC = more potent).

**Effects of volatile anesthetics**:
- ↓ BP (vasodilation, ↓ contractility).
- ↓ tidal volume + ↓ response to CO₂.
- Bronchodilation.
- Uterine relaxation (problematic in C-section).
- Trigger **malignant hyperthermia** (with succinylcholine).

## Malignant hyperthermia (MH)

**Trigger**: volatile anesthetics + succinylcholine in susceptible (RyR1 mutation).

**Mechanism**: uncontrolled Ca²⁺ release from sarcoplasmic reticulum → sustained muscle contraction + heat + ATP demand.

**Presentation**: hypercarbia (earliest sign!), masseter rigidity, tachycardia, hyperthermia (later), rhabdomyolysis, hyperK, acidosis, eventually DIC + death.

**Treatment**:
1. **Dantrolene** 2.5 mg/kg IV - blocks RyR1; repeat to total 10 mg/kg if needed.
2. Stop volatiles + succinylcholine.
3. 100% O₂ + hyperventilate.
4. Active cooling.
5. Correct hyperK, acidosis, arrhythmias.
6. Monitor for recrudescence; dantrolene q4–6h × 24 h.

Mortality 5–10% with treatment; >70% without.

## Neuromuscular blocking agents

### Depolarizing - succinylcholine

**Mechanism**: nicotinic ACh receptor agonist that persistently depolarizes endplate → desensitization block.

**Pharmacokinetics**: rapid (60 s), short (5 min); metabolized by plasma pseudocholinesterase.

**Uses**: rapid sequence intubation (RSI).

**Side effects**:
- **Fasciculations** then paralysis - causes muscle pain post-op.
- **Hyperkalemia** - especially burns, crush injury, denervation injuries (>48 h), severe sepsis, MS, ALS (upregulated immature ACh receptors).
- **Malignant hyperthermia** trigger.
- **Pseudocholinesterase deficiency** - prolonged paralysis (genetic or acquired).
- ↑ ICP/IOP/gastric pressure.
- Bradycardia (esp. peds, second dose).

### Non-depolarizing - "curium" + "curonium"

| Drug | Onset / Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Rocuronium** | 1 min / 30-40 min | Alternative to succinylcholine for RSI (high dose); reversible by sugammadex |
| **Vecuronium** | 2-3 min / 30-40 min | |
| **Cisatracurium** | 2 min / 45 min | **Hofmann elimination** (no organ dependence) - liver/renal failure friendly |
| **Pancuronium** | 3 min / 60-90 min | Long; vagolytic → tachycardia |
| **Atracurium** | 2 min / 30 min | Hofmann elimination + ester hydrolysis; histamine release (caution in asthma) |

**Reversal**:
- **Neostigmine + glycopyrrolate** - AChE inhibition pushes ACh into competition with non-depol blocker. Glycopyrrolate blocks muscarinic side effects.
- **Sugammadex** - cyclodextrin that physically encapsulates rocuronium/vecuronium → instant reversal; faster + cleaner than neostigmine; expensive. Reverses rocuronium even from deep blockade.

## Local anesthetics

### Mechanism

Block voltage-gated Na+ channels in nerve axons → no action potential. Bind from inside the cell - need to pass through membrane (uncharged form) then ionize at intracellular pH (charged form binds Na channel).

**Smaller, myelinated fibers blocked first**: pain (Aδ + C) → temperature → touch → pressure → motor (Aα). "Sympathetic block" precedes sensory block in spinal/epidural.

### Two chemical classes

| Class | Suffix | Drugs | Metabolism | Allergy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Amides** | "...caine" with **two i's** | Lidocaine, Bupivacaine, Mepivacaine, Ropivacaine, Prilocaine, Articaine | Hepatic (CYP3A4 + others) | Very rare allergy |
| **Esters** | "...caine" with **one i** | Procaine, Tetracaine, Cocaine, Benzocaine, Chloroprocaine | Plasma pseudocholinesterase → PABA | More allergy (PABA) |

**Trick**: "Lidocaine" = li-do-ca-ine = 2 "i"s = amide. "Procaine" = pro-ca-ine = 1 "i" = ester.

### Drug-specific points

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Lidocaine** | Workhorse; injection 1-2%, topical, IV antiarrhythmic |
| **Bupivacaine** | Long-acting; high cardiotoxicity (worse than other amides - **liposomal bupivacaine** longer-acting for blocks) |
| **Ropivacaine** | Less cardiotoxic than bupivacaine; long-acting |
| **Mepivacaine** | Intermediate duration |
| **Procaine** | Short-acting ester; rarely used now |
| **Tetracaine** | Ophthalmic; spinal anesthesia |
| **Benzocaine** | Topical; **methemoglobinemia** (treat with methylene blue) |
| **Cocaine** | Ester + sympathomimetic (NE reuptake block); ENT topical (vasoconstrictor benefit) |

### Adjuncts

- **Epinephrine** - vasoconstriction → prolongs effect + ↓ systemic absorption + acts as marker for intravascular injection. **Avoid in end-arterioles** (fingers, toes, penis, nose, ears - "fingers, nose, toes, hose"). Modern view: low dose with lidocaine on digits probably safe but traditionally avoided.
- **Sodium bicarbonate** - alkalinize → ↑ unionized form → faster onset, less pain on injection.
- **Hyaluronidase** - spreads block.

### Local anesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST)

**Mechanism**: inadvertent IV injection → high serum levels → Na-channel block throughout body.

**Progression**:
1. Tingling lips/tongue, tinnitus, metallic taste.
2. Drowsiness, agitation, slurred speech.
3. Seizure.
4. Cardiac: hypotension, bradycardia, prolonged PR/QRS/QT, asystole; **bupivacaine** especially → refractory VF.

**Treatment**:
1. **Stop injection**.
2. **20% Intralipid** (lipid emulsion) bolus + infusion - "lipid sink" for lipophilic drug.
3. Supportive: airway, oxygen, BZD for seizure, ACLS modifications (low-dose epi, avoid vasopressin/CCB).
4. Cardiopulmonary bypass / ECMO for refractory.

### Methemoglobinemia

**Caused by**: benzocaine (topical mucosal sprays), prilocaine, dapsone, nitrites, sulfonamides.

**Mechanism**: oxidation of Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ in hemoglobin → can't carry O₂ + leftward shift.

**Presentation**: cyanosis with normal pO₂; pulse ox falsely reads ~85%; "chocolate" blood.

**Treatment**: **methylene blue** IV (reduces Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺ via NADPH-dependent reductase). Caveat: G6PD deficiency → hemolysis.

## Sedation depth scale

| Depth | Response | Airway | Spont. Breathing | CV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Minimal** | Normal to voice | OK | OK | OK |
| **Moderate ("conscious")** | Purposeful to voice/touch | OK | OK | OK |
| **Deep** | Purposeful to repeated/painful stim | May need help | May need help | OK |
| **General anesthesia** | Unarousable | Often needed | Often needs help | May need help |

## Spinal vs epidural

| | Spinal | Epidural |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Subarachnoid (CSF) | Epidural space |
| Volume | 1-3 mL | 10-20 mL |
| Onset | 5 min | 15-30 min |
| Duration | Single shot, 1-3 h | Catheter for hours-days |
| Density | Dense block | Lighter, titratable |
| Hypotension | Common | Less |
| Headache | "Spinal headache" if dural puncture | Rare |

**Post-dural puncture headache (PDPH)**: positional headache from CSF leak. Treat with hydration, caffeine, blood patch.

<Clinical>
**RSI cocktail**: pre-oxygenate → induction (etomidate 0.3 mg/kg or propofol 1.5 mg/kg or ketamine 1-2 mg/kg) → paralytic (succinylcholine 1-1.5 mg/kg or rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg) → intubate. Sellick maneuver controversial.

**MH crisis**: immediate dantrolene (call MH hotline, have cart ready in OR). Recrudescence common - continue dantrolene 24h.

**LAST during interscalene block**: stop injection, lipid emulsion 1.5 mL/kg bolus then 0.25 mL/kg/min infusion, ACLS with low-dose epi (10–100 μg). Bupivacaine arrest is **prolonged** - may need ECMO.

**Awake craniotomy**: scalp block with lido + bupi + epi; dexmedetomidine sedation; intermittent ketamine for prone repositioning. Patient must speak during surgery.

**Pseudocholinesterase deficiency**: prolonged paralysis after succinylcholine. Ventilate until reversed. Test family. Avoid succ + mivacurium in future.
</Clinical>
