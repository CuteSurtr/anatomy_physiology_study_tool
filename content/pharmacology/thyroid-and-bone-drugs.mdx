## Hypothyroidism

### Levothyroxine (T4) - the standard

**Mechanism**: synthetic T4; converted to active T3 in tissues.

**Pharmacokinetics**: t½ ~7 days → daily dosing; steady state 4–6 weeks.

**Dosing**:
- Adult full replacement: 1.6 μg/kg/day.
- Elderly or cardiac disease: start 25–50 μg, titrate.
- Pregnancy: ↑ requirement ~30% in 1st trimester.
- Take **on empty stomach** (morning, 30–60 min before food); calcium, iron, antacids, PPIs, soy interfere with absorption.

**Monitoring**: TSH at 6 weeks after dose change, then 6–12 months.

**Side effects**: under-replacement → persistent hypothyroidism; over-replacement → A-fib, osteoporosis, anxiety, palpitations.

### Liothyronine (T3) - adjunct only

Faster onset; rarely used alone (irregular hormone delivery). Sometimes added to T4 in patients who feel poorly on T4 alone; limited evidence.

### Myxedema coma

Severe hypothyroidism + altered mental status, hypothermia, hypotension. Treat with **IV levothyroxine** ± T3 + **stress-dose hydrocortisone** (rule out concomitant adrenal insufficiency first - if you give T4 first in Schmidt syndrome, you precipitate adrenal crisis). Supportive care, treat precipitant (sepsis, MI).

## Hyperthyroidism

### Methimazole - first-line antithyroid

**Mechanism**: inhibits thyroid peroxidase → ↓ iodination of tyrosine + coupling → ↓ T4/T3 synthesis.

**Pros**: once-daily, more potent than PTU, fewer rare severe ADRs.

**Side effects**:
- **Agranulocytosis** (0.5%) - sudden severe sore throat + fever → stop drug + CBC.
- Hepatotoxicity (cholestatic).
- Rash, arthralgia.
- **Teratogenic in 1st trimester** (methimazole embryopathy: aplasia cutis, choanal atresia, omphalocele) - switch to PTU.

### Propylthiouracil (PTU)

**Mechanism**: same as methimazole + blocks peripheral T4→T3 conversion.

**Reserved** for: 1st trimester pregnancy, thyroid storm (peripheral conversion benefit).

**Side effects**:
- Hepatotoxicity - **fulminant hepatic failure** (rare; black box).
- ANCA-positive vasculitis.
- Same agranulocytosis risk.

### Radioactive iodine (I-131)

Definitive treatment for Graves disease + toxic multinodular goiter. Causes hypothyroidism (~80% by 1 year) → lifelong levothyroxine.

**Contraindications**: pregnancy, breastfeeding, severe ophthalmopathy (can worsen).

**Pretreatment**: methimazole to euthyroid, then stop 3-5 days before I-131.

### Potassium iodide (SSKI, Lugol)

**Mechanism**: high-dose iodide blocks thyroid hormone release (**Wolff-Chaikoff effect**); also blocks organification briefly. Effect is transient (~10 days).

**Uses**: thyroid storm (after PTU), pre-thyroidectomy (↓ vascularity), radiation emergency (blocks I-131 uptake).

**Caveat**: in iodine-deficient patients with autonomously functioning nodules, iodide can paradoxically trigger thyrotoxicosis (**Jod-Basedow phenomenon**).

### β-blockers

Symptomatic treatment of hyperthyroidism (tremor, tachycardia, anxiety). **Propranolol** preferred - non-selective + ↓ peripheral T4→T3 conversion at high dose.

### Thyroid storm

**Aggressive treatment, mortality 20%**:
1. **Propranolol** IV.
2. **PTU** (preferred over methimazole for peripheral conversion).
3. **Iodide** (potassium iodide or Lugol) - 1 h **after** PTU/methimazole loading (otherwise iodine feeds new hormone synthesis).
4. **Glucocorticoid** (hydrocortisone) - ↓ peripheral T4→T3, treat possible coexisting adrenal insufficiency.
5. **Cooling**, IV fluids, treat precipitant (infection, surgery, DKA, MI).

## Calcium / bone metabolism drugs

### Bisphosphonates

**Alendronate, Risedronate, Ibandronate, Zoledronate, Pamidronate**

**Mechanism**: bind hydroxyapatite → ingested by osteoclasts → inhibit farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase → osteoclast apoptosis. ↓ bone resorption.

**Pharmacology**: oral poorly absorbed (1%); take on empty stomach with full glass of water + stay upright 30 min (esophagitis). IV options (zoledronate annual; pamidronate q3–4 months).

**Uses**: postmenopausal osteoporosis, glucocorticoid-induced, malignancy hypercalcemia, Paget disease, bone metastases.

**Side effects**:
- **Esophagitis** (oral) - take properly.
- **Hypocalcemia** - ensure Ca + vit D replete first.
- **Osteonecrosis of jaw (ONJ)** - rare; dental work before starting; IV >> oral risk.
- **Atypical femur fracture** - rare; bilateral subtrochanteric; reconsider after 5 years (drug holiday).
- **Flu-like reaction** with first IV dose (zoledronate).
- **Renal impairment** - avoid GFR &lt;30.

**Duration**: 5 years oral or 3 years IV → "drug holiday" if low risk.

### Denosumab

**Mechanism**: human monoclonal Ab vs **RANKL** → blocks RANK binding on osteoclast precursor → ↓ osteoclast formation/activity.

**Use**: osteoporosis (60 mg SC q6 months), bone metastases (120 mg SC q4 weeks).

**Pros**: no GI issues, works in CKD.

**Cons**:
- **Rebound vertebral fractures** if stopped abruptly - **must transition to bisphosphonate** if discontinued.
- Hypocalcemia.
- ONJ, atypical femur fracture (same as bisphosphonate).
- Infections (cellulitis).

### Anabolic agents - "build bone"

| Drug | Mechanism | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Teriparatide** (PTH 1-34) | Intermittent PTH stimulates osteoblasts > osteoclasts | Severe osteoporosis | 2-year lifetime limit (rat osteosarcoma signal) |
| **Abaloparatide** (PTHrP analog) | Similar to teriparatide | Severe osteoporosis | 2-year limit |
| **Romosozumab** | Anti-sclerostin Ab → ↑ osteoblast + ↓ osteoclast | Severe osteoporosis post-fracture | 1-year course; **CV signal** - avoid if recent MI/stroke (black box) |

Sequence: anabolic first → then antiresorptive (bisphosphonate or denosumab) to maintain gains.

### Calcitonin

Salmon calcitonin (Miacalcin) - inhibits osteoclasts. Modest fracture reduction; mostly historical now. Useful **short-term** for acute hypercalcemia + acute vertebral fracture pain. Tachyphylaxis within weeks.

### Calcium + vitamin D

Always pair with osteoporosis drugs (1200 mg elemental Ca + 800 IU D₃ daily). Don't bother with mega-doses (no benefit beyond 1000–2000 IU vitamin D for typical adults; toxicity at sustained >10,000 IU).

| Vitamin D form | Use |
|---|---|
| **Cholecalciferol (D₃)** | Standard supplementation |
| **Ergocalciferol (D₂)** | Less bioavailable; used in high-dose Rx |
| **Calcifediol (25-OH-D)** | Skipping liver hydroxylation - useful in liver disease |
| **Calcitriol (1,25-(OH)₂-D)** | Active form; for CKD, hypoparathyroidism, vitamin D-resistant rickets |
| **Paricalcitol, Doxercalciferol** | Vitamin D analogs for secondary hyperparathyroidism in CKD (less ↑ Ca/PO₄) |

### Calcimimetics

**Cinacalcet, Etelcalcetide** - allosteric activators of calcium-sensing receptor on parathyroid → ↓ PTH secretion.

**Uses**: secondary hyperparathyroidism in dialysis, parathyroid carcinoma, severe primary hyperparathyroidism unable to undergo surgery.

**Side effects**: nausea, vomiting, hypocalcemia.

### Phosphate binders (for CKD)

**Calcium acetate, Sevelamer, Lanthanum, Sucroferric oxyhydroxide, Ferric citrate**

Bind dietary PO₄ in gut → reduce serum PO₄ in CKD.

- Calcium-based: cheap; risk of vascular calcification.
- Non-calcium-based: more expensive; less calcium load.

### Treatment of acute hypercalcemia

1. **IV NS** - aggressive hydration; ↑ urinary Ca excretion.
2. **Furosemide** - after rehydration only (don't dehydrate further); ↑ Ca excretion.
3. **Bisphosphonate (IV zoledronate or pamidronate)** - sustained effect 2–4 weeks; onset 2–4 days.
4. **Calcitonin SC** - fastest onset (hours), but tachyphylaxis.
5. **Glucocorticoid** - for vitamin D-mediated (lymphoma, sarcoid).
6. **Calcimimetic** - parathyroid carcinoma.
7. **Dialysis** - severe, refractory.

### Treatment of hypocalcemia

- **Symptomatic / severe**: IV calcium gluconate (preferred, less tissue irritation than CaCl) 1–2 g over 10 min, then drip.
- **Mild**: oral calcium + vitamin D.
- Correct **magnesium** first (low Mg blocks PTH).
- Correct underlying cause (vitamin D deficiency, hypoparathyroidism, CKD).

<Clinical>
**Levothyroxine + iron / calcium**: separate by 4 hours. Patient with poorly controlled TSH despite increasing dose - check for absorption interference (iron, PPI, calcium, soy, malabsorption).

**Pregnancy + hyperthyroidism**: PTU 1st trimester, switch to methimazole 2nd-3rd. Goal: lowest dose maintaining maternal free T4 in upper-normal range.

**Bisphosphonate for malignancy hypercalcemia**: pamidronate 60–90 mg IV or zoledronate 4 mg IV; onset 2–4 days; lasts weeks. Pair with calcitonin SC for rapid effect.

**Osteoporosis post-fracture**: severe risk → consider romosozumab or teriparatide as initial agent, transition to bisphosphonate or denosumab after 1–2 years.

**Patient stopping denosumab**: never stop alone - transition to bisphosphonate to prevent rebound vertebral fractures.
</Clinical>
