## Polyenes - bind ergosterol

### Amphotericin B

**Mechanism**: binds ergosterol in fungal membrane → forms pore → leakage → death.

**Spectrum**: broad - **Candida, Cryptococcus, Aspergillus, mucor, Histoplasma, Blastomyces, Coccidioides, Sporothrix, Leishmania**.

**Formulations**:
- **Deoxycholate (conventional)** - cheapest; most toxic.
- **Lipid complex (ABLC)** - lower nephrotoxicity.
- **Liposomal (L-AmB, AmBisome)** - lowest toxicity; preferred when affordable.

**Uses**: severe / disseminated fungal infections, cryptococcal meningitis, mucormycosis.

**Side effects**:
- **Nephrotoxicity** - dose-related; ATN; pre-hydrate with NS, avoid concurrent nephrotoxins.
- **Hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia** - distal tubular wasting; supplement.
- **Infusion reactions** - rigors, fever, hypotension - pretreat with acetaminophen, diphenhydramine ± meperidine; slow rate; liposomal less.
- **Anemia** (normocytic, ↓ EPO).
- **Phlebitis** - central line if prolonged.

"**Amphoterrible**" - clinicians' nickname for the side effects.

### Nystatin

Polyene like amphotericin but **too toxic for systemic use** → only topical/oral suspension.

Uses: oral candidiasis ("swish and swallow"), GI candidiasis, vaginal candidiasis.

## Azoles - inhibit ergosterol synthesis

**Mechanism**: inhibit fungal CYP51 (lanosterol 14-α-demethylase) → ↓ ergosterol synthesis → defective membrane. **Fungistatic** generally.

| Drug | Spectrum + use | Pearls |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluconazole** | Candida (most spp.; not krusei/glabrata), Cryptococcus | CSF penetration; renally cleared; few drug interactions; long t½ |
| **Itraconazole** | Sporothrix, Blastomyces, Histoplasma, Coccidioides | Erratic absorption; capsule needs acid (avoid PPI); solution OK on empty stomach; negative inotrope (CHF black box) |
| **Voriconazole** | Aspergillus, Fusarium, Scedosporium, Candida | First-line for invasive aspergillosis; visual disturbances ("light flashes"), phototoxicity, hepatotoxicity, CYP interactions |
| **Posaconazole** | Mucormycosis prophylaxis + treatment; Aspergillus; Candida prophylaxis in neutropenic | Take with food (oral suspension) or empty stomach (delayed-release tablet) |
| **Isavuconazole** | Aspergillus, Mucor | Newest; **shortens** QT (uniquely); fewer interactions than vori |
| **Ketoconazole (oral)** | Rarely used (hepatotoxicity, adrenal suppression - off-label for Cushing) | Topical = OK (shampoo for seborrheic dermatitis, tinea) |

**Common azole side effects**:
- **Hepatotoxicity** - monitor LFT.
- **CYP3A4 inhibition** (esp. ketoconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole, posaconazole) - many drug interactions (↑ statins, warfarin, cyclosporine, methadone, etc.).
- **QT prolongation** (except isavuconazole).
- **Voriconazole** - photopsia, photosensitivity, fluorosis.
- **Itraconazole** - negative inotrope; black box for HF.
- **Posaconazole, voriconazole** - require monitoring + dose adjustment for absorption.

## Echinocandins

**Caspofungin, Micafungin, Anidulafungin** - inhibit **β-(1,3)-D-glucan synthase** → defective fungal cell wall.

**Spectrum**: Candida (all species, including azole-resistant), Aspergillus (alternative). NO activity vs Cryptococcus, mucor, dimorphics.

**Use**: invasive candidiasis first-line; salvage for invasive aspergillosis.

**Pharmacology**: IV only (large MW). Hepatic clearance. Few drug interactions.

**Side effects**:
- Well-tolerated.
- **Histamine release** (rare; slow infusion).
- Hepatotoxicity (mild).
- ↑ caspofungin levels with **cyclosporine** + ↓ with **rifampin**.

## Flucytosine (5-FC)

**Mechanism**: cytosine deaminase (only in fungi) → 5-FU → ↓ DNA + RNA synthesis. **Fungistatic**.

**Use**: **always combined** with amphotericin B for **cryptococcal meningitis** (induction phase) and severe Candida; resistance develops rapidly with monotherapy.

**Side effects**: marrow suppression (dose-related; monitor CBC), hepatotoxicity, GI.

## Allylamines

**Terbinafine** - inhibits squalene epoxidase → ↓ ergosterol + ↑ toxic squalene buildup.

**Use**: **dermatophyte infections** - tinea pedis, tinea corporis, onychomycosis (nails). PO 6 weeks fingernails, 12 weeks toenails.

**Side effects**:
- **Hepatotoxicity** - baseline LFT; rare.
- **Taste disturbance / loss** - persistent in some.
- Headache, GI.
- Rare: SJS, lupus-like, hematologic.

**Topical**: tinea corporis, tinea cruris, pedis.

## Griseofulvin

**Mechanism**: deposits in keratin, inhibits microtubules → only effective against dermatophytes.

**Use**: tinea capitis (only one with efficacy at scalp); other tineas (largely replaced by terbinafine which is more effective + better tolerated).

**Side effects**: hepatotoxicity, **disulfiram-like reaction** with alcohol, CYP induction, photosensitivity, teratogen (pregnancy category X).

## "Drug of choice" by fungal infection

| Infection | First-line |
|---|---|
| **Mucormycosis** | **Liposomal amphotericin B** + surgical debridement + control diabetes/immunosuppression; posaconazole / isavuconazole alt |
| **Invasive aspergillosis** | **Voriconazole** (or isavuconazole); echinocandin alt |
| **Cryptococcal meningitis** | Induction: amphotericin B + flucytosine × 2 weeks → consolidation fluconazole 400 mg × 8 weeks → maintenance fluconazole 200 mg × ≥1 yr |
| **Invasive candidiasis (bloodstream)** | **Echinocandin** first-line; step-down to fluconazole if susceptible |
| **Oropharyngeal candidiasis** | Nystatin swish; clotrimazole troche; fluconazole PO if extensive |
| **Esophageal candidiasis** | Fluconazole PO; echinocandin if severe |
| **Vulvovaginal candidiasis** | Fluconazole 150 mg × 1; topical azole |
| **Pneumocystis (PCP)** - technically a fungus | TMP-SMX (high dose) + steroids if PaO₂ &lt;70 or A-a gradient >35 |
| **Histoplasmosis / Blastomycosis** (severe) | Liposomal amphotericin B → step down to itraconazole 12+ months |
| **Coccidioidomycosis** | Fluconazole or itraconazole; amphotericin for severe |
| **Sporothrix** | Itraconazole 3-6 months |
| **Tinea capitis** | Terbinafine PO or griseofulvin PO |
| **Onychomycosis** | Terbinafine PO (6-12 weeks) |
| **Tinea pedis / cruris / corporis** | Topical azole or terbinafine |
| **Tinea versicolor** | Topical ketoconazole / selenium sulfide; oral fluconazole if extensive |

## Antifungal pearls

### Drug interactions

Voriconazole + carbamazepine, rifampin, phenytoin → induce voriconazole metabolism → subtherapeutic. Levels must be monitored.

Posaconazole oral suspension needs food + acidic environment; switch to delayed-release tablet for reliable PK.

Itraconazole + drugs that ↓ stomach acid (PPI, H2 blocker, antacids) → ↓ itraconazole absorption (capsule form).

### Pregnancy

- Amphotericin B - generally safe.
- Topical azoles - safe.
- Fluconazole - high doses (>150 mg) avoided 1st trimester (Tetralogy of Fallot association).
- Voriconazole - teratogen; avoid.
- Terbinafine - probably safe; limited data.

### PJP/PCP prophylaxis indications

- CD4 &lt;200 in HIV.
- Solid organ transplant.
- Long-term high-dose steroids (prednisone ≥20 mg/d for ≥4 weeks).
- Hematologic malignancy.
- Selected congenital immunodeficiencies.

**Choices**: TMP-SMX (1st), dapsone (G6PD test), atovaquone (well-tolerated), aerosolized pentamidine (least effective, lung-only).

<Clinical>
**Patient with chronic sinus infection + diabetic ketoacidosis + black eschar in nasal cavity** = **mucormycosis** (rhino-orbital). Emergency: liposomal amphotericin B + surgical debridement + tight glucose control. Posaconazole/isavuconazole as step-down.

**Cryptococcal meningitis in HIV**: amphotericin B + flucytosine × 2 weeks (induction); then fluconazole 400 mg × 8 weeks (consolidation); then 200 mg fluconazole × at least 1 year (maintenance). LP for opening pressure (high ICP common - serial therapeutic LPs critical).

**Invasive aspergillosis in HSCT patient**: voriconazole. Monitor levels (genetic CYP2C19 variation). If voriconazole fails or intolerable, switch to isavuconazole or echinocandin.

**Fluconazole-resistant Candida**: switch to echinocandin (caspofungin, micafungin, anidulafungin) or amphotericin. Recheck speciation - **C. krusei intrinsically fluconazole-resistant**; **C. glabrata** variable.

**Voriconazole therapeutic drug monitoring**: trough 1-5 mg/L. Genetics matter (CYP2C19 poor/extensive/ultra-rapid metabolizers).
</Clinical>
