## Insulins - the matrix

| Type | Drug | Onset | Peak | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rapid** | Lispro, Aspart, Glulisine | 15 min | 1 h | 3–5 h |
| **Short** | Regular | 30 min | 2–3 h | 5–8 h |
| **Intermediate** | NPH | 1–2 h | 4–8 h | 12–18 h |
| **Long** | Glargine, Detemir | 1–2 h | flat | 20–24 h |
| **Ultra-long** | Degludec | 1 h | flat | >42 h |

**Basal-bolus** (T1DM, complex T2DM): long-acting basal once daily + rapid-acting before meals (carb counting).

**Premixed (70/30, 75/25)**: NPH + regular or NPH + analog; convenient but less flexible. BID before breakfast + dinner.

**Insulin pumps**: deliver rapid insulin via continuous SC infusion + meal boluses; tighter control.

**Adverse effects**:
- **Hypoglycemia** - the main risk. Symptoms: tremor, sweating, palpitations, hunger, confusion → seizure, coma. Treat with juice/glucose (15 g) or IV D50 or glucagon IM.
- **Weight gain** (~3 kg average).
- **Lipohypertrophy** at injection sites (rotate sites).
- **Hypokalemia** (drives K intracellular - used acutely in hyperkalemia).

**Sliding-scale insulin**: reactive coverage in hospital. Often replaced by basal-bolus protocols since SSI is associated with poor control + hypoglycemia.

## Metformin (biguanide) - first-line for T2DM

**Mechanism**: ↓ hepatic gluconeogenesis (primary), ↑ insulin sensitivity, no insulin secretion → no weight gain, no hypoglycemia.

**Pros**: cheap, weight neutral or slight loss, CV benefit, low hypoglycemia risk, A1c reduction ~1–2%.

**Side effects**:
- **GI** (diarrhea, nausea, abdominal pain) - 20%, dose-related, often self-limited; start at low dose + titrate; XR formulation helps.
- **B12 deficiency** with long-term use - check annually.
- **Lactic acidosis** - rare but serious; risk in CKD (eGFR &lt;30 contraindicated; caution at 30–45), liver failure, acute illness, IV contrast.

**Hold metformin** before IV contrast if GFR borderline; before surgery; in acute illness.

## Sulfonylureas

**Glipizide, Glyburide, Glimepiride** - close ATP-sensitive K channels in pancreatic β-cell → depolarization → insulin release.

**Pros**: cheap, effective (A1c -1 to -2%).

**Cons**:
- **Hypoglycemia** - significant, esp. with glyburide (longest-acting; **avoid in elderly** per Beers).
- **Weight gain** (~2–5 kg).
- No CV benefit (UGDP signal historical).
- "Burnout" - β-cell exhaustion over time.

**Glipizide** preferred over glyburide in elderly + CKD (shorter t½, hepatic metabolism).

## Meglitinides - "short SUs"

**Repaglinide, Nateglinide** - same mechanism as SUs but shorter; dose with meals.

Use: meal-related hyperglycemia in mild T2DM; minimal hypoglycemia between meals.

## Thiazolidinediones (TZDs / glitazones)

**Pioglitazone, Rosiglitazone** - PPARγ agonist → ↑ insulin sensitivity in muscle + adipose.

**Pros**: durable A1c reduction, modest HDL ↑, possible NASH benefit, CV benefit in IRIS (pioglitazone + stroke).

**Cons**:
- **Weight gain** (~3–5 kg, fluid + fat).
- **Edema + HF exacerbation** (avoid in NYHA III-IV - black box).
- **Bone loss + fractures** (esp. postmenopausal women).
- **Bladder cancer** (pioglitazone - mild signal).
- **Rosiglitazone** - MI signal (RECORD; restrictions later lifted).

## DPP-4 inhibitors ("gliptins")

**Sitagliptin, Saxagliptin, Linagliptin, Alogliptin** - inhibit dipeptidyl peptidase-4 → ↑ endogenous GLP-1 + GIP → ↑ glucose-dependent insulin secretion + ↓ glucagon.

**Pros**: weight-neutral, low hypoglycemia, well-tolerated, daily oral.

**Cons**:
- Modest A1c reduction (~0.5–0.8%).
- **Pancreatitis** (rare class effect).
- **HF hospitalization** with saxagliptin (SAVOR-TIMI 53), alogliptin.
- Expensive.

**Linagliptin** is the only one not requiring renal dose adjustment.

## GLP-1 receptor agonists ("-tides")

**Exenatide, Liraglutide, Semaglutide, Dulaglutide, Albiglutide**

**Mechanism**: GLP-1 receptor → ↑ glucose-dependent insulin + ↓ glucagon + delayed gastric emptying + ↑ satiety (CNS appetite suppression).

**Tirzepatide** = dual GIP/GLP-1; more potent.

**Pros**: weight loss (~3–10 kg), CV benefit (LEADER liraglutide, REWIND dulaglutide, SUSTAIN-6 semaglutide), low hypoglycemia, renal protection.

**Cons**:
- **Nausea + vomiting** (start low + titrate).
- Injection (most; oral semaglutide available - take fasting).
- **Pancreatitis** (rare).
- **Thyroid C-cell tumors** in rodents (relevant to MTC - avoid in personal/family history MTC, MEN2).
- Expensive.

**Semaglutide** SC (Ozempic, Wegovy) + oral (Rybelsus). Wegovy/Zepbound (tirzepatide) FDA-approved for **obesity** without diabetes.

## SGLT2 inhibitors ("-flozins")

**Canagliflozin, Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin, Ertugliflozin** - inhibit SGLT2 in proximal tubule → glucosuria + natriuresis.

**Pros**:
- Modest A1c (-0.5–1%), weight loss, BP reduction.
- **CV benefit** (EMPA-REG, CANVAS, DECLARE).
- **Renal benefit** (DAPA-CKD, EMPA-KIDNEY, CREDENCE - slow progression of CKD, ↓ proteinuria).
- **HF benefit** in HFrEF AND HFpEF, regardless of diabetes.

**Cons**:
- **Genital mycotic infections** (5–10%).
- **UTI**.
- **Euglycemic DKA** - rare but serious; hold around surgery + acute illness.
- **Volume depletion / hypotension** (esp. with diuretics).
- **Fournier gangrene** (rare, perineal necrotizing infection).
- **Amputation signal** with canagliflozin (CANVAS - LEAP).
- **Bone fractures** with canagliflozin.

## α-glucosidase inhibitors

**Acarbose, Miglitol** - inhibit brush border α-glucosidase → delay carb absorption → blunt postprandial glucose spike.

**Pros**: weight-neutral, no hypoglycemia.

**Cons**: GI gas, bloating, diarrhea (often dose-limiting). Modest A1c -0.5%.

Rarely used.

## Amylin analog

**Pramlintide** - synthetic amylin (pancreatic β cell co-hormone); ↓ gastric emptying, ↓ glucagon, ↑ satiety.

Use: adjunct in T1DM (and T2DM on insulin) for postprandial control. Co-administer with mealtime insulin.

Side effects: hypoglycemia (when combined with insulin), nausea, vomiting.

## Bile acid sequestrant - colesevelam

FDA-approved for T2DM with modest A1c benefit. GI side effects + many drug interactions. Rarely first-choice.

## ADA stepwise approach (T2DM 2024)

1. **Lifestyle + metformin** for everyone (unless contraindicated).
2. If ASCVD or high risk: add **GLP-1 agonist with CV benefit** (liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide) regardless of A1c.
3. If HF or CKD: add **SGLT2 inhibitor**.
4. Otherwise stepwise: add per individualized priority (weight loss → GLP-1 or SGLT2; cost → SU; hypoglycemia avoidance → DPP-4, SGLT2, GLP-1).
5. Add **basal insulin** if A1c remains uncontrolled.
6. Intensify to **basal-bolus** as needed.

## DKA management (high yield)

1. **IV fluids** - NS 1L bolus then 250–500 mL/h; switch to ½ NS once volume restored.
2. **IV insulin** - 0.1 U/kg/h drip; check glucose hourly.
3. **K replacement** - critical; insulin drives K intracellular. Start KCl when K ≤5.3. Don't start insulin if K &lt;3.3.
4. **Bicarbonate** - only if pH &lt;6.9 (controversial).
5. **Transition** to SC insulin when bicarb >18, anion gap closed, patient eating; **overlap** SC + IV by 1–2 h.

## HHS (hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state) - elderly T2DM

Higher glucose (>600), higher osmolality (>320), minimal ketosis. Higher mortality than DKA. Treat similarly: aggressive fluids, insulin, K. Slower glucose correction to avoid cerebral edema.

<Clinical>
**T1DM newly diagnosed kid + DKA**: aggressive fluids (but **slow** in pediatrics - cerebral edema risk), insulin drip 0.05–0.1 U/kg/h, K replacement, monitor neuro status closely. Transition to basal-bolus (0.5–1 U/kg/day, half basal half bolus).

**Sick-day rules T1DM**: never stop insulin (DKA risk). Check ketones q4h. ↑ fluids. May need supplemental insulin.

**Hypoglycemia unawareness**: chronic hypoglycemia blunts catecholamine response. Allow glucose to run higher for 2 weeks to restore awareness.

**Steroid-induced hyperglycemia**: dexamethasone causes both fasting + postprandial spikes. Glucose monitoring; add basal + bolus insulin as needed. Resolves with steroid taper.

**Metformin + IV contrast**: hold metformin 48 h after if GFR was borderline. Restart once GFR confirmed unchanged.
</Clinical>

<HighYield variant="boards">
- Metformin first-line; contraindicated GFR &lt;30; risk of lactic acidosis with renal failure, alcohol, sepsis.
- Glyburide → hypoglycemia in elderly (Beers list). Use glipizide.
- TZD → fluid retention, HF, bone loss, bladder cancer.
- GLP-1 RA → CV benefit + weight loss + nausea. Avoid in MTC/MEN2.
- SGLT2 → HF + CKD benefit; **euglycemic DKA**; genital infections; Fournier gangrene.
- DKA: IV fluids → K → insulin (in that priority order). K must be ≥3.3 before insulin.
- Somogyi vs dawn phenomenon: Somogyi (nocturnal hypo → rebound hyperglycemia) - reduce evening NPH. Dawn (early-morning growth hormone surge) - shift NPH to bedtime.
- Insulin glargine is **24 h flat**; degludec >42 h. Best for once-daily basal.
- Pramlintide adjunct to insulin for postprandial control in T1DM.
- Patient with T2DM + CVD → GLP-1 RA or SGLT2 with proven CV benefit (regardless of A1c).
- Patient with HF (any LVEF) → SGLT2 mortality benefit.
</HighYield>
