## Acid suppression

### PPIs (proton pump inhibitors)

**Omeprazole, Pantoprazole, Esomeprazole, Lansoprazole, Rabeprazole, Dexlansoprazole**

**Mechanism**: prodrug activated in acidic environment of parietal cell canaliculus → covalent binding to H+/K+-ATPase → irreversible inhibition. New pumps must be made to recover acid secretion (24-48 h).

**Use**: GERD, peptic ulcer, H. pylori eradication, NSAID gastropathy prevention, Zollinger-Ellison syndrome, prevention of UGI bleed in critical illness.

**Side effects** (most are mild + uncertain in chronic use):
- ↓ Mg, Ca, B12 absorption.
- ↑ risk: C. diff, community-acquired pneumonia, hip fracture (debated), CKD (debated), interstitial nephritis.
- Acute interstitial nephritis (rare).
- **Drug interactions**: ↓ clopidogrel activation via CYP2C19 (esp. omeprazole) - use pantoprazole if combined.

**Best practice**: time-limited use; deprescribe when possible.

### H2 receptor antagonists

**Famotidine, Cimetidine, Nizatidine, Ranitidine (withdrawn for NDMA)**

**Mechanism**: block H2 receptors on parietal cells → ↓ acid (less complete than PPIs).

**Use**: GERD, dyspepsia (less common since PPIs).

**Side effects**:
- **Cimetidine**: anti-androgen (gynecomastia, ED, libido ↓), inhibits CYPs (drug interactions), confusion in elderly.
- **Famotidine**: clean profile; rare cytopenias.
- Tolerance with chronic use.

### Antacids

**Aluminum hydroxide, Magnesium hydroxide, Calcium carbonate, Sodium bicarbonate**

**Mechanism**: neutralize gastric acid.

**Side effects**:
- Aluminum: constipation, hypophosphatemia.
- Magnesium: diarrhea.
- Calcium: rebound acid (gastrin release), milk-alkali syndrome.
- Sodium: ↑ BP, alkalosis.
- All: chelate other drugs (tetracyclines, FQs, iron, levothyroxine - 2-4 h separation).

### Mucosal protectants

| Drug | Mechanism | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Sucralfate** | Sulfated polysaccharide + Al; binds ulcer bed in acidic environment | Stress ulcer prophylaxis (alternative to PPI for low VAP risk in ICU); local effect |
| **Misoprostol** | PGE1 analog → ↑ mucus + bicarb + ↓ acid | NSAID-induced ulcer prevention; abortion; postpartum hemorrhage |
| **Bismuth subsalicylate** | Coats ulcer + antimicrobial + ↓ inflammation | Traveler's diarrhea, H. pylori quadruple, dyspepsia |

**Misoprostol** = teratogen + abortifacient; contraindicated in pregnancy unless intended.

### H. pylori treatment regimens

**Goal**: ≥90% eradication.

| Regimen | Components | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Quadruple bismuth** (preferred where clarithromycin resistance >15%) | PPI + bismuth + tetracycline + metronidazole | 10-14 days |
| **Triple** (avoid if local clari resistance high) | PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin (or metronidazole) | 10-14 days |
| **Concomitant** | PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin + metronidazole | 10-14 days |
| **Levofloxacin** | PPI + levofloxacin + amoxicillin | 10-14 days |

Confirm eradication with stool antigen or urea breath test 4 weeks post-therapy.

## Antiemetics

| Class | Drugs | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **5-HT3 antagonists** | Ondansetron, Granisetron, Palonosetron, Dolasetron | Chemo + post-op; pregnancy hyperemesis (off-label) |
| **NK1 antagonists** | Aprepitant, Fosaprepitant, Rolapitant, Netupitant | Highly emetogenic chemo (delayed-phase) |
| **D2 antagonists** | Metoclopramide, Prochlorperazine, Promethazine | Migraine N/V, gastroparesis, chemo |
| **H1 antagonists** | Diphenhydramine, Meclizine, Cyclizine | Motion sickness, vestibular |
| **Anticholinergic** | Scopolamine (transdermal) | Motion sickness |
| **Cannabinoids** | Dronabinol, Nabilone | Refractory chemo N/V |
| **Atypical antipsychotic** | Olanzapine | Breakthrough chemo emesis |
| **Steroid** | Dexamethasone | Chemo emesis (with 5-HT3 + NK1) |
| **GABA-A** | Lorazepam | Anticipatory nausea before chemo |

### Ondansetron

**Mechanism**: 5-HT3 receptor antagonist on vagal afferents + CTZ.

**Use**: chemo, post-op, hyperemesis, gastroenteritis.

**Side effects**: **QT prolongation** (especially high IV doses), constipation, headache, serotonin syndrome (with other serotonergic).

### Aprepitant / NK1 antagonists

Block substance P → blocks delayed emesis (24-72 h post-chemo). Combined with 5-HT3 + dex for highly emetogenic chemo.

CYP3A4 substrate + inhibitor + inducer → many drug interactions (warfarin, OCs, dex).

### Metoclopramide

**Mechanism**: central D2 antagonist + 5-HT4 agonist + 5-HT3 antagonist. Prokinetic (UGI).

**Use**: diabetic gastroparesis, refractory GERD, chemo N/V, post-op ileus (controversial).

**Side effects**:
- **EPS** (acute dystonia, tardive dyskinesia with chronic use - black box).
- Drowsiness, fatigue, hyperprolactinemia.
- **Limit duration to 12 weeks**.
- Avoid in Parkinson disease, severe depression.

### Promethazine, Prochlorperazine

Phenothiazines with D2 + H1 + M antagonism.

**Side effects**: EPS, sedation, anticholinergic, **promethazine IV** → tissue necrosis if extravasated.

## Prokinetics

- **Metoclopramide** (above).
- **Erythromycin** - motilin agonist; **diabetic gastroparesis**, post-op ileus rescue. Tachyphylaxis. Brief courses.
- **Domperidone** - peripheral D2 antagonist; gastroparesis (not FDA-approved US; available elsewhere).
- **Prucalopride** - 5-HT4 agonist; **chronic constipation**.

## Laxatives + bowel preps

| Class | Drug | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Bulk-forming** | Psyllium, methylcellulose | ↑ fiber, ↑ stool volume |
| **Stool softener** | Docusate (Colace) | Surfactant; mostly placebo - not very effective in trials |
| **Osmotic** | Polyethylene glycol (Miralax), lactulose, sorbitol, milk of magnesia | Draw water into lumen |
| **Stimulant** | Bisacodyl, senna, castor oil | ↑ peristalsis |
| **Saline** | Magnesium citrate | Osmotic + mild stimulant |
| **Lubricant** | Mineral oil | Coats; aspiration pneumonia risk |
| **Enema** | Tap water, soap suds | Distal evacuation |
| **Chloride channel activator** | Lubiprostone | ↑ Cl secretion → ↑ water; chronic constipation, IBS-C, opioid-induced |
| **Guanylate cyclase-C agonist** | Linaclotide, Plecanatide | ↑ cGMP → secretion; IBS-C, chronic constipation |
| **NHE3 inhibitor** | Tenapanor | ↑ Na excretion to gut; IBS-C |
| **Peripheral opioid antagonist** | Methylnaltrexone, Naloxegol, Alvimopan | Opioid-induced constipation; alvimopan for postop ileus |

## Antidiarrheals

| Drug | Mechanism | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Loperamide** | μ-opioid agonist (peripheral; doesn't cross BBB via P-gp) | Acute + chronic diarrhea; not in dysentery (toxic megacolon) |
| **Diphenoxylate / Atropine (Lomotil)** | μ-opioid + antimuscarinic (atropine to discourage abuse) | Acute diarrhea |
| **Bismuth subsalicylate** | Antisecretory + antimicrobial | Traveler's diarrhea, H. pylori adjunct |
| **Octreotide** | Somatostatin analog | Carcinoid syndrome, VIPoma, refractory secretory diarrhea, variceal bleed |
| **Eluxadoline** | Mu-agonist + δ-antagonist + κ-agonist | IBS-D; pancreatitis risk (avoid w/o GB) |
| **Rifaximin** | Non-absorbed rifamycin | IBS-D, hepatic encephalopathy, traveler's diarrhea |
| **Cholestyramine** | Bile acid sequestrant | Bile acid diarrhea (post-cholecystectomy, ileal disease) |
| **Crofelemer** | Cl channel modulator | HIV/ART-related diarrhea |

## IBD-specific drugs

### 5-ASA (mesalamine derivatives)

**Mesalamine, Sulfasalazine, Olsalazine, Balsalazide**

**Mechanism**: ↓ leukotrienes + prostaglandins; topical anti-inflammatory in colon.

**Use**: mild-moderate UC (mainstay), Crohn's (limited evidence vs UC).

**Delivery formulations** (where it releases):
- **pH-dependent** (terminal ileum + colon).
- **Time-released**.
- **Bacterial cleavage** (sulfasalazine - colon).
- **Rectal** (suppositories, foam, enema) for distal disease.

**Side effects**:
- Sulfasalazine: sulfa allergy, hemolysis G6PD, male infertility (reversible), folate antagonist.
- General: rare interstitial nephritis, pancreatitis, hepatitis.

### Glucocorticoids

**Budesonide** (high first-pass; ileum/right colon-targeted), prednisone (systemic), IV methylprednisolone (severe). Always **time-limited**.

## Bile + biliary

| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| **Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA)** | Primary biliary cholangitis (improves LFT, slows progression); cholesterol gallstones (slow); bile reflux |
| **Obeticholic acid** | FXR agonist; PBC second-line |
| **Cholestyramine, Colesevelam** | Pruritus from cholestasis; bile acid malabsorption diarrhea; ↓ digoxin/warfarin/levothyroxine absorption (4-h separation) |

## Pancreatic + hepatic

| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| **Pancrelipase / Creon** | Pancreatic insufficiency (CF, chronic pancreatitis); take with meals |
| **Lactulose** | Hepatic encephalopathy (acidifies colon, traps NH4+); osmotic laxative |
| **Rifaximin** | Add to lactulose for hepatic encephalopathy maintenance |
| **L-ornithine L-aspartate** | Adjunct for HE (improves ammonia metabolism) |
| **N-acetylcysteine** | Acetaminophen overdose (replenishes glutathione); also CIN prophylaxis (debated) |
| **Pirfenidone, Nintedanib** | Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (not GI but related antifibrotic) |

## Portal hypertension + variceal bleeding

| Drug | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Non-selective β-blocker** (propranolol, nadolol, carvedilol) | Primary + secondary prevention of variceal bleed; ↓ portal pressure |
| **Octreotide / Somatostatin** | Acute variceal bleed - splanchnic vasoconstriction |
| **Terlipressin** | Vasopressin analog; hepatorenal syndrome, variceal bleed (now in US 2022) |
| **Vasopressin** | Older variceal bleed treatment; less used (ischemia) |

## Hepatorenal syndrome

**Terlipressin + albumin** - new US approval (2022). Or octreotide + midodrine + albumin.

## SIBO + IBS

| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| **Rifaximin** | SIBO + IBS-D (alters gut flora) |
| **Antispasmodics** (dicyclomine, hyoscyamine) | IBS abdominal cramps |
| **TCAs / SSRIs** | IBS adjunct (visceral hyperalgesia) |
| **Linaclotide, Plecanatide, Lubiprostone, Tenapanor** | IBS-C |
| **Eluxadoline** | IBS-D (no gallbladder, pancreatitis caveats) |

| Drug | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **GLP-1 agonists** (semaglutide, liraglutide) | ↑ satiety, ↓ gastric emptying | **Wegovy** (semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly) - 15% weight loss |

<Clinical>
**Severe nausea/vomiting hospitalized**: ondansetron 4-8 mg IV + dexamethasone 8-12 mg IV. Add aprepitant if anticipated prolonged + highly emetogenic stimulus.

**Diabetic gastroparesis**: metoclopramide 5-10 mg before meals (limit 12 weeks). Erythromycin 50-100 mg before meals (tachyphylaxis). Dietary - small frequent low-fat low-fiber meals.

**Opioid-induced constipation**: scheduled stimulant laxative + osmotic. If refractory → peripheral mu antagonist (methylnaltrexone SC, naloxegol or naldemedine PO).

**Hepatic encephalopathy maintenance**: lactulose titrated to 2-3 soft BMs/day + rifaximin 550 mg BID. Check ammonia not necessary if clinical response.

**SBP prophylaxis (ascites + cirrhosis)**: TMP-SMX or norfloxacin/ciprofloxacin. After SBP, lifelong prophylaxis.

**Acute UGI bleed**: PPI IV bolus + drip (esp. ulcer); octreotide if variceal suspected; transfuse to Hb 7 (8 in cardiac disease); urgent endoscopy.

**Chronic UC mild-moderate**: oral mesalamine ± topical (suppository or enema for distal disease). Step up to biologic if not at target.
</Clinical>
