## Approach to the poisoned patient

1. **ABCs** + IV access + cardiac monitoring + glucose + naloxone trial if depressed.
2. **History** - what, how much, when, why, intentional vs accidental.
3. **Tox screen** - urine immunoassay limited; serum levels for: acetaminophen, salicylate, ethanol, methanol, ethylene glycol, lithium, digoxin, phenytoin, valproate, carbamazepine.
4. **Decontamination** - rarely useful; specific situations only.
5. **Antidote** - if exists.
6. **Enhanced elimination** - hemodialysis for "I STUMBLED" - Isopropyl alcohol, Salicylate, Theophylline, Uremia, Methanol, Barbiturates (long-acting), Lithium, Ethylene glycol, Depakote (valproate).
7. **Supportive care** - the foundation.

## Toxidromes

| Syndrome | Pupils | Mental | HR/BP | Skin | Bowel | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Opioid** | Pinpoint | Coma | ↓ HR/BP, ↓ RR | Normal | ↓ | Heroin, fentanyl, oxycodone |
| **Sedative-hypnotic** | Variable | Coma | ↓ BP, ↓ RR | Normal | Normal | BZD, barbs, alcohol |
| **Cholinergic (SLUDGE)** | Miosis | Variable | ↓ HR, ↓ BP | **Sweaty** | ↑ (diarrhea), urination, lacrimation, salivation | Organophosphates, carbamates, nerve agents |
| **Anticholinergic** | **Mydriasis** | Agitation, hallucinations | ↑ HR, ↑ BP, ↑ T | **Hot, red, dry** | ↓ | TCAs, antihistamines, scopolamine, jimsonweed |
| **Sympathomimetic** | Mydriasis | Agitation | ↑ HR, ↑ BP, ↑ T | **Sweaty** | Normal/↑ | Cocaine, amphetamine, ecstasy, PCP |
| **Hallucinogenic** | Mydriasis | Hallucinations | ↑ HR | Normal | Normal | LSD, mushrooms, MDMA |
| **Serotonin syndrome** | Mydriasis | Agitation, confusion | ↑ HR, ↑ BP, ↑ T | Sweaty | **Diarrhea**, hyperactive | SSRI + serotonergic |
| **NMS** | Normal | Encephalopathy | ↑ HR, ↑ BP, ↑ T | Sweaty | Normal | D2 antagonist |
| **Withdrawal (BZD/alcohol)** | Normal/dilated | Agitation, seizure | ↑ HR, ↑ BP | Sweaty | Normal | Stop alcohol/BZD |

**Cholinergic** vs **anticholinergic** = "sweat" distinguishes. Anticholinergic = **dry**. Cholinergic = **wet**.

## Decontamination

| Method | When useful |
|---|---|
| **Activated charcoal** | Within 1 h of ingestion of most drugs; not for: alcohols, metals, hydrocarbons, caustics, iron, lithium |
| **Whole-bowel irrigation** (polyethylene glycol) | Iron, lithium, body packers, large pill bezoar |
| **Gastric lavage** | Rarely indicated; recent (&lt;1 h) life-threatening ingestion with no antidote |
| **Skin / eye decon** | Organophosphates, caustics |
| **Hemodialysis** | "I STUMBLED" - drugs with low Vd, low protein binding, low MW |

**Syrup of ipecac**: no longer recommended.

## Major antidotes

### Acetaminophen → **N-acetylcysteine**

Mechanism: replenishes glutathione → conjugates NAPQI. Most effective within 8-10 h. Treat per Rumack-Matthew nomogram (acute, 4-h level) or empirically if late, staggered, sustained-release, or unknown timing.

Protocols: 21-h IV (loading + 2 maintenance bags) or 72-h PO.

### Opioid → **Naloxone**

Reversal of respiratory depression. 0.4-2 mg IV/IM/IN; repeat q2-3 min. Short t½ (~30 min) → may need repeated doses for long-acting opioids (methadone, fentanyl patches). Withdrawal precipitated in chronic users.

### Benzodiazepine → **Flumazenil**

Competitive BZD antagonist. **Avoid routine use** - can precipitate seizures in chronic BZD users + with mixed overdose (TCA seizure unmasked). Reserved for iatrogenic procedural over-sedation.

### Anticholinergic → **Physostigmine**

AChE inhibitor that crosses BBB. Reverses central + peripheral anticholinergic toxicity. **Avoid in TCA overdose with widened QRS** - can cause asystole + seizures. Safer for plant ingestion (jimsonweed), antihistamine, scopolamine.

### Cholinergic (organophosphate) → **Atropine + Pralidoxime (2-PAM)**

Atropine: blocks muscarinic effects (titrate to drying secretions; may need very high doses).

Pralidoxime: regenerates AChE before "aging." Most effective within 24-48 h. Treats nicotinic (NMJ) symptoms that atropine doesn't.

### TCA overdose → **Sodium bicarbonate**

QRS >100 ms or arrhythmia: NaHCO3 1-2 mEq/kg IV bolus. Alkalinization + Na load overcomes Na-channel block. Target pH 7.45-7.55.

Avoid physostigmine (asystole, seizures).

### Beta-blocker overdose → **Glucagon**

Glucagon binds its own Gs receptor → ↑ cAMP → bypasses β-receptor block. Bolus + drip. Adjuncts: high-dose insulin/euglycemia ("HIE"), Ca, lipid emulsion (for lipophilic propranolol), pacing, ECMO.

### CCB overdose → **Calcium chloride/gluconate + HIE + glucagon**

Similar approach to β-blocker; **calcium** central. HIE (high-dose insulin) particularly helpful. Methylene blue or lipid emulsion in refractory.

### Digoxin → **Digoxin immune Fab**

For life-threatening arrhythmias, hyperK in acute OD, dig level >10, ingested dose >10 mg adult / >4 mg child. Calculate dose by serum level or empiric 10-20 vials acute, 6 vials chronic.

### Iron → **Deferoxamine**

Chelates iron → urinary excretion (urine turns "vin rosé" / pink). Indications: serum iron >500, symptomatic, severe overdose. **Deferasirox, Deferiprone** are oral chelators for chronic iron overload (thalassemia).

### Lead → **Succimer (DMSA), CaEDTA, Dimercaprol (BAL)**

- **Succimer** PO - asymptomatic kids with level 45-69.
- **EDTA + BAL** combination - encephalopathy.
- **BAL** - alone in severe.
- **Penicillamine** - alternative oral chelator (also for Wilson disease).

### Mercury, Arsenic → **Dimercaprol (BAL), Succimer**

### Copper (Wilson disease) → **Penicillamine, Trientine, Zinc**

### Methanol, Ethylene glycol → **Fomepizole + sodium bicarbonate + hemodialysis**

Fomepizole = alcohol dehydrogenase inhibitor → prevents metabolism to toxic metabolites (formic acid, glycolic + oxalic acid). Hemodialysis if severe acidosis, end-organ damage, or level >50.

Ethanol IV is alternative if fomepizole unavailable (competes for ADH).

### Salicylate → **Sodium bicarbonate + hemodialysis**

Alkalinize urine (pH >7.5) → ion-trap salicylate in urine → ↑ excretion. Hemodialysis if level >100, severe acidosis, AMS, pulmonary edema, renal failure.

### Cyanide → **Hydroxocobalamin (preferred) or Sodium thiosulfate + nitrite**

- Hydroxocobalamin (Cyanokit) binds CN directly → cyanocobalamin (excreted in urine). Pink/red skin discoloration normal.
- Older "cyanide kit": amyl nitrite inhaled → sodium nitrite IV (creates methemoglobin to bind CN) → sodium thiosulfate (sulfur donor to detoxify CN to thiocyanate). Avoid in smoke inhalation (oximetry already low).

### Carbon monoxide → **100% O₂ ± hyperbaric**

Reduces CO half-life. Hyperbaric for: loss of consciousness, neurologic deficit, pregnancy with COHb >15%, COHb >25% any patient, persistent symptoms despite normobaric O₂.

### Methemoglobinemia → **Methylene blue**

Reduces Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺ via NADPH-dependent reductase. **Avoid in G6PD** deficiency (hemolysis). Causes: benzocaine, prilocaine, dapsone, nitrites, sulfonamides.

### NMS → **Dantrolene + bromocriptine + cooling**

### Malignant hyperthermia → **Dantrolene + cooling**

### Serotonin syndrome → **Cyproheptadine + BZD + cooling**

### Local anesthetic toxicity (LAST) → **20% Intralipid**

Lipid "sink" for lipophilic drug. Bolus 1.5 mL/kg + drip 0.25 mL/kg/min.

### Sulfonylurea hypoglycemia → **Octreotide + dextrose**

Octreotide suppresses insulin release. Dextrose alone has rebound risk.

### Heparin → **Protamine**

1 mg per 100 U heparin. Partial for LMWH.

### Warfarin → **Vitamin K + 4F-PCC**

PCC (4-factor) for major bleeding; FFP if PCC unavailable.

### DOAC reversal

- **Dabigatran** → idarucizumab (Praxbind), hemodialysis.
- **Apixaban, Rivaroxaban, Edoxaban** → andexanet alfa or 4F-PCC.

### Snake venom → **Crotalidae polyvalent immune Fab (CroFab)**

Specific to pit vipers (rattlesnake, copperhead, water moccasin). For coral snake (rare) → coral snake antivenom (limited supply).

### Black widow → **Antivenin**, calcium gluconate for cramping, BZD.

### Brown recluse → supportive (no specific antivenin proven).

## Environmental poisonings

### Mushroom poisoning

| Type | Onset | Toxin | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Amanita phalloides** | 6-24 h | Amatoxin (RNA pol II) → fulminant liver failure | Silibinin, NAC, supportive, liver transplant |
| **Inocybe / Clitocybe** | minutes | Muscarine | Atropine |
| **Amanita muscaria** | minutes | Ibotenic acid | Supportive |
| **Psilocybin** | minutes | 5-HT receptor | Supportive |
| **Gyromitra** | hours | Gyromitrin → MMH | Pyridoxine |

### Plant ingestions

- **Datura/Jimsonweed**: anticholinergic syndrome → physostigmine.
- **Oleander / Foxglove / Lily of valley**: cardiac glycoside → dig Fab.
- **Yew (Taxus)**: cardiotoxic alkaloids → Na bicarb for QRS widening, supportive.
- **Cyanogenic plants** (apricot pits, cassava): CN poisoning.

### Marine

- **Tetrodotoxin (puffer fish)**: Na-channel block → paralysis. Supportive.
- **Ciguatera (reef fish)**: hot/cold reversal. Supportive.
- **Scombroid (spoiled fish)**: histamine release. Antihistamines.

### Carbon monoxide

Sources: fire, faulty heating, generators in garages. Hgb O₂-affinity ↑ + ↓ delivery. **Cherry-red lips** late finding. Pulse ox falsely normal (can't distinguish COHb from O₂Hb). Co-oximetry needed.

### Cyanide

Sources: house fires (synthetic combustion), industrial, suicide (potassium cyanide), bitter almonds. Inhibits cytochrome oxidase → "histotoxic hypoxia." Lactic acidosis + bright red retinal veins + bitter almond breath (only 50% can smell).

## Specific drug overdoses

### Acetaminophen

- **Toxic dose**: >150 mg/kg or >7.5 g (adults).
- **Phases**: 1 (asx + GI), 2 (RUQ pain + ↑ LFT), 3 (hepatic failure), 4 (recovery or transplant).
- **NAC**: full course based on nomogram (4-h level) or empiric.

### Salicylate

- **Acute toxic** >150 mg/kg; chronic at lower levels.
- Acid-base: early respiratory alkalosis (medulla stim) → anion-gap metabolic acidosis.
- **Mixed disorder** + tinnitus + hyperventilation = key clue.
- Treatment: GI decon (activated charcoal × 1-2 doses), IV NaHCO3 (alkalinize urine + blood + brain), hemodialysis severe.

### Alcohols (toxic alcohols)

| Substance | Source | Metabolite | Findings | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Methanol** | Windshield wash, moonshine | Formic acid | Visual loss (snowfield), AGMA, osmolar gap | Fomepizole + bicarb + dialysis + folate |
| **Ethylene glycol** | Antifreeze | Glycolic + oxalic acid | AGMA, osmolar gap, calcium oxalate crystals in urine, AKI | Fomepizole + bicarb + dialysis + thiamine + pyridoxine |
| **Isopropyl** | Rubbing alcohol | Acetone | Ketosis WITHOUT acidosis | Supportive; hemodialysis if severe |

**Osmolar gap** before ADH metabolizes → as metabolism proceeds, gap ↓ and AGMA develops.

### Lithium

- **Acute**: GI symptoms predominate, mild neuro.
- **Chronic**: severe neuro (tremor, ataxia, confusion, seizure).
- Treatment: IV NS, hemodialysis if level >4 acute, >2.5 chronic, or severe symptoms.

### Iron

Phases: GI hemorrhage → "quiet phase" → shock + acidosis → hepatic + cardiac toxicity. **Deferoxamine** for severe. Avoid charcoal (doesn't bind iron).

### Theophylline

- Severe arrhythmia + seizures.
- Activated charcoal (multi-dose).
- β-blocker for arrhythmia (paradoxical but works).
- BZD for seizures.
- Hemodialysis.

### Cocaine

- Sympathomimetic + Na-channel block.
- **β-blocker contraindicated** (unopposed α → worsening MI, HTN).
- **BZD** (lorazepam) - calms agitation + secondarily lowers BP/HR.
- **Nitroglycerin, phentolamine, CCB** for HTN/chest pain.
- Aspirin for MI.

### Methamphetamine

Similar to cocaine. Supportive + BZD + treat hyperthermia aggressively.

### MDMA

Hyperthermia + hyponatremia (combined excess water intake + SIADH). Cooling + careful fluids.

### LSD, hallucinogens

Supportive. BZD for severe anxiety. Avoid antipsychotics (lower seizure threshold).

### Opioids

Naloxone (above). Watch for re-sedation with long-acting opioids. Withdrawal management.

### Bath salts (cathinones), spice (synthetic cannabinoid)

Variable presentations: psychosis, agitation, hyperthermia. Supportive + BZD.

## Pediatric toxicology pearls

- **"One pill killers"** (1-2 doses can kill toddler): TCAs, calcium channel blockers, β-blockers, opioids (esp. methadone, fentanyl patches), oral hypoglycemics (sulfonylureas), camphor, lindane, methyl salicylate (Bengay), iron, naphthalene mothballs.
- **Always check** acetaminophen + salicylate levels in intentional ingestions.

## Pregnancy + overdose

Most antidotes safe. Special considerations:
- Cyanide kit: avoid amyl/sodium nitrite (fetal methemoglobinemia) - use hydroxocobalamin.
- NAC safe in pregnancy.

<Clinical>
**Unknown overdose ED workup**: ABCs + glucose + naloxone + thiamine + dextrose. Labs: CMP, CBC, troponin, lactate, blood gas, **acetaminophen + salicylate** levels minimum, ethanol level, drug screen (interpret cautiously). EKG (TCA, Na-channel block).

**Mixed sedative overdose - acetaminophen + alcohol + BZD + opioid**: airway protection, naloxone IV, NAC for acetaminophen, avoid flumazenil (mixed overdose seizure risk), supportive ICU.

**Pediatric exploratory ingestion**: usually 1-2 doses. Check container, calculate, look up "one pill killers." Activated charcoal if airway protected + within 1 h + indicated.

**Suspected intoxication with no obvious cause but anion-gap acidosis**: methanol or ethylene glycol high on differential. Get osmolar gap + lactate. Fomepizole empirically + dialyze if severe.

**Suspected serotonin syndrome from polypharmacy**: stop all serotonergics. Cyproheptadine 12 mg load. IV fluids + BZD + cooling + ICU.
</Clinical>
