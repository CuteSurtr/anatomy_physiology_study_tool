JNC 8 / ACC-AHA first-line classes for essential HTN: **thiazide diuretic, ACE-i or ARB, dihydropyridine CCB**. β-blockers reserved for compelling indications (CAD, HF, A-fib).

## ACE inhibitors

**Mechanism**: block angiotensin-converting enzyme → ↓ AT II → ↓ vasoconstriction + ↓ aldosterone + ↓ ADH; also ↑ bradykinin (vasodilation + cough + angioedema).

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Lisinopril** | Most prescribed; no prodrug; 24-h dosing |
| **Enalapril** | Prodrug (active = enalaprilat); IV form available |
| **Captopril** | First in class; SH group → more taste disturbance, neutropenia; short half-life (BID/TID) |
| **Ramipril, perindopril** | Long-acting |

**Indications**: HTN, HFrEF (mortality benefit), post-MI, diabetic nephropathy (proteinuria), CKD with proteinuria.

**Side effects**:
- **Dry cough** (~10–20% - bradykinin-mediated, switch to ARB).
- **Angioedema** (1%, more common in Black patients - bradykinin; can be life-threatening; **icatibant** or fresh frozen plasma can rescue).
- **Hyperkalemia** (↓ aldosterone).
- **Acute kidney injury** in **bilateral renal artery stenosis** or single functioning kidney with RAS (efferent constriction is the only thing maintaining GFR).
- **Teratogenic** (2nd/3rd trimester) - renal dysgenesis, oligohydramnios. Avoid in pregnancy.

## Angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs)

**Mechanism**: block AT1 receptor selectively. Same efficacy as ACE-i without bradykinin → no cough, less (but not zero) angioedema.

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Losartan** | First in class; uricosuric (helps gout) |
| **Valsartan** | HF-approved |
| **Olmesartan** | Avoid - sprue-like enteropathy rare association |
| **Telmisartan** | Long half-life; PPAR-γ activity (metabolic benefit) |

Same indications + contraindications as ACE-i. Don't combine ACE-i + ARB (no added benefit; ↑ hyperK + AKI).

## Calcium channel blockers (CCBs)

Two clinically distinct families:

### Dihydropyridines (vascular-selective; -dipine suffix)
**Amlodipine, Nifedipine, Felodipine, Nicardipine, Clevidipine**
- ↓ SVR by relaxing arteriolar smooth muscle.
- Minimal effect on cardiac contractility/conduction.
- **Side effects**: peripheral edema (precapillary dilation), flushing, headache, reflex tachycardia.
- Amlodipine = workhorse for HTN. Nicardipine + clevidipine = IV for hypertensive emergencies.
- **Nifedipine immediate-release** in HTN: AVOID - rapid swings → MI.

### Non-dihydropyridines (cardiac-selective)
**Verapamil, Diltiazem**
- ↓ HR, ↓ AV conduction, modest vasodilation.
- Uses: rate control in A-fib, SVT, angina.
- **Constipation** (verapamil > diltiazem) - α-substantial.
- **Avoid combining with β-blocker** - additive heart block + bradycardia.
- **AVOID in HFrEF** - negative inotropic effect worsens HF.

## Thiazide diuretics

**HCTZ, Chlorthalidone, Indapamide, Metolazone**

**Mechanism**: block Na⁺/Cl⁻ cotransporter (NCC) in distal convoluted tubule.

**Side effects**: ↑ Ca, ↑ glucose, ↑ uric acid, ↑ lipids, ↓ Na, ↓ K, ↓ Mg, sulfa allergy cross-reactivity.

**Chlorthalidone > HCTZ** for cardiovascular outcomes (longer half-life, more potent). Switch matters.

**Avoid in**: hypokalemia, hyponatremia, gout, severe renal failure (use loop instead when GFR &lt;30).

## Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists

**Spironolactone, Eplerenone**

**Mechanism**: aldosterone receptor block in collecting duct → ↑ Na excretion + ↑ K retention.

**Indications**: resistant HTN, primary hyperaldosteronism (Conn), HFrEF (mortality benefit), liver cirrhosis + ascites.

**Side effects**: hyperK (esp. with ACE-i/ARB), gynecomastia + sexual dysfunction (spiro - androgen receptor block; eplerenone selective for MR → fewer endocrine effects).

## β-blockers (covered in detail in [adrenergic antagonists](/pharmacology/adrenergic-antagonists))

Indications when HTN coexists with: HFrEF, post-MI, A-fib rate control, migraine, essential tremor, pheochromocytoma (after α). Not first line for uncomplicated HTN.

## α-blockers

**Prazosin, doxazosin, terazosin, tamsulosin**. Used for HTN with concomitant BPH; not first-line. First-dose orthostatic hypotension. Doxazosin in ALLHAT trial showed inferiority for HF.

## Central α2-agonists

**Clonidine, α-methyldopa, guanfacine, dexmedetomidine**

**Mechanism**: presynaptic α2 in brainstem → ↓ sympathetic outflow.

| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| **Clonidine** | Resistant HTN, ADHD, opioid + alcohol withdrawal symptoms |
| **α-methyldopa** | **HTN in pregnancy** - safe with proven track record |
| **Guanfacine** | ADHD; less sedating than clonidine |
| **Dexmedetomidine** | ICU sedation; anesthetic adjunct |

**Side effects**: sedation, dry mouth, **rebound HTN** if discontinued abruptly. Coombs-positive hemolytic anemia + drug-induced lupus with methyldopa.

## Direct vasodilators

| Drug | Mechanism | Use | Toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hydralazine** | Arteriolar; ↑ cGMP | Severe HTN; pregnancy; **HFrEF with isosorbide dinitrate** (esp. Black patients - V-HEFT, A-HEFT) | Reflex tachy, lupus-like (slow acetylators) |
| **Minoxidil** | K-channel opener → arteriolar | Resistant HTN; topical for alopecia (Rogaine) | Hypertrichosis, pericardial effusion |

## Hypertensive emergency (BP >180/120 with end-organ damage)

End-organ damage = encephalopathy, intracerebral hemorrhage, eclampsia, MI, aortic dissection, AKI, pulmonary edema. Treat with IV drugs. Goal: ↓ MAP by ~25% in first hour, then to ~160/100 over 6 h.

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Sodium nitroprusside** | Arterial + venous dilator; instant action; cyanide toxicity with prolonged use |
| **Nitroglycerin** | Venous > arterial; for HTN + cardiac ischemia |
| **Labetalol** | Combined α + β IV bolus or drip; good in dissection, pregnancy, intracranial bleed |
| **Esmolol** | Short β1-selective IV; dissection (lowers shear with HR) |
| **Nicardipine** | IV DHP-CCB; titratable; good in stroke, pregnancy |
| **Clevidipine** | Lipid-emulsion DHP-CCB; ultra-short, very titratable |
| **Hydralazine** | IV bolus; pregnancy + eclampsia |
| **Fenoldopam** | D1 agonist; preserves renal perfusion in AKI |
| **Phentolamine** | α-block for catecholamine excess (pheo, MAOI crisis, cocaine) |

**Aortic dissection**: lower BP AND heart rate (↓ shear stress). Use **esmolol or labetalol FIRST** to block reflex tachycardia, then add nitroprusside if needed.

**Eclampsia**: labetalol IV or hydralazine IV + **magnesium sulfate** (prevents seizures - the real lifesaver).

## Compelling indications - which class for which comorbidity

| Comorbidity | Preferred classes |
|---|---|
| Diabetes / CKD / proteinuria | ACE-i or ARB |
| HFrEF | ACE-i/ARB + β-blocker + spironolactone + diuretic |
| Post-MI | β-blocker + ACE-i |
| A-fib (rate control) | β-blocker or diltiazem/verapamil |
| Migraine | β-blocker (esp. propranolol) |
| BPH | α1-blocker |
| Pregnancy | Labetalol, methyldopa, nifedipine, hydralazine |
| Hyperthyroid | β-blocker (propranolol) |
| Black patients (no CKD/HF) | Thiazide or CCB; ACE-i less effective monotherapy |
| Older adults | Thiazide or CCB |
| Pheochromocytoma | α-block FIRST, then β |

"**A**llhat **B**ehind **C**hlorthalidone **D**iuretic" - the ALLHAT trial established thiazide as a first-line equal to ACE-i and CCB.

**HTN drug categories causing edema**: dihydropyridine CCBs (amlodipine), minoxidil. Loop or amiloride doesn't help much - just lower dose or switch.

**Cough specific to ACE-i** (not ARB) - bradykinin. Swap to ARB.
</Mnemonic>

<Clinical>
**Resistant HTN** (≥3 drugs including a diuretic + not at goal): check adherence, salt intake, OSA, primary aldosteronism, renal artery stenosis, NSAIDs, cocaine. Add **spironolactone** as 4th drug (PATHWAY-2 trial - superior to bisoprolol, doxazosin).

**HTN in pregnancy** - **Methyldopa, labetalol, nifedipine, hydralazine**. AVOID ACE-i, ARB, atenolol, nitroprusside (CN toxicity to fetus).

**A patient on lisinopril develops swollen lips at 3 AM** - angioedema. STOP ACE-i forever. ARB **may** be tried after delay (rare cross-reactivity).
</Clinical>

- First-line HTN classes: thiazide, ACE-i/ARB, CCB. β-blocker only with compelling indication.
- Compelling indication for ACE-i = diabetic nephropathy, HFrEF, post-MI.
- "Pregnancy + HTN" = methyldopa, labetalol, nifedipine, hydralazine.
- "HTN + BPH" = α1-blocker (doxazosin, terazosin, tamsulosin).
- "HTN emergency in dissection" = β-blocker FIRST (esmolol, labetalol), then add nitroprusside.
- "HTN + asthma" = avoid non-selective β (propranolol); cardio-selective (metoprolol) acceptable.
- Hydralazine + isosorbide dinitrate = HFrEF in Black patients (BiDil); also pregnancy.
- Verapamil + β-blocker = heart block / asystole risk; avoid combination.
- ACE-i in **bilateral RAS** → AKI (efferent constriction lost).
- Clonidine withdrawal → rebound HTN (taper or substitute).
- Spironolactone gynecomastia → switch to eplerenone (selective MR).
