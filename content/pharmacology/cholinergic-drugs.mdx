Cholinergic drugs activate the parasympathetic nervous system and the sweat-gland sympathetics that also use ACh. They split by **directly activating the receptor** (cholinomimetics) or **blocking the enzyme that destroys ACh** (AChE inhibitors).

## Receptor types

| Receptor | Type | Location | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M1** | Gq → IP3/DAG | CNS, enteric ganglia | Memory, gastric acid |
| **M2** | Gi → ↓ cAMP | Heart (SA, AV node) | ↓ HR, ↓ AV conduction |
| **M3** | Gq → IP3/DAG | Smooth muscle, glands, eye | Contraction, secretion, miosis + accommodation |
| **N (nicotinic)** | Ligand-gated cation channel | NMJ (Nm), autonomic ganglia (Nn), adrenal medulla, CNS | Depolarization |

Parasympathetic = M1/M2/M3. Nicotinic = NMJ + ganglia + adrenal medulla.

## Direct-acting cholinomimetics

| Drug | Receptors | Use | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bethanechol** | M3 (resistant to AChE) | Postoperative ileus, urinary retention (no obstruction) | "Bethany, call me to **bowel** + **bladder**" |
| **Pilocarpine** | M3 (uncharged - crosses ocular epithelium) | Open-angle + acute angle-closure glaucoma; xerostomia (Sjögren) | Contracts ciliary muscle → opens trabecular meshwork |
| **Carbachol** | M + N | Glaucoma (intraocular) | Resistant to AChE |
| **Cevimeline** | M3 | Sjögren xerostomia | Newer, more selective |
| **Methacholine** | M | Bronchial challenge test for asthma | Provokes bronchospasm in hyperreactive airways |
| **Nicotine** | Nicotinic | Smoking cessation (patch, gum, lozenge) | NRT - the safer way to get nicotine without combustion products |
| **Varenicline** | α4β2 nicotinic partial agonist | Smoking cessation | Better than NRT in trials; black box for neuropsych |

## Indirect-acting - acetylcholinesterase inhibitors

These prevent ACh breakdown → accumulation at every cholinergic synapse. Effect = parasympathetic excess + neuromuscular activation.

### Reversible AChE inhibitors

| Drug | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Neostigmine** | Postoperative ileus, urinary retention, **myasthenia gravis** (long-term), **NM blockade reversal** (paired with glycopyrrolate to block muscarinic effects) | Quaternary amine - no CNS |
| **Pyridostigmine** | Myasthenia gravis (long-term, oral) | Longer half-life than neostigmine |
| **Edrophonium** | **Tensilon test** for MG diagnosis (historical) | Very short acting; rarely used now (ice-pack test or AChR antibodies) |
| **Physostigmine** | Anticholinergic toxicity (TCAs, scopolamine, atropine) | Tertiary amine → crosses BBB. Avoid if TCA has caused widened QRS |
| **Donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine** | Alzheimer disease (mild–moderate) | Modest cognitive benefit; GI side effects |
| **Tacrine** | Alzheimer (historical) | Hepatotoxic; replaced |

### Irreversible AChE inhibitors

**Organophosphates** (parathion, malathion, sarin, soman, VX) covalently inactivate AChE. Used as insecticides + nerve agents. Cause cholinergic crisis.

### Cholinergic toxicity - "SLUDGE-M / DUMBELS"

<Mnemonic>
**DUMBBELSS** for organophosphate / cholinergic toxicity:
- **D**iarrhea
- **U**rination
- **M**iosis
- **B**ronchospasm
- **B**radycardia
- **E**xcitation of skeletal muscle (fasciculations) + CNS
- **L**acrimation
- **S**alivation
- **S**weating

Treatment: **atropine** (blocks muscarinic effects - watch the bronchospasm, salivation, bradycardia) + **pralidoxime (2-PAM)** (reactivates AChE before it "ages" - give early). Atropine doesn't address skeletal muscle / NMJ fasciculations; 2-PAM does.
</Mnemonic>

## Clinical applications - depth

### Myasthenia gravis vs Lambert-Eaton

| | Myasthenia gravis | Lambert-Eaton |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Antibody to **postsynaptic AChR** (nicotinic) | Antibody to **presynaptic Ca²⁺ channel** (P/Q type) |
| Strength with activity | **Worsens** (fatigability) | **Improves** |
| Reflexes | Normal | Reduced |
| Associated cancer | Thymoma (15%) | Small cell lung cancer (50%) |
| Treatment | Pyridostigmine; steroids; thymectomy; IVIG / plex for crisis | Treat underlying cancer; 3,4-DAP; immunosuppression |
| Response to AChEI | **Improves** | Minimal |

**Myasthenic crisis** (weakness from too little drug) vs **cholinergic crisis** (weakness from too much drug, with miosis, fasciculations, sweating). Edrophonium historically distinguished but rarely used today.

### Glaucoma drugs at a glance

| Class | Drug | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| α-agonist | Brimonidine | ↓ aqueous production |
| β-blocker | Timolol | ↓ aqueous production |
| Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor | Acetazolamide (PO), dorzolamide (top.) | ↓ aqueous production |
| Prostaglandin (FP agonist) | Latanoprost | ↑ uveoscleral outflow |
| Cholinomimetic | Pilocarpine | ↑ trabecular outflow (ciliary muscle contraction) |

### Smoking cessation
- **Nicotine replacement** (patch, gum, lozenge) - reduces withdrawal, doesn't quit you.
- **Varenicline** - α4β2 partial agonist; best efficacy; suicidality black box removed (2016).
- **Bupropion** - NE/DA reuptake; doubles quit rate.

## Side effects of AChE inhibitors

**Muscarinic excess** = SLUDGE + bradycardia + bronchoconstriction + nausea/vomiting/diarrhea + miosis.

**Nicotinic excess** (NMJ + ganglia) = fasciculations → fatigue → paralysis (depolarizing block). Hypertension and tachycardia from sympathetic ganglion stimulation can dominate early before parasympathetic dominance takes over.

<Clinical>
**Pesticide poisoning in farm workers**: presents with pinpoint pupils, drooling, sweating, fasciculations, bradycardia, bronchorrhea ("killed by their own secretions"). Treat **immediately** with atropine titrated to drying of secretions (often needs huge doses, far more than ACLS dosing) + pralidoxime. Decontaminate skin to prevent caregiver exposure.

**Reversal of non-depolarizing neuromuscular blockade** (rocuronium, vecuronium) at end of surgery: neostigmine + glycopyrrolate (the glyco blocks the unwanted muscarinic bradycardia, salivation). Newer alternative: **sugammadex** - cyclodextrin that physically encapsulates rocuronium/vecuronium; faster, cleaner, expensive.

**Donepezil** in Alzheimer disease produces modest symptomatic benefit (~1 year delay in functional decline); doesn't alter the disease. Nausea, diarrhea, vivid dreams are dose-limiting. Memantine (NMDA antagonist) used in moderate–severe disease, often combined with donepezil.
</Clinical>

<HighYield variant="boards">
- Pilocarpine treats **both** acute angle-closure glaucoma (emergency) and Sjögren xerostomia.
- Bethanechol = bowel + bladder atony (no obstruction); not for ileus from mechanical obstruction.
- Physostigmine has CNS effect; neostigmine does NOT. Use physostigmine to reverse anticholinergic toxicity affecting the brain.
- Atropine treats **all** muscarinic toxicity, including organophosphate, but doesn't help nicotinic (use 2-PAM).
- Glaucoma + asthma: avoid timolol (non-selective β-blocker → bronchospasm). Use brimonidine or PG analog.
- "Diaphoresis without flushing" + miosis after a wedding gift box → suspect a nerve agent (sarin in subway). VX is the most persistent and lethal.
- A myasthenic given succinylcholine → exaggerated depolarizing block (more receptors blocked at baseline). Rocuronium also riskier - reduced dose.
</HighYield>
