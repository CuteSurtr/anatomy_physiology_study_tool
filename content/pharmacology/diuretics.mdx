## Nephron map - where each diuretic acts

| Segment | Transporter | Diuretic |
|---|---|---|
| **Proximal tubule** | Na⁺/H⁺ exchanger; carbonic anhydrase | **Acetazolamide** (CA-inhibitor) |
| **Proximal + Loop of Henle** | Free water permeability | **Mannitol** (osmotic) |
| **Thick ascending limb** | Na-K-2Cl cotransporter (NKCC2) | **Loop diuretics** (furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide, ethacrynic) |
| **Distal convoluted tubule** | Na-Cl cotransporter (NCC) | **Thiazides** (HCTZ, chlorthalidone, indapamide, metolazone) |
| **Collecting duct - principal cells** | ENaC + ROMK | **K-sparing**: amiloride, triamterene (block ENaC); spironolactone, eplerenone (block aldo receptor) |
| **Collecting duct - V2 receptor** | Aquaporin-2 insertion | **ADH antagonists** (tolvaptan, conivaptan) |

## Loop diuretics

**Furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide, ethacrynic acid**

**Mechanism**: NKCC2 inhibitor in thick ascending limb. Massive diuresis (~25% of filtered Na). Disrupts countercurrent multiplier → ↓ medullary tonicity.

**Pharmacokinetics**:
- Furosemide PO bioavailability ~50% (variable); IV more reliable.
- **Bumetanide** 1 mg = furosemide 40 mg PO. More predictable.
- **Torsemide** longer half-life, more reliable PO absorption (good in HF).
- **Ethacrynic acid** - only loop **without sulfa group**; use in sulfa allergy.

**Indications**: HF (acute pulmonary edema + chronic), edema (cirrhosis, nephrotic), hypercalcemia (↑ Ca excretion).

**Electrolyte profile**: ↓ K⁺, ↓ Mg²⁺, ↓ Ca²⁺, ↓ Na, ↑ uric acid, contraction alkalosis.

<Mnemonic>
"Loops Lose calcium" - opposite of thiazides which Trap calcium.
</Mnemonic>

**Side effects** (OH DANG):
- **O**totoxicity (esp. ethacrynic; dose + IV-push speed dependent; with aminoglycoside).
- **H**ypokalemia.
- **D**ehydration.
- **A**llergy (sulfa) - except ethacrynic.
- **N**ephritis (interstitial - rare).
- **G**out (↓ uric acid excretion).

## Thiazides + thiazide-like

**Hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone, indapamide, metolazone**

**Mechanism**: Na-Cl cotransporter (NCC) block in DCT. ~5% of filtered Na excreted.

**Indications**: HTN (first-line), nephrolithiasis (↓ urinary Ca → ↓ Ca-stone formation), nephrogenic DI (paradoxical), mild edema.

**Electrolyte profile**: ↓ Na, ↓ K, ↓ Mg, ↑ Ca, ↑ uric acid, ↑ glucose, ↑ LDL.

**Chlorthalidone > HCTZ** for CV outcomes (longer t½, more potent).

**Metolazone** is unique - works in CKD (whereas HCTZ loses effect when GFR &lt;30). Used as **synergistic adjunct to loop** in resistant edema.

**Side effects**: hypokalemia (esp. when added to loop), hypercalcemia, hyperglycemia, hyperuricemia, sulfa allergy, hyponatremia.

## Potassium-sparing diuretics

| Mechanism | Drugs |
|---|---|
| **ENaC blockers** | Amiloride, Triamterene |
| **Aldosterone (MR) antagonists** | Spironolactone, Eplerenone |

Weak diuretics; main role is K-sparing companion to loop/thiazide, plus specific indications.

### Amiloride + triamterene
- Block apical Na channel in principal cell. **Block K-sparing benefit also blocks H+ secretion** → can cause mild metabolic acidosis.
- Indications: companion drug, **Liddle syndrome** (ENaC gain-of-function), lithium-induced DI.

### Spironolactone + eplerenone
- Aldosterone receptor block at MR.
- Indications: HFrEF (mortality benefit - **RALES**, **EMPHASIS-HF**), primary hyperaldosteronism (Conn), cirrhosis with ascites, resistant HTN.
- **Spironolactone** also blocks androgen receptor + ↓ testosterone synthesis → gynecomastia, sexual dysfunction, menstrual irregularity. Useful in PCOS hirsutism, but cosmetic side effects in men.
- **Eplerenone** is selective for MR → no endocrine effects.
- Both cause **hyperkalemia** (esp. with ACE-i, ARB, K supplements, NSAIDs).

## Osmotic diuretics

**Mannitol**
- Filtered at glomerulus, not reabsorbed → osmotic gradient draws water.
- Uses: ↑ ICP (cerebral edema), ↑ IOP (acute glaucoma), drug-induced toxicity (rarely).
- **Contraindicated** in pulmonary edema, anuria, severe dehydration. Risk of pulmonary edema if cardiac function compromised (initial ↑ intravascular volume).

## Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor

**Acetazolamide**
- ↓ CA activity in PT → bicarbonate excretion → mild diuresis + **type 2 RTA** (proximal). 
- Causes hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis (used clinically for metabolic alkalosis treatment).
- Uses: **glaucoma** (↓ aqueous humor production), **altitude sickness** prophylaxis (metabolic acidosis → respiratory compensation pre-acclimatizes), idiopathic intracranial hypertension (pseudotumor cerebri), urinary alkalinization (cystine stones, salicylate overdose).
- Side effects: paresthesias (CO2 retention), Renal stones (Ca-phosphate, ↑ urinary pH), Acidosis, Sulfa allergy.

## ADH (vasopressin) antagonists ("aquaretics")

**Conivaptan** (V1a + V2 block) IV, **Tolvaptan** (V2 block) oral.

- Block V2 receptor → ↓ aquaporin-2 → free water diuresis without Na loss.
- Use: euvolemic + hypervolemic **hyponatremia** (SIADH, HF). Tolvaptan also for **autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD)** progression.
- **Black box**: hepatotoxicity (tolvaptan); rapid Na correction → osmotic demyelination (central pontine myelinolysis) - correct ≤ 8 mEq/L per 24 h.

## Side effect mnemonic - "K-sparing vs K-wasting"

- **K-wasting**: loops, thiazides, acetazolamide → hypokalemia.
- **K-sparing**: amiloride, triamterene, spironolactone, eplerenone → hyperkalemia.

## Combinations + sequential nephron blockade

In resistant edema (HF + CKD), combine:
1. Loop (block TAL).
2. Thiazide-like (block DCT - metolazone preferred in CKD).
3. Spironolactone (block principal cell aldosterone effect).

This sequential blockade overcomes compensatory Na reabsorption downstream of a single diuretic ("braking phenomenon"). Watch K (paradoxically can swing either way), creatinine, and weight.

## Electrolyte derangements - the cheat sheet

| Diuretic | Na | K | Ca | Mg | uric acid | pH |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Loop** | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | ↑ | alkalosis (contraction) |
| **Thiazide** | ↓ | ↓ | ↑ | ↓ | ↑ | alkalosis |
| **K-sparing** | ↓ | ↑ | – | – | – | acidosis (esp. amiloride) |
| **Acetazolamide** | ↓ | ↓ | – | – | – | acidosis (HCO₃ loss) |
| **Osmotic** | ↓ then ↑ | ↓ | – | – | – | – |
| **ADH antag** | ↑ | – | – | – | – | – |

<Clinical>
**Acute decompensated HF** - IV furosemide 1–2× home oral dose, or start at 40 mg if naïve. DOSE trial: bolus vs continuous infusion - no clear advantage; bolus is fine. Add metolazone PO 30 min before furosemide for resistant edema.

**Cirrhosis with ascites** - spironolactone + furosemide in 100:40 ratio (e.g., spiro 100 + furosemide 40). Aldosterone is elevated in cirrhosis → spiro is the workhorse. Titrate to weight loss &lt;1 kg/day with peripheral edema, &lt;0.5 kg/day without.

**Hypercalcemia** - aggressive IV NS first, then loop diuretic (furosemide) to ↑ Ca excretion. Don't use thiazide - it ↑ Ca retention.

**SIADH** - free water restriction first. If hyponatremia &lt;125, severe symptoms, give 3% saline. Tolvaptan if persistent. Don't correct >8 mEq/L per 24 h.

**Altitude sickness** - acetazolamide 125 mg BID starting 24 h before ascent. Tingling in fingers is expected. Caution in sulfa allergy.
</Clinical>

<HighYield variant="boards">
- Furosemide is a **sulfa drug**. Patient with severe sulfa allergy + need for loop → **ethacrynic acid**.
- Mannitol contraindicated in pulmonary edema (initial volume expansion).
- Thiazides cause hypercalcemia → can unmask hyperparathyroidism on screening labs.
- Spironolactone gynecomastia → switch to eplerenone (selective MR).
- Acetazolamide for altitude sickness, glaucoma, urinary alkalinization, pseudotumor cerebri.
- Tolvaptan in ADPKD slows disease progression (TEMPO trial); expensive + hepatotoxic.
- "Diuretic of choice in HF" = loop (furosemide). For mortality benefit, also add spironolactone if K and Cr permit.
- Hyperkalemia from K-sparing + ACE-i + diabetic + CKD is a frequent ED visit.
</HighYield>
