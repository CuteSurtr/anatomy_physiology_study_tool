## Fluoroquinolones

**Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Moxifloxacin, Ofloxacin, Delafloxacin, Gemifloxacin**

**Mechanism**: inhibit DNA gyrase (topoisomerase II) + topoisomerase IV → DNA fragmentation. **Bactericidal**.

**Spectrum** by generation/example:
- **Ciprofloxacin**: best Gram-neg coverage (incl. Pseudomonas); some Gram-pos; not strep pneumoniae.
- **Levofloxacin**: + better Gram-pos (Strep pneumoniae) + atypicals → CAP coverage.
- **Moxifloxacin**: best Gram-pos + anaerobes; ↓ Pseudomonas.
- **Delafloxacin**: includes MRSA.

**Uses**:
- Cipro: complicated UTI, pyelonephritis, Pseudomonas, traveler's diarrhea, anthrax prophylaxis, prostatitis.
- Levo: CAP, pyelonephritis, complicated UTI.
- Moxi: CAP, intra-abdominal (with metronidazole or alone).
- Topical: eye drops (myo-, oph-), otic drops.

**Pharmacology**: oral bioavailability excellent (≥80%); IV → PO transition usually possible. **Don't take with cations** (Ca, Mg, Fe, Al, Zn, dairy) - chelation ↓ absorption.

**Side effects + black box**:
- **Tendon rupture** (Achilles esp.) - 1 in 1,000; risk ↑ in age >60, steroids, organ transplant. Discontinue at first tendon pain.
- **Peripheral neuropathy** - sometimes permanent.
- **CNS** - headache, dizziness, insomnia, seizures (high dose, esp. with theophylline).
- **QT prolongation** - moxifloxacin worst.
- **Aortic aneurysm + dissection** - 2x risk; avoid in known AAA.
- **Hypoglycemia / hyperglycemia** (esp. moxi).
- **Arthropathy in kids** (cartilage damage) - avoid in pregnancy + kids unless no alternative (CF, anthrax).
- **C. diff** - frequent.
- **Photosensitivity**.

**Resistance**: mutations in gyrA/parC. Rising → narrow when possible.

## Metronidazole

**Mechanism**: prodrug; reduced to active form in anaerobic environment → free radicals → DNA fragmentation. **Bactericidal**.

**Spectrum**: anaerobes (Bacteroides, Clostridium) + protozoa (Giardia, Entamoeba, Trichomonas, Gardnerella).

**Uses**:
- **Anaerobic infections below the diaphragm** (intra-abdominal - with cef; pelvic; abscess).
- **C. diff** - PO; oral metronidazole now SECOND-line (vancomycin or fidaxomicin first; metronidazole IV for severe/fulminant adjunct).
- **Bacterial vaginosis** (PO or vaginal).
- **Trichomoniasis** (single 2g dose).
- **Giardiasis, amebiasis**.
- **H. pylori** (combination).

**Side effects**:
- **Disulfiram-like reaction** with ethanol - avoid alcohol during + 3 days after.
- **Metallic taste**.
- Headache, GI upset.
- **Peripheral neuropathy** with prolonged use.
- **Encephalopathy + cerebellar dysfunction** rare.
- **Avoid 1st trimester** pregnancy historically; safer than thought, often used.

## Rifamycins

**Rifampin, Rifabutin, Rifaximin, Rifapentine**

**Mechanism**: bind bacterial RNA polymerase → ↓ RNA synthesis. **Bactericidal**.

**Spectrum**: mycobacteria (TB, MAC, leprosy), N. meningitidis, S. aureus (biofilm). Used as part of **combination** therapy (resistance develops rapidly with monotherapy).

**Uses**:
- **TB** (with isoniazid, pyrazinamide, ethambutol = RIPE).
- **Meningococcal prophylaxis** (close contacts).
- **MRSA prosthetic joint** (adjunct to debulking agent).
- **Latent TB** (rifampin × 4 months alternative to INH 9 months).
- **Rifaximin** (poorly absorbed) - **hepatic encephalopathy**, **traveler's diarrhea**, **IBS-D**.
- **Rifabutin** - MAC, TB in HIV (less CYP3A4 induction than rifampin → preferred with protease inhibitors).

**Side effects**:
- **Orange-red discoloration** of urine, sweat, tears, lenses (warn patient!).
- **CYP induction** - massive. ↓ levels of OCs, warfarin, statins, antiretrovirals, calcineurin inhibitors, methadone, many others.
- **Hepatotoxicity** (especially with INH).
- **Flu-like syndrome** (intermittent dosing).
- **Cytopenias** (rare).

## Sulfonamides + Trimethoprim (TMP-SMX)

**Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX, Bactrim, Septra)**

**Mechanism**:
- Sulfonamide blocks dihydropteroate synthase (PABA → folate).
- Trimethoprim blocks dihydrofolate reductase.
- **Sequential block** of folate synthesis = synergistic.

**Spectrum**: broad - many Gram+ (incl. CA-MRSA, MSSA), many Gram- (E. coli, Klebsiella, etc.), Stenotrophomonas, Listeria, Pneumocystis jirovecii, Toxoplasma, Nocardia.

**Uses**:
- **Uncomplicated UTI** (1st-line).
- **CA-MRSA skin** infection.
- **Pneumocystis (PCP)** treatment + prophylaxis in HIV (CD4 &lt;200), transplant, leukemia.
- **Toxoplasmosis** (combined with leucovorin).
- **Nocardiosis**.
- **Stenotrophomonas maltophilia**.

**Side effects**:
- **Hyperkalemia** - TMP blocks ENaC (amiloride-like).
- **Hyponatremia, ↑ creatinine** (TMP competes with creatinine secretion).
- **Marrow suppression** - megaloblastic anemia (folate), thrombocytopenia.
- **Stevens-Johnson syndrome** - serious sulfa allergy.
- **Hemolysis** in **G6PD deficiency**.
- **Kernicterus** in late pregnancy (sulfa displaces bilirubin from albumin).
- **Drug interactions**: ↑ warfarin (potentiation), ↑ methotrexate, ↑ phenytoin, ↑ sulfonylurea hypoglycemia.

### Other sulfa drugs

- **Sulfadiazine**: toxoplasmosis (with pyrimethamine).
- **Sulfisoxazole**: rarely used.
- **Sulfasalazine**: IBD + RA (prodrug → sulfapyridine + 5-ASA).
- **Sulfacetamide**: ophthalmic.
- **Dapsone**: Pneumocystis prophylaxis (G6PD test first), leprosy, dermatitis herpetiformis.

### Sulfa allergy

Reports range from minor rash → SJS/TEN. **True sulfa allergy** = sulfonamide ABX (TMP-SMX, sulfadiazine). **Other "sulfa" drugs** (loop, thiazide, sulfonylureas, celecoxib, dapsone, acetazolamide) have variable cross-reactivity:
- Loop, thiazide, sulfonylureas: traditional teaching avoided in true sulfa allergy; modern data shows cross-reactivity low (5%).
- Severe history (SJS/TEN, DRESS) → strict avoidance of all sulfa-containing drugs.

## Pyrimethamine

DHFR inhibitor (like TMP, more active vs protozoal). Used for **toxoplasmosis** (with sulfadiazine + leucovorin) + **malaria** (with sulfadoxine = Fansidar; less used now).

Side effects: folate deficiency → megaloblastic anemia; co-administer **leucovorin** (folinic acid) to protect host cells.

## Nitrofurantoin

**Mechanism**: prodrug; reduced to active intermediates → multiple bacterial enzyme inhibition. **Bacteriostatic**.

**Use**: **uncomplicated UTI** (cystitis only - doesn't achieve therapeutic levels in tissue; not for pyelonephritis).

**Pharmacology**: concentrated in urine; oral; renally cleared.

**Side effects**:
- **GI** (nausea common).
- **Pulmonary toxicity** - acute pneumonitis (1-2 weeks) or chronic fibrosis (months-years).
- **Hepatotoxicity** - acute or chronic.
- **Peripheral neuropathy**.
- **Hemolysis in G6PD** deficiency.
- **AVOID** if CrCl &lt;30 (won't concentrate in urine), 3rd trimester pregnancy near delivery (hemolysis in newborn G6PD), neonates.

## Fosfomycin

**Mechanism**: blocks bacterial cell wall (MurA enzyme - earliest peptidoglycan step). **Bactericidal**.

**Use**: **uncomplicated UTI** in non-pregnant women; **single 3-g oral dose**. Also IV for MDR Gram-negatives.

**Side effects**: GI, headache. Minimal.

## Methenamine

**Mechanism**: prodrug → formaldehyde in acidic urine (pH &lt;5.5).

**Use**: chronic UTI prophylaxis; works only with acidic urine - combine with vitamin C or ammonium chloride.

**Side effects**: GI, rash, **AVOID with sulfa** (precipitates).

## Antibiotic empiric matrix - quick reference

### UTI

| Type | Choice |
|---|---|
| Uncomplicated (women, &lt;65, no comorbid) | Nitrofurantoin 100 mg BID × 5 d, or TMP-SMX 1 DS BID × 3 d, or fosfomycin 3 g × 1 |
| Pyelonephritis (uncomplicated) | Ciprofloxacin 500 mg BID × 7 d, or levofloxacin, or TMP-SMX (after sens) × 14 d |
| Pyelonephritis (admission) | Ceftriaxone 1 g IV, then PO when better |
| Catheter-associated | Cefepime or piperacillin-tazobactam ± aminoglycoside; tailor to sensitivities |
| Prostatitis | Ciprofloxacin × 4-6 weeks |

### Common conditions
- **Strep throat** = penicillin VK or amoxicillin × 10 d.
- **Otitis media** = amoxicillin (high-dose) × 10 d.
- **Sinusitis (bacterial)** = amox-clav (high-dose).
- **Bronchitis** = usually viral - no antibiotic.
- **CAP** outpatient = amoxicillin or doxycycline (or macrolide where resistance low).
- **CAP** inpatient = ceftriaxone + azithromycin OR levofloxacin/moxifloxacin.
- **HAP** = piperacillin-tazobactam or cefepime + vancomycin if MRSA risk.
- **Aspiration pneumonia** = amox-clav PO or piperacillin-tazobactam IV.
- **Skin/soft tissue** = cephalexin (no MRSA risk) or TMP-SMX or doxycycline.
- **Animal bite** = amox-clav (Pasteurella + anaerobes).
- **Diabetic foot** mild = amox-clav; severe = piperacillin-tazobactam + vancomycin.
- **Travelers' diarrhea** = azithromycin or fluoroquinolone (short course).
- **Bacterial gastroenteritis** = often supportive; azithromycin or ciprofloxacin for severe Salmonella, Shigella, Campylobacter (resistance varies).
- **H. pylori** = clarithromycin triple therapy (CLAMP - clarithromycin + amoxicillin + PPI) or bismuth quadruple (BMT + PPI) where macrolide resistance high.

<Clinical>
**Tendon rupture warning**: counsel patients on fluoroquinolones to stop drug + see clinician at first tendon pain. Highest risk in elderly + steroid-treated + transplant patients.

**Patient with prosthetic valve endocarditis** = vancomycin + gentamicin + **rifampin** (biofilm penetration). Rifampin held until bacteremia clears (avoid resistance).

**Pregnancy + UTI**: nitrofurantoin (avoid late 3rd trimester), fosfomycin, cephalexin, amox-clav. AVOID fluoroquinolone, tetracycline.

**Recurrent UTI prevention**: methenamine + vitamin C, low-dose nitrofurantoin nightly, postcoital TMP-SMX. Vaginal estrogen in postmenopausal women.

**Patient on warfarin starts TMP-SMX** for UTI: INR will jump in 3-5 days. Check INR; some clinicians empirically reduce warfarin 20% during TMP-SMX course.

**Latent TB treatment**: INH × 9 months, OR INH + rifapentine weekly × 12 doses (3HP), OR rifampin × 4 months.
</Clinical>
