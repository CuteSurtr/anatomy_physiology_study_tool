Muscarinic antagonists block parasympathetic effects. Practically every drug class with the suffix "-pine," "-zine," or "-tropium" has some anticholinergic activity.

## Atropine - the prototype

Tertiary amine, crosses BBB. Competitive at all M subtypes.

**Effects** (memorize the cluster):
- **Eye** - mydriasis + cycloplegia (loss of accommodation) - useful for fundoscopy; raises IOP in narrow-angle glaucoma → contraindicated.
- **Heart** - low dose: paradoxical bradycardia (presynaptic M1 block at cardiac vagal); standard dose: tachycardia (M2 block at SA).
- **Airway** - bronchodilation, ↓ secretions.
- **GI** - ↓ motility, ↓ secretion.
- **GU** - urinary retention (M3 block at detrusor).
- **Sweat** - anhidrosis + hyperthermia (sympathetic sweat glands paradoxically use ACh).
- **CNS** - sedation low dose; agitation, hallucinations, delirium high dose.

**Uses**: bradycardia (ACLS, organophosphate poisoning), preanesthetic drying agent, mydriatic.

## By organ system - "the antimuscarinic shopping list"

### Eye - mydriatics + cycloplegics
| Drug | Duration | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Tropicamide** | ~6 h | Routine fundoscopy |
| **Cyclopentolate** | 24 h | Pediatric refraction (needs cycloplegia) |
| **Homatropine** | 1–3 days | Uveitis (prevents synechiae) |
| **Atropine** | 7–10 days | Severe uveitis; amblyopia therapy ("pharmacologic patching") |

### Airway - bronchodilators (COPD + asthma adjunct)
| Drug | Onset | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ipratropium** | Short-acting; 4–6 h | COPD, acute asthma adjunct |
| **Tiotropium** | Long-acting; 24 h | COPD maintenance |
| **Umeclidinium / Aclidinium / Glycopyrronium** | LAMA | COPD, often combined with LABA/ICS |

Quaternary amine = no CNS effects. Limited systemic absorption when inhaled.

### Cardiovascular
- **Atropine** - bradycardia, AV block, organophosphate poisoning.
- **Glycopyrrolate** - perioperative drying + as paired drug with neostigmine for NMJ reversal. No CNS.

### GI - antispasmodic + antidiarrheal
| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| **Dicyclomine** | IBS antispasmodic |
| **Hyoscyamine** | IBS, biliary colic |
| **Atropine + diphenoxylate (Lomotil)** | Antidiarrheal (atropine added to discourage opioid abuse) |
| **Loperamide** | Antidiarrheal (peripheral opioid - not technically anticholinergic) |

### GU - overactive bladder
| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Oxybutynin** | First-gen; substantial dry mouth, constipation, confusion |
| **Tolterodine** | Better tolerated than oxybutynin |
| **Solifenacin / Darifenacin** | M3-selective; less CNS effect |
| **Trospium** | Quaternary - no CNS |
| **Mirabegron** | β3 agonist alternative (relaxes detrusor by sympathetic mimetic, not anticholinergic) |

### CNS
| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| **Scopolamine** | Motion sickness (transdermal); preoperative sedation + amnesia |
| **Benztropine, trihexyphenidyl** | Drug-induced parkinsonism + acute dystonia from antipsychotics (NOT used in idiopathic PD) |
| **Diphenhydramine** | Antihistamine with strong anticholinergic effect; first-gen → sedation; also useful for acute dystonia |
| **Dimenhydrinate, meclizine** | Motion sickness; vertigo |

## Drugs with significant "hidden" anticholinergic activity

The **anticholinergic burden** concept matters in elderly patients (falls, delirium, cognition):

- **TCAs** (amitriptyline > nortriptyline) - very strong; many side effects come from this.
- **Antihistamines (1st-gen)** - diphenhydramine, hydroxyzine, chlorpheniramine.
- **Antipsychotics** (low-potency typicals like chlorpromazine, thioridazine; clozapine, olanzapine).
- **Skeletal muscle relaxants** - cyclobenzaprine (structurally a TCA).
- **Quinidine, disopyramide** (class IA antiarrhythmics) - significant antimuscarinic.
- **Some antiemetics** - promethazine, prochlorperazine.

## Anticholinergic toxidrome - "the mnemonic"

<Mnemonic>
- **Hot as Hades** - anhidrosis → hyperthermia (block of sympathetic sweat glands' M3)
- **Blind as a bat** - mydriasis + cycloplegia
- **Dry as a bone** - dry mouth, dry skin, ↓ secretions
- **Red as a beet** - cutaneous vasodilation (sympathetic compensation for heat)
- **Mad as a hatter** - agitation, confusion, hallucinations (tertiary amines cross BBB)
- **Full as a flask** - urinary retention
- **Heart races on** - tachycardia
- **The bowel and bladder lose their tone, the gut and pupils blow** - ileus + urinary retention + mydriasis
</Mnemonic>

**Treatment**: supportive care + active cooling. **Physostigmine** can reverse pure muscarinic toxicity (e.g., from scopolamine, plant ingestion like jimsonweed) - but **avoid in TCA overdose** with widened QRS because it can precipitate seizures + asystole. Sodium bicarbonate is the antidote for TCA cardiotoxicity (Na-channel blockade).

## Side effects in normal use

Even at therapeutic doses, M-blockers cause:
- **Dry mouth** (most common; offer sugar-free gum, water).
- **Constipation** - dose-limiting for OAB drugs.
- **Urinary retention** - exacerbates BPH; avoid in men with significant retention.
- **Blurred vision** - particularly bothersome on initial dose; tolerance often develops.
- **Cognitive impairment** - in elderly; AVOID anticholinergics where possible.
- **Tachycardia** - generally minor at therapeutic doses but matters in cardiac disease.

## Special cases

### Acute angle-closure glaucoma triggered by anticholinergic
A patient with shallow anterior chamber gets pupillary dilation → iris bunches up → blocks trabecular meshwork → ↑ IOP → eye pain, headache, halos, ↓ vision. Trigger: tropicamide, sympathomimetic, but also indirect anticholinergic (Benadryl, TCAs). Emergency = pilocarpine + acetazolamide + topical β-blocker + laser iridotomy.

### Atropine vs scopolamine on the CNS
Atropine at typical doses is mildly stimulating CNS; scopolamine is sedating + amnestic at low doses. That's why scopolamine is in patches for motion sickness.

### Jimsonweed / "Devil's trumpet"
Datura stramonium - tropane alkaloids (atropine + scopolamine). Recreational toxidrome with severe anticholinergic delirium, dilated pupils, dry mucosa. Classic teen ED visit. Treat supportively; physostigmine for severe.

<Clinical>
**Why anticholinergics fail in idiopathic Parkinson**: by the time tremor + bradykinesia are obvious, dopamine deficiency dominates. Anticholinergics (benztropine, trihexyphenidyl) help tremor a little but worsen cognition - use **only** in young patients with tremor-predominant disease, never in elderly. For drug-induced parkinsonism from antipsychotics, anticholinergics work very well because the underlying dopamine system is intact.

**Mirabegron alternative**: β3 selective agonist on the detrusor. Good for older adults who can't tolerate the cognitive side effects of oxybutynin. Watch BP (~5 mmHg ↑).
</Clinical>

<HighYield variant="boards">
- Atropine + glycopyrrolate are the antimuscarinics paired with **neostigmine** at the end of surgery to block its bradycardia + secretions while it reverses the NMJ blockade.
- Ipratropium can be used in acute asthma; tiotropium is **not** indicated for acute attacks.
- **Solifenacin vs oxybutynin**: solifenacin is M3-selective and better tolerated; oxybutynin is cheaper but more cognitive side effects.
- "The little old lady with delirium after starting Benadryl for sleep" = anticholinergic burden in elderly.
- Pupil dilation from anticholinergic (e.g., scopolamine patch worn behind ear, finger then eye) is unilateral and **non-reactive** - distinguishes from third nerve palsy (other CN III findings).
- A patient on benztropine for haloperidol-induced dystonia who develops fever + dry skin + delirium = anticholinergic toxicity. Discontinue + cool + supportive.
</HighYield>
