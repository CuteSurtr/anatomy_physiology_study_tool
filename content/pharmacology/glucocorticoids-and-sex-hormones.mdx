## Glucocorticoids

### Potency comparison

| Drug | Anti-inflammatory (vs cortisol) | Mineralocorticoid (vs cortisol) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hydrocortisone** | 1 | 1 | Short (8 h) |
| **Cortisone** | 0.8 | 0.8 | Short |
| **Prednisone** | 4 | 0.8 | Intermediate (12–36 h) |
| **Prednisolone** | 4 | 0.8 | Intermediate |
| **Methylprednisolone** | 5 | 0.5 | Intermediate |
| **Triamcinolone** | 5 | 0 | Intermediate |
| **Dexamethasone** | 25 | 0 | Long (>36 h) |
| **Betamethasone** | 25 | 0 | Long |
| **Fludrocortisone** | 10 | 250 | Intermediate - **mineralocorticoid** for Addison |

**Equivalent doses**: 20 mg hydrocortisone ≈ 5 mg prednisone ≈ 4 mg methylprednisolone ≈ 0.75 mg dexamethasone.

### Mechanism

Diffuse across membrane → cytoplasmic GR → translocate to nucleus → transactivate anti-inflammatory genes (annexin A1) + transrepress NF-κB → ↓ COX-2, cytokines, adhesion molecules, IL-2, etc. Also stabilize lysosomal membranes, ↓ capillary permeability.

### Adverse effects (chronic use - iatrogenic Cushing)

- **Metabolic**: ↑ glucose (diabetogenic), ↑ lipids, central obesity, fat redistribution ("buffalo hump", moon face), striae, ↑ appetite/weight.
- **Bone**: osteoporosis, avascular necrosis (esp. femoral head), growth retardation in kids.
- **Skin**: thinning, striae, easy bruising, poor wound healing.
- **GI**: peptic ulcer (esp. when paired with NSAIDs), pancreatitis.
- **Eye**: cataracts (posterior subcapsular), glaucoma.
- **CNS**: mood (euphoria → depression → psychosis), insomnia.
- **Immune**: opportunistic infections, masked fever; reactivation of TB, strongyloides, hepatitis B.
- **Endocrine**: HPA suppression → adrenal insufficiency on withdrawal.
- **CV**: HTN (mineralocorticoid effect), edema.
- **Hematologic**: neutrophilia (demargination), lymphopenia, eosinopenia.

### Tapering + stress doses

- Doses >20 mg prednisone for >3 weeks → assume HPA suppression.
- **Taper** slowly to allow HPA recovery; can take months for full recovery.
- **Stress dose** in patients on chronic steroids: hydrocortisone 100 mg IV at induction + 50 mg q6h × 24 h for major surgery; smaller for minor stress.

### Therapeutic uses (a few hundred...)
- Asthma, COPD (inhaled + oral burst).
- Adrenal insufficiency (hydrocortisone + fludrocortisone).
- Autoimmune (RA, SLE, vasculitis, IBD).
- Allergy (anaphylaxis adjunct, severe contact dermatitis).
- Transplant rejection.
- Malignancy (lymphoma, leukemia, MM as chemotherapy; brain edema; antiemetic).
- Pre-natal lung maturation (betamethasone, dexamethasone).
- Cerebral edema (dexamethasone for tumor; not for traumatic brain injury - CRASH trial → harm).

### Specific steroid pearls

- **Inhaled steroids (fluticasone, budesonide)** for asthma/COPD → **oral candidiasis** (rinse mouth) + dysphonia. Minimal systemic effects at low doses.
- **Topical (clobetasol, betamethasone)** - skin atrophy, telangiectasia, perioral dermatitis, masking infection. Use lowest potency that works for shortest time.
- **Intra-articular** (triamcinolone) - rapid relief; ≤3-4 injections/year per joint; risk of accelerated cartilage damage with overuse.
- **Dexamethasone** preferred for: cerebral edema (poor mineralocorticoid effect), antenatal lung maturity, CINV antiemetic, COVID pneumonia (RECOVERY trial).

## Sex hormones

### Estrogens

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Estradiol (17β)** | Endogenous; oral, patch, vaginal, injectable forms |
| **Conjugated equine estrogens (Premarin)** | Mixed estrogens; oral |
| **Ethinyl estradiol** | Oral contraceptives; resistant to first-pass metabolism |

**Uses**: hormone replacement (menopausal symptoms), contraception, hypogonadism, transgender therapy.

**Side effects**: thromboembolism, breast/endometrial cancer (unopposed → endometrial), HTN, gallstones, migraine.

**Contraindications**: history of VTE, ER+ breast cancer, undiagnosed vaginal bleeding, hepatic dysfunction, smoker >35 (combined OC), pregnancy.

### Progestins

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Progesterone** | Endogenous; vaginal/IM forms |
| **Medroxyprogesterone (MPA)** | Oral / Depo-Provera (IM q3 months) |
| **Norethindrone** | Mini-pill (progestin-only OC) |
| **Levonorgestrel** | OC + emergency contraception (Plan B), IUD (Mirena) |
| **Drospirenone** | Spironolactone-like → anti-androgen + anti-mineralocorticoid (Yaz, Yasmin) |

**Side effects**: irregular bleeding, mood changes, weight gain, ↓ libido, BMD ↓ (Depo).

### Oral contraceptives

**Combined OC** (estrogen + progestin) - main mechanism is **suppressing LH surge** → no ovulation. Also: cervical mucus thickening, endometrial atrophy.

- 91-day extended cycle: continuous active pills with infrequent periods.
- 28-day pack: 21 active + 7 inactive (placebo + iron).
- **Side effects**: VTE, MI/stroke (esp. smokers >35), HTN, breast tenderness, nausea, breakthrough bleeding.
- **Beneficial effects**: ↓ ovarian + endometrial cancer, ↓ bone loss, regularization of cycles, acne improvement.
- **Drug interactions**: rifampin, anticonvulsants (phenytoin, carbamazepine, topiramate), St. John's wort → ↓ efficacy. Antibiotics generally OK except rifampin.

**Progestin-only OC** ("mini-pill"): safer in VTE history, breastfeeding. Stricter timing (3-hour window).

**Long-acting reversible contraception (LARC)**:
- Copper IUD - 10 years, hormone-free.
- Levonorgestrel IUD - 3-8 years.
- Etonogestrel implant (Nexplanon) - 3 years.

**Emergency contraception**:
- **Levonorgestrel** (Plan B) - within 72 h; best within 24.
- **Ulipristal** (Ella) - within 120 h.
- **Copper IUD** - within 5 days; also long-term contraception thereafter.

### Mifepristone

Progesterone receptor antagonist + glucocorticoid receptor antagonist.

Uses: medical abortion (paired with **misoprostol** - PGE1 analog for uterine contraction), Cushing syndrome refractory to surgery, postpartum endometritis (rarely).

### SERMs (Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulators)

Tissue-selective - agonist in some tissues, antagonist in others.

| Drug | Bone | Breast | Endometrium | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tamoxifen** | Agonist | Antagonist | Agonist (↑ endometrial cancer) | ER+ breast cancer (pre- and postmenopausal) |
| **Raloxifene** | Agonist | Antagonist | No effect | Osteoporosis + breast cancer prevention |
| **Clomiphene** | Antagonist (hypothalamus → ↑ FSH/LH) | | | Ovulation induction |
| **Ospemifene** | Agonist (vagina) | Antagonist (breast) | Mixed | Postmenopausal dyspareunia |

**Tamoxifen side effects**: hot flashes, **endometrial cancer**, VTE, retinal deposits.
**Raloxifene side effects**: hot flashes, VTE; **no endometrial risk**.

### Aromatase inhibitors

**Anastrozole, Letrozole, Exemestane** - block conversion of androgens → estrogens in peripheral tissues.

**Use**: ER+ breast cancer in **postmenopausal** women (no ovarian estrogen production to outcompete).

Side effects: hot flashes, arthralgia, osteoporosis (no estrogen at all - check BMD).

### Androgens

| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| **Testosterone** (gel, patch, IM cypionate/enanthate, oral undecanoate) | Hypogonadism |
| **Methyltestosterone** | Hypogonadism (oral but hepatotoxic) |
| **Oxandrolone** | Anabolic; muscle wasting |
| **DHEA** | OTC supplement; mild androgenic |

**Side effects**: erythrocytosis (monitor Hgb), prostate growth, acne, gynecomastia (aromatization to estrogen), HDL ↓, mood changes, sleep apnea worsening, infertility (HPG axis suppression).

**Contraindications**: prostate cancer, breast cancer (men), uncontrolled HF.

### Androgen-blocking drugs

| Drug | Mechanism | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Finasteride / Dutasteride** | 5α-reductase inhibitor → ↓ DHT | BPH, androgenic alopecia (men) |
| **Spironolactone** | AR antagonist + ↓ androgen synthesis | PCOS hirsutism, transgender therapy |
| **Flutamide / Bicalutamide / Apalutamide** | AR antagonist | Prostate cancer |
| **Enzalutamide** | Newer AR antagonist | Castration-resistant prostate cancer |
| **Leuprolide / Goserelin** | GnRH agonist (continuous → desensitization → ↓ LH/FSH after initial flare) | Prostate cancer; endometriosis; precocious puberty (paradoxical use) |
| **Degarelix** | GnRH antagonist - no flare | Prostate cancer |
| **Abiraterone** | CYP17 inhibitor → ↓ adrenal androgens | Castration-resistant prostate cancer |

### Erectile dysfunction

**Phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE5) inhibitors**: **Sildenafil, Tadalafil, Vardenafil, Avanafil**.

**Mechanism**: ↓ cGMP breakdown in corpus cavernosum → sustained smooth muscle relaxation + erection.

**Side effects**: headache, flushing, dyspepsia, blue-tinted vision (sildenafil PDE6), priapism, NAION (rare).

**Contraindications**: **nitrates** within 24 h (sildenafil) or 48 h (tadalafil) - profound hypotension. α-blockers - cautious co-use.

**Alternative**: alprostadil (PGE1) intracavernosal, vacuum devices, implants.

### Anabolic steroids - abuse

Used illegally for muscle building. Side effects: HPG suppression → testicular atrophy + infertility, acne, gynecomastia (aromatization), aggression, mood changes, hepatic adenoma (esp. oral 17α-alkylated), erythrocytosis, premature physeal closure in adolescents.

<Clinical>
**Adrenal crisis** treatment: IV **hydrocortisone** 100 mg bolus + IV NS + dextrose. Treat presumed sepsis. Continue stress-dose hydrocortisone for ~24 h.

**HRT decisions**: WHI study showed CEE/MPA increased breast cancer + VTE but decreased fractures; estrogen-only (no uterus) increased stroke + VTE without breast cancer signal. Decision: limited duration, lowest dose for vasomotor symptoms; not for prevention.

**Tamoxifen vs aromatase inhibitor**: premenopausal → tamoxifen (AIs don't work, plenty of ovarian estrogen). Postmenopausal → AI (more effective than tamoxifen for ER+ breast cancer). Watch BMD on AI.

**Steroid taper** for "burst" therapy (e.g., asthma 40 mg prednisone × 5 days) - no taper needed if &lt;3 weeks duration. Longer courses → individualized taper.
</Clinical>
