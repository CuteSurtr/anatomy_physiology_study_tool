## COX biology

| Isoform | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **COX-1** | Constitutive (most tissues) | Gastric protection (PGE₂), platelet TXA₂ aggregation, renal hemodynamics |
| **COX-2** | Induced at inflammation; also constitutive in kidney, endothelium | Inflammation prostaglandins; renal Na+/H₂O handling; endothelial prostacyclin |

NSAIDs inhibit COX → ↓ prostaglandins → analgesia + anti-inflammation + antipyresis. COX-1 inhibition explains GI + platelet side effects; COX-2-selective drugs spare COX-1 but lose prostacyclin/TXA₂ balance.

## Aspirin - the unique

- **Irreversibly** acetylates COX-1 (and weakly COX-2).
- Platelets can't synthesize new COX → effect persists 7-10 days (platelet lifespan).
- **Low dose (81 mg)** for cardiovascular prevention.
- **Higher dose (325 mg+)** loses CV selectivity (also inhibits endothelial prostacyclin).
- **Side effects**:
  - GI bleed (esp. with anticoagulant, steroid, NSAID, alcohol).
  - **Bronchospasm in AERD/Samter triad** (asthma + nasal polyps + ASA sensitivity).
  - **Reye syndrome** in kids with viral illness - avoid &lt;16 yo.
  - **Salicylate toxicity** - tinnitus, hyperventilation (early respiratory alkalosis), then anion-gap metabolic acidosis. Treat: urine alkalinization (NaHCO₃), dialysis if severe.

## Non-selective NSAIDs

| Drug | t½ | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ibuprofen** | 2 h | OTC; QID dosing |
| **Naproxen** | 14 h | BID; less CV risk than other NSAIDs (PRECISION trial) |
| **Indomethacin** | 4-5 h | Most potent for inflammation; gout flare, PDA closure (neonate), pericarditis |
| **Diclofenac** | 2 h | Topical, oral; ↑ CV risk |
| **Ketorolac** | 6 h | IV/IM; equipotent to morphine for moderate pain; **5-day max** (renal toxicity) |
| **Meloxicam** | 20 h | Preferential COX-2; QD |
| **Piroxicam** | 50 h | Long t½ → ↑ GI risk |

### Side effects (all NSAIDs)

- **GI** - dyspepsia, ulcer, bleed, perforation. Risk ↑ with: age >65, prior ulcer, steroid, anticoagulant, alcohol, smoking, high dose, multiple NSAIDs.
  - Co-prescribe **PPI** for high-risk patients.
- **Renal** - afferent arteriolar constriction (PGE-mediated dilation lost) → pre-renal AKI; chronic interstitial nephritis (analgesic nephropathy); hyperK; hypertension. Worst in: elderly, CKD, HF, dehydration, with diuretic/ACE-i.
- **CV** - ↑ MI, stroke (esp. high-dose, non-naproxen). Avoid in CHF.
- **HTN** - PGE-mediated renal Na excretion lost.
- **Aspirin/NSAID hypersensitivity (AERD)** - urticaria, bronchospasm.
- **Drug interactions** - blunt antihypertensives (esp. ACE-i, ARB, β-blocker, diuretic), ↑ lithium, ↑ methotrexate.

## COX-2 selective NSAIDs

**Celecoxib** is the only one still widely available (rofecoxib, valdecoxib withdrawn for CV signal).

**Pros**: less GI bleeding (~50% reduction vs non-selective).

**Cons**:
- Still has CV risk (loss of endothelial PGI₂ balance) - similar to non-selective NSAIDs per PRECISION trial.
- Renal effects still present.
- **Sulfa allergy** - cross-reactivity with celecoxib (true sulfa allergy patients).

**Use**: high GI risk + low CV risk who need NSAID. Combine with low-dose aspirin if needed for CV - but adds back GI risk.

## NSAID-specific clinical uses

- **Gout flare**: indomethacin or naproxen (high dose).
- **PDA closure in neonates**: indomethacin or ibuprofen IV (close the duct - PGE₂ keeps it open).
- **Pericarditis**: high-dose NSAID + colchicine.
- **Bartter syndrome**: indomethacin reduces excess PGE.
- **Dysmenorrhea**: ibuprofen, naproxen.
- **Migraine**: high-dose NSAID often effective; combine with triptan if severe.
- **Renal colic**: ketorolac IV equivalent to opioids; preferred for less sedation/respiratory depression.

## Acetaminophen

Mechanism unclear (central COX inhibition + cannabinoid metabolite + serotonergic). Analgesic + antipyretic, **not anti-inflammatory** at typical doses, no GI/platelet/renal effects → safe in pregnancy + most chronic conditions.

Max dose 3-4 g/day (less in liver disease, ethanol, malnutrition). Toxicity = NAPQI hepatic necrosis. Antidote = **N-acetylcysteine**.

## Migraine-specific drugs

### Triptans - 5-HT₁B/₁D agonists

**Sumatriptan, Rizatriptan, Zolmitriptan, Eletriptan, Naratriptan, Frovatriptan, Almotriptan**

**Mechanism**: vasoconstriction of dilated cranial vessels + inhibition of trigeminal neurogenic inflammation + central pain inhibition.

**Use**: acute migraine; abortive (not preventive).

**Side effects**: chest tightness, paresthesias, flushing, mild ↑ BP, drowsiness.

**Contraindications**:
- **CAD, stroke, uncontrolled HTN, prinzmetal angina** - vasoconstriction.
- **Hemiplegic migraine, basilar migraine**.
- Within 24 h of ergot or another triptan.
- MAOI within 14 days (sumatriptan).

### Ergots

**Ergotamine, Dihydroergotamine (DHE)** - 5-HT₁ + α-adrenergic + dopamine agonist; old generation.

**Use**: acute migraine, status migrainosus (DHE IV).

**Side effects**: nausea/vomiting (severe), peripheral vasospasm, ergotism (gangrene with overuse). Many drug interactions (CYP3A4 substrate).

### CGRP antagonists

**Erenumab, Galcanezumab, Fremanezumab, Eptinezumab** - monoclonal antibodies vs CGRP or its receptor; for **prevention**.

**Atogepant, Rimegepant, Ubrogepant** - small-molecule oral; ubrogepant + rimegepant for acute; rimegepant + atogepant for prevention.

**Mechanism**: block CGRP-mediated neurogenic inflammation + vasodilation.

**Pros**: well-tolerated, no vasoconstriction (safer in CV disease than triptans).

**Cons**: expensive; constipation; injection-site reactions.

### Lasmiditan

5-HT₁F agonist (no vasoconstriction). Acute migraine; can drive ≥8 h after dose. Sedation.

### Migraine prevention

- **First-line**: topiramate, propranolol, amitriptyline, valproate.
- **Second-line**: CGRP mAb, OnabotulinumtoxinA (Botox) for chronic migraine (≥15 days/month).
- **Newer oral CGRP** (atogepant): better tolerated than topiramate.

## Skeletal muscle relaxants - brief overview

| Drug | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Baclofen** | GABA-B agonist (spinal) | Spasticity (MS, SCI); withdrawal syndrome if abrupt stop |
| **Tizanidine** | α2 agonist (central) | Spasticity; hepatotoxic |
| **Cyclobenzaprine** | TCA-like; central | Acute musculoskeletal; anticholinergic |
| **Methocarbamol** | Central depression | Acute MSK; sedating |
| **Carisoprodol** | Metabolized to meprobamate (BZD-like) | Abuse potential; avoid |
| **Dantrolene** | Blocks RyR1 → ↓ Ca release from SR | Malignant hyperthermia, NMS, spasticity |
| **Botulinum toxin** | Blocks ACh release at NMJ | Focal spasticity, dystonia, migraine, cosmetic |

## Adjuvant analgesics (neuropathic pain)

- **TCAs** (amitriptyline, nortriptyline) - 1st line diabetic neuropathy, postherpetic neuralgia.
- **SNRIs** (duloxetine, venlafaxine) - diabetic neuropathy, fibromyalgia.
- **Gabapentinoids** (gabapentin, pregabalin) - neuropathic pain, restless legs, fibromyalgia. Renal dose adjust. Misuse potential, especially gabapentin combined with opioids.
- **Carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine** - trigeminal neuralgia first-line.
- **Topical lidocaine** patches - postherpetic neuralgia, localized neuropathic.
- **Topical capsaicin** - depletes substance P; burning at start.

<Clinical>
**Acute musculoskeletal pain** in a young healthy adult - acetaminophen ± topical/oral NSAID. Avoid opioids except brief severe.

**Acute gout flare**: indomethacin 50 mg TID OR naproxen 750 mg then 250 mg TID OR colchicine 1.2 mg → 0.6 mg in 1 h OR oral prednisone 40 mg × 5 days. Pick based on comorbidity (NSAID avoided in CKD/HF; colchicine avoided in renal failure; steroid avoided in poorly controlled diabetes).

**Migraine in pregnancy**: acetaminophen 1st line (acute). Avoid NSAIDs (3rd trimester - PDA closure), triptans (controversial 1st trimester; sumatriptan probably OK), ergots (contraindicated - uterotonic).

**Patient with daily NSAID for OA + on lisinopril**: blood pressure climbs. Stop NSAID, consider topical diclofenac (less systemic), or capsaicin, duloxetine.
</Clinical>
