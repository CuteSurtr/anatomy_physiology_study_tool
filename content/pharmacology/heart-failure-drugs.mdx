## HFrEF (LVEF &lt;40%) - "the four pillars"

Mortality benefit drugs - start ALL four early, titrate to target dose. (GDMT = guideline-directed medical therapy.)

1. **ARNI** (sacubitril/valsartan) or ACE-i/ARB
2. **β-blocker** (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, or bisoprolol)
3. **MRA** (spironolactone or eplerenone)
4. **SGLT2 inhibitor** (dapagliflozin or empagliflozin) - benefit in HFrEF **regardless of diabetes**

### ARNI - the newest pillar

**Sacubitril + valsartan**: combines neprilysin inhibition (↑ natriuretic peptides BNP/ANP → vasodilation, natriuresis) + AT1 blockade.

- **PARADIGM-HF**: 20% mortality reduction vs enalapril.
- Switch from ACE-i → wait 36 h before starting ARNI (angioedema risk).
- Side effects: hypotension, hyperK, AKI, angioedema (less than ACE-i).
- BNP rises (neprilysin block - use NT-proBNP for monitoring instead).

### ACE inhibitors / ARBs

Mortality benefit since CONSENSUS, SOLVD. Goal: titrate to **target dose** in trials (e.g., lisinopril 20–40 mg, enalapril 10–20 BID, ramipril 10).

If ACE-i intolerant due to cough → ARB (valsartan, candesartan, losartan).
If both intolerated → hydralazine + isosorbide dinitrate.

### β-blockers - **only three** with mortality benefit

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Carvedilol** | β + α1; "Start LOW, go SLOW" |
| **Metoprolol succinate** | XR form, daily |
| **Bisoprolol** | β1-selective, daily |

Do NOT use metoprolol tartrate (BID, no XR), atenolol, or short-acting β-blockers for HF.

Start at low dose only when patient is euvolemic/stable; titrate every 2 weeks.

### MRA (spironolactone, eplerenone)

**RALES** (NYHA III-IV, spiro, ↓ mortality 30%). **EMPHASIS-HF** (NYHA II, eplerenone, ↓ mortality 24%).

Watch K (don't start if K >5.0 or Cr >2.5 mg/dL; check at 1 week, 1 month, then quarterly).

### SGLT2 inhibitors

**Dapagliflozin (DAPA-HF), Empagliflozin (EMPEROR-Reduced)** - both reduce HF hospitalization + CV mortality in HFrEF regardless of diabetes.

Mechanism in HF unclear but includes: natriuresis, ↓ preload + afterload, anti-fibrotic, improved myocardial metabolism (ketones), ↓ inflammation.

Side effects: euglycemic DKA (rare in non-diabetic), genital mycotic infections, volume depletion.

## HFrEF - "the second-line" drugs

| Drug | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hydralazine + isosorbide dinitrate (BiDil)** | Add to standard therapy esp. in Black patients; or substitute for ACE-i/ARB intolerance | A-HEFT trial; reduces mortality |
| **Ivabradine** | If on max β-blocker + LVEF ≤35% + HR ≥70 sinus rhythm | If channel blocker in SA node; reduces HF hospitalization (SHIFT) |
| **Digoxin** | Persistent symptoms despite GDMT; A-fib | Reduces hospitalization (no mortality benefit); narrow TI |
| **Vericiguat** | Recent decompensation + low GFR / hi BNP | Soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator (VICTORIA) |
| **Omecamtiv mecarbil** | Investigational cardiac myosin activator | GALACTIC-HF mixed |

## HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%)

For decades nothing improved mortality. Now:
- **SGLT2 inhibitor** (empagliflozin EMPEROR-Preserved, dapagliflozin DELIVER) - reduces HF hospitalization in HFpEF/HFmrEF.
- **MRA** (spironolactone) - TOPCAT trial (mixed results; signal in Americas subgroup).
- **ARNI** - PARAGON-HF (borderline; mostly hospitalization benefit at lower LVEF).
- Diuretics for symptomatic volume overload.
- Treat comorbidities: HTN, A-fib, CAD, obesity.

## Acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF)

### Wet + warm (most common - congestion without shock)
- **IV loop diuretic** (furosemide 1–2× home dose).
- **IV vasodilator**: nitroglycerin (preload), nitroprusside (preload + afterload), nesiritide (rare now).
- O₂ + NIPPV for pulmonary edema.

### Wet + cold (cardiogenic shock with congestion)
- **Inotrope** + diuretic.
- Inotrope choices:
  - **Dobutamine** (β1 > β2) - inotropy + mild vasodilation.
  - **Milrinone** (PDE3 inhibitor) - inotropy + vasodilation; useful with β-blocker resistance + pulmonary HTN.
  - **Norepinephrine** if MAP &lt;65 to maintain perfusion.
- Consider mechanical support (Impella, IABP, ECMO) if pharmacologic failure.

### Dry + cold (low output without congestion)
- Cautious fluid + inotrope.

### Dry + warm (compensated)
- Optimize chronic GDMT.

## Inotropes for inpatient use

| Drug | Receptors | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Dobutamine** | β1 selective | ↑ contractility + HR, mild ↓ SVR |
| **Milrinone** | PDE3 inhibitor → ↑ cAMP | "Inodilator"; arrhythmia + hypotension risk |
| **Levosimendan** | Ca-sensitizer (not FDA approved in US) | Inotropy + vasodilation; long effect after stop |
| **Digoxin** | Na/K-ATPase inhibitor → ↑ intracellular Na → ↓ Na/Ca exchanger → ↑ intracellular Ca | Mild inotropy; HR control |

**β-receptor downregulation** in chronic HF reduces dobutamine efficacy. Milrinone bypasses this (works downstream of receptor).

## Digoxin - the old workhorse

**Mechanism**: blocks myocyte Na/K-ATPase → ↑ Na intracellular → ↓ Na/Ca exchanger activity → ↑ Ca intracellular → ↑ contractility. Also vagal effect → ↓ AV conduction.

**Uses**: HFrEF (symptomatic on GDMT - reduces hospitalization), A-fib rate control (esp. in HF or hypotension).

**Pharmacology**: renal clearance, narrow TI, t½ ~36 h.

**Toxicity (digoxin toxicity to know)**:
- **GI**: nausea, vomiting, anorexia (earliest).
- **CNS**: confusion, **yellow-green visual halos** (chromatopsia).
- **Cardiac**: bradycardia, AV block, **bidirectional VT** (pathognomonic), atrial tachycardia with block.
- **K⁺**: acute toxicity → hyperK (digoxin blocks Na/K-ATPase). Chronic toxicity in setting of hypokalemia (diuretic-induced) → worse arrhythmia.

**Precipitants**: hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypercalcemia, renal failure, hypothyroidism, quinidine, amiodarone, verapamil (↓ digoxin clearance).

**Antidote**: **digoxin immune Fab (Digibind, DigiFab)** for severe toxicity (life-threatening arrhythmia, K >5 in acute overdose). Don't electrically cardiovert in dig toxicity (asystole risk - use low energy if must).

## Ivabradine

**Mechanism**: blocks **If ("funny") channel** in SA node → ↓ HR only (no inotropy/lusitropy effect, no AV slowing).

**Use**: HFrEF (LVEF ≤35%) on max-tolerated β-blocker still with HR ≥70 sinus rhythm.

**Caveat**: useless in A-fib (no SA node firing to slow).

**Side effects**: bradycardia, **luminous phenomena** (phosphenes - flashing lights, transient), A-fib.

## Hydralazine + isosorbide dinitrate

- Hydralazine = arteriolar dilator → ↓ afterload.
- Isosorbide dinitrate = venous dilator → ↓ preload.
- Combined → A-HeFT showed mortality benefit in Black patients with NYHA III-IV.
- Side effects: reflex tachycardia, headache, nitrate tolerance (use asymmetric dosing).

<Clinical>
**Initiation sequence for new HFrEF diagnosis**: start ARNI/ACE-i + low-dose β-blocker simultaneously (or β first if hypotensive). Add MRA + SGLT2i within weeks. Titrate every 2 weeks. Goal: target doses within 3–6 months.

**Patient on diuretic monotherapy for HF for 5 years**: undertreated. Add ACE-i, β-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i sequentially.

**HF + low BP**: still try GDMT - start LOW and go SLOW with all 4 pillars. Hydralazine + ISDN if can't tolerate ACE-i/ARB.

**ICD + CRT** for sudden cardiac death prevention - LVEF ≤35% despite GDMT for 3 months. CRT if QRS ≥130 ms LBBB morphology.
</Clinical>

<HighYield variant="boards">
- "The 4 pillars of HFrEF" = ARNI/ACEi, β-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i.
- **Only 3 β-blockers** with HFrEF mortality benefit: carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol.
- Digoxin reduces hospitalization, NOT mortality. Yellow-green halos + bidirectional VT classic.
- "Visual halos" + GI upset + bradycardia in elderly on diuretic → check dig level.
- Ivabradine works only in sinus rhythm; useless in A-fib.
- ARNI replaces ACE-i. 36-h washout from ACE-i before ARNI. Angioedema box.
- "Cardiogenic shock with adequate MAP" = dobutamine or milrinone.
- "Cardiogenic shock with low MAP" = norepinephrine + inotrope.
- BiDil (hydralazine + ISDN) for HFrEF in Black patients + as ACE/ARB-intolerant alternative.
