## Active TB - first-line therapy

**RIPE** regimen for **fully sensitive** TB:

| Drug | Phase | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Rifampin** | Both 2-month intensive + 4-month continuation | Bactericidal; CYP induction nightmare |
| **Isoniazid** | Both phases | Bactericidal; INH-only resistant TB common - so RIPE all 4 drugs used initially |
| **Pyrazinamide** | Intensive phase only (first 2 months) | Bactericidal; sterilizes "persisters" |
| **Ethambutol** | Intensive phase only | Bacteriostatic; held when sensitivities confirm fully sensitive |

**Duration**: 6 months total (2 intensive + 4 continuation) for pulmonary TB.

**Longer duration**:
- 9 months for CNS TB, bone/joint, miliary, cavitary with persistent culture positivity.
- 9-12 months for HIV-positive with delayed culture conversion.

**Daily preferred over thrice-weekly**; observed therapy (DOT) for adherence.

### Isoniazid (INH)

**Mechanism**: prodrug activated by mycobacterial catalase-peroxidase (KatG) → inhibits **mycolic acid** synthesis.

**Side effects**:
- **Hepatotoxicity** - age-related (children safer); monitor LFT.
- **Peripheral neuropathy** - depletes B6 (pyridoxine) - **co-prescribe B6 25-50 mg/day**.
- **Drug-induced lupus** (slow acetylators - check NAT2).
- **Anion-gap metabolic acidosis** in overdose → seizures (Vitamin B6 antidote IV).
- **Hemolysis in G6PD** (rare).
- **CYP inhibitor** (modest); CYP2E1.

**Resistance**: mutations in KatG (no activation) or InhA (target).

### Rifampin

(See [other antimicrobials](/pharmacology/antimicrobials-other) for full detail.)

- Orange body fluids.
- Massive CYP3A4 induction.
- Hepatotoxicity (combined with INH).
- Use **rifabutin** with HIV PIs (less CYP induction).

### Pyrazinamide

**Mechanism**: activated by bacterial pyrazinamidase → low-pH activity (effective on intracellular bacteria).

**Side effects**:
- **Hyperuricemia** (gout flare).
- **Hepatotoxicity** (combined with INH + rifampin).
- Photosensitivity.
- GI.

### Ethambutol

**Mechanism**: blocks arabinosyl transferase → no arabinogalactan in cell wall.

**Side effects**:
- **Optic neuritis** - decreased visual acuity + red-green color blindness. Reversible if stopped early. **Visual screening before + during**.
- Hyperuricemia.
- Renally cleared → adjust for CKD.

## Side effect quick map

| Drug | "Big" side effect |
|---|---|
| Isoniazid | Neuropathy (B6); hepatitis; lupus |
| Rifampin | Orange fluids; CYP induction; hepatitis |
| Pyrazinamide | Hyperuricemia; hepatitis |
| Ethambutol | Optic neuritis |
| Streptomycin | CN VIII; nephrotoxicity |

## Latent TB

**Indication**: positive **TST (≥5/10/15 mm by risk)** or **IGRA (QuantiFERON-Gold)** + active TB ruled out.

| Regimen | Duration |
|---|---|
| **INH 300 mg/day + B6** | 9 months (gold standard) |
| **INH + rifapentine weekly** ("3HP") | 12 doses (12 weeks; DOT) |
| **Rifampin daily** | 4 months |
| **INH + rifampin daily** | 3-4 months |

3HP and 4R now preferred for shorter duration + better adherence than 9-month INH.

## MDR-TB (multidrug-resistant)

Defined as resistant to at least **INH + rifampin**.

Treatment: long courses with second-line drugs - "BPaL(M)" 6-month regimen approved 2024:
- **Bedaquiline** (diarylquinoline) - mycobacterial ATP synthase inhibitor; **QT prolongation**.
- **Pretomanid** (nitroimidazole) - approved for MDR/XDR.
- **Linezolid** - efficacy in TB; long-course toxicity (neuropathy, marrow).
- **Moxifloxacin** ± (the "M" in BPaL-M).

Plus older second-line:
- **Fluoroquinolones** (levo, moxi).
- **Aminoglycosides** (amikacin) or **capreomycin** (cyclic peptide).
- **Cycloserine** - D-alanine analog; **neuropsychiatric** (seizures, psychosis).
- **Ethionamide** - similar to INH; **GI severe**, hepatitis, hypothyroidism.
- **PAS** (para-aminosalicylic acid) - GI side effects; hypothyroidism.

XDR-TB = MDR-TB + resistance to fluoroquinolone + injectable - **even worse**, longer + harsher.

## TB in HIV

- **All HIV patients screened for TB**.
- Start ART within 2 weeks of TB treatment if CD4 &lt;50; within 8 weeks if higher (avoiding IRIS).
- **Avoid rifampin with protease inhibitors** - use **rifabutin** (less CYP induction).
- Watch for **IRIS** (immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome) - paradoxical worsening on starting ART.

## TB in pregnancy

All first-line drugs except streptomycin (CN VIII to fetus) are safe. Standard RIPE × 6 months. Pyrazinamide controversial historically but now considered acceptable.

## Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC)

**Disseminated MAC** in HIV (CD4 &lt;50) or pulmonary MAC in lung disease.

**Treatment**: **3-drug regimen**:
- **Macrolide** (azithromycin or clarithromycin) - cornerstone.
- **Ethambutol**.
- **Rifampin** (pulmonary) or **rifabutin** (HIV).

Plus aminoglycoside (amikacin or streptomycin) for severe/cavitary disease.

**Duration**: 12 months after culture conversion.

**Prophylaxis** in HIV (CD4 &lt;50): azithromycin 1200 mg weekly. (Not needed if on ART with rising CD4.)

## Leprosy (Hansen disease)

| Form | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Paucibacillary** (TT, BT - tuberculoid) | Rifampin + dapsone × 6 months |
| **Multibacillary** (BB, BL, LL - lepromatous) | Rifampin + dapsone + clofazimine × 12 months |

WHO multidrug therapy (MDT) - drugs supplied free by WHO.

### Drug-specific notes

- **Dapsone**: sulfa-related; G6PD test first → hemolysis. Methemoglobinemia.
- **Clofazimine**: reddish-brown skin discoloration; GI.
- **Rifampin**: as above.

**Erythema nodosum leprosum (ENL)** - type 2 reaction in LL leprosy; treat with **thalidomide** (teratogen - iPLEDGE) or steroids.

**Reversal reaction (type 1)** in borderline forms: corticosteroids.

## TB-INH overdose

INH overdose → refractory seizures + anion-gap acidosis (lactate). Mechanism: INH depletes B6, which is cofactor for GABA synthesis → low GABA → seizures.

Antidote: **IV pyridoxine** (B6) - 1 gram of B6 for each gram of INH ingested, or empiric 5 g IV.

## Therapeutic drug monitoring + adverse event management

| Side effect | Action |
|---|---|
| AST/ALT >3× ULN + symptoms, or >5× ULN no symptoms | Stop hepatotoxic drugs (INH, rifampin, pyrazinamide); reintroduce sequentially |
| Optic neuritis | Stop ethambutol; reversible if caught early |
| Hyperuricemia + gout | NSAID/colchicine; may need to stop pyrazinamide |
| Severe rash | Stop suspect drug; antihistamine; consider sulfa cross |
| Peripheral neuropathy | B6 (if INH); evaluate other causes |

<Clinical>
**TB regimen in elderly patient who develops jaundice after 6 weeks of RIPE**: stop all TB drugs. Once ALT normalizes, reintroduce sequentially over weeks. INH probably the culprit (most hepatotoxic in elderly).

**Pregnant patient with active TB**: standard RIPE × 6 months. PZA acceptable. Avoid streptomycin (CN VIII fetal). After delivery, breastfeeding compatible.

**HIV patient on ART (tenofovir/emtricitabine/dolutegravir) diagnosed with TB**: switch to rifabutin-based regimen if continuing dolutegravir (rifampin would tank dolutegravir levels). Or change ART. Coordinate with ID.

**LTBI in elderly**: weigh risks - 9-month INH has higher hepatotoxicity in elderly. 3HP or 4-month rifampin often preferred.

**MAC pulmonary disease in COPD patient**: azithromycin + ethambutol + rifampin × 12 months after culture conversion. Surgery for localized disease unresponsive.
</Clinical>
